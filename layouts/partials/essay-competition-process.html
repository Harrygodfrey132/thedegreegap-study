How The Degree Gap supports an entry

Tutoring for {{ $comp }}, with the student doing the writing

Every prize on this site asks for the student's own work, and that's the only kind of help worth paying for. A tutor's job here is the one it would be for a hard A-Level essay: help choose a question the student can argue, point at the reading, and mark drafts the way a judge reads them. We don't write essays, and we won't polish one into something the student couldn't defend at interview.

1

Pick the question, then the reading

Most entries go wrong in the first week, when a student picks the question that sounds most impressive rather than the one they can argue inside the word limit. We start with a consultation, usually with Harry or Joe, and match the student with a tutor who read the subject at university. The first session is a diagnostic: which question, what the student already thinks, and the two or three things they should read before they type a sentence.

2

Argue, don't summarise

Judges read hundreds of essays that explain what Locke or Keynes said. They shortlist the ones that take a position and hold it against the best objection. Sessions run on our own platform, with a shared whiteboard and a recording the student can rewatch, and most of the time goes on structure: the claim, the strongest counterargument, and where the evidence actually sits. The student writes every draft.

3

Edit for the reader, then check the rules

The last fortnight is cutting. A 2,000-word limit is a discipline, and so is a bibliography in the format the organiser asks for. The tutor reads the final draft as a judge would, flags the paragraph that repeats itself and the claim with nothing behind it, and the student submits through the official portal themselves, on time, with whatever declaration the competition requires.
