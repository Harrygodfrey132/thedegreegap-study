Why it matters

A competition essay is the best supercurricular a sixth former can do

Not because of the prize. Because it forces a student to pick a question, read past the specification, take a position and defend it in a fixed word count, then hand it to a stranger to judge. Nothing on the A-Level course asks for that.

01

It gives the personal statement something real

Reading-list paragraphs are the weakest part of most statements. An essay the student researched, argued and submitted is evidence, and the argument inside it is what an admissions tutor wants to talk about at interview.

02

It teaches the skill A-Level doesn't

School essays reward coverage. A judged essay rewards one claim, defended against the strongest objection, in 2,000 words or fewer. That's the skill undergraduate work runs on, and most students meet it for the first time in a competition.

03

The deadline does the motivating

Summer term drifts. A submission date months away, with the questions already published, gives a Year 12 a reason to read beyond the syllabus while the exams are still a year off. And a shortlisting is something a school reference can name.
