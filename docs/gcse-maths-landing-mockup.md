# GCSE Maths Landing Page — Optimal Structure & Mockup

Built for paid Google Ads traffic. The whole job of this page is to take a parent who just clicked a paid ad for *"GCSE Maths tutor"* and turn them into a free-consultation booking with Joe or Harry — fast, with high trust, low friction.

## Part 1: Verdict on the 12 pages I analysed

**For paid landing page experience, the winner is Explore Learning.** Their page does five things better than anyone else:

1. Clean above-the-fold with hero image, one H1, one subheading, one CTA
2. Pricing transparency with side-by-side packages (£20–£39 per session)
3. Trust-signal stacking (Trustpilot Excellent, Education Investor Award, named testimonials, 93% pass rate)
4. Free trial as the primary conversion offer (removes commitment friction)
5. Single, repeated CTA throughout the page — "Book a free trial" appears 5+ times

**For premium positioning, the winner is Keystone.** Their tutor-credential density (Oxbridge, hours taught, named students) makes them feel like the most trustworthy option even with zero pricing transparency. But their conversion design is brutal — no clear single CTA, no pricing, no hero image. Wouldn't convert paid traffic well.

**For trust-signal density, Tutorful wins.** 11k tutors, 3.1m lessons, 200k happy students, 81k 5-star reviews, 90% improve by one grade — every credibility hook stacked above the fold.

**Where your current page sits:** 7/10. Strong founder voice. Trust strip with award + reviews + parent quote is good. But the form-first design (asking for phone number before the parent has been sold on the idea) is brutal for paid traffic, and the page below the form is mostly a thin tutor showcase and FAQ. Not enough storytelling, not enough emotional resonance, no clear "what changes for my child" section.

## Part 2: Patterns that win on paid landing pages

Across the 12 pages, these are the consistent winners:

1. **Hero image of an actual person** (student, tutor, parent — not a stock library)
2. **Single primary CTA above the fold**, repeated 4-6 times on the page
3. **Pricing transparency** — even just "from £X/hr" beats hiding it
4. **Free trial / free consultation** as the offer, not the actual booking
5. **Trust signal stacking** — Trustpilot/Google + award + named testimonials, all visible without scrolling
6. **Real outcomes with named students** — "Daljit's son: 2 grades higher than mock" beats "98% pass rate"
7. **Founder/team presence** — agencies that feel like a real team convert better than directories
8. **Diagnostic-first methodology** — explaining *how* the tutoring works builds trust
9. **Objection-handling FAQ** — addresses cost, tutor fit, timeline, format

And the consistent losers:

1. **Hidden pricing** (Keystone, Hampstead & Frognal) — kills paid conversion
2. **Form-first hero** (your current page) — asks for commitment before building trust
3. **Massive tutor directory** (Sherpa, Tutorful) — overwhelming for parents who want guidance
4. **Stock photos** (Tutorspot, Tooting) — kills authenticity
5. **Generic value props** — "personalised tutoring", "world-class" — invisible to parents

## Part 3: Strategic positioning for The Degree Gap's page

Three principles drive every design choice:

**1. You are not a marketplace.** Don't try to compete with MyTutor or Tutorful on tutor volume. Your differentiator is the consultation-first, founder-vetted match. Lean into it. Show 3-5 tutors as a preview, not a grid of 50.

**2. The free consultation is the primary CTA, not booking a tutor.** Parents on paid traffic aren't ready to book — they're researching. The page's only job is to turn them into a phone call with Joe or Harry. Everything funnels toward that.

**3. Show, don't tell the founder vetting.** Don't write "we vet our tutors carefully" — write *"Joe and Harry personally interview every tutor on the platform. Only 3% of applicants make it through."* The specifics are what convert.

**Sample answers (use your existing FAQ bank as the source, then sharpen):**

> **How do I know if my child actually needs a tutor?**
> Grades slipping despite effort. Confidence dropping. Putting the work in but not seeing the result. Those are the clearest signs. Sometimes it's simpler than that — some students just want to go further than the classroom pace allows. Either is a good reason to get in touch. We will tell you honestly on the call if we don't think tutoring is the right move.

> **What if my child does not get on with the first tutor?**
> Tell us and we will sort it. No awkward conversation, no process. We would rather find the right match than have a student sitting through sessions that aren't working.

> **How long until we see a grade improvement?**
> Most students notice something within the first few sessions — not always in grades straight away, but in how the subject feels. Less daunting. Clearer. Grade improvements usually follow within a half term of regular weekly sessions.

> **Do you do GCSE Maths in person or just online?**
> All sessions run through Lessonspace, our shared whiteboard. The tutor can mark a past paper on screen and walk your child through it line by line. Most students concentrate better one-to-one online than they do in a class of thirty. We do not do in-person.

> **What if my child has tried tutoring before and it did not work?**
> Tell us what happened. We hear this a lot. Usually it's that the tutor didn't actually diagnose the gap — they just covered content the school had already covered. We start with a diagnostic past paper, find the actual gap, and build from there.

> **Can the first session really be free?**
> Yes. We would rather you see whether the tutor is right for your child than have them sit through paid sessions that don't work. No catch, no card details upfront.

## Part 5: Mobile considerations

For paid Google Ads traffic, mobile is 70%+ of clicks. Key mobile rules:

1. **Hero must fit one screen** — H1 + subhead + 1 CTA, image collapses below CTAs
2. **Trust strip becomes a single horizontal scrollable row** on mobile (not two lines)
3. **Tutor cards and grade-jump cards become carousels**, not grids
4. **Sticky bottom CTA bar** — once the user scrolls past the hero, a sticky bar appears at the bottom with `[Book free call]` and `[Call now]` buttons. This is the highest-converting pattern on mobile paid traffic.
5. **FAQ accordions stay collapsed by default** — don't auto-expand on mobile, it bloats scroll
6. **Form fields use proper input types** — `tel` for phone, autocomplete attributes set, so iOS/Android prefill works
7. **The WhatsApp nudge** (the existing one) loads at 3 seconds — keep it, it's good for mobile

## Part 6: Conversion path

Single primary action throughout: **book a free 15-minute call with Harry**.

The page intentionally has only ONE primary CTA repeated 6+ times. Secondary CTAs (phone, WhatsApp) are always present but stylistically smaller. There is no "view profile", "see all tutors", "browse subjects" CTA competing for attention.

**The funnel from paid click:**
1. Ad: "GCSE Maths Tutors | Founder-Vetted | Free 15-min Call"
2. Land on this page → hero matches the ad copy
3. Scroll → confirms with 9 more sections of trust-building
4. Click the CTA → form with 3 fields → confirmation message → email + WhatsApp follow-up
5. Phone call with Harry within 24 hours → conversion to a paying client

Expected metrics (rough baseline):
- Bounce rate: under 50% (current paid landing pages of this quality typically 35-50%)
- Click-through to CTA form: 8-12% of landing visitors
- Form completion to call booked: 70-80%
- Call to conversion: 25-35% (your existing close rate, you tell me)

## Part 8: What we deliberately don't include

These appear on competitor pages and we leave them off:

- **Tutor directory / "see all tutors" link** — we are not a marketplace
- **Multiple subject cross-sells in the hero** — confuses paid traffic intent
- **Video testimonials** — production cost is high and Google Ads parents don't watch videos before clicking
- **Stock photography** — kills authenticity
- **Comparison tables with named competitors** — looks defensive
- **Cancellation policy mid-page** — handle in FAQ, not as a section
- **Money-back guarantee headlines** — the free first session does the same job with less risk to you
- **Blog recommendations** — dilutes the conversion path
