# Story

The founders' origin story and how to write from it. This is the human credibility behind The Degree Gap: two people who were tutored themselves, saw first-hand the difference the right match made, and built the company they wished existed. Where `stats.md` holds the hard credentials, this file holds the narrative and the voice it should give the writing.

## The origin (facts)

- The Degree Gap is run by **Harry Godfrey and Joe Clark, childhood friends**.
- **Both were tutored growing up, and both had two tutors: one excellent and one only okay.** So each of them lived the contrast first-hand and learned early that it isn't tutoring in the abstract that works, it's the *match*. Personalisation and the right fit are the whole difference.
- **Harry's tutoring made him fall in love with learning**, and that changed how he revised. His maths tutor taught him strategies, and far more of the curriculum than a student is "meant" to know, and he went on to beat his GCSE targets across several subjects.
- **Joe also scored strongly across the board.**
- **Both had A-Level Economics tutoring, and both achieved A\*AA after being predicted lower.** They credit a large part of that to tutoring, and the pattern was consistent target-beating, not a one-off. **Harry went on to study Economics at the LSE.**
- **At university they both got back into tutoring** and found they loved helping others the way they had been helped.
- Before The Degree Gap, **Harry ran an undergraduate tutoring agency** filling subject gaps for university economics students. Between them they had worked with many students.
- They **built The Degree Gap together to broaden that work all the way from GCSE to university**, and to specialise in personal statement guidance.

## What they do now

- Work with schools across the UK.
- Run **webinars four times a year**.
- Write a lot, and are **in schools during the day running workshops**, including "Dragon's Den" revision workshops, staying close to what students actually go through day to day.
- Run the tutoring company in the evenings.
- **Long-term vision:** to be recognised educational experts and a trusted authority in the tutoring space, not just a matching service.

## How to use this in writing

- **Lead credibility copy with lived experience, not operator credentials.** The strongest answer to "why trust you" is "we were the kid with the tutor." Founders who were students who benefited, not businesspeople who spotted a market. This is the emotional root of the brand.
- **The core belief is a changed relationship with learning, not a grade bump.** Harry "fell in love with learning" and the grades followed. Reach for that: the right tutor changes how a student *feels* about a subject first. Pairs with `beliefs.md` ("confidence comes before the grade", "we match, not assign").
- **The match is the whole thesis.** They each had two tutors, one excellent and one only okay, and that lived contrast is where "we match, not assign" and consultation-first come from. Use it to justify why families choose profiles and get re-matched without friction, rather than being auto-assigned.
- **Frame outcomes as beating predictions.** "Predicted lower, beat the target" is the on-brand outcome shape (bands, not points, per `beliefs.md`). The founders' own A\*AA is *their* story, quoted as personal experience, and is never implied as a client outcome or a promise.
- **Founder-led and first-person.** "Harry and Joe", "we". Observational and warm (see `tone.md`). This backstory is where the "we've watched", "we've sat across the kitchen table" intimacy is actually earned, because they have.
- **Community and authority, when the page calls for it.** Schools, workshops, webinars, and writing position them as educators who give back and stay close to students, not a faceless agency. Use in credibility and bigger-picture sections.
- **Where it fits:** About and founder sections, "meet your hosts", why-us and credibility blocks, consultation framing, webinar and school-partnership pages, personal statement pages (where the founders' own university journey lands with the audience). Keep it **out of pain-led heroes** — heroes lead with the parent's worry, not our backstory (per `beliefs.md`).

## Guardrails

- The A\* and grade specifics are the **founders' own**, told as their experience. Never phrase them as something a client will get. No grade promises (`beliefs.md`, `tone.md`).
- Still **an agency, not a principal**, and still **online-only** — the story doesn't change how the business is described (`beliefs.md`).
- **No school snobbery.** The "one good tutor, one not so good" contrast is about tutor match, never about schools being better or worse.
- Hard, citable numbers (LSE, 5,000+ hours, Forbes/Independent, awards) live in `stats.md` — pull credentials from there, use this file for the narrative and voice.
