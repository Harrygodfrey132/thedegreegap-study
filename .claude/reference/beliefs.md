# Beliefs

What The Degree Gap thinks is true about tutoring. These shape every page.

## On tutoring itself

- **The first session is diagnostic.** A good tutor finds the gap before designing the plan. Racing through the textbook from session one is the wrong move.
- **Gap-tracing beats curriculum-marching.** Most struggles in Year 11 trace back to a Year 8 or 9 topic nobody re-explained. The plan rebuilds from that root, not from where the syllabus is today.
- **Exam-board matching matters.** AQA Maths and Edexcel Maths are not the same paper. We match tutor to spec, not just subject.
- **Exam technique is half of grade movement.** Knowing the content and writing it in 1h 30m are different skills. Past papers, marked the way examiners mark, are most of the work.
- **The classroom isn't the problem; the class size is.** Class of 32, teacher dealing with disruption, pace set by the middle — that's the gap a tutor fills. We never attack teachers or schools.

## On who we are

- **We're online-only, and we mean it.** Every session runs through Lessonspace. We don't do "in person", "face-to-face" or "home visits". Online means a student gets matched with the right specialist in the country, not the nearest one geographically.
- **We match, not assign.** Families see profiles. If a tutor isn't the right fit, we re-match without friction.
- **Price is from £37/hour, set per tutor.** The rate depends on subject and tutor experience and is agreed before booking.
- **All tutors are graduates,** many from Russell Group universities. Every tutor is vetted on subject knowledge, how they explain things, and how they work with young people. A degree alone isn't enough.

## On the audience

- **Parents are the buyer.** Pages should make a parent nod, not a teenager. The student is the user; the parent is the one searching, comparing, and paying.
- **Most parents are not at the niche end.** Most aren't applying to Oxbridge or HSBC. Hero copy speaks to broadly-felt pain (predicted grades, mocks, the GCSE-to-A-Level jump). Niche framings (Medicine, Russell Group A*, degree apprenticeships) belong in body sections, not the hero.
- **The pain is specific, not abstract.** A parent doesn't search "we need tutoring". They search "GCSE Maths tutor [city]" because they've watched a specific topic slip for three months. Pages should reflect that specificity.

## On exam outcomes

- **We don't promise grades.** Never. Pages quote real grade jumps from real reviews, never fabricated numbers.
- **Grade movement happens in bands, not points.** A predicted B into a predicted A. A grade 5 into a confident 7. Not "+3.7 grade points".
- **Confidence comes before the grade.** Most parents notice tone-of-voice change before they see a mark scheme change. We mention this, but we don't lead with it — leading with "confidence" reads soft and unverifiable.

## On the site's purpose

- **Each page targets one search intent.** Location pages target *"[city] tutors"*. Level pages target *"[city] GCSE/A-Level tutors"*. We don't try to rank a single page for everything.
- **Local detail is the moat.** Naming Sandringham School, Adastral Park, Rolls-Royce Sinfin, the Russell Group conditional offer pattern — that's what makes a St Albans page un-copyable to Derby. Generic copy is wasted copy.
- **Reviews are the trust anchor.** Real, verbatim, never paraphrased. Anchored grade-jump cards must cite the same person whose review provided the jump.

## What we don't believe

- That schools are failing. They aren't — they're stretched.
- That tutoring is a luxury. For specific gaps and specific stakes, it's the most efficient intervention available.
- That more sessions = more results. Weekly hour-long, consistent, with a clear plan beats irregular crammed blocks.
- That a parent wants a corporate experience. They want a knowledgeable person on the other end of an email who knows what their child needs.
