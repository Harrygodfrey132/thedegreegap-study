# FAQ bank for location, level and level-subject pages

The candidate pool for FAQ blocks. Each location-level-subject page picks **4-5 from this bank**, NOT all of them. Rules for picking are at the bottom of this file.

Every entry has:
- **Tag** — one of `GEO` (geographic / locally-specific), `AEO` (answer-engine optimised, generic process or comparison question), `E.E.A.T` (expertise, experience, authority, trust), `Conversion` (objection handler, cost, fit).
- **Fits** — which page types the question applies to (GCSE, A-Level, both, subject-agnostic, subject-specific).
- **Question** (with `{Location}`, `{Level}`, `{Subject}` placeholders).
- **Answer skeleton** — what the answer should cover. Always rewrite in the page's voice and fill in specifics (real schools, real exam boards, real grade thresholds, real local employers). Never use the skeleton verbatim.

## GEO — Geographic and locally-anchored

### G01 — Sixth-form grade requirements

**Fits:** GCSE all subjects
**Q:** What grade do you need for A-Level {Subject} at {Location} sixth forms?
**A skeleton:** Open with the typical grade requirement at the city's strongest sixth forms (name 3-4 by name). For Maths and Sciences this is usually grade 7 minimum, with Further/Triple often asking 8. For Humanities the threshold is often grade 6 or strong 6. End with the 12-16 week window from current grade to target.

### G02 — Local attainment context

**Fits:** GCSE all subjects
**Q:** How does GCSE {Subject} performance in {Location} compare to the national average?
**A skeleton:** Place the LA-level attainment qualitatively (above / close to / below national). For high-achieving areas, frame the challenge as competing for grade 7+. For low-achieving areas, frame as foundations-to-higher-tier shift. Never cite specific Progress 8 or Attainment 8 numbers without a verified gov.uk source.

### G03 — Online vs in-person

**Fits:** any location, any level, any subject
**Q:** Is {Level} {Subject} tutoring in {Location} available in person or only online?
**A skeleton:** All sessions run online via Lessonspace. Frame the upside as: matched on exam board not postcode. Mention the local catchment villages or postcodes ("{Location}, plus families in {nearby village 1}, {nearby village 2}, {postcode pattern} all access the same network"). For commuter towns, mention the no-school-night-drive angle.

### G04 — Local apprenticeship / employer pathways

**Fits:** GCSE and A-Level, where the location has named employers nearby
**Q:** Can a {Location} {Level} {Subject} tutor help my child apply for a {named local employer} apprenticeship?
**A skeleton:** Yes. Name the employer's typical predicted-grade threshold (most degree apprenticeships ask AAB at A-Level or grade 5 in core GCSEs). Reference the application essay component if relevant.

### G05 — Hardest topic in this subject locally

**Fits:** GCSE and A-Level, named-subject pages
**Q:** What is the hardest topic in {Level} {Subject} for {Location} students?
**A skeleton:** Name 2-3 specific topic blocks that decide top marks for this subject in this region's typical exam boards. For high-achieving areas, lean into top-mark-band pain. For low-achieving areas, lean into the foundation-to-higher tier shift.

### G06 — Compare to neighbouring catchment

**Fits:** locations with a clear neighbouring high-attainment area (e.g. Watford → St Albans, Norwich → Cambridge)
**Q:** Is the {Level} {Subject} standard in {Location} similar to {nearby high-attainment city}?
**A skeleton:** Acknowledge the difference honestly. Frame the local approach without disparaging either area. Useful for areas with comparison anxiety.

## AEO — Answer-engine and process questions

### A01 — Sessions to grade improvement

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How many {Level} {Subject} tutoring sessions does it typically take to improve a grade?
**A skeleton:** 8-12 weekly sessions for one grade band, provided practice is happening between lessons. Faster if the gap is exam technique (question style, mark scheme), slower if foundational (gap from earlier years). Mention the first-session diagnostic that shapes the plan from session two.

### A02 — Exam board differences

**Fits:** all pages, but especially GCSE Maths, Sciences, English
**Q:** What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR {Level} {Subject}, and does it matter for tutoring?
**A skeleton:** Yes, it matters at higher grade boundaries. Core content overlaps; question style, mark scheme structure and weighting of certain topic blocks differs. Name one specific paper or topic-block difference between two of the boards. End with "we confirm the exam board in the consultation call".

### A03 — When to start in Year 11

**Fits:** GCSE pages
**Q:** When is the best time to start {Level} {Subject} tutoring in Year 11?
**A skeleton:** September to February of Year 11, before March mocks set sixth-form predicted grades. Name the local sixth forms that use mock results for conditional offers. Acknowledge that April/May start is still useful for shorter highest-yield programmes.

### A04 — Year 10 start

**Fits:** GCSE pages
**Q:** My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start {Level} {Subject} tutoring?
**A skeleton:** Year 10 is ideal, especially for grade 8/9 targets. Time to close foundation gaps before Year 11 content stacks on top. Year 10 tutoring is more concept-building than exam-focused.

### A05 — Year 12 vs Year 13 start

**Fits:** A-Level pages
**Q:** Year 12 or Year 13, when's the right time to start {Level} {Subject} tutoring in {Location}?
**A skeleton:** Year 12 most common, especially where Maths/Sciences are the worry. Closes the GCSE-to-A-Level jump before Year 12 mocks set Year 13 trajectory. After Christmas of Year 13 is too late for most UCAS predicted grade windows.

### A06 — Topic diagnosis

**Fits:** GCSE and A-Level, subject pages
**Q:** How can I tell which {Level} {Subject} topics my child is losing marks on?
**A skeleton:** The signal is question-number patterns on a recent mock paper. Map the typical question-block structure for this subject's exam (e.g. for AQA Higher Maths Paper 1: Q1-10 arithmetic, Q11-16 ratio/geometry, Q17-22 algebra/circle theorems/iterative methods). If marks are consistently lost in the top block, it's exam technique not subject knowledge.

### A07 — Why is my child struggling with this subject

**Fits:** GCSE pages
**Q:** Why is my child struggling with {Level} {Subject}?
**A skeleton:** Trace the typical foundation moment for this subject (e.g. Maths: Year 7 fractions or Year 8 negatives; English: not learning to plan an essay; Sciences: rote-learning content without method). Tutor's week-one job is finding which moment it was.

### A08 — Inside a typical session

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** What happens in a typical {Level} {Subject} tutoring session?
**A skeleton:** 60-minute session in three parts. First 15 min: review last week's homework, mark, surface misconceptions. Middle 30 min: new content or technique drill on the topic bleeding most marks. Final 15 min: exam-paper practice on that topic with mark scheme walkthrough.

### A09 — Homework expectations

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How much {Level} {Subject} homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?
**A skeleton:** 60-90 minutes between weekly sessions. Usually 2-3 past-paper question sets on the topic from that lesson. More than 90 min hits diminishing returns.

### A10 — Tutor vs revision alone

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How do I know if my child needs a {Level} {Subject} tutor or just more revision?
**A skeleton:** Diagnostic is what their working looks like on a recent past paper. Clean working but losing marks at top of paper = exam technique gap (tutor fastest fix). Blank or crossed-out working on multi-step questions = method confidence gap (tutor essential). Already 80%+ across mocks = targeted practice, not a full programme.

### A11 — Calculator vs non-calculator (Maths-specific)

**Fits:** GCSE Maths
**Q:** Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper for GCSE Maths?
**A skeleton:** Both decide the grade, weighted equally. The non-calculator paper rewards arithmetic fluency and algebraic manipulation; the calculator paper rewards problem-solving and accuracy on multi-step calculations. Most students lose more marks on the non-calculator paper at top of paper, more on Paper 3 calculator at the back. A tutor's diagnostic identifies which paper is bleeding more marks first.

### A12 — Foundation vs higher tier (GCSE-only, subject-applicable)

**Fits:** GCSE Maths, Sciences
**Q:** Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE {Subject}?
**A skeleton:** Higher tier caps at grade 9, floors at grade 4 (sometimes 3). Foundation caps at grade 5, floors at 1. If a student is sitting at a borderline 5 / 6 in Year 10 mocks, higher tier is usually right but needs targeted preparation. If at a 3 / 4, foundation reduces risk. A tutor's role around this decision is the diagnostic in Year 10 to recommend confidently.

### A13 — Multi-subject tutor

**Fits:** GCSE and A-Level
**Q:** Can the same tutor help with both {Level} {Subject} and another subject?
**A skeleton:** Some tutors cover adjacent subjects (Maths-Physics-Computer Science, English Lang and Lit, Biology-Chemistry). Most tutors specialise. For students taking triple Science with Maths, a Maths-Physics tutor often gives a small coordination advantage. For Maths plus English or Humanities, you'll want separate specialists.

### A14 — Predicted grade vs actual

**Fits:** GCSE and A-Level
**Q:** Why is my child's {Level} {Subject} predicted grade lower than I expected?
**A skeleton:** Predicted grades come from school mock performance plus teacher judgement. The most common gap parents notice is between what the student knows in class and what they can perform under timed mock conditions. A tutor's first job is reading recent mock papers to find the difference, then rebuilding under-pressure performance week by week.

### A15 — How long is a session

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How long is each {Level} {Subject} tutoring session?
**A skeleton:** Most sessions are 60 minutes. Some Year 12/13 A-Level sessions run to 90 minutes for content-heavy subjects (Sciences, Maths). For Year 10 and younger, 45-minute sessions sometimes work better. Discussed in the consultation call.

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How do you vet your {Level} {Subject} tutors?
**A skeleton:** Roughly one in eight applicants makes it onto the platform. Written application, subject knowledge test, structured interview on clarity under pressure, reference checks.

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** Are your {Level} {Subject} tutors qualified teachers or university students?
**A skeleton:** Both, depending on match. Network includes qualified secondary teachers, Russell Group graduates, and current undergrads/postgrads who recently sat the exact exam. For top-of-band students, a high-achieving recent graduate often has the advantage on current mark scheme detail.

### E03 — SEN / neurodiversity

**Q:** Can a {Level} {Subject} tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?
**A skeleton:** Yes. Matching always asks about learning differences. Many tutors specialise in working with neurodivergent students. Lessonspace's visual whiteboard handles chunked explanations and explicit scaffolds well. Mention it in the consultation call and we match accordingly.

### E04 — Industry credentials

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** What credentials does The Degree Gap have as a tutoring agency?
**A skeleton:** Voted Tutoring Specialists of the Year at the 2025 and 2026 Prestige Awards. 100+ five-star Google reviews. Co-founders include an LSE Economics graduate. Founders speak at schools and run webinars for thousands of parents.

### E05 — Track record on grade lifts

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** What grade improvements have your {Level} {Subject} tutors actually delivered?
**A skeleton:** Reference specific named-student grade jumps that match the page's theme (high-achieving page: 7 to 9 jumps; low-achieving page: 4 to 6 jumps). Honesty matters here: don't promise outcomes, describe what's typical.

## Conversion — Objection handlers, cost, fit, friction

### C01 — Tutor fit guarantee

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** What happens if the tutor is not the right fit for my child?
**A skeleton:** Free 15-minute video meeting with the recommended tutor before any paid session. If first session doesn't feel right, swap within 24 hours, no charge. Roughly one in eight applicants makes the platform, so there's depth in the network.

### C02 — Cost

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How much does {Level} {Subject} tutoring cost in {Location}, and what is included?
**A skeleton:** From £37/hr. Rate varies by tutor experience and is agreed before any session. Hourly rate covers live session, prep time, resources/practice, post-lesson feedback. No materials fees, no joining fee, no contract.

### C03 — Worth the money

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** Is {Level} {Subject} tutoring actually worth the money?
**A skeleton:** Honest answer: depends on the gap. For students below their target band, a two-band lift usually unlocks bigger doors (sixth-form access, university route eligibility) that justify the cost. For students cruising at target, the marginal lift is smaller. The consultation call is built to give the honest answer including "tutoring isn't what your child needs right now" when true.

### C04 — Budget option

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** Can I do {Level} {Subject} tutoring on a budget?
**A skeleton:** Start with one weekly session in Year 10 and add a second only in the 8 weeks before mocks, rather than two sessions year-round. Or shorter intensive block (6-8 weekly sessions targeting one topic block) for borderline-grade situations.

### C05 — Holiday sessions

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** Can my child have {Level} {Subject} tutoring during school holidays?
**A skeleton:** Yes. Holiday weeks are often where the most focused progress happens, especially intensive blocks at half-term and Easter for Year 11 / Year 13. Online format means scheduling stays flexible.

### C06 — Parent observation

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** Can I sit in on the {Level} {Subject} tutoring session?
**A skeleton:** Yes, most tutors welcome it for the first session. Most parents step back after that because the student opens up more without an audience. You'll still see what was covered via post-session notes.

### C07 — Child resistance

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** What if my child doesn't want a tutor?
**A skeleton:** Most common worry, most important to handle gently. Students often resist before they meet the tutor, then engage within 2-3 sessions because it's not school-shaped (one adult, full attention, no comparison). Free 15-min video meeting lets your child meet the tutor first. If they genuinely don't want to continue after one or two sessions, pause without question. Their decision.

### C08 — Results day fail

**Fits:** GCSE pages
**Q:** What happens if my child gets their {Level} results and didn't hit the predicted grade?
**A skeleton:** We work with families through results day and the November resit window. 8-week block in September/October typically lifts grade 3 to 4, or 4 to 5 (most common resit targets). For students who needed grade 7 for sixth form but landed 6, we work with the sixth form to put a remediation plan in place.

### C09 — Cancellation / commitment

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How do I cancel or pause {Level} {Subject} tutoring?
**A skeleton:** No contract. Pause any time with 24-48 hours notice for the next session. Pay only for sessions taken. No exit fees, no commitment beyond the next week.

### C10 — Match speed

**Fits:** all pages
**Q:** How quickly can my child start {Level} {Subject} tutoring in {Location}?
**A skeleton:** Usually within a few days. Free 15-min consultation, then 2-3 tutor profiles within 24 hours, free 15-min meeting with chosen tutor, first paid session typically inside a week. Most {Location} families are matched within 24 hours of the consultation call.

## Selection rules (when building a page, follow these)

**Pick 4-5 FAQs per page. Never more than 6.**

**The mandatory mix:**
1. **One GEO question** — local-relevance signal for both Google local SEO and AI answer engines looking for geographically-anchored authority. (G01-G06)
2. **One or two AEO questions** — at least one process/comparison question for Featured Snippet and AI Overview pickup. (A01-A15)
3. **One Conversion question** — objection handler at the bottom of the page. (C01-C10)
4. **One E.E.A.T question** — trust signal, ideally early. (E01-E05)

**Rotation across sibling pages:**
- No two same-location pages (e.g. GCSE Maths St Albans and GCSE English St Albans) should share more than 2 FAQs. Pick different GEO/AEO angles for each subject.
- No two same-subject pages across cities (GCSE Maths St Albans vs GCSE Maths Norwich) should share more than 3 FAQs. The G01-G06 questions vary naturally because the schools and attainment context differ; the AEO and E.E.A.T questions can be the same.
- Track picks in a comment at the bottom of each frontmatter for future audit.

**Order on the page:**
1. Lead with the GEO question (highest local signal for Google + AI engines).
2. Then 1-2 AEO process/comparison questions (Featured Snippet pickup).
3. Then E.E.A.T (trust signal).
4. Close with the Conversion question (objection handler before the final CTA).

**Theme alignment:**
- For a high-achieving-area page (Theme A), prefer FAQs that talk about top mark band, grade 7+, sixth-form competition, and grade 8/9 stretch.
- For a low-achieving-area page (Theme B), prefer FAQs about foundation gaps, the foundation-to-higher tier shift, and grade 4/5 lift.
- For a mixed area page (Theme C), pick FAQs that handle both ends.

**Answer length:**
- 60-110 words per answer. Long enough to be useful, short enough to be skimmed.
- Lead with the direct answer in the first sentence (Featured Snippet rule).
- Then the specifics (local schools, exam boards, named topics).
