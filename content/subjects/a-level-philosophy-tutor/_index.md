faq_items:
  - q: "Do the tutors know the exam?"
    a: "Yes. In England, A-Level Philosophy is set by AQA, so our tutors work to that course. There are two papers. The first covers epistemology, the study of knowledge, and moral philosophy. The second covers the metaphysics of God and the metaphysics of mind."
  - q: "Why does my child lose marks on the short questions?"
    a: "Because the short questions want precision, not a general idea. Asked to define knowledge or set out an argument, your child needs the exact wording and every step in order. Students who understand a topic well still drop marks by being vague. A tutor helps them learn tight definitions and practise writing arguments as numbered premises leading to a conclusion."
  - q: "What can a tutor do about the 25-mark essays?"
    a: "By showing your child how to argue for one side the whole way through. A strong essay states its view early, explains the key arguments clearly, raises the best objection and says whether it works. Many essays list views without ever deciding. The tutor marks practice essays and helps your child make each paragraph push the argument forward."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with moral philosophy?"
    a: "Yes. Your child has to explain utilitarianism, Kant and Aristotle's virtue ethics, apply them to practical issues, and handle meta-ethics, the questions about what moral words mean. That's a lot to keep straight. The tutor helps them see how each theory works, where it struggles, and how to use it on a real question."
  - q: "The God and mind topics feel abstract. Is that normal?"
    a: "Very. The ontological argument and the debate between dualism and physicalism can feel like word puzzles at first. The tutor slows it down, takes one argument at a time and asks your child to explain it back in their own words. Once the structure is clear, the objections make far more sense."
