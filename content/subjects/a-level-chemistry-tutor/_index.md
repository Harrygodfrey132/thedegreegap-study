  - q: "Why is the jump from GCSE to A-Level Chemistry so hard?"
    a: "Because almost everything gets deeper at once. Bonding turns into orbitals, energetics becomes Born-Haber cycles and entropy, and organic chemistry starts asking for mechanisms with curly arrows. Lots of students who found GCSE fine hit this in the first term of Year 12 and think they've lost it. They haven't. A tutor works through that step up at your child's pace, not the whole class's."
  - q: "How much maths is there in A-Level Chemistry?"
    a: "More than most families expect. At least a fifth of the marks are maths, from logs for pH and Ka to rate equations and the numbers in a Born-Haber cycle. If rearranging equations never quite clicked at GCSE, it shows up here. A tutor teaches the maths inside the Chemistry, so the numbers stop quietly costing marks."
  - q: "My child knows the content but the longer answers keep losing marks. Why?"
    a: "It's the thing we hear most about A-Level Chemistry. Usually the knowledge is there, but the answer describes when the question asked your child to explain or evaluate, and those are marked differently. A tutor marks past papers the way an examiner does, shows your child exactly where the marks slipped, and practises the wording until the answer earns what they already know."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. Our tutors cover AQA, Edexcel, OCR A and OCR B (Salters), and we'll pick tutors who know your child's one. The boards set similar chemistry but frame the calculations and the practical questions in their own way. Not sure which board it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can help you work it out on the call."

The practicals themselves happen at school, but the marks for them are in the written papers, on method, errors and results, and that's exactly what a tutor can practise online. Every lesson has a replay too."
