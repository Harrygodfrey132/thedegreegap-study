faq_items:
  - q: "My child failed GCSE Maths. What happens next?"
    a: "If they're staying in education until 18, they'll carry on with Maths at college or sixth form and sit it again, either in November or next summer. Before anything else, ask the school for the mark, not just the grade. It tells you how far off your child really was, and that decides almost everything else."
  - q: "They've failed it three times now. Is it worth trying again?"
    a: "Yes, but not in the same way. If every attempt so far has been a resit class and a pile of past papers, another round of that usually ends in the same place. What tends to shift it is someone sitting with your child's papers, spotting the few topics and habits that keep costing marks, and working on just those. Lots of students get their 4 on the third or fourth go."
  - q: "How many students fail GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Far more than most parents realise. In summer 2025, around 207,000 students aged 17 and over sat GCSE Maths in England, and only about one in six got a grade 4 or above (JCQ figures). So a second or third attempt puts your child in a very big group. Resit classes tend to be large and the course is short, which is exactly where one-to-one time helps."
  - q: "Our child failed both Maths and English. Should they resit both at once?"
    a: "Not automatically. Two resits in the same few weeks is a lot to carry, especially on top of a new college course. It's often better to go for the closer one first, get it done, and give the other a proper run at the next series. One clean pass beats two near misses."
  - q: "When is the next GCSE Maths resit?"
    a: "This year's November exams are in the first full week of November, but entries closed in early October. After that it's summer 2027. The GCSE exams run from 10 May to 18 June, and results day is Thursday 19 August. The school, college or exam centre will give you the exact paper dates for your child's board."
  - q: "Can my child resit GCSE Maths privately?"
    a: "Yes. You book the exam as a private candidate at a school, college or exam centre that accepts them, and pay the centre's fee. It's worth ringing a few, because fees vary a lot. Book early, too, as summer entries usually close in February or March."

    a: "Yes, at any age, and it's something parents ask us about more often than you'd think. Adults aged 19 and over without a grade 4 can usually study for the GCSE free at a local college, so that's worth checking first. We can match you with a tutor too, alongside a college course or instead of one."
