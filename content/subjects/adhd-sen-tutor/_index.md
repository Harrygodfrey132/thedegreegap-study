faq_items:
  - q: "Do you cover where we live?"
    a: "Yes, we cover the whole of the UK. All our lessons are online, so it doesn't matter whether you're in a city or a village. It also means we can pick the tutor who's the best fit for your child's needs, rather than whoever happens to live nearby, and your child can learn from somewhere they feel settled."
  - q: "My child has ADHD and finds it hard to focus. How does an hour of tutoring work?"
    a: "It doesn't have to be one long, unbroken hour. The tutor breaks the work into short pieces your child can finish, builds in a break to move when it's needed, and keeps one thing on screen at a time. Lots of children find a calm one-to-one lesson much easier to stay with than a busy classroom."
  - q: "Have the tutors worked with dyslexia, autism or other additional needs before?"
    a: "Yes. Tell us about your child on the call and we'll pick tutors who've worked with children with similar needs before and know how to adapt. Every tutor on our platform is interviewed personally and checked on patience and how they are with young people, not just on subject knowledge."
  - q: "We don't have a diagnosis. Can we still get help?"
    a: "Of course. Lots of families come to us with no diagnosis, just things they've noticed at home or that school has mentioned. We don't diagnose, and you don't need a label to get a patient tutor. Tell us what you're seeing and we'll match with that in mind."
  - q: "Will tutoring fix my child's difficulties?"
    a: "That's not how we think about it, because there's nothing about your child that needs fixing. What a tutor can do is teach in a way that suits how your child learns, in small steps, and help them feel more capable as they go. If we don't think tutoring is the right support for your child right now, we'll tell you honestly on the call."
  - q: "Does online tutoring actually work for a child who gets overwhelmed?"
    a: "Often, yes. There's no class of thirty, nobody watching and no putting a hand up in front of everyone. Lessons use a platform called Lessonspace, where your child and the tutor share a whiteboard, so things can be shown visually and one step at a time. Every lesson has a replay too, so nothing is lost if your child needs to go back over it."
  - q: "Can you help with exam access arrangements like extra time?"
    a: "We can't arrange them, because your child's school and the exam boards decide those. What a tutor can do is help your child practise with the extra time, reader or rest breaks they've been given, so the exam feels familiar rather than strange on the day."
  - q: "How much does it cost?"
    a: "Lessons start from £37 an hour. The exact price depends on the tutor, and we'll always agree it with you before anything is booked. There's no contract, no joining fee and nothing to pay upfront. You just pay for the lessons you have, and the first chat with us and the video meeting with your tutor are free."
  - q: "What if my child doesn't click with the tutor?"
    a: "Just let us know and we'll find someone else, with no charge and no awkward conversation. Trust matters more here than almost anything, and we'd much rather find the right person than have your child sit through lessons that don't feel right. It's also why you hear from two or three tutors before choosing."
