# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faqs:
  - q: "My child has ADHD and struggles to focus for long. How does an hour of tutoring work?"
    a: "It doesn't have to be an unbroken hour. One-to-one means the tutor flexes to your child, not the other way round. They chunk the work into short pieces, build in a break to move when it's needed, and keep one thing on the screen at a time. For a lot of children, a calm one-to-one session holds their attention far better than a busy classroom ever did."
  - q: "Do your tutors have experience with dyslexia, autism or other learning needs?"
    a: "Yes. When you get in touch, tell us what your child needs and we factor it into who we suggest. We match families with tutors who have worked with these needs before and know how to adapt, and every tutor is vetted on patience and how they work with young people as well as on subject knowledge. A degree on its own is never enough for us."
  - q: "Is one-to-one online really better for a child who gets overwhelmed?"
    a: "Often, yes. There's no class of thirty, no audience, no putting your hand up in front of everyone. Sessions run on Lessonspace, our shared online whiteboard, from wherever your child feels comfortable, usually home. Most children settle into it quickly. We do not do in-person."
  - q: "Can you help my child get exam access arrangements like extra time?"
    a: "Access arrangements are decided by your child's school and the exam boards, not by us, so we can't grant them. What a tutor can do is help your child practise with the extra time, reader or rest breaks they've been given, so the way the exam runs feels familiar rather than strange on the day."
  - q: "What if my child doesn't get on with the first tutor?"
    a: "Tell us and we'll sort it, no awkward conversation and no process to go through. Trust matters more here than almost anything, and we'd far rather find the right person than have your child sitting through sessions with someone who isn't clicking."
  - q: "Will tutoring 'fix' my child's difficulties?"
    a: "No, and that's not how we think about it. There is nothing wrong with your child that needs fixing. What we can offer is a patient tutor who teaches in a way that suits how your child learns and helps them feel more capable, one step at a time. If we don't think tutoring is the right support for your child right now, Joe will tell you honestly on the call."
