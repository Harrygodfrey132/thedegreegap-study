  - q: "There's so much content. How does a tutor help my child keep on top of it?"
    a: "Mostly by changing how they revise it. Re-reading notes is one of the weakest ways to hold a course this big. A tutor quizzes your child across topics and comes back to older ones on purpose, so respiration or gene expression is still there when the exam needs it. Most students tell us the course stops feeling like a pile that keeps growing."
  - q: "My child knows the topics but keeps losing marks on the longer questions. Why?"
    a: "Usually because A-Level Biology marks the reasoning, not just the facts. The longer answers want a clear chain of points that matches the command word, and the synoptic questions want two or three topics linked together. On AQA, Paper 3 also has a 25-mark essay. A tutor goes through real mark schemes with your child until they can see what the examiner is counting, then practises it until the structure comes naturally."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with the maths, the stats and the required practicals?"
    a: "Yes, and they often trip students up more than the Biology does. Roughly a tenth of the marks are maths, things like standard deviation, chi-squared and the t-test, and plenty of students have never been shown how to run them properly. The practicals come up as written questions on method, variables and results, so a tutor works through those past-paper questions with your child too."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. Our tutors cover AQA, OCR A, OCR B and both Edexcel routes, including Salters-Nuffield, and we'll pick tutors who know your child's one. The boards examine the same Biology quite differently, from the synoptic paper to how the practicals come up. Not sure which board it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can help you work it out on the call."
  - q: "Does online tutoring actually work for Biology?"
    a: "It does, and a lot of students find it easier. Lessons use a platform called Lessonspace, where your child and the tutor work on the same whiteboard, so the tutor can sketch a nephron, mark an answer on screen or map out how two topics link. Every lesson has a replay too, which is really handy the week before a mock."
