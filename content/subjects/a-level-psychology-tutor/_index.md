  - q: "My child knows the studies but keeps losing marks. What's going wrong?"
    a: "Nine times out of ten it isn't the content. It's the evaluation, AO3 on the mark scheme, and applying the theory to the scenario in the question. Lots of students write everything they know about a study and stop there, which caps the mark however hard they've revised. A tutor shows them how to turn a describe-only answer into an argued one."
  - q: "What does 'more evaluation needed' actually mean?"
    a: "It means the answer explains the study or theory but doesn't weigh it up. A good evaluation point makes a criticism or a strength, backs it with evidence, and says why it matters for the argument. On the longer essays, like AQA's 16-markers, that's where the top-band marks sit. A tutor marks your child's essays against the AO1 and AO3 bands so they can see the difference for themselves."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with research methods and the statistics?"
    a: "Yes, and it's one of the most teachable parts of the course. Choosing the right statistical test, levels of measurement, whether a design is related or unrelated: these follow rules once someone walks your child through them properly. Most students find research methods far less frightening after a few focused lessons than they did staring at it alone."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. Our tutors cover AQA, Edexcel and OCR, and we'll pick tutors who know your child's one. The boards weight research methods differently, and each has its own set of studies and question style, from Edexcel's clinical psychology to OCR's core studies. Not sure which board it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can help you work it out on the call."
