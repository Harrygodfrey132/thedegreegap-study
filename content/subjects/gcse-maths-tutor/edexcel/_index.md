assess_heading: "Edexcel GCSE Maths Paper 1, 2 and 3: what each paper covers"
assess_intro: "Pearson Edexcel GCSE Maths (spec 1MA1) is three papers at either Foundation or Higher, all sat in the same exam series. Each is 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks and a third of the grade, and Pearson says the content for each tier is tested across all three. Individual papers aren't graded. The grade comes from the total."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1 (1MA1/1F or 1H): non-calculator"
    detail: "1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, no calculator. Any topic can appear, so expect algebra, geometry and probability alongside the arithmetic. In 2025, Edexcel's examiners said division and work with decimals caught out some Higher candidates on this paper."
  - name: "Paper 2 (1MA1/2F or 2H): calculator"
    detail: "1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, calculator allowed. After the 2025 exams, Pearson's examiners asked Higher students not to round until the final answer, and to leave answers in exact form, with surds, when the question asks for it."
  - name: "Paper 3 (1MA1/3F or 3H): calculator"
    detail: "1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, calculator allowed, and worth the same as the other two. Some questions are set in real-life contexts and some are pure Maths, a mix the specification promises on every paper."
  - name: "Topics and tiers"
    detail: "Number, algebra, ratio and proportion, geometry and measures, probability and statistics. On Foundation (grades 1 to 5), number and ratio each make up 22 to 28% of the marks. On Higher (grades 4 to 9, with an allowed grade 3), algebra is the biggest share at 27 to 33%."
  - name: "The Exam Aid (formula sheet)"
    detail: "Pearson calls its formula sheet the Exam Aid, and Ofqual requires one with every GCSE Maths paper in summer 2027. The 2026 Higher version lists the trapezium, prism and circle formulae, Pythagoras and the trig ratios, the quadratic formula, the sine and cosine rules, ½ab sin C, compound interest and two probability rules. Your child can't take their own copy in. A clean one comes inside the paper."
board_points_heading: "Where Edexcel GCSE Maths marks are won and lost"
board_points:
  - title: "P marks for the process"
    body: "Edexcel's mark schemes give P marks for the correct stages of a problem-solving question, and examiners can still award them after an arithmetic slip. A tutor teaches your child to write every stage where the examiner can see it, so one wrong sum near the end can still leave the process marks banked."
  - title: "C marks for a clear conclusion"
    body: "A C mark needs a fully correct statement with nothing contradicting it, and Edexcel's examiners point out that a right statement written next to a wrong one loses the mark. Your child practises finishing 'explain' and 'decide' questions with one short sentence that answers what was asked."
  - title: "Proofs and worded problems"
    body: "In 2025 Edexcel's examiners found Higher candidates unsure what counts as a proof, and Foundation candidates finding context questions and multi-stage working hard. A tutor goes through the Edexcel versions of these one at a time, so your child knows what a full answer looks like."

faq_items:
  - q: "How can I tell if my child sits Edexcel GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Check the front of a mock or past paper for 'Pearson Edexcel' and the code 1MA1, usually written as 1MA1/1F or 1MA1/1H. Pearson is the company that runs Edexcel, so you'll see both names. The school's exam timetable will list it too, and if it's still unclear we'll help you work it out on the call before matching anyone."
  - q: "What's on Edexcel GCSE Maths Paper 1, and how is it different from Papers 2 and 3?"
    a: "Paper 1 is the non-calculator paper, and it can test any topic on your child's tier. Pearson's specification says the content for each tier is assessed across all three papers, so Papers 2 and 3 cover the same topics with a calculator allowed. If you want a topic list, the specification sets it out under number, algebra, ratio, geometry, probability and statistics."
  - q: "Does Edexcel give a formula sheet for GCSE Maths in 2027?"
    a: "Yes. Ofqual's rules mean every GCSE Maths paper in summer 2027 comes with a formulae sheet, which Pearson has called the Exam Aid, in a Foundation and a Higher version. In May 2026 Ofqual confirmed the sheets continue from 2028 for the rest of the current specification. Cone and sphere formulae still appear inside the questions that need them, so they aren't on the sheet."
  - q: "Where can we get Edexcel GCSE Maths past papers and mark schemes?"
    a: "Pearson's qualifications website has a free past papers search for 1MA1, with mark schemes and examiners' reports. Papers sat in the last 12 months are for teachers only, so students see a padlock on those. A tutor can work through the older ones with your child and mark them against Pearson's scheme, P and C marks included."
  - q: "When were the Edexcel GCSE Maths grade boundaries for 2026 published?"
    a: "On results day, 20 August 2026, on the grade boundaries page of Pearson's qualifications website. Each boundary is a total mark across all three papers, because Pearson doesn't grade the papers one by one. They change every year with how hard the papers were. So treat last year's numbers as a guide for mocks, not a target."
  - q: "Is Edexcel GCSE Maths easier than AQA or OCR?"
    a: "No. Every board teaches the same national content and assesses the same three skills, in proportions set by Ofqual, so a grade 6 on Edexcel means the same as a 6 anywhere else. What differs is the feel of the paper and the mark scheme, such as Edexcel's P marks for process and C marks for communication. A tutor who knows those habits is what helps."
