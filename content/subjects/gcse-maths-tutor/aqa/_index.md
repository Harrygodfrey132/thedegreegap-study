papers:
  - name: "Paper 1: non-calculator"
    detail: "1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, no calculator. Questions run from single-mark ones up to multi-step problems, and AQA says the demand rises as the paper goes on. In summer 2026 it came first, in mid-May, with Papers 2 and 3 in June."
  - name: "Paper 2: calculator"
    detail: "1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, calculator allowed. Like Paper 1, it can test any topic, from algebra to statistics, not just number. AQA's mark schemes take a mark off for rounding too early, so keeping the full calculator value until the end matters here."
  - name: "Paper 3: calculator"
    detail: "1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, calculator allowed, and worth exactly the same as the other two. The grade comes from the total out of 240 across all three papers, so a disappointing Paper 1 can still be made up later."
  - name: "Topics and tiers"
    detail: "Six areas: number, algebra, ratio and proportion, geometry and measures, probability, and statistics. Foundation (grades 1 to 5) is about a quarter number and a quarter ratio. Higher (grades 4 to 9) is about 30% algebra, and a Higher student who just misses a 4 is given an allowed grade 3."
  - name: "The formula sheet for 2027"
    detail: "Every AQA paper in 2027 comes with a formulae sheet insert. Foundation lists the trapezium, prism and circle formulae, Pythagoras, the three trig ratios, compound interest and one probability rule. Higher adds the quadratic formula, the sine and cosine rules, ½ab sin C and a second probability rule. Speed, density and angle facts aren't on it, and cone and sphere formulae are given in the question."

  - title: "Pacing a paper that gets harder"
    body: "AQA builds each paper from short questions up to multi-step problems, so it's easy to lose time early on. A tutor runs timed AQA papers with your child and agrees a plan: bank the early marks briskly, then leave proper time for the longer questions at the back."
  - title: "Holding on to the accuracy marks"
    body: "AQA's mark schemes take a mark off for rounding too early, and when a question asks for working, an answer on its own usually scores nothing. A tutor gets your child carrying the full calculator value through, writing each step down and checking the answer is in the form asked for."
  - title: "Committing to one answer"
    body: "If a child leaves two methods or two answers on the page, AQA examiners mark both, and the wrong one costs marks. Crossed-out work that's been replaced isn't marked either. Your child practises finishing one method cleanly and only crossing out when they've something better to put instead."

  - q: "What's on AQA GCSE Maths Paper 1?"
    a: "Any topic from your child's tier can come up on AQA Paper 1. It's the non-calculator paper, 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks, and AQA's specification says content from any part of the course may be assessed on any of the three papers. So Papers 2 and 3 cover the same ground with a calculator. What makes Paper 1 different is doing the number work, fractions and, on Higher, surds by hand."
  - q: "Is there a formula sheet for AQA GCSE Maths in 2027?"
    a: "Yes. For the summer 2027 exams every AQA GCSE Maths paper comes with a formulae sheet insert, and AQA says it's the same sheet as in 2026. It lists formulae like the quadratic formula and the sine rule on Higher, but your child still has to work out which one a question needs."

    a: "On AQA's own website, using its past papers finder, where you can filter for GCSE Mathematics 8300.
