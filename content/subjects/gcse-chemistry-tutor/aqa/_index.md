assess_heading: "AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462): what's on Paper 1 and Paper 2"
assess_intro: "Separate Chemistry with AQA is two exams at the end of the course, each worth half the grade, both sat at the same tier. Here's the current specification (version 1.2, January 2026), paper by paper."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1: topics 1 to 5"
    detail: "1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, 50% of the GCSE. Atomic structure and the periodic table, bonding and structure, quantitative chemistry, chemical changes and energy changes. In practice that's moles, concentration, yield and atom economy, the reactivity series, acids and salts, electrolysis, and chemical cells and fuel cells."
  - name: "Paper 2: topics 6 to 10"
    detail: "1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, the other 50%. Rates and equilibrium, organic chemistry (crude oil, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids and polymers), chemical analysis (chromatography, gas tests, identifying ions), the atmosphere, and using resources, from drinking water to the Haber process. AQA says it can also draw on topics 1 to 3."
  - name: "Question types, tiers and calculator"
    detail: "Multiple choice, structured, short answer and open response, including six-mark answers marked in levels. Foundation covers grades 1 to 5 and Higher grades 4 to 9. Your child needs a scientific calculator, and the paper asks them to show clearly how they work out every calculation."
  - name: "The AQA periodic table"
    detail: "Each paper comes with AQA's periodic table enclosed. For each element it gives the symbol, name, atomic (proton) number and relative atomic mass, rounded to whole numbers apart from copper (63.5) and chlorine (35.5). There's no equation sheet in Chemistry."
  - name: "Eight required practicals"
    detail: "Four belong to Paper 1's topics (making a salt, titration, electrolysis, temperature changes) and four to Paper 2's (rates, chromatography, identifying ions, purifying water). Questions drawing on them make up at least 15% of the marks."

# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faq_items:
  - q: "How do I know if my child is doing AQA GCSE Chemistry 8462?"
    a: "Look for AQA and the code 8462 on a mock paper, the exam timetable or a revision guide from school. That's separate (triple) Chemistry, entered as 8462F for Foundation or 8462H for Higher. If you see 8464 instead, your child is on AQA Combined Science: Trilogy, where Chemistry is taught alongside Biology and Physics in one course. Still unsure? We'll work it out with you on the call."
  - q: "What topics are on AQA GCSE Chemistry Paper 1?"
    a: "Paper 1 covers topics 1 to 5: atomic structure and the periodic table; bonding, structure and the properties of matter; quantitative chemistry; chemical changes; and energy changes. It lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is out of 100 marks. Because quantitative chemistry is in it, this is the paper with most of the moles, concentration and yield calculations, plus four of the eight required practicals."
  - q: "What's on AQA Chemistry Paper 2?"
    a: "Topics 6 to 10: the rate and extent of chemical change, organic chemistry, chemical analysis, chemistry of the atmosphere, and using resources. It's also 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks. AQA's specification says Paper 2 can draw on topics 1 to 3 as well, so bonding and moles can turn up again. The other four required practicals, including chromatography and the ion tests, belong to these topics."
  - q: "Do you get a periodic table in AQA GCSE Chemistry?"
    a: "Yes. A copy of AQA's periodic table is enclosed with each paper. It shows the relative atomic mass, symbol, name and atomic (proton) number of each element, with masses rounded to whole numbers except copper and chlorine. That's the only reference sheet. Ofqual's formula and equation sheets for 2025 to 2027 cover Maths, Physics and Combined Science, not separate Chemistry, so the methods for moles and concentration have to be learned."
  - q: "Where can I find AQA GCSE Chemistry past papers and mark schemes?"
    a: "On AQA's own website, using its past papers and mark schemes finder: choose GCSE, Chemistry and 8462, then the tier your child sits. A past paper does the most good when someone marks it against the mark scheme with your child afterwards, and that's a big part of what a tutor does in lessons, one question type at a time."
  - q: "Where are the AQA GCSE Chemistry grade boundaries for 2026?"
    a: "AQA published the June 2026 grade boundaries on its website for results day, 20 August 2026. For 8462 the grade is set on your child's total across both papers, out of 200, and the boundaries shift a little each year to allow for how hard that year's papers were. So use them as a guide to what a grade needed last summer, not a fixed target for 2027."
  - q: "Is AQA Chemistry harder than Edexcel or OCR?"
    a: "No. Every board follows the same Department for Education content and the same assessment objectives set by Ofqual, and a grade 7 means the same whichever board awarded it. The difference is style. AQA says its papers use plainer language and fewer contexts, and it puts the whole quantitative chemistry topic in Paper 1. That's why we match your child with a tutor who knows AQA's habits."
