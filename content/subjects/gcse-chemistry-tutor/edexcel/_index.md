assess_heading: "Edexcel GCSE Chemistry (1CH0) paper by paper: topics, marks and timings"
assess_intro: "Pearson Edexcel separate Chemistry is two exams of ten compulsory questions each, sat in May and June, with both papers at the same tier. Here's how the current specification (Issue 4) splits the content."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1 (1CH0/1F or 1CH0/1H)"
    detail: "1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, 50% of the GCSE. Topic 1, key concepts, then states of matter and mixtures, chemical changes (acids, salts, electrolysis), extracting metals and equilibria, and Separate chemistry 1: transition metals and corrosion, concentration and titration, yield and atom economy, gas volumes, the Haber process and fuel cells."
  - name: "Paper 2 (1CH0/2F or 1CH0/2H)"
    detail: "1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, the other 50%. Topic 1 again, then groups in the periodic table (alkali metals, halogens, noble gases), rates of reaction and energy changes, fuels and Earth science, and Separate chemistry 2: tests for ions, hydrocarbons, polymers, alcohols and carboxylic acids, and nanoparticles."
  - name: "Question styles and tiers"
    detail: "Multiple choice, short answers, calculations and extended open responses. Questions marked with an asterisk, like the six-mark ones, also give credit for a logically structured answer. Foundation targets grades 1 to 5 and Higher 4 to 9, and 27 marks on each paper appear in both tiers."
  - name: "Periodic table and calculator"
    detail: "There's no separate sheet. The periodic table is printed on the back cover of each paper, giving relative atomic mass, symbol, name and atomic (proton) number. Calculators are allowed, and when a question needs the volume of one mole of gas, Edexcel gives it as 24 dm³."
  - name: "Eight core practicals"
    detail: "Inks by distillation and chromatography, pH change with calcium hydroxide, copper sulfate crystals, electrolysis of copper sulfate, an acid-alkali titration, rates (marble chips and thiosulfate), identifying ions in salts, and burning alcohols. Schools confirm each student has done all eight, and the papers ask about them."

# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faq_items:
  - q: "How do I know if my child is doing Edexcel GCSE Chemistry?"
    a: "Check a mock paper or the exam timetable for Pearson Edexcel and the code 1CH0, with an F or H in the paper code (1CH0/1H, for example) to show the tier. That's separate Chemistry. If the code is 1SC0, your child is on Edexcel Combined Science, which covers Biology, Chemistry and Physics in one course. And if you can't find it anywhere, we'll help you pin it down on the call."
  - q: "What topics are on Edexcel GCSE Chemistry Paper 1?"
    a: "Paper 1 covers Topic 1 (key concepts in chemistry), Topic 2 (states of matter and mixtures), Topic 3 (chemical changes), Topic 4 (extracting metals and equilibria) and Topic 5 (separate chemistry 1). That takes in acids and salts, electrolysis, the reactivity series, titration, yield, atom economy and fuel cells. It's 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and ten questions."
  - q: "What topics are on Edexcel Chemistry Paper 2?"
    a: "Paper 2 tests Topic 1 again, plus Topic 6 (groups in the periodic table), Topic 7 (rates of reaction and energy changes), Topic 8 (fuels and Earth science) and Topic 9 (separate chemistry 2). So expect Group 1, 7 and 0 trends, rates graphs, crude oil and the atmosphere, tests for ions, polymers, alcohols and carboxylic acids. Same length and marks as Paper 1."
  - q: "Do you get a periodic table in Edexcel GCSE Chemistry?"
    a: "Yes, it's printed on the back cover of every Edexcel Chemistry paper, so there's no loose sheet to keep track of. It gives each element's relative atomic mass, symbol, name and atomic number, with masses rounded to whole numbers except copper and chlorine. There's no equation sheet, because Ofqual's formula and equation sheets cover Maths, Physics and Combined Science only. The molar gas volume, 24 dm³, is given in a question when it's needed."
  - q: "Where can I find Edexcel GCSE Chemistry past papers?"
    a: "On Pearson's own website, through its past papers page. Search for GCSE Chemistry 1CH0 and you'll find question papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. Doing one under timed conditions is useful. But going through it afterwards with someone who knows the Edexcel mark scheme is where the marks are found, and that's how tutors on our platform use them."
  - q: "Where can I see the Edexcel GCSE Chemistry grade boundaries for 2026?"
    a: "Pearson published the June 2026 GCSE grade boundaries on its grade boundaries page on 20 August 2026, results day. For 1CH0 the boundaries apply to the total of both papers, out of 200, and they're set separately for Foundation and Higher. They move from year to year with the difficulty of the papers, so last summer's figures are a rough guide for 2027, never a guarantee."
  - q: "Is Edexcel Chemistry harder than AQA?"
    a: "Not officially, and not in the grades. All the boards teach the same DfE content and assess the same Ofqual objectives, and a grade means the same on any certificate. Edexcel's style is its own, though: Topic 1 on both papers, ten compulsory questions per paper, starred extended answers and the periodic table on the back cover. A tutor who knows that style can get your child ready for it."
