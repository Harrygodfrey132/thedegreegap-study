  - q: "My child's essays on the film and book read like a plot summary. Can a tutor help?"
    a: "Yes, and it's the most common reason families come to us for A-Level French. The marks go to analysis and a clear argument, not retelling the story. The tutor works from your child's own film and book, shows them how to build a case in French and how to fold a quotation into a point, then marks practice essays so the feedback is the real thing."
  - q: "The grammar has jumped since GCSE. Is that normal?"
    a: "Very normal. A-Level French leans on things GCSE barely touched, the subjunctive most of all, plus harder tenses and the passive. The tutor drills them until the endings stop being a guess, because the essays and the translation into French both reward getting them right. Once the grammar settles, the writing feels a lot less scary."
  - q: "What's the Individual Research Project, and can a tutor help with it?"
    a: "The IRP is part of the speaking exam. Your child researches a topic of their own linked to the French-speaking world, then presents it and discusses it in French with the examiner. A tutor helps them pick a topic with enough to talk about, shape the presentation and practise the follow-up questions out loud, so the real thing doesn't feel like an ambush."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. AQA and Edexcel both build A-Level French around a film, a literary text and an independent research project, but they set different works and lay out the papers their own way. Eduqas has its own set works too. We'll pick tutors who know your child's board. Not sure which it is? We can work it out together on the call."
