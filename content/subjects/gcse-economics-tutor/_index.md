faq_items:
  - q: "Why does my child lose marks on the long Economics questions?"
    a: "Usually because the answer explains but never weighs up. The longer questions want your child to set out a chain of reasons, then look at the other side and reach a clear judgement. Many students stop after the first part. A tutor shows them how to build in the evaluation with phrases like it depends on, and practises it on real questions until it feels normal."
  - q: "My child finds the maths in Economics hard. Can a tutor help?"
    a: "Yes. The calculations at GCSE are not advanced, but they do trip people up. Your child may need percentage changes, averages, reading data from a table or graph, and some boards include things like price elasticity. A tutor goes through each type slowly, then mixes them up in practice, so your child knows what to do when one appears in the exam."
  - q: "How important are the diagrams?"
    a: "Very. A supply and demand diagram that's labelled properly can win marks on its own, and a messy one can lose them. The tutor gets your child drawing them quickly and neatly, with the axes, curves and shifts all clear, and then explaining what the diagram shows in words."
  - q: "Which exam boards do you cover?"

The boards cover similar ideas but phrase their questions differently and reward evaluation in their own way. We'll choose a tutor who knows your child's board. If you're not sure which one it is, have a look at a mock paper."
