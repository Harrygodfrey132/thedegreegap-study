faq_items:
  - q: "My child knows the content but the History grade won't move. Why?"
    a: "It's the thing we hear most about History, and it's rarely about knowing more. Usually the answer retells events instead of arguing a point, or it never reaches a clear judgement at the end. A tutor teaches your child to use what they know as evidence for an argument, so the knowledge that's already there starts scoring."
  - q: "How do you help with the source and interpretation questions?"
    a: "A tutor works on reading a source for its provenance, who wrote it, when and why, and using it as evidence rather than just describing it. For interpretations, it's about explaining why two historians see things differently and backing a view with knowledge. It's a technique, and it can be practised until it feels routine."
  - q: "What about the long essay questions?"
    a: "They're usually where the grade is won or lost, because they reward a structured argument, not everything your child can remember. On AQA that's the 16-mark questions. The tutor works on planning quickly, writing paragraphs that make a point and prove it, and reaching a judgement before the clock runs out."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes, and it matters more in History than parents expect. AQA, Edexcel and OCR set different papers and weight the source and interpretation work differently. Edexcel's Paper 3, for example, puts sources and interpretations in the same exam. We pick tutors who know your child's board. Not sure which it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can work it out on the call."
  - q: "How much does it cost?"
    a: "Lessons start from £37 an hour. The exact price depends on the tutor, and we'll always agree it with you before anything is booked. There's no contract, no joining fee and nothing to pay upfront. You just pay for the lessons you have, and the first chat with us and the video meeting with your tutor are free."
  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
    a: "Just let us know and we'll find someone else, with no charge and no awkward conversation. It happens, and we'd much rather sort it than have your child sit through lessons that aren't working. It's also why you hear from two or three tutors before choosing, rather than being handed one."
  - q: "Does online tutoring actually work for History?"
    a: "It does. On Lessonspace the tutor can put a source or your child's essay on the shared screen and mark it up with them in real time, showing where a paragraph describes when it needed to analyse. Every lesson has a replay too, which is handy for going back over an essay plan before a mock."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia or finds long essays hard. Can you still help?"
    a: "Yes. We'll ask about it on the call, and lots of tutors on our platform work with children who learn differently. One-to-one lessons let the tutor break an essay into small, manageable steps and build a plan your child can lean on in the exam. Tell us what works for your child and we'll pick tutors with that in mind."
  - q: "Do you cover where we live?"
    a: "Yes, we cover the whole of the UK. All our lessons are online, so it doesn't matter whether you're in a city or a village. It also means we can pick the tutor who's the best fit for your child, rather than whoever happens to live nearby."
