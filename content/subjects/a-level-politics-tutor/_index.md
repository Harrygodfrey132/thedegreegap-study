faq_items:
  - q: "Why do the long essays keep losing marks?"
    a: "Most of the time the essay explains both sides and never decides. Edexcel's essays are worth 30 marks and AQA's 25, and the top band on both rewards a clear line of argument held from start to finish. A tutor shows your child how to set out a judgement early, weigh each point against it and end with a conclusion that answers the question."
  - q: "My child's examples are out of date. Does that matter?"
    a: "It does. Examiners want recent evidence, and an essay leaning on the same old textbook cases looks thin. A tutor helps your child build a bank of examples from the last few years, such as recent elections, Supreme Court rulings and votes in Parliament or Congress, and use them to prove a point rather than just drop them in."
  - q: "How does a tutor help with comparing the UK and the US?"
    a: "Comparison questions ask your child to put the two systems side by side, for example how each protects rights or how much power each leader really has. The trap is writing two separate descriptions. The tutor shows them how to compare point by point and explain why the differences exist, using rational, cultural and structural explanations where the board asks for them."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with political ideas?"
    a: "Yes. Liberalism, conservatism and socialism are on the course, plus one more ideology, such as feminism or nationalism. The marks go to comparing thinkers and showing where an ideology agrees and argues with itself. The tutor helps your child learn the key thinkers, from Locke to Burke to Marx, and use them to make an argument."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
