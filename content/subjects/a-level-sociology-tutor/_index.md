faq_items:
  - q: "What does 'apply the item' mean?"
    a: "Many of the longer questions start with a short passage, called an item, and ask your child to use it. Application, AO2 on the mark scheme, is the skill being marked. Students often ignore the item, or quote it without saying why it matters. A tutor shows them how to pick out each clue in the item and link it to a theory or a study, which is where plenty of marks are waiting."
  - q: "Why is theory and methods so hard?"
    a: "Because it asks your child to think about Sociology itself: how sociologists gather evidence and which big theories they use. On AQA there's also a methods in context question, where your child judges a research method for studying a set issue in education. A tutor breaks each method down into strengths and weaknesses that can be applied to any topic."
  - q: "My child mixes up the theories. Can a tutor help?"
    a: "Yes. Functionalism, Marxism, feminism, interactionism and postmodernism come up in almost every topic, and each sees the same issue differently. The tutor helps your child build a clear picture of what each one says and how they criticise each other. Then evaluation stops being a list and turns into an argument."
  - q: "How does a tutor help with the long essays?"
    a: "On AQA the biggest questions are worth 30 marks and reward a clear argument that uses studies, theories and the item to weigh up a view. The tutor marks practice essays against the mark scheme, shows your child where description took over from evaluation, and helps them plan answers quickly under timed conditions."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. AQA, OCR and Eduqas all offer A-Level Sociology. They share the big theories but choose different topics and set the questions in their own way. We only suggest tutors who know the board your child sits. If you aren't sure which it is, look on a mock paper or ask us on the call."
