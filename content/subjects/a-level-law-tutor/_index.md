faq_items:
  - q: "My child knows the cases but loses marks on the scenario questions. Why?"
    a: "It's the problem we hear about most with A-Level Law. Knowing the rule is only the start. The marks come from taking each fact in the scenario, matching it to the right legal test, backing it with a case and reaching a conclusion about liability. Many students write out everything they know instead. A tutor gives them a clear method and practises it on past questions until it's second nature."
  - q: "What does 'evaluate the law' mean, and why is it so hard?"
    a: "It means judging whether the law works. Is it fair, clear and up to date? Does it match what people think is right? Students often describe the law well and then stop. The tutor helps your child form a view, find the criticisms and the reforms that have been suggested, and support each point. The Offences Against the Person Act 1861, often called out of date, is a good place to practise."
  - q: "My child has never studied Law before. Is that a problem?"
    a: "Not at all. Most students start A-Level Law with no background, so it's very common. The tutor builds the basics first: how judges set precedent, how Parliament makes law, and the legal terms your child needs to use precisely. Once that's solid, the scenario questions get much easier."
  - q: "There are so many cases to learn. Can a tutor help with that?"
    a: "Yes. Your child doesn't need every detail of every case. They need the name, the key facts and the rule it set, tied to the topic it supports. A tutor helps them build short case notes and tests them in lessons, so the right case comes to mind when a question calls for it."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"

The tutor we suggest will know your child's board and its papers. If you don't know which it is, check a mock paper, or we'll work it out with you on the call."
