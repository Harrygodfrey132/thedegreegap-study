faq_items:
  - q: "My child freezes in the French speaking exam. Can a tutor help?"
    a: "Yes, and it's one of the most common reasons families come to us for French. The speaking exam is a performance as much as a test, so the tutor rehearses it out loud, the role-play and the photo card, again and again. Your child builds a bank of opinions and reasons to fall back on, so there's far less to freeze over on the day."
  - q: "The tenses keep slipping in the writing. What does a tutor do about that?"
    a: "It's where a lot of French marks quietly go. The tutor drills the past, present and future until the endings stop being a guess, and sorts out the two that trip most students up, the passé composé and the imperfect. The writing and speaking both reward using more than one tense, so the work pays off twice."
  - q: "How is GCSE French examined?"
    a: "As four separate papers: Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing, each worth a quarter of the grade, at Foundation or Higher tier. A grade rarely slips across all four at once, so the tutor's first job is finding which skill is costing the most marks and starting there."
  - q: "The listening paper goes so fast. Is there anything that helps?"
    a: "There is. The real skill is knowing what to listen for. The tutor works through real listening papers with your child, picking out tense markers and small negatives like ne...jamais that change a whole answer, and predicting the likely vocabulary from the question before the recording starts."

They all test the same four skills but word the writing tasks and set up the speaking differently, so we pick tutors who know your child's board. Not sure which it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can work it out on the call."
