  - q: "Can one tutor really cover Biology, Chemistry and Physics?"
    a: "Yes, and it's exactly what we match for. The tutor has to move comfortably from a Biology six-mark answer to a Chemistry calculation to a Physics equation in the same hour, and plenty of tutors are strong in one science and thin in another. If just one of the three is pulling the grade down, the tutor can spend more time there while keeping the other two ticking over."
  - q: "How does Combined Science work, and why does one weak science matter so much?"
    a: "It's one course covering all three sciences, worth two GCSEs and graded as a pair, like 5-5. With AQA Trilogy that's six papers, two each for Biology, Chemistry and Physics. Because the grade comes from all of them together, a weak paper in one science pulls the whole thing down."
  - q: "There's so much to revise. How does a tutor stop it going a mile wide and an inch deep?"
    a: "By swapping re-reading for quizzing. A tutor pulls back the Biology from three weeks ago while the new Chemistry goes in, so topics are revisited before they fade. The aim is enough depth to handle a six-mark question that draws on two topics at once, not a thin skim of everything that's gone by the exam."
  - q: "Do the tutors cover the required practicals?"
    a: "Yes. The required practicals span all three sciences and come up in the written papers, so they count as much as any topic. Tutors go through the ones students find slippery, like osmosis in potato cylinders, making a soluble salt, specific heat capacity and resistance in a wire, and teach your child to answer on method, variables and results."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. Tutors on our platform cover AQA Trilogy, Edexcel Combined and both OCR routes, Gateway and Twenty First Century, at Foundation and Higher tier, and we'll pick tutors who know your child's one. Not sure which it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can help you work it out on the call."
  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
    a: "Just let us know and we'll find someone else, with no charge and no awkward conversation. It happens, and we'd much rather sort it than have your child sit through lessons that aren't working. It's also why you hear about two or three tutors before choosing, rather than being handed one."
  - q: "Does online tutoring actually work for science?"
    a: "It does, and a lot of children find it easier. On Lessonspace your child and the tutor share the same whiteboard, so the tutor can sketch a circuit or a cell, work through a calculation one line at a time and see exactly where a mark went. Every lesson has a replay too, which is handy before a test."
