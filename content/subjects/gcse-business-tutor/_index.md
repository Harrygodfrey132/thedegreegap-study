faq_items:
  - q: "My child knows the content. Why are the long answers marked so low?"
    a: "It's the most common thing we hear about GCSE Business. The longer questions don't just want facts. They want your child to use the business in the case study, explain what would happen next and then make a decision. An answer about businesses in general, with no mention of the one in the question, can only get so far. A tutor practises this with real past questions until it becomes a habit."
  - q: "What does using the case study actually mean?"
    a: "It means picking out details from the text, like the size of the firm, its customers or its figures, and building the answer around them. So rather than saying a price cut would raise sales, your child says why it might work for this café but not for that luxury brand. Examiners reward that kind of link, and it's very teachable."
  - q: "Are there calculations in GCSE Business?"
    a: "Yes, and they're some of the easiest marks to win back. Your child may need to work out revenue, costs and profit, profit margins, percentage changes, break-even or a cash flow forecast, depending on the board. Small slips like a missing pound sign or the wrong formula add up. The tutor makes sure each one is solid and shows your child how to set out the working."
  - q: "Which exam boards do your tutors cover?"
    a: "GCSE Business is offered by AQA, Edexcel, OCR and Eduqas. They cover similar topics but set their case studies and longer questions in different ways, so we'll match your child with someone who knows their board. If you don't know which one they're on, it'll be printed on any mock paper, or we can find out together."
