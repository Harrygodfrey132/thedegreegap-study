faq_items:
  - q: "My child codes at home. Why are they struggling with the GCSE?"
    a: "Because the exam tests code in a way home projects don't. Your child has to write programs by hand, read someone else's code and say what it does, and fix errors without a computer to run it. There's also a lot of theory, like binary, networks and logic gates, that needs exact wording. A tutor helps with both halves, so the coding confidence turns into marks."
  - q: "What is a trace table, and why does it matter?"
    a: "A trace table is a grid your child fills in to follow an algorithm line by line, writing down what each variable holds as it runs. Questions on them come up a lot, and they're easy to lose marks on through one small slip. The tutor practises them until your child can work through a loop or a condition calmly and check their answer."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with the algorithms and pseudocode?"
    a: "Yes. Your child needs to know the standard searching and sorting algorithms, such as binary search and bubble sort, and be able to explain or write them. They also need to read and write pseudocode, or the board's own reference language. A tutor works through these slowly, with plenty of practice, until your child can do them from memory."
  - q: "Which exam boards do you cover?"
    a: "GCSE Computer Science is set by AQA, OCR and Edexcel. Each one splits the course into a theory paper and a programming paper, but they set the questions differently, and OCR has its own exam reference language. Your child's tutor will be someone familiar with that board's papers. The board's name will be on any mock paper if you're unsure."
