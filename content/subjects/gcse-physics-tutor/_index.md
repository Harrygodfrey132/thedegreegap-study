  - q: "My child understands the physics but loses marks on the calculations. Why?"
    a: "It's the thing we hear most about Physics, and it's nearly always the maths, not the physics. Rearranging the equation the wrong way, dropping a unit, or leaving an answer in centimetres when the question wanted metres. Or writing the answer with no working, so there's nothing for the examiner to give marks for. A tutor marks past papers the way an examiner does, spots where the marks go, and practises the method with your child until it sticks."
  - q: "What does a tutor actually do in a Physics lesson?"
    a: "Usually the first lesson is a look at a recent paper or mock to see where the marks are going. After that it's the topics costing the most, whether that's electricity, forces or waves, plus the maths underneath: rearranging, standard form and units. Then exam questions on that topic, marked the way the exam board marks them."
  - q: "Do the tutors cover the required practicals?"
    a: "Yes. The practicals come up in the written exams, so they matter as much as any topic. Tutors go through the ones students find tricky, like the resistance of a wire, specific heat capacity, density and force and extension on a spring, and teach your child to answer questions on the method, the variables and the results."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. Our tutors cover AQA, Edexcel and OCR, at Foundation and Higher tier, for separate Physics and for the Physics in Combined Science. We'll pick tutors who know your child's board. Not sure which one it is? It's usually on the front of a mock paper, or we can work it out together on the call."
  - q: "Does online tutoring actually work for Physics?"
    a: "It does, and it suits Physics well. Lessons use a platform called Lessonspace, where your child and the tutor share a whiteboard, so the tutor can rearrange an equation line by line, sketch a circuit or a force diagram, and see every step of your child's working. Every lesson has a replay too, which is handy before a test."
  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
    a: "Just let us know and we'll find someone else, with no charge and no awkward conversation. It happens, and we'd much rather sort it than have your child sit through lessons that aren't working. It's also why you see two or three tutors before choosing, rather than being handed one."
  - q: "My child doesn't really want a tutor. Is that normal?"
    a: "Very normal, and it's probably the thing parents mention most. It usually changes once they meet the tutor, because it feels nothing like a Physics lesson at school.
