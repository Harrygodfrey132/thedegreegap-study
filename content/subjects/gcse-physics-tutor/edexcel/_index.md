assess_heading: "Edexcel GCSE Physics 1PH0: what's on Paper 1 and Paper 2"
assess_intro: "Pearson Edexcel GCSE Physics (1PH0) is two papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks, worth 50% each. Paper 1 covers Topics 1 to 7 and Paper 2 covers Topics 8 to 15, with Topic 1, the key concepts of physics, on both. Each paper has ten questions and your child answers all of them. Calculators are allowed on both."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1 (1PH0/1F or 1PH0/1H)"
    detail: "Topics 1 to 7: key concepts, motion and forces, conservation of energy, waves, light and the electromagnetic spectrum, radioactivity, and astronomy. Expect speed and acceleration (with momentum on Higher), refraction, half-life and nuclear equations, and the evidence for the Big Bang. 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks."
  - name: "Paper 2 (1PH0/2F or 1PH0/2H)"
    detail: "Topic 1 again, then Topics 8 to 15: work and power, forces and their effects (vectors and moments), electricity and circuits, static electricity, magnetism and the motor effect, electromagnetic induction and transformers, the particle model (density, specific heat capacity, gas pressure) and forces and matter (springs, pressure in fluids). 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks."
  - name: "Foundation or Higher"
    detail: "Your child sits both papers at the same tier. Foundation targets grades 1 to 5 and Higher targets 4 to 9, and 27 marks on each paper are questions that appear on both tiers. Question types run from multiple choice and short answers to calculations and extended open response."
  - name: "The equations list in the exam"
    detail: "Pearson includes a clean copy of its equations list with the papers in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The current list has the equations your child would normally recall as well as the ones always given, plus a short extra section for separate Physics. Units aren't on it: Topic 1 expects your child to know SI units, prefixes from giga to nano and conversions like hours to seconds."
  - name: "The eight core practicals"
    detail: "Done in class, written up, then examined in the papers: force, mass and acceleration with trolleys, waves in a solid and a fluid, refraction in glass blocks, thermal radiation from different surfaces, I-V graphs and series and parallel circuits, density, specific heat capacity of water and melting ice, and stretching a spring."
board_points_heading: "Where Edexcel Physics marks slip, and how a tutor helps"
board_points:
  - title: "Topic 1, on both papers"
    body: "Edexcel puts the key concepts on both papers: SI units, prefixes, converting units, significant figures and standard form. They sound basic. But a kilowatt left as kilowatts, or minutes left as minutes, can cost the last mark on a calculation your child set up well. A tutor drills conversions until they happen without thinking."
  - title: "The calculation hidden in the context"
    body: "Plenty of Edexcel questions describe a situation first, a kettle or a car braking, and only then ask for a number. Your child has to pull out the values, choose the equation and do two or three steps in the right order. A tutor teaches them to mark up the question and write every step, so the method marks are safe even if the final answer slips."
  - title: "Core practicals and astronomy in words"
    body: "Edexcel asks your child to describe a core practical method, plot or read the results and suggest an improvement. Paper 1 also wants clear written explanations in astronomy, like why red-shift supports the Big Bang. Those are writing skills as much as physics, so a tutor practises them with real past paper questions and the mark scheme beside them."

# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faq_items:
  - q: "How can I tell if my child sits Edexcel GCSE Physics 1PH0?"
    a: "Check a mock paper or the exam timetable for Pearson Edexcel and the code 1PH0, which is separate Physics. Papers are labelled 1PH0/1F or 1PH0/1H, so the last letter tells you the tier too. If it says 1SC0, your child is doing Edexcel Combined Science instead. Not sure? We'll check it together on the call before matching a tutor."
  - q: "What's the difference between Edexcel Physics Paper 1 and Paper 2?"
    a: "The topics. Paper 1 is Topics 1 to 7: key concepts, motion and forces, energy, waves, light and the electromagnetic spectrum, radioactivity and astronomy. Paper 2 is Topic 1 again plus Topics 8 to 15: forces doing work, forces and their effects, electricity, static, magnetism, electromagnetic induction, the particle model and forces and matter. Both are 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and ten questions."
  - q: "Does Edexcel give an equation sheet in GCSE Physics in 2027?"
    a: "Yes. Pearson includes a clean copy of its equations list with the summer 2027 papers, as it did in 2025 and 2026, following a government decision for those three years. Ofqual has since confirmed sheets will continue from 2028. The list won't tell your child which equation to use or give the units, so choosing and rearranging still need practice."
  - q: "Should my child sit Foundation or Higher in Edexcel Physics?"
    a: "It depends on where they're working now, and the school makes the final entry. Foundation papers target grades 1 to 5 and Higher papers target 4 to 9, and 27 marks on each paper are the same questions on both tiers. A tutor can show you where your child really sits by marking a past paper, which helps when the tier conversation comes up at school."
  - q: "Where can I find Edexcel GCSE Physics past papers and mark schemes?"
    a: "On Pearson's own qualifications website, under past papers, with the mark schemes and examiner reports. Papers sat in the last 12 months are open only to teachers, so schools can use them as mocks. Once your child has done a paper, a tutor marks it with them against the Edexcel mark scheme, so they can see which lines of working earned marks and which didn't."
  - q: "Where are the Edexcel GCSE Physics grade boundaries for 2026?"
    a: "On Pearson's grade boundaries page, where the June 2026 GCSE boundaries were published on results day, 20 August 2026. There's an overall document and a separate one with notional boundaries for each paper. Boundaries are the marks needed for each grade that summer and shift a little every year, so they're best used as a rough guide when you mark a mock."
  - q: "Is Edexcel GCSE Physics harder than AQA?"
    a: "No. Both follow the same government subject content and the same assessment objectives, and both are graded 9 to 1. The difference is how the papers are put together. Edexcel puts energy and waves on Paper 1 alongside motion and forces, keeps the units and key concepts on both papers, and often wraps a calculation in context. A tutor who knows those habits makes them feel normal."
