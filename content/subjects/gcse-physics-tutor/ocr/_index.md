assess_heading: "OCR Gateway Physics A (J249): the four papers and what each covers"
assess_intro: "OCR Gateway Physics A (J249) has four papers, but your child sits two of them: Papers 1 and 2 at Foundation, or Papers 3 and 4 at Higher. Each is 1 hour 45 minutes and 90 marks, worth 50% of the GCSE. The course is eight topics, P1 to P8, plus P9, the practical skills, which run through every paper."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1 (Foundation) or Paper 3 (Higher)"
    detail: "J249/01 or J249/03. Topics P1 to P4, plus P9: matter (density, changes of state, pressure), forces (motion, Newton's laws, forces in action), electricity (static and simple circuits) and magnetism and magnetic fields. 1 hour 45 minutes, 90 marks."
  - name: "Paper 2 (Foundation) or Paper 4 (Higher)"
    detail: "J249/02 or J249/04. Topics P5 to P8, plus P9: waves in matter, radioactivity, energy (work, power, efficiency) and global challenges, which takes in stopping distances, the National Grid, mains electricity and space. These papers are synoptic, so P1 to P4 can come back. 1 hour 45 minutes, 90 marks."
  - name: "Section A and Section B"
    detail: "Every paper opens with Section A, 15 marks of multiple choice. Section B is the other 75 marks: short answers on practical work and maths, structured questions, and one six-mark Level of Response question, marked by level rather than point by point."
  - name: "Foundation or Higher"
    detail: "Foundation (Papers 1 and 2) covers grades 5 to 1 and Higher (Papers 3 and 4) covers 9 to 4, with an allowed grade 3 for a Higher student who narrowly misses a 4. Your child is entered for one tier, so it's always Papers 1 and 2 or Papers 3 and 4, never a mix."
  - name: "The Equation Sheet for June 2027"
    detail: "OCR has published a four-page J249 Equation Sheet for June 2027, given as an insert in the paper. It lists every physics equation in the specification, grouped by topic from P1 to P8, with Higher-only ones marked HT. Units aren't on it, and OCR still expects every answer in the right SI unit."
  - name: "The PAGs"
    detail: "Eight Practical Activity Groups: materials, forces, motion, measuring waves, energy, circuit components, series and parallel circuits, and interactions of waves. Your child does at least eight practicals in class, and 15% of the exam questions assess practical skills."
board_points_heading: "Three things an OCR tutor works on with your child"

  - title: "Section A, the 15 multiple-choice marks"
    body: "Fifteen marks on every paper come from multiple choice, and they go quickly when a child rushes or guesses. A tutor teaches your child to work each one out on paper before looking at the options, and to check units and powers of ten, so a near miss doesn't cost a mark."
  - title: "Graphs, data and the PAG questions"
    body: "Gateway gives your child a table or a set of results and asks them to plot a graph, read off a value, spot the odd result and say what it shows. Four small skills. A tutor practises a clear routine for each one, because a sensible conclusion drawn from a messy graph still loses marks."
  - title: "The six-marker and the synoptic questions"
    body: "Papers 2 and 4 cover P5 to P8 but can reach back into P1 to P4, so an energy question might need circuits from P3. And each paper has a six-mark question marked by level. A tutor revises across the whole course and shows your child how to plan a six-mark answer in a minute before writing it."

  - q: "What topics are on OCR GCSE Physics Paper 1?"
    a: "Paper 1, or Paper 3 at Higher, covers P1 to P4: matter, forces, electricity, and magnetism and magnetic fields, plus the P9 practical skills. Paper 2, or Paper 4 at Higher, covers P5 to P8: waves in matter, radioactivity, energy and global challenges, and it can ask about P1 to P4 as well. Each paper is 1 hour 45 minutes and 90 marks."

Your child still has to pick the right equation, rearrange it and give the unit."
