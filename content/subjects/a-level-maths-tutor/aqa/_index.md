assess_heading: "What's on each AQA A-Level Maths paper (7357)"
assess_intro: "Your child sits all three papers at the end of Year 13. Each one is 2 hours, 100 marks and a third of the A-Level, with a calculator allowed and no coursework. AQA sets the grade on the total out of 300, so a weak paper can be made up on another. And because Papers 2 and 3 can test any of the Paper 1 pure, the pure really does run through everything."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1: Pure (7357/1)"
    detail: "Pure only: proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences and series, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation, integration and numerical methods. It usually opens with a few one-mark multiple-choice questions before the longer problems."
  - name: "Paper 2: Pure and Mechanics (7357/2)"
    detail: "Section A is pure, and this is the only AQA paper that can test vectors. Section B is mechanics: units, kinematics (projectiles included), forces and Newton's laws, and moments. Read the value of g each question gives: the June 2025 paper used 9.8 in some questions and 9.81 in one."
  - name: "Paper 3: Pure and Statistics (7357/3)"
    detail: "Section A is pure again. Section B is statistics: sampling, data presentation, probability, the binomial and Normal distributions, and hypothesis testing. Some questions are set on AQA's large data set and expect your child to know it."
  - name: "The formula booklet"
    detail: "Every paper comes with AQA's 'Formulae for A-level Mathematics' booklet, version 2.0 since September 2025. It gives the binomial expansion, series sums, small-angle approximations, compound-angle identities, Newton-Raphson, the trapezium rule, the suvat equations and the binomial distribution. There are no statistical tables, so probabilities come from the calculator, and basics like the quadratic formula aren't in it."
  - name: "The large data set"
    detail: "Since 2020 it's been 3,827 cars from the Department for Transport's vehicle database: five makes (BMW, Ford, Toyota, Vauxhall and Volkswagen), registered in 2002 or 2016, with keepers in London, the North West or the South West, plus engine size, mass and emissions. It isn't in the exam room."

# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faq_items:
  - q: "How do I know if my child sits AQA for A-Level Maths?"
    a: "Look at the front of a mock or past paper. AQA A-Level Maths papers are labelled 7357/1, 7357/2 and 7357/3, and the materials list asks for the AQA formulae booklet. The school's exam timetable will say so too. If you still can't tell, we'll check it with you on the free call before we match anyone, because each board lays out the papers differently."
  - q: "What's on AQA A-Level Maths Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3?"
    a: "Paper 1 is pure only: proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials and logs, differentiation, integration and numerical methods. Paper 2 can test any of that, plus vectors, then has a mechanics section. Paper 3 can test the Paper 1 pure, then has a statistics section. Each paper is 2 hours and 100 marks."
  - q: "What's in the AQA A-Level Maths formula booklet?"
    a: "The harder formulae, not the basics. The current booklet (version 2.0, from September 2025) has the binomial expansion, arithmetic and geometric series, small-angle approximations, compound-angle identities, Newton-Raphson, the trapezium rule, the suvat equations and the binomial distribution. It has no statistical tables. The quadratic formula, laws of logs, double-angle formulae, sine and cosine rules, and the chain and product rules aren't in it, so your child has to learn them."
  - q: "What is the AQA A-Level Maths large data set?"
    a: "It's a spreadsheet of 3,827 cars taken from the Department for Transport's vehicle database, used for AQA A-level exams since 2020 and still the one on AQA's site in October 2026. There's no copy in the exam. But some Paper 3 questions assume your child knows it: in June 2025, one asked why a sample of Nissan cars couldn't be fairly compared with it."
  - q: "Where can I find AQA A-Level Maths past papers and mark schemes?"
    a: "On AQA's own website, on the 7357 assessment resources page, with the mark schemes and examiners' reports next to them. AQA makes papers public about a year after they're sat, so the June 2025 papers are the newest you'll find there in October 2026. A past paper does most good when someone marks it the way an examiner would, and that's a big part of what a tutor does."
  - q: "Where are the 2026 AQA A-Level Maths grade boundaries?"
    a: "On AQA's grade boundaries page, where the June 2026 A-level tables went up on results day, 13 August 2026. For Maths the boundary is set on the total out of 300 across all three papers, not paper by paper. Boundaries shift a little every year depending on how hard the papers were, so last year's figure is a rough guide, not a target."
  - q: "Is AQA A-Level Maths harder than Edexcel?"
    a: "No. The content is set by the Department for Education and is the same for every board, and an A from AQA counts the same as an A from Edexcel. What differs is the layout. AQA puts mechanics in Paper 2 and statistics in Paper 3, each after a pure section, while Edexcel keeps two papers all pure. Some students prefer one style, but that comes down to practice."
