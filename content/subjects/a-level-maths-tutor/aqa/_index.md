# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faqs:
  - q: "Which AQA A-Level Maths papers does my child sit?"
    a: "Three, all at the end of Year 13. Paper 1 is pure only. Paper 2 is pure with a mechanics section. Paper 3 is pure with a statistics section. Each is two hours and 100 marks, it is fully linear, and there is no coursework, so the whole grade comes down to those three exams."
  - q: "How is AQA 7357 different from Edexcel or OCR?"
    a: "AQA spreads the applied content into the pure papers rather than isolating it. Edexcel keeps its two pure papers separate and bolts all the applied into one paper; AQA puts mechanics in Paper 2 and statistics in Paper 3, each next to pure. Same maths, different shape. What matters is that your child is taught for the paper they actually sit, which is why we match on the exam board, not just the subject."
  - q: "My child got a good GCSE grade but the Year 12 pure has fallen apart. Is that normal?"
    a: "Very. AQA's papers assume the GCSE algebra is automatic, then stack proof, heavier functions and calculus on top. Plenty of students who walked out of GCSE Maths grinning hit a wall in the first term. It is the single biggest step in the curriculum, and it is fixable. We start with a diagnostic to find exactly which bit of the pure is leaking marks."
  - q: "What is the large data set and does it matter?"
    a: "It is a set of real data AQA releases before the exam, and the Paper 3 statistics questions are built around it. Students who have never opened it lose marks on context they could have seen coming. A tutor works through it with your child so those questions feel familiar."
  - q: "Can my child use a formula booklet in the exam?"
    a: "Yes. AQA provides a formula booklet and students can use a graphical or scientific calculator. But the booklet is large, and knowing where things are, plus which formulae are not in it and have to be memorised, is its own skill. Tutors make sure your child is not hunting through pages under time pressure."
