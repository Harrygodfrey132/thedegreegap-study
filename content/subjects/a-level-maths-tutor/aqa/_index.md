assess_heading: "How AQA A-Level Maths is examined, paper by paper"
assess_intro: "AQA (spec 7357) is fully linear: three papers at the end of Year 13, each 2 hours and 100 marks, with no coursework and nothing banked from Year 12. Unlike Edexcel, AQA doesn't keep the applied content in one place. Mechanics sits in one paper and statistics in another, each next to pure, so the pure runs through all three."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1: Pure"
    detail: "2 hours, 100 marks, pure only. Proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials, differentiation and integration. It's the base the other two papers build on."
  - name: "Paper 2: Pure and Mechanics"
    detail: "2 hours, 100 marks. More pure, then a mechanics section on kinematics, forces and Newton's laws. Your child has to switch from pure technique to modelling a moving object in the same exam."
  - name: "Paper 3: Pure and Statistics"
    detail: "2 hours, 100 marks. Pure again, plus statistics built around the large data set AQA releases in advance. If your child hasn't met that data before exam day, the context questions give away easy marks."
  - name: "The formula booklet"
    detail: "AQA gives students a formula booklet and allows a graphical or scientific calculator. The booklet is long, though, and some formulae aren't in it, so knowing what to look up and what to learn is its own skill."
