assess_heading: "OCR A-Level Maths A (H240), paper by paper"
assess_intro: "This is OCR's Mathematics A, the H240 specification. There are three papers, all sat at the end of Year 13, each 2 hours, 100 marks and a third of the A-Level. Questions are printed in one booklet and answered in a separate Printed Answer Booklet. Your child's three marks are added up and the grade is set on that total."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1: Pure Mathematics (H240/01)"
    detail: "One 100-mark section of pure: proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences and series, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation, integration, numerical methods and vectors. OCR's 2025 report adds that GCSE knowledge, such as working with non-right-angled triangles, is fair game too."
  - name: "Paper 2: Pure Mathematics and Statistics (H240/02)"
    detail: "About 50 marks of pure, then about 50 of statistics: sampling, data presentation, probability, the binomial and normal distributions, and hypothesis testing. Some statistics questions are set on OCR's large data set and reward students who've worked with it."
  - name: "Paper 3: Pure Mathematics and Mechanics (H240/03)"
    detail: "About 50 marks of pure, then about 50 of mechanics: units, kinematics (projectiles included), forces and Newton's laws, and moments. Unless a question says otherwise, g = 9.8."
  - name: "The formulae pages"
    detail: "OCR doesn't hand out a separate booklet for Maths A. The formulae are printed on pages 2 and 3 of each question paper: series sums, the binomial series, the quotient rule, integration by parts, small-angle approximations, compound-angle identities, the trapezium rule, Newton-Raphson, the binomial distribution, a normal percentage-points table and the suvat equations."
  - name: "The large data set"
    detail: "Census data for England and Wales from 2001 and 2011: how people travel to work, and the age structure of the population, for each local authority. It's the only large data set listed for H240 on OCR's site in October 2026. Your child won't have a copy in the exam, though some data may be printed in a question."
board_points_heading: "Where OCR marks slip, and what a tutor does about it"
board_points:
  - title: "Reading the command words"
    body: "OCR's specification spells out what 'determine', 'show that', 'verify' and 'hence' each expect. 'Find' can be answered with the calculator. Under 'show detailed reasoning', the calculator can only check: the method has to be written out. A tutor goes through each one with your child until the right amount of working becomes a habit."
  - title: "Using the formulae at the front"
    body: "OCR's 2025 Paper 1 report noted that students who did less well weren't familiar with the formulae printed at the start of the paper. A tutor makes sure your child knows exactly what's given there, and learns the rest, like the double-angle formulae and the chain rule, by heart."
  - title: "Answering in context, not from memory"
    body: "The same report said too many students gave learnt, standard answers to a modelling question instead of answering what was asked. Paper 2's census questions work the same way. A tutor practises explanations that use the context, so the written marks become easier to pick up."

# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faq_items:
  - q: "How do I know if my child sits OCR A or OCR B (MEI)?"
    a: "Check the paper codes on a mock or past paper. OCR A papers are H240/01, H240/02 and H240/03. OCR B (MEI) papers start H640, and MEI's third paper is called Pure Mathematics and Comprehension. Both come from OCR, which now goes by Cambridge OCR, but they're separate specifications with different papers, so we check which one on the call before we match a tutor."
  - q: "What's on OCR A-Level Maths Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3?"
    a: "For OCR A, Paper 1 is all pure: proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials and logs, calculus, numerical methods and vectors. Paper 2 is about 50 marks of pure and 50 of statistics. Paper 3 is about 50 marks of pure and 50 of mechanics. Each paper is 2 hours and 100 marks, and the pure sections can test any pure topic."
  - q: "Is there a formula booklet for OCR A-Level Maths?"
    a: "Not a separate one for OCR A. The formulae are printed on pages 2 and 3 of each question paper, covering things like the binomial series, the quotient rule, integration by parts, Newton-Raphson, the binomial distribution and the suvat equations. The quadratic formula, laws of logs, double-angle formulae, sine and cosine rules, and the chain and product rules aren't given, so your child has to learn them."
  - q: "What is the OCR A-Level Maths large data set?"
    a: "For OCR A it's census data from 2001 and 2011, covering how people travel to work and the age structure of every local authority in England and Wales. Some Paper 2 questions assume your child knows it. In June 2025 one asked which local authority matched a pattern in the age data. MEI uses different data sets, with Large Data Set 9 for summer 2027."
  - q: "Where can I find OCR A-Level Maths past papers and mark schemes?"
    a: "On OCR's own website, on the A Level Mathematics A (H240) assessment page, with the mark schemes and examiners' reports for each series. In October 2026 the newest set there is June 2025. MEI's are on its own qualification page. Doing a paper is a good start, but having a tutor mark it with your child, the way an examiner would, is where most of the progress comes from."
  - q: "Where are OCR's 2026 A-Level Maths grade boundaries?"
    a: "On OCR's grade boundaries page, which has the June 2026 AS and A Level boundaries as a PDF. OCR adds up your child's marks from all three papers and compares that total with the A-Level boundaries, so no single paper decides the grade. The figures move a little each year with how the papers went, so treat an older boundary as a guide."
  - q: "Is OCR A-Level Maths harder than AQA or Edexcel?"
    a: "No. All three boards teach the same Department for Education content, and the grades mean the same. OCR's style is its own, though: it defines its command words closely and asks for detailed reasoning on some questions, so method matters a lot. That's a reason to have a tutor who knows OCR, rather than a sign it's tougher."
