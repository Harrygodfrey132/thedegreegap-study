faq_items:
  - q: "My child says German grammar makes no sense. Can a tutor fix that?"
    a: "Usually, yes. It tends to come down to a few rules taught once and never really settled: the verb going second, the verb jumping to the end after words like weil and dass, and the endings that change with the case. A tutor takes them one at a time, with lots of short practice, until your child stops guessing. Those same rules lift the writing, the speaking and the translation, so it's time well spent."
  - q: "My child goes blank in the speaking exam. What can a tutor do?"
    a: "Lots of practice out loud, with one person who's on their side. The tutor runs through the role-play, the photo card and the conversation until your child has ready answers for the common topics. They also learn a few phrases to buy thinking time. It's far easier to stay calm when you've said it all before."
  - q: "What changed in GCSE German recently?"
    a: "The course was updated for students sitting it from summer 2026. It now works from a set list of vocabulary, the listening includes a short dictation and the speaking includes reading a passage aloud. If your child's notes or past papers feel out of step, that's probably why. Our tutors know the newer papers, so lessons match what your child will actually sit."
  - q: "The translation questions keep losing marks. Why?"
    a: "Because they test accuracy more than anything else. A sentence can be nearly right and still drop marks for a missed tense, a wrong ending or a word left out. The tutor shows your child how to check each sentence against the original, and practises translating both ways, German to English and English to German."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. GCSE German is set by AQA, Edexcel and Eduqas, and each one words the speaking tasks and the writing questions in its own way. We'll pick tutors who know your child's board and tier. If you're not sure which it is, look on the front of a mock paper, or we'll help you find out on the call."
