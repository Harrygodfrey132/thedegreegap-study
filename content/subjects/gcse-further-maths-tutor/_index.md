faq_items:
  - q: "What exactly is GCSE Further Maths?"
    a: "It's the name most people use for an extra Maths qualification that some schools offer their strongest Maths students, usually alongside GCSE Maths. Most schools enter AQA's Level 2 Certificate in Further Maths, and some use OCR's Additional Maths FSMQ instead. Strictly speaking, neither one is a GCSE. Both go past the GCSE content into topics like calculus that normally wait until A-Level."
  - q: "How is the AQA certificate examined and graded?"
    a: "There are two written papers, one without a calculator and one with. It's graded 9 to 4 rather than 9 to 1, so the grades on offer start at a 4. If your child is sitting OCR's Additional Maths instead, tell us on the call and we'll look for a tutor who knows that paper."
  - q: "Why is my child finding it hard when they're good at Maths?"
    a: "Because it moves fast and asks for a different kind of thinking. Matrices, differentiation and algebraic proof are new to almost everyone, and there's often less class time to take them in. Your child may be used to Maths coming easily, so the first topic that doesn't can knock their confidence. A tutor slows down where school can't and makes sure each method is solid before moving on."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with GCSE Maths at the same time?"
    a: "Yes, and lots of families ask for both. The two overlap a good deal, so the tutor can sharpen the harder Higher tier topics while teaching the new Further Maths ones. Let us know on the call what the school expects for each, and we'll plan around it."
  - q: "Is it worth doing if my child wants to take A-Level Maths?"
    a: "For a lot of students, yes. Plenty of it overlaps with the start of A-Level Maths, so Year 12 feels like less of a jump. It isn't usually needed to get into sixth form, though, so if it's eating into GCSE revision, that's worth thinking about. We're happy to talk it over on the call."
  - q: "Do you cover where we live?"
    a: "Yes, anywhere in the UK. Every lesson takes place online, so a family in a small village has the same choice of tutors as one in a big city. That counts for more with Further Maths, because fewer tutors teach it and you're unlikely to find a strong one round the corner."
  - q: "How much does it cost?"
    a: "From £37 an hour. Each tutor sets their own rate, and you'll know the price before you book anything. There's no contract and no sign-up fee, and you're never asked to pay for a block of lessons in advance. The call with us and the first video meeting with the tutor cost nothing."
  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
    a: "Tell us, and we'll introduce someone new at no cost. It isn't awkward. It happens now and then, and it's far better than your child carrying on with lessons that aren't helping. Seeing two or three profiles at the start makes a good first match more likely too."
