  - q: "There's so much to learn in GCSE Biology. Can a tutor help it stick?"
    a: "Yes, and it's one of the most common reasons parents come to us for Biology. A tutor swaps that for quizzing your child and coming back to topics every few weeks, so it's still there in the exam, not just the night before."
  - q: "My child knows the facts but keeps losing marks on the six-mark questions. Why?"
    a: "Very common, and it's usually technique rather than knowledge. The longer answers are marked on building a clear chain of reasoning and on matching the command word, so 'describe' and 'explain' need different answers. A tutor goes through real mark schemes with your child until they can see what the examiner is counting, then practises it until it comes naturally."
  - q: "Do the tutors cover the required practicals?"
    a: "Yes. The practicals come up as questions in the written papers, so they matter as much as any topic. Tutors go through the ones students find slippery, like osmosis in potato cylinders, using a microscope, the food tests and sampling with quadrats, and teach your child to answer on method, variables and results rather than just remembering they once did it."
  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
