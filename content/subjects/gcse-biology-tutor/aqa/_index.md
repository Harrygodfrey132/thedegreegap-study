# ───────────── THE SPECIFICATION ─────────────
assess_heading: "AQA GCSE Biology 8461: Paper 1 and Paper 2 topics"
assess_intro: "AQA GCSE Biology (spec 8461) is two written exams, both sat at the end of the course, at Foundation tier (grades 1 to 5) or Higher tier (grades 4 to 9). Each paper is 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks, and counts for half the grade. There's no coursework. The questions mix multiple choice, short answers and longer open responses, and the required practicals come back as written questions. Here's what sits where."
papers:
  - name: "Paper 1 topics (8461/1F or 8461/1H)"
    detail: "Topics 1 to 4. Cell biology: cells, microscopy, mitosis, stem cells, diffusion and osmosis. Organisation: digestion and enzymes, the heart and blood, plant tissues. Infection and response: pathogens, vaccination, antibiotics, and monoclonal antibodies on Higher. Bioenergetics: photosynthesis and respiration."
  - name: "Paper 2 topics (8461/2F or 8461/2H)"
    detail: "Topics 5 to 7, with the same 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks. Homeostasis and response: the nervous system, brain and eye, hormones, blood glucose and the kidneys. Inheritance, variation and evolution: meiosis, DNA, genetic crosses, cloning and evolution. Ecology: ecosystems, material cycles, biodiversity and food security."
  - name: "The ten required practicals"
    detail: "Questions on them make up at least 15% of the marks. Six belong to Paper 1 topics: microscopy, antibiotics on bacteria, osmosis, food tests, amylase and pH, and light and photosynthesis. Four belong to Paper 2: reaction time, seedlings and light or gravity, quadrat sampling, and the decay of milk."
  - name: "On the day: calculator, ruler, no equation sheet"
    detail: "The papers ask for a ruler and a scientific calculator. There's no formula or equation sheet in GCSE Biology, so magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object, and πr² for the antibiotics plates, have to be known. At least 10% of the marks test maths."

# ───────────── FAQ ─────────────
faq_items:
  - q: "What's the spec code for AQA GCSE Biology, and how do I know my child sits it?"
    a: "AQA GCSE Biology is spec 8461. The papers are labelled 8461/1F and 8461/2F at Foundation, or 8461/1H and 8461/2H at Higher, so a mock paper or the exam timetable will tell you. If it says Combined Science: Trilogy or Synergy instead, your child is on the combined course, not separate Biology. We'll check it with you on the call too."
  - q: "What topics are on AQA GCSE Biology Paper 1?"
    a: "Paper 1 covers Topics 1 to 4: cell biology, organisation, infection and response, and bioenergetics. In practice that's cells and microscopy, mitosis and stem cells, diffusion and osmosis, digestion and enzymes, the heart and blood, disease, vaccination and antibiotics, then photosynthesis and respiration. Six of the ten required practicals belong to these topics. It's 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks."
  - q: "What's on AQA GCSE Biology Paper 2?"
    a: "Paper 2 covers Topics 5 to 7: homeostasis and response, inheritance, variation and evolution, and ecology. That's the nervous system, the brain and eye, hormones, blood glucose and the kidneys, then meiosis, DNA, genetic crosses, evolution and classification, and finally ecosystems, material cycles and biodiversity. It carries the same 100 marks as Paper 1, and the other four required practicals sit here."
  - q: "Is there a formula sheet for AQA GCSE Biology?"
    a: "No. Ofqual's formula and equation sheets for the 2025 to 2027 exams are for GCSE Maths, Physics and Combined Science, so there's no sheet in the summer 2027 Biology papers. Your child needs a ruler and a scientific calculator. The formulas the specification uses, like magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object and πr² for the antibiotics plates, have to be learned."
  - q: "Where can we find AQA GCSE Biology past papers and mark schemes?"
    a: "On AQA's own website. Its past paper finder, at aqa.org.uk/pastpapers, has the 8461 question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports. Past papers are the best practice going, but only if they're marked the way AQA marks them. That's a big part of what a tutor does: going through each answer with your child against the mark scheme and fixing the habit that cost the mark."
  - q: "When were the AQA GCSE Biology grade boundaries for 2026 published?"
    a: "AQA published the June 2026 GCSE grade boundaries at 8am on results day, 20 August 2026, on its grade boundaries page. Each boundary is the lowest total mark across both papers, out of 200, that earns a grade. They're set after marking, so a harder paper doesn't count against anyone, which is why they move every year. Useful as a guide, never as a target."
  - q: "Is AQA Biology harder than Edexcel or OCR?"
    a: "No. All three follow the same Ofqual rules: the same 9 to 1 grades and the same split of marks between knowing, applying and analysing. What's different is the feel. AQA keeps each paper to its own topics, and says its questions use plain language and fewer contexts. So the choice of board matters less than practising with that board's own papers."
