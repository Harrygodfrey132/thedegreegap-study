faq_items:
  - q: "Do you cover where we live?"
    a: "Yes, we cover the whole of the UK. All our lessons are online, so it doesn't matter whether you're in a city or a village. It also means we can pick the tutor who's the best fit for your child, rather than whoever happens to live nearby."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs a reading coach?"
    a: "A few things tend to give it away. Reading aloud that's slow or full of guesses, a reading-age letter that came back lower than you hoped, or a child who used to love stories and now finds every excuse not to open a book. Any of those is a good reason to talk to us, and we'll tell you honestly if we don't think a coach is what your child needs."
  - q: "My child says they hate reading. Is that normal?"
    a: "Very normal, and it's one of the most common things parents tell us. A child who says they hate reading has often just found it hard for a long time, so avoiding it feels easier. A patient tutor starts below the point of struggle, finds books worth finishing and lets small wins do the rest. We can't promise a bookworm, but the dread usually lifts."
  - q: "What does a reading coach actually do in a lesson?"
    a: "The first lesson is mostly listening: your child reads aloud, gently, with no clock running, and the tutor hears where it slows down. Reading is several skills stacked together, sounding words out, reading smoothly, understanding the meaning and wanting to keep going. After that first read, lessons build on whichever one has come loose."
  - q: "What age is reading coaching right for?"
    a: "Mostly primary and lower secondary, the years when reading is meant to become automatic and school moves on assuming it has. Age matters less than where your child is with reading, though. Tell us what's going on on the call and we'll tell you honestly whether it's a good fit."
  - q: "Does online tutoring actually work for reading?"
    a: "It does, and most children settle in quickly. Lessons use a platform called Lessonspace, where your child and the tutor read off the same screen, and the tutor can highlight a word or bring up a new page in a second. Every lesson has a replay too, so you can see how it's going."
  - q: "My child is dyslexic. Can you still help?"
    a: "Yes. Tell us about it on the call and we'll pick tutors who've worked with children who find reading hard and know how to go at their pace. Reading and writing can be tiring for a dyslexic child, and that has nothing to do with how bright they are. Lessons give it the time it takes, without anyone making your child feel slow."
  - q: "How much does it cost?"
    a: "Lessons start from £37 an hour. The exact price depends on the tutor, and we'll always agree it with you before anything is booked. There's no contract, no joining fee and nothing to pay upfront. You just pay for the lessons you have, and the first chat with us and the video meeting with your tutor are free."
  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
    a: "Just let us know and we'll find someone else, with no charge and no awkward conversation. It happens, and for a child who's gone off reading, liking the person they read with matters a lot. It's also why you hear from two or three tutors before choosing, rather than being handed one."
