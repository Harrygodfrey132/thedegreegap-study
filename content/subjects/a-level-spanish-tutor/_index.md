faq_items:
  - q: "My child speaks Spanish well. Why is the translation into Spanish losing marks?"
    a: "Because speaking fluently and translating precisely are different skills, and it's one of the most common things we hear. The translation into Spanish is where accuracy is tested hardest. The tutor works through past translations line by line, on the things that decide the marks: the subjunctive, ser and estar, por and para, and getting the tense to match the English exactly."
  - q: "The essays on the book and film come back as \"too descriptive\". What does a tutor do?"
    a: "That comment is often the gap between a middle grade and a top one. Most students know the story of a book like La casa de Bernarda Alba or a film like Volver inside out, but retelling the plot earns very little. The tutor teaches your child to take a theme, tie it to specific moments and build an argument in Spanish, then marks practice essays the way the board does."
  - q: "The subjunctive still trips my child up. Is that worth tutoring?"
    a: "Yes, because it runs through everything at A-Level, the translation, the essays and the speaking. The tutor sorts out when the subjunctive is needed, after wishes, doubts, emotions and certain linking words, until it becomes a habit rather than a guess. Once that clicks, the writing gets more accurate and more ambitious at the same time."
  - q: "Can a tutor help with the speaking exam and the Individual Research Project?"
    a: "Yes. The IRP is part of the speaking exam, and it rewards preparation more than natural fluency. The tutor helps your child pick a focused topic they can talk about with confidence, build the discussion around it and practise the kind of follow-up questions the examiner asks, all out loud."
  - q: "Do the tutors know my child's exam board?"
    a: "Yes. AQA and Edexcel both test translation both ways, essays on a book and a film, and an independent research project in the speaking, but they set different works and lay out the papers their own way. On AQA, for example, the translation sits in Paper 1 and the essays in Paper 2, while Edexcel puts the translation into Spanish and the essays together in Paper 2. We'll pick tutors who know your child's board. Not sure which it is? We can work it out on the call."

  - q: "What if we don't click with the tutor?"
