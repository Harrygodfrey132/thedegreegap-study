faq_items:
  - q: "My child loves Psychology but struggles in the exam. Why?"
    a: "It's often because the exam needs more than interest. Your child has to remember who did each study, what they found and what's good or bad about it, then use that in a written answer. Lots of students know the topic in a chatty way but can't put it in exam terms. A tutor helps them learn the key studies properly and turn them into answers that score."
  - q: "What are research methods, and why do they matter?"
    a: "Research methods is how psychologists plan and run their studies. Your child needs to understand things like variables, sampling, ethics and the different types of experiment, and to spot the flaws in a study they've never seen. It's tested a lot and it catches many students out. A tutor works through it with clear examples and lots of practice questions."
  - q: "Is there maths in GCSE Psychology?"
    a: "There is some, and it surprises a few families. Your child may need to work out a mean, median or mode, a percentage or a ratio, and read or draw simple graphs and tables. None of it is hard on its own, but it's easy to rush. The tutor makes sure your child is comfortable with each skill."
  - q: "How do they get top marks on the longer answers?"
    a: "By describing clearly and then evaluating. Your child needs to explain a theory or study, then give strengths and weaknesses backed by evidence, and link it all back to the question. A tutor marks practice answers the way an examiner would and shows your child exactly what was missing, one answer at a time."
