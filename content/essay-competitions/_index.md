## Also worth knowing

A few competitions come up in searches that don't get a full guide here, either because they're small, because they've changed shape, or because the prize is a discount on the organiser's own courses.

**The Royal Geographical Society School Essay Competition**, run with the Financial Times, is the one live geography essay prize for sixth formers. Key Stage 5 geography students write up to 1,000 words on a set question, the 2026 one on what drives migration and how the patterns are shifting, with a deadline of 9 November 2026. The winner is published on the FT and RGS websites. Free to enter, and a good fit for a Geography or Economics applicant. The Society's better-known Young Geographer of the Year is a poster or StoryMap competition, not an essay.

**Northeastern University London**, formerly New College of the Humanities, ran a well-regarded subject essay competition for Year 12 for years, with cash prizes and a January deadline. In 2026 it replaced that with an AI Futures Challenge in February, with a £1,000 scholarship to the university for winners. Check which is running before a student writes to the old brief.

**Immerse Education's essay competition** is free, open to 13 to 18-year-olds worldwide and asks for 500 words. The prize is a full or partial scholarship to Immerse's own paid summer and online programmes. It's a fine writing exercise. Treat the prize as a discount rather than an award.

**Minds Underground** and **Medic Mentor** both run free essay competitions for school students, and both are commercial tutoring or application-support companies. Good practice, and the feedback can be useful. A certificate from either carries less weight in a reference than a commendation from a university department, so don't let one crowd out the free prizes above.

**Not open to school students**, despite how they look in search results: the Uehiro Oxford Prize in Practical Ethics (UK university students only), the Inns of Court and Bar Council essay prizes (student members and Bar trainees), and the Royal Society of Medicine and BMA writing prizes (medical students and doctors).
