## Ten GCSE revision tips that work

Most revision advice sounds sensible, but not all of it works. These ten tips come from what our tutors see in lessons every week, and from the research on how memory works. They hold for A-Level too.

### 1. Test yourself instead of re-reading

Close the book, write down everything you can remember, then check what you missed. Flashcards, practice questions and a blank-page brain dump all count. Re-reading feels productive because the notes look familiar, but recognising something isn't the same as being able to recall it in an exam.

### 2. Space it out

Come back to each topic several times, a few days or weeks apart, rather than in one long go. It feels slower, and that's the point: each time you pull a topic back out of memory, it sticks a little better. A topic revised in October, January and March will still be there in May.

### 3. Mix topics up

Switch between topics, and between subjects, within a session. Twenty questions on one topic in a row feels smooth, but in the exam the questions arrive mixed up, and part of the skill is spotting which method each one needs. Mixed practice trains exactly that.

### 4. Start with what costs the most marks

Rate every topic red, amber or green, then start with the reds that come up most often. Mock papers are the best guide you've got, because the questions that lost marks this term are next week's plan.

### 5. Do past papers against the clock

From the spring, do full papers or whole sections in the time the exam allows. Timing is a skill of its own, and plenty of students who know the content still run out of time on the long questions. The exam boards put past papers and mark schemes on their websites.

### 6. Mark them with the mark scheme

Marking your own paper is where most of the learning happens. The mark scheme shows what examiners reward, which is often a particular word, step or unit. Sort every lost mark into one of three piles: didn't know it, misread the question, or ran out of time. Each pile needs a different fix.

### 7. Keep sessions short

Work in sessions of 30 to 45 minutes, with a five to ten minute break between them. Two or three focused sessions in an evening do more than one long one, and they're far easier to keep up from now until the exams.

### 8. Put your phone in another room

Not face down on the desk, but in another room. Every notification pulls your attention away, and it takes a while to get back to where you were. Save the phone for the breaks.

### 9. Explain it out loud

If you can explain a topic to someone else without your notes, you know it. If you can't, you've found what to revise next. A parent, a brother or sister, or an empty chair will all do.

### 10. Protect your sleep

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 8 to 10 hours a night for teenagers. Sleep is when the brain holds on to what it learned that day, so swapping sleep for an extra hour of revision is rarely a good deal, least of all the night before an exam.
