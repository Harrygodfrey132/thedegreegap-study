October's TMUA and ESAT results came out in mid-November. If your child applied to Oxford or Cambridge, that score is already with the colleges. If they're applying to Imperial, LSE, Warwick, Durham or UCL instead, there's still a January sitting, and **booking for it closes at 6pm on 21 December 2026**.

For 2027 entry these two tests sit at the front of the shortlist for maths, computer science, economics, engineering and the sciences at the most selective universities in the UK. The rules about who can sit when, and who sees the score, catch families out every year. This guide covers what the tests are, which courses use them, how the scoring works, what score you need, and how to use the weeks before January. If you'd like to talk it through, [book a free 30-minute call](/book-a-call/).

## What the TMUA and ESAT are

Both tests are run by UAT-UK, set up by the University of Cambridge and Imperial College London. Candidates sit them on a computer at a Pearson VUE test centre. A third UAT-UK test, the TARA, covers critical thinking, problem solving and a short writing task.

**The TMUA** (Test of Mathematics for University Admission) is two multiple-choice papers of 20 questions each, with 75 minutes per paper. Paper 1 tests whether you can apply maths you already know to unfamiliar problems. Paper 2 tests mathematical reasoning and logic: what follows from what, and whether an argument actually holds. The content is drawn from higher-tier GCSE and AS-Level maths. You can't use a calculator, and you get one overall score.

**The ESAT** (Engineering and Science Admissions Test) is made up of 40-minute multiple-choice modules taken back to back. Mathematics 1 is compulsory for everyone. Most candidates then take two more from Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Mathematics 2, depending on the course. Each module gets its own score.

Neither test takes marks off for a wrong answer, so leaving a question blank is always a mistake.

## Which universities use them for 2027 entry

| University | TMUA | ESAT | January sitting allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cambridge | Mathematics, Computer Science, Economics | Engineering, Natural Sciences, Chemical Engineering and Biotechnology, Veterinary Medicine | No (rare exceptions) |
| Oxford | Mathematics and its joint courses, Computer Science and its joint courses | Engineering Science, Physics, Biomedical Sciences | No (rare exceptions) |
| Imperial | Mathematics, Economics, Finance and Data Science, Computing | Engineering courses, Physics | Yes |
| LSE | Economics, Econometrics and Mathematical Economics | Not used | Yes, though LSE encourages October |
| Warwick | Mathematics (TMUA or STEP), and other maths-heavy courses | Not used | Yes |
| Durham | Mathematics (as a route to a lower offer) | Not used | Yes |
| UCL | Economics | Some engineering | Yes |

The test can be compulsory for one course and optional for another at the same university, so always check the specific course page before you book.

## October or January: who can still sit

Oxford and Cambridge applicants have to sit in October. If you missed the October window, you can't use January for an Oxford or Cambridge application, apart from a few narrow exceptions. For everyone else, UAT-UK says there's no advantage to either sitting.

Two rules matter more than the date.

**You get one attempt per admissions cycle.** If your child sat the TMUA in October for Cambridge, that's their score for Warwick and LSE too. January isn't a second chance.

**You can't hide your score.** Results go automatically to every participating university on your UCAS application whose course requires or accepts the test. Add your UCAS Personal ID to your UAT-UK profile so the match works.

There's also a timing quirk. The January window runs from 4 to 8 January and the [UCAS deadline](/blog/ucas-deadline-2027/) is 6pm on 13 January, but January results don't arrive until 8 February. Your child will submit their UCAS choices before they know their score, so choose courses based on predicted grades and timed practice papers, not on hope.

If your child gets extra time or other access arrangements at school, for dyslexia for example, the 7 December deadline is the one to diarise. Our guide to [GCSE support for a dyslexic child](/blog/gcse-tutoring-dyslexic-child/) explains how access arrangements work.

Each test costs £78 for candidates in the UK and the Republic of Ireland and £133 elsewhere. UK candidates in financial need can apply for a bursary.

## How the scores actually work

Scores run from 1.0 to 9.0, to one decimal place. UAT-UK uses a statistical method that puts question difficulty and candidate ability on the same scale and equates different versions of the test, so a 6.0 on one version means the same as a 6.0 on another. That's also why there's no raw-mark conversion table.

The scale is anchored to the October cohort, with the median candidate at around 4.5. Remember who that cohort is: students applying for some of the most competitive courses in the country. A 4.5 means the middle of a very strong field, not "average".

There's no pass mark, and UAT-UK doesn't accept appeals against scores.

## What score do you need?

Most universities don't publish a cut-off. Oxford and Cambridge weigh the score alongside GCSEs, predicted grades, school context and the rest of the application. The universities that do say something are worth knowing about, because for them a strong TMUA can change the offer itself.

- **Warwick Mathematics:** in the last admissions cycle most offers went to applicants with a TMUA score of 5.0 or above, though some lower scorers still received offers.
- **Warwick Economics:** the TMUA is optional, but the highest scorers may receive a lower offer.
- **Durham Mathematics:** a suitable performance in an accepted maths test such as the TMUA or STEP can lower the offer by a grade.

You'll also see "typical" scores of 6.5 or more quoted for Oxford, Imperial and LSE. These are estimates; none of those universities publishes such a figure.

The practical point for parents: at Warwick and Durham, preparation isn't only about clearing a hurdle. A good score can take a grade off the A-Level offer.

## If the October result was disappointing

You can't resit this cycle, so put your energy where it can still make a difference.

- **Keep it in proportion.** The median is around 4.5 among a very strong group. Oxford and Cambridge look at the whole application.
- **Prepare for interview anyway.** If an invitation arrives, the interview can carry real weight. Our [Oxford and Cambridge interview guide](/blog/oxford-cambridge-interview-preparation-2026/) explains how to prepare.
- **Focus on the A-Level route.** At Warwick and Durham Mathematics, the standard A-Level offer still stands without a top test score. Hitting top grades in Maths and Further Maths is now the priority; our guide to [the hardest A-Levels](/blog/hardest-a-levels/) explains why Further Maths is tougher than its results suggest.
- **Check your UCAS choices.** If your child used fewer than five, they can still add a course, perhaps one that doesn't use the test.

## A plan for the January sitting

From late November there are about five or six weeks until the January window. That's enough to make a real difference if the work is focused.

**Week 1: book and diagnose.** Book the test now, because slots at local centres fill up. Request access arrangements before 7 December if needed. Then sit one full past paper under timed conditions, with no preparation, to see where the gaps are.

**Weeks 2 and 3: close content gaps.** For the TMUA, that usually means AS-Level pure maths not yet taught in school: coordinate geometry, sequences and series, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation and integration. For Paper 2, practise the logic: necessary versus sufficient conditions, "if and only if", proof by contradiction and spotting the faulty step in an argument. For the ESAT, work through the specification for each module.

**Week 4: timed sections.** The TMUA gives you 75 minutes for 20 questions, a little under four minutes each. ESAT modules are 40 minutes each. Practise skipping a hard question and coming back to it, and never leave an answer blank.

**Christmas: full mocks.** Sit two complete mocks under exam conditions and review every wrong answer properly. Our [free revision timetable](/revision-timetable/) can help fit this around time off.

**1 to 3 January: light review and logistics.** Revisit the error log, check the test centre route and pack ID.

For materials, start with the official practice papers on the UAT-UK website.

## In Year 12? Plan for 2028 entry now

UAT-UK hasn't yet published dates for 2028 entry. If this year's pattern holds, booking will open in the summer and the October sitting will be in mid-October 2027. The summer after Year 12 exams is the main preparation window, because by then most of the AS content the TMUA draws on has been taught.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you resit the TMUA or ESAT in January if October went badly?

No. You can sit each test once per admissions cycle. If you sat in October, that score is the one every participating university on your UCAS application will see.

### What is a good TMUA score?

There is no pass mark. The scale runs from 1.0 to 9.0 and the median October candidate scores around 4.5. Warwick says most of its Mathematics offers last cycle went to applicants scoring 5.0 or more.

### Can Oxford or Cambridge applicants sit the TMUA or ESAT in January?

No. Oxford and Cambridge applicants must sit in October, apart from a small number of exceptions such as some mature applicants to Cambridge.

### How much does the TMUA cost?

Each UAT-UK test costs £78 in the UK and the Republic of Ireland and £133 elsewhere. UK candidates in financial need can request a bursary, with a deadline of 6pm on 14 December 2026 for the January sitting.

### When is the deadline to book the January 2027 TMUA or ESAT?

Booking closes at 6pm on 21 December 2026. The deadline for access arrangements is earlier, at 6pm on 7 December 2026. The test window runs from 4 to 8 January 2027.

### When do January TMUA and ESAT results come out?

Results for the January 2027 sitting are released on 8 February 2027, after the UCAS deadline of 13 January. Scores go automatically to the participating universities on your UCAS application whose course requires or accepts the test.
