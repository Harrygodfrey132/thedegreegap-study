If you've gone looking for a tutor on First Tutors recently, you'll have found a closure notice instead of a search page. After more than 20 years, one of the UK's best-known tutor directories shut in spring 2026. A lot of parents are now trying to work out where to look instead, often with mocks coming up and not much time to spend on it.

This guide covers what happened to First Tutors, the main **First Tutors alternatives** in the UK, how each one actually works, and how to choose. I run a tutoring company myself, so I've been clear about where we fit and kept the rest as fair as I can.

## The quick answer

- **Most like First Tutors:** Tutor Hunt and Superprof. You search for tutors yourself, near you or online.
- **Online platforms with checks built in:** Tutorful and MyTutor.
- **Specialist and university-level tutoring:** Spires.
- **A smaller company with classes and resources too:** [The Education Hotel](https://educationhotel.co.uk/).
- **If you'd rather someone matched your child for you:** an agency such as [The Degree Gap](/book-a-call/).

## What happened to First Tutors?

First Tutors closed in spring 2026. Its website now says: "After more than 20 years of trading, First Tutors has made the difficult decision to close." It gives no reason. Customers with questions are asked to get in touch by email, with a warning that replies will be slow.

For anyone who hasn't used it, First Tutors was a directory. You searched by subject and postcode, read profiles, and messaged tutors. When a tutor accepted, you paid First Tutors a one-off finder's fee for their contact details, then paid the tutor directly for every lesson. Plenty of families liked it because it was simple, local and cheap.

**If your child already has a tutor from First Tutors, you don't need to change anything.** Your arrangement was always with the tutor. If it's working, carry on.

Since the closure, a handful of new sites have started calling themselves the replacement for First Tutors. Some may turn out well. But a new site has no track record yet, so check how it checks its tutors before you use it.

## Three kinds of alternative

Most of the confusion comes from comparing things that work differently. There are three broad types.

1. **Tutor marketplaces.** You search and contact tutors yourself. Usually the cheapest per hour, and the closest to how First Tutors worked. The checking is mostly up to you.
2. **Online tutoring platforms.** A company takes on tutors who pass its checks, and you book and pay through its website. Lessons are online, often recorded.
3. **Agencies.** Someone talks to you first and suggests tutors who fit your child. It costs a bit more, and you don't have to sift through hundreds of profiles yourself.

None of these is right for everyone. It depends on how much time you have, whether you want lessons at home or online, and how sure you are about what your child needs.

## The main First Tutors alternatives

Details below are from each company's own website, checked in November 2026. Prices and terms change, so it's worth checking before you book.

### Tutor Hunt

The closest like-for-like replacement. You search by postcode or for online tutors, and you can message as many tutors as you like for free. Tutors set their rates, and the price on their profile already includes Tutor Hunt's fees. You pay through the site after each lesson, not in advance.

Tutor Hunt says every tutor must hold an enhanced DBS certificate, which its staff review, and tutors upload their qualifications and ID.

**Good for:** families who liked First Tutors' search-it-yourself approach, including those who want lessons at home.

### Superprof

A large marketplace covering school subjects alongside music, languages and hobbies. To contact tutors you buy a Student Pass, which Superprof's own guide lists at £39 a month, and you can cancel it at any time. You then pay the tutor directly at whatever rate they set.

**Good for:** families comfortable doing their own checks who want a wide choice, in person or online.

### Tutorful

An online platform with lessons from £20 an hour. Tutorful says it accepts about 1 in 8 tutors who apply, and that tutors have an enhanced DBS check and at least two years' experience. You book and pay through the platform, lessons are recorded, and if a tutor isn't the right fit, Tutorful says it will cover your next lesson with a new one.

**Good for:** families who want to browse profiles themselves, with the safety checks done for them.

### MyTutor

An online platform for Key Stage 2 up to GCSE and A-Level. Most of its tutors are university students or recent graduates who sat the same exams not long ago. MyTutor says it accepts about 1 in 8 applicants, interviews them and requires an enhanced DBS check, with tutors from £26 an hour.

**Good for:** students who'd rather learn from someone close to their own age, and families on a tighter budget.

### Spires

An online platform where you post what you need and tutors respond with their rate. Spires covers school subjects and goes further than most, into university and postgraduate tutoring. It says it accepts about 7% of applicants and checks them with an interview, a demonstration lesson and an enhanced DBS check.

**Good for:** older students and specialist subjects.

### The Education Hotel

[The Education Hotel](https://educationhotel.co.uk/) is a smaller tutoring company run by its director, Jemma Smith, an Oxford graduate with a teaching qualification. It offers one-to-one tuition online and in person, plus termly group classes and revision courses, from the 11+ through GCSE and iGCSE to A-Level and IB, including support for children with special educational needs. Tutors are screened, DBS-checked and interviewed by the director, and families get access to a members' club with practice papers and learning resources.

**Good for:** families who want a smaller, personal service, or classes and resources alongside one-to-one lessons.

### The Degree Gap

That's us. We're an online tutoring agency for GCSE, A-Level, the IB and the 11+. Every family starts with a free call, usually about 30 minutes, with me or my co-founder Joe. Within 24 hours of that call you get two or three tutor profiles matched to your child and their exam board, and your child meets the one you like on a free 15-minute video call before anything is booked.

Joe and I interview every tutor ourselves, and about 3% of applicants make it onto the platform. Lessons are online, recorded, and start from £37 an hour, with no contract. If the tutor isn't right, we find another.

**Good for:** families who'd rather talk it through and have the matching done for them.

## How to choose

A few questions sort most families out quickly.

**Do you want lessons at home or online?** If it has to be at home, Tutor Hunt, Superprof and The Education Hotel all offer in-person tutoring. If online is fine, you can choose from tutors anywhere in the country, which matters most for less common subjects and specific exam boards.

**How much time do you have to search?** Marketplaces give you the most choice, but you're the one reading profiles and checking references. If it's November and your child's mocks are in January, a matched service saves you a fortnight.

**Do you know what your child needs?** If you know it's "AQA GCSE Maths, Higher tier, mostly algebra", searching yourself is easy. If all you know is that the grades have slipped and they won't talk about it, a conversation first usually helps more than a list of profiles.

**What happens if it doesn't work out?** Ask before you book. Some services replace the tutor for you; with others, you start the search again.

**Check the safeguarding.** Whichever route you choose, make sure anyone tutoring your child has a current enhanced DBS certificate, and that the first lesson or two happen where you can see or hear them.

## Common questions

### What happened to First Tutors?

First Tutors closed in spring 2026, after more than 20 years of trading. Its website now shows a closure notice and gives no reason. The company says customers with questions can still contact it by email, but warns that replies are likely to be slow.

### Can I keep the tutor I found through First Tutors?

Yes. First Tutors charged families a one-off fee to get a tutor's contact details, and lessons were then paid to the tutor directly. Your arrangement was always with the tutor, so if it's working, you can simply carry on.

### What is the closest alternative to First Tutors?

Tutor Hunt and Superprof work most like First Tutors: you search for tutors near you or online and contact them yourself. The main difference is how you pay. Tutor Hunt takes payment through its site after each lesson, while Superprof charges a monthly fee to contact tutors and you pay the tutor directly.

### Is it cheaper to find a tutor through a marketplace or an agency?

Marketplaces are usually cheaper per hour, because you do the searching yourself. Online platforms tend to start at around £20 to £26 an hour, and agencies cost a little more because someone matches your child for you and replaces the tutor if it isn't working. The Degree Gap starts from £37 an hour, with no contract.

### Are online tutors as good as tutors who come to your home?

For most GCSE and A-Level students, yes. Online lessons let you choose from tutors across the country rather than whoever lives nearby, and many platforms record lessons so your child can watch them back. Some younger children and some children who struggle to focus on a screen do better in person.
