"Which A-Levels are hardest?" is one of the first questions Year 11 students ask, and most answers online are opinion. The real answer has two parts. Some subjects award fewer top grades because of who takes them. Others are genuinely graded more harshly than the rest, and Ofqual's own research shows which.

This guide uses the 2026 results to explain both, then sets out how to choose. If you'd like to talk through your child's options, [book a free 30-minute call](/book-a-call/).

## A-Level results by subject in 2026

These are the UK results for the most popular A-Levels in summer 2026, from the Joint Council for Qualifications (JCQ), sorted by the share of top grades (percentages rounded to the nearest whole number):

| Art and design | 42,620 | 35% | 99% |
| Physics | 45,800 | 32% | 96% |
| Chemistry | 65,541 | 32% | 96% |

| Biology | 73,925 | 28% | 96% |

At first glance, further maths and maths look easiest and business studies looks hardest. That's misleading.

## Why top-grade rates don't tell you which subjects are hardest

Further maths has the highest share of top grades because almost everyone who takes it is a very strong mathematician. Subjects like business studies and psychology are taken by a much wider range of students, so the overall spread of grades is wider.

The better question is how the same student would do in different subjects. Ofqual has studied exactly that. Its research on inter-subject comparability found that the sciences are among the most severely graded A-Levels, with languages not far behind. FFT Education Datalab, using a similar method, found physics graded most severely of all.

Ofqual decided not to change grading in the sciences and languages, but committed to make sure they don't become more severely graded in future.

The pass rates point the same way. Among the larger subjects, physics and chemistry had some of the lowest pass rates in 2026.

## So which A-Levels are hardest?

On the evidence, the subjects where the same student is likely to get a lower grade are:

- **Physics**, the most severely graded subject in the research and one with the lowest pass rates in 2026
- **Chemistry**, with heavy content and a lot of maths
- **Further maths**, which is hard in itself, even though its takers make its results look easy
- **Biology**, mainly because of the volume of content
- **Modern languages**, which the research also finds among the more severely graded subjects

Maths sits just behind them. It isn't graded as severely, but the jump from GCSE is one of the biggest of any subject.

## The jump from GCSE

A-Level maths starts roughly where the hardest GCSE algebra leaves off, so students who found grade 8 and 9 topics hard at GCSE often struggle in the first term. That's why many sixth forms ask for a grade 7 in GCSE maths, and an 8 or 9 for further maths.

Physics and chemistry depend heavily on maths too. A student with a 6 in GCSE maths may find A-Level physics much harder than their science grade suggests.

Essay subjects are hard in a different way. History, English literature and politics ask for extended, well-argued writing under time pressure, which is a big step up from GCSE answers.

## How to choose A-Levels

Difficulty is only one factor. In order of importance:

1. **Check what your child's likely degree needs.** Medicine needs chemistry and usually biology. Engineering needs maths and usually physics. Law needs no particular subject. Our guides to [A-Levels for medicine](/blog/a-levels-for-medicine/) and [A-Levels for law](/blog/a-levels-for-law/) go into detail.
2. **Choose subjects they're good at and enjoy.** Two years is a long time to study something they dislike, and enjoyment drives effort.
3. **Use GCSE grades as a guide.** A grade 7 or above in a subject, or in maths for the sciences, is a good sign they'll cope with the A-Level.
4. **Don't pick "easy" subjects to game the system.** Strong grades in subjects your child enjoys are worth more than a combination chosen to look impressive or to avoid hard work.
5. **Balance the workload.** Three essay subjects or three content-heavy sciences can be tough to manage. A mix often works better.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the hardest A-Level?

On the evidence, physics. Research by Ofqual and FFT Education Datalab finds it among the most severely graded A-Levels, and it had one of the lowest pass rates of the larger subjects in 2026.

### What are the easiest A-Levels?

No A-Level is easy, and subjects with more top grades aren't necessarily easier. The research suggests the sciences and languages are graded more severely than most other subjects, but the best choice is still the one your child is good at and enjoys.

### Is A-Level maths hard?

It's a big step up from GCSE. That's why many sixth forms ask for a grade 7 in GCSE maths. Students who are confident with grade 8 and 9 algebra tend to do well.

### Why does further maths have so many A* grades?

Because almost everyone who takes it is a very strong mathematician. Its high share of top grades reflects the students rather than an easy subject.

### What A-Levels should I take?

Start with what your likely degree needs, then choose subjects you're good at and enjoy. Use GCSE grades as a guide, and balance essay subjects with others so the workload is manageable.

### How many A-Levels should I take?

Three is the standard for university offers. A fourth, such as further maths, can help for some maths-heavy degrees, but three strong grades are worth more than four weaker ones.
