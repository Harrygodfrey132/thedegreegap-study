In Year 9, many families are asked to choose between triple science and combined science, often with little explanation of what the choice actually changes. The honest answer is that both routes can lead to science A-Levels, medicine and engineering. The real differences are workload, depth and how your child feels about science.

This guide explains how the two routes differ, what sixth forms and universities really ask for, and how to decide. If you'd like to talk through your child's options, [book a free 30-minute call](/book-a-call/).

## The difference at a glance

Both routes cover biology, chemistry and physics. With AQA, the most common board, they compare like this:

| | Combined Science | Triple Science |
|---|---|---|
| GCSEs awarded | 2 | 3 |
| Grades | One double grade, such as 7-6 | A separate grade for each science |
| Exam papers | 6 papers of 1 hour 15 minutes | 6 papers of 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Total exam time | 7 hours 30 minutes | 10 hours 30 minutes |
| Content | The core of each science | Everything in combined, plus extra topics and more depth |
| Lesson time | Standard science lessons | More science lessons, often using one option slot |

Combined science is the default route in most English state schools. Triple science is usually offered as an option, or to top sets.

## How combined science grades work

Combined science gives two grades that sit next to each other on a 17-point scale, from 9-9 at the top to 1-1 at the bottom. Higher tier covers the top of the scale and Foundation the bottom. A student with 7-6 has two GCSEs, one at grade 7 and one at grade 6. Sixth forms read them that way.

Your child sits two papers each in biology, chemistry and physics, and the marks are combined into the double grade. A weak physics paper can be balanced by strong biology, which some students find reassuring.

## Is triple science harder?

It's more work rather than harder work. Triple science covers more content in more depth, and its papers are longer. But grade for grade, a 7 in triple biology isn't a higher standard than the biology part of a 7-7 in combined science.

One thing that does change is the competition. Triple science students tend to be strong scientists, which is one reason grade boundaries on the triple papers often sit higher.

## Do you need triple science for A-Level science?

Usually not. Most sixth forms accept combined science for A-Level biology, chemistry and physics, typically asking for grades such as 6-6 or 7-7. Check the entry requirements of the sixth forms your child is considering, because they vary.

Triple science does make the jump to A-Level a little smoother, especially in chemistry and physics, because some of the extra content comes up again early in Year 12. Students who took combined science can close that gap with some reading over the summer.

## Do you need triple science for medicine?

No. Medical schools look at A-Level choices and grades, GCSE grades overall, the admissions test and the interview. They don't generally require triple science at GCSE. What matters is strong science grades, whichever route your child takes, followed by the right A-Levels. ## The national picture is changing

Fewer students have been taking triple science in recent years, while combined science entries have risen.

The government has also accepted a recommendation from the Curriculum and Assessment Review that every pupil should have the right to take triple science. Ministers plan to help schools build their triple science offer before making it a legal entitlement, so it won't apply to current Year 9 students. For now, whether triple is available still depends on your child's school.

## How to decide

These questions usually settle it:

- **Is your child doing well in science?** Students on track for a 7 or above by the end of Year 9 tend to cope well with triple.
- **Do they enjoy it?** Triple means more science lessons and more revision. A student who loves science gets more out of that time.
- **Are they thinking about science A-Levels?** Triple helps, but it isn't required.
- **What does triple cost at your school?** If it takes an option slot, think about the subject your child would give up.
- **How is the rest of their workload?** A student who struggles with the volume of revision may do better with strong combined grades than stretched triple grades.

If your child is borderline, combined science with strong grades is almost always a better outcome than triple science with weaker ones. For a tutor's view of the same choice, see [GCSE Science in our options guide](/gcse-options/science/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is triple science harder than combined science?

It's more work rather than a higher standard. Triple science covers more content in more depth, with longer exam papers: six papers of 1 hour 45 minutes with AQA, compared with six of 1 hour 15 minutes for combined.

### Is combined science worth two GCSEs?

Yes. Combined science gives two GCSE grades, shown together as a double grade such as 7-6, and sixth forms and employers count it as two GCSEs.

### Do you need triple science for A-Level biology, chemistry or physics?

Usually not. Most sixth forms accept combined science, often asking for grades like 6-6 or 7-7. Check the entry requirements of each sixth form, because they vary.

### Do you need triple science to study medicine?

No. Medical schools focus on A-Level subjects and grades, overall GCSE performance, the admissions test and the interview. Strong science grades matter more than which route your child took.

### Can my child switch from triple to combined science?

Often, yes, especially early in Year 10, because combined science covers the same core content. Speak to the school's head of science, who can explain the options and any timing limits.

### Will every school have to offer triple science?

The government has committed to giving all pupils an entitlement to triple science. It plans to help schools build their offer first, so no date has been set yet for when it becomes a requirement.
