If your child is in Year 11 and thinking about medicine, the A-Levels they choose this spring decide which medical schools they can apply to in two years' time. The good news is that the safest choice is simple. The detail matters at the edges, and that's where families get caught out.

This guide explains the subject rules, the grades medical schools ask for, and what else decides a place. If you'd like to talk through your child's plans, [book a free 30-minute call](/book-a-call/).

## The short answer: chemistry and biology

Taking chemistry and biology together meets the subject requirements at nearly all of the UK's standard medicine courses for school leavers. Add a third A-Level your child is likely to do well in, and almost every medical school is open to them.

## What medical schools actually require

Every UK medical school names at least one compulsory science, but the rules vary:

- **About half make chemistry compulsory.** Oxford, for example, requires chemistry plus at least one of biology, physics or maths.
- **About a third make biology compulsory.** Some accept either chemistry or biology, plus a second science.
- **About a quarter require both.** Imperial College London asks for chemistry and biology plus a third subject.
- **Some accept psychology as the second science.** Leicester, for example, asks for chemistry or biology plus one of biology, chemistry, physics, maths or psychology.

Some schools also exclude certain subjects from the three, such as General Studies or Critical Thinking. Always check the specific medical schools your child is interested in before they finalise their choices.

## What grades do you need?

The most common standard offer is AAA. The most selective schools usually ask for A*AA, and Cambridge asks for more.

Many medical schools also make contextual offers to eligible students, often one grade lower than the standard offer. These usually depend on factors such as the school your child attends or where they live.

## Choosing the third A-Level

The third subject matters less than many families think. Medical schools want to see that your child can handle a demanding course, not a particular combination. Good options include:

- **Maths or physics**, which suit students who enjoy problem-solving and keep doors open to other science degrees
- **Psychology**, which is popular and accepted as a second science at some schools
- **An essay subject**, such as English, history or a language, which shows range and builds writing skills that help with the personal statement

The best rule is to choose the subject your child is most likely to get an A in. A third subject with a B can rule out a medical school as surely as the wrong science.

## Do you need four A-Levels?

No. Medical schools make offers based on three A-Levels. Four weaker grades are worth less than three strong ones, and the extra subject takes time away from the UCAT, work experience and the personal statement.

## Grades are only the start

Medicine is one of the most competitive degrees in the UK. Each year around 25,000 people apply by the October deadline, while the number of funded medical school places in England has been held flat for several years.

That means strong A-Level predictions get your child through the door, but the decision usually comes down to:

- **The UCAT**, sat in the summer of Year 12 (see our [UCAT tutoring guide](/blog/ucat-tutoring-2027/))
- **The personal statement**, written in Year 12 and Year 13
- **Work experience or volunteering**, and what they learned from it
- **The interview**, often in multiple mini interview (MMI) format

Some medical schools also score GCSE results, so strong GCSEs still matter. Whether your child took [triple or combined science](/blog/triple-vs-combined-science-gcse/) matters far less than the grades.

## What to do in Year 11

1. Shortlist three or four medical schools and check their A-Level requirements on their own websites.
2. Choose chemistry and biology, unless there's a strong reason not to, and pick a third subject your child enjoys and is strong in.
3. Check the sixth form's entry requirements for each subject, especially chemistry, which often asks for a grade 7 in science and maths.
4. Focus on GCSEs now. They matter for sixth form entry and, at some medical schools, for the application itself.
5. Start thinking about volunteering. Regular, long-term volunteering in a caring role often says more than a short hospital placement.

## Frequently asked questions

### What A-Levels do you need for medicine?

Chemistry and biology, plus a third subject, keep almost every UK medical school open. Together they meet the subject rules at nearly all of the standard medicine courses for school leavers.

### Do you need chemistry for medicine?

At about half of UK medical schools, yes. Others accept biology with another science instead. Because chemistry is the subject asked for most often, leaving it out narrows your child's choices considerably.

### Can you do medicine without biology?

Yes, at some medical schools. Oxford, for example, requires chemistry plus at least one of biology, physics or maths. But taking biology keeps far more options open.

### What is the best third A-Level for medicine?

The one your child is most likely to get an A in. Maths, physics, psychology and essay subjects such as English or history are all common choices.

### What grades do you need for medicine?

AAA is the most common standard offer, and the most selective schools ask for A*AA or higher. Many schools make contextual offers, often one grade lower, to eligible students.

### Is psychology a good A-Level for medicine?

It can be. Some medical schools accept it as a second science alongside chemistry or biology, and others accept it as a third subject. Check the specific schools your child is considering.
