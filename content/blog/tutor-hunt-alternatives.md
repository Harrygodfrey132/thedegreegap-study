Tutor Hunt has been around for years, and plenty of families find good tutors there. But it isn't the only way to do it, and it doesn't suit everyone. Some parents don't have the evenings to read through dozens of profiles. Some want lessons that are recorded, or someone to talk to before they pick. And since First Tutors closed in 2026, more families are comparing their options than usual.

This guide explains how Tutor Hunt works, why families look for a **Tutor Hunt alternative**, and the main options in the UK. I co-founded one of them, so I've said where we fit and tried to be fair about the rest.

## How Tutor Hunt works

Tutor Hunt is a tutor marketplace. You search by postcode or for online tutors, read profiles and message as many tutors as you like, free. Tutors set their own rates, and the rate you see already includes Tutor Hunt's fees, with nothing added on top. You add your payment details before the first lesson, and you're charged 24 hours after each lesson has happened.

Tutor Hunt says every tutor must hold an enhanced DBS certificate, which its staff review, and tutors upload their ID and qualifications.

It's a solid option if you know what you're looking for and you're happy to do the choosing yourself.

## Why families look for something else

The parents we speak to usually mention one of these:

- **Too much choice.** A search for GCSE Maths can bring up hundreds of tutors, and it's hard to tell from a profile who'll actually click with your child.
- **Not knowing what to ask for.** "Help with Maths" covers a lot. The right tutor for a child stuck on a grade 4 is often different from the right one for a child chasing a 9.
- **Wanting someone to talk to.** Some families want advice first, from someone who'll tell them honestly whether tutoring is the answer at all.
- **Paying the tutor directly.** Some prefer arranging payment straight with the tutor.
- **Wanting more than lessons.** Group classes, revision courses or practice resources alongside the one-to-one sessions.

## The main Tutor Hunt alternatives

Details below come from each company's own website, checked in November 2026. Prices change, so check before you book.

### If you want to search yourself and pay the tutor directly: Superprof

Superprof works the most like Tutor Hunt, but with a different way of paying. You buy a Student Pass to contact tutors (Superprof's own guide lists it at £39 a month, and you can cancel any time), then pay each tutor directly at the rate they set. Lessons can be in person or online. Superprof covers far more than school subjects, which is handy if you're also looking for music or language lessons.

First Tutors used to be the other big name here, but it closed in spring 2026. We've written about [what happened to First Tutors and the alternatives](/blog/first-tutors-alternatives/).

### If you want vetted tutors and online lessons: Tutorful

Tutorful is an online platform with lessons from £20 an hour. It says it accepts about 1 in 8 tutors who apply, and that its tutors have an enhanced DBS check and at least two years' experience. Booking and payment happen through the platform, lessons are recorded, and if a tutor isn't the right fit, Tutorful says it will cover your next lesson with someone new.

### If your child would rather learn from a recent student: MyTutor

MyTutor is online, for Key Stage 2 up to GCSE and A-Level. Most of its tutors are university students or recent graduates who sat the same exams not long ago, which some teenagers find easier to open up to. MyTutor says it accepts about 1 in 8 applicants, interviews them and requires an enhanced DBS check. Tutors start from £26 an hour.

### If you need a specialist or university-level tutor: Spires

On Spires, you post what you need and tutors respond with their rate. It covers school subjects but also goes well beyond them, into university and postgraduate tutoring. Spires says it accepts about 7% of the tutors who apply, after an interview, a demonstration lesson and an enhanced DBS check.

### If you want a smaller company with classes and resources: The Education Hotel

[The Education Hotel](https://educationhotel.co.uk/) is run by its director, Jemma Smith, an Oxford graduate with a teaching qualification, who interviews the tutors herself. As well as one-to-one tutoring online and in person, it runs termly group classes and revision courses. It covers the 11+, GCSE and iGCSE, A-Level and the IB, and supports children with special educational needs. Families also get access to a members' club with practice papers and learning resources.

### If you'd rather be matched than search: The Degree Gap

[The Degree Gap](/book-a-call/) is the agency Joe Clark and I run, for GCSE, A-Level, the IB and the 11+. Instead of a search page, every family starts with a free call, usually about 30 minutes, with one of us. We ask about your child, their subjects and their exam boards. Within 24 hours you get two or three tutor profiles, and your child meets the one you like on a free 15-minute video call before you book anything.

We interview every tutor ourselves, and about 3% of applicants make it onto the platform. Lessons are online and recorded, from £37 an hour, with no contract. If the match isn't right, we find another tutor.

## Which one is right for your child?

If you know exactly what your child needs and you enjoy choosing, a marketplace like Tutor Hunt or Superprof gives you the most options for the money.

If you'd like checks done for you but still want to browse, Tutorful and MyTutor are the obvious next step.

And if you're not sure what your child needs, or you just don't have the time to sift through profiles before mocks, it's worth talking to someone first. That's the gap an agency fills. Our guide to [GCSE tutoring costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-gcse-tutor-cost-per-hour-uk/) explains what you'd typically pay with each option.

Whatever you choose, check that anyone tutoring your child has a current enhanced DBS certificate, and ask what happens if the first tutor isn't right.

## Common questions

### Is Tutor Hunt safe to use?

Tutor Hunt says every tutor must hold an enhanced DBS certificate, which its staff review, and that tutors upload their ID and qualifications. Messages go through the site and you pay after each lesson. As with any service, check the tutor's DBS yourself and keep the first lessons somewhere you can see or hear them.

### Does Tutor Hunt charge parents a fee?

There's no sign-up fee, and messaging tutors is free. The rate on a tutor's profile already includes Tutor Hunt's fees, and you pay through the site 24 hours after each lesson.

### What's the difference between a tutoring marketplace and an agency?

On a marketplace you search, message and choose a tutor yourself. An agency talks to you first, suggests tutors who fit your child, and replaces the tutor if it isn't working. Agencies usually cost a little more per hour, in exchange for the matching and the support.

### What's the best Tutor Hunt alternative for GCSE?

It depends on how you like to choose. Tutorful and MyTutor let you browse vetted online tutors, and MyTutor's tutors are mostly recent students who sat the same exams. If you'd rather talk it through and be matched to a tutor who knows your child's exam board, an agency such as The Degree Gap starts with a free call.

### Should I choose a local tutor or an online one?

A local tutor makes sense for younger children or anyone who finds screens hard to focus on. For most GCSE and A-Level students, online works well and widens the choice to tutors across the country, which matters for specific exam boards and less common subjects.
