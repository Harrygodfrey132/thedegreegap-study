# Answers from Shihab's email of 12 October 2026, lightly tidied.
faqs:
  - q: "Why would you pick Business for GCSE?"
    a: |
      As someone who enjoyed Maths and the creative side of English Literature, I like that Business Studies uses both. Students rely on logic and reasoning, and they also have to think outside the box.

      It often puts the student in the shoes of an owner, coming up with ideas for how a business can thrive or recover from decline.
  - q: "Who should think twice before picking it?"
    a: |
      Business Studies involves memorising a lot of content and terminology, and writing short essays that build an argument. If that doesn't appeal, it may not be the subject for them.

      But for students who feel encouraged to take it on, it's a great subject that blends arguing a case, creativity and numeracy.
  - q: "What's the biggest misconception about GCSE Business?"
    a: |
      That it's only for people who want to run their own business. While I was doing my Business Studies degree, friends would often ask me, "So are you going to own a business?" I was very unsure. What I was sure about were the skills I'd develop: numeracy for finance, and the theory of leadership and management, which I could use in all sorts of jobs.

      When I became a full-time tutor, I used them all. I learned how to market myself to families when I had no experience in 2016, how to create value for my students so I stood out from other tutors, and how to lead and motivate students to be the best they can be.

      So it can certainly help if your child wants to own a business one day. But it's the skills they learn along the way that last.
  - q: "What catches students out?"
    a: |
      How much it takes to get the top grades. Students sometimes underestimate the application needed to score high marks, especially on the case studies. That's the main reason families get in touch with me.

      The numeracy in the finance topics can also be a problem, for example break-even analysis and profit ratios.
  - q: "How would you suggest students revise for Business?"
    a: |
      Three ways work best.

      1. **Exam practice.** Like many subjects, the more papers students do, the more they train the mind to come up with ideas and build a chain of reasoning. Even after one paper, they often notice how similar their answers are to a previous one, and can pull ideas from it. It brings a kind of flow when they're writing: like cruising along in second gear while someone else is straining in fourth.
      2. **Flashcards**, to stay on top of the terminology.
      3. **Ten or fifteen minutes on a big case study**, just brainstorming ideas. It makes students recall content and practise finding points they can use. Even if they don't write the full answer, it's a great way to revise.
  - q: "What can parents do at home to help?"
    a: |
      Testing with flashcards helps in any subject, but parents can also take an active part in their child's learning. Ask questions like:

      - Why is the minimum wage going up? That starts a conversation about inflation.
      - Why do people prefer iPhones to Samsung phones? That's brand loyalty and product design.
      - Why do people choose Aldi over Sainsbury's? That's market positioning and value.

      Conversations like these put your child in the teacher's seat. If they can explain these ideas well, they know their stuff.
