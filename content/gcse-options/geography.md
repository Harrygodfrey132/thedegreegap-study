# Answers from Jon's email of 22 October 2026, lightly tidied.
faqs:
  - q: "Why would you pick Geography for GCSE?"
    a: |
      I see Geography as an incredibly important subject in these modern times. Themes such as climate change, hazardous weather, development and globalisation, among many others, deepen our understanding and help us make informed decisions and form well-rounded, objective opinions.

      There is a lot of misinformation and media bias in the world, and being able to see through it is a really valuable skill.
  - q: "Why might you not want to pick Geography?"
    a: |
      Some people feel that career opportunities in Geography are limited. What we find, though, is that Geography develops a wide range of transferable skills that matter in many career paths, such as:

      - analysing information and data
      - forming an argument
      - writing, reading and research
      - map reading
      - presenting results
  - q: "What's the biggest misconception about GCSE Geography?"
    a: |
      Some people think it's just about knowing where things are and reading maps. In fact it covers a wide range of incredibly interesting topics, including resource security and energy management, ecology, tectonic hazards, hurricanes, urbanisation and sustainability.

      What Geography really does is open up a way of thinking about the world in terms of processes, connections and relationships, which can be an incredibly valuable way to see and understand things.
  - q: "What do students find hardest?"
    a: |
      Often it's forming an argument, then getting it across within the confines of an exam question, using case study examples to illustrate the point. But all of this can be taught and practised, and it's something I work on extensively with my students.
  - q: "How would you suggest students revise for Geography?"
    a: |
      Knowing the key terms and key processes is critical. Geography has its own kind of language, with key terms to describe different processes, and marks are awarded for using them. It's also important to know the case studies, and the key landforms and processes.

      Mind maps help students recall and describe the key terms and their definitions, as well as the key factors in each case study. Working through past papers and mark schemes is also a very effective way to get familiar with the types of questions asked, and what examiners expect from an answer.
  - q: "What can parents do at home to help?"
    a: |
      Getting the textbooks and revision workbooks for your child's course is very helpful, as they're excellent study aids. I don't think many schools let students take their books home, so they're good to have.

      Keeping an eye on topic progress, tracker tests and grades also helps, as does testing your child on the key elements of each topic.
