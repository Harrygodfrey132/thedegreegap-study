faq_heading: "Common questions about GCSE options"
# The questions people type into Google about options, answered plainly. Also
# sent to Google as FAQ structured data (partials/seo.html).
faqs:
  - q: "When do you choose GCSE options?"
    a: "Most schools in England ask students to choose in Year 9, usually in the spring term, after an options evening. A few schools start GCSE courses a year early, so their students choose in Year 8. Your school's options booklet will give the exact deadline."
  - q: "How many GCSE options do you pick?"
    a: "Usually three or four, on top of English, Maths and Science. Most students finish with nine or ten GCSEs in total, depending on the school and whether they take Triple Science."
  - q: "Which GCSEs are compulsory?"
    a: "English Language, Maths and Science, and in almost every school English Literature too. Some schools also enter every student for Religious Studies. PE is compulsory, but usually not as a GCSE."
  - q: "Does it matter which GCSE options you choose?"
    a: "Less than most families fear. Sixth forms and universities look closely at English and Maths, and at the grade in any subject your child wants to continue. Options matter most for A-Levels that build on the GCSE: most sixth forms expect a language, or a creative subject such as Art, to have been taken at GCSE first."
  - q: "Is Triple Science better than Combined Science?"
    a: "Not automatically. Both can lead to A-Level sciences. Triple Science covers more content and counts as three GCSEs, while Combined Science counts as two. Some sixth forms prefer Triple for students planning two or more sciences at A-Level, so it is worth checking theirs."
  - q: "History or Geography: which should my child take?"
    a: "Both count towards the EBacc and both are well respected, so choose on interest and strengths. History is mostly extended writing, with source analysis. Geography mixes writing with maps, data and fieldwork."
  - q: "Which GCSEs are needed for medicine or law?"
    a: "For medicine, strong grades in English, Maths and the sciences matter most, and Triple Science is a sensible choice where it is offered, because Chemistry and usually Biology follow at A-Level. Law has no required GCSEs. Essay subjects such as History and English Literature build the right skills, and a strong English grade helps."
  - q: "Can you change your GCSE options?"
    a: "Often, in the first few weeks of Year 10, if there is space in the class and the timetable allows. After that it gets harder, because the course will have moved on. If your child is having second thoughts, raise it with the school early."
