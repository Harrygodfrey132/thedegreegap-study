faqs:
  - q: "Why is Science so important?"
    a: |
      Science is one of the most fundamental subjects any student can learn. It lays down the basic principles of scientific experimentation, and builds a greater understanding of some of life's most important concepts. It also develops critical thinking, shaping students into thinkers who can question the world around them using evidence.
  - q: "What's the difference between Combined and Triple Science?"
    a: |
      Combined Science covers the core concepts in Biology, Chemistry and Physics, but in less depth than Triple Science. Triple Science includes all the Combined Science material, then stretches students further with more complex topics, and treats each science as a subject in its own right.

      The exams differ too. With most exam boards, both routes have two papers in each science, six in all. In Combined Science, the six papers together give a double award: two GCSE grades, such as 7-7 or 7-6. In Triple Science the papers are longer, and Biology, Chemistry and Physics are graded separately, giving three GCSEs.
  - q: "How should a family decide which one to take?"
    a: |
      Combined or Triple Science is a decision many parents are unsure about. A good starting point is whether your child wants to carry on with science after GCSEs. If they're considering A-Level sciences, Triple Science may help, as some of the extra content is built on at A-Level.

      That said, Combined Science can also give an excellent foundation for further study, and top grades may be more manageable for some students. The most important thing is which route will best support your child in reaching their goals.
  - q: "Where do students lose the most marks?"
    a: |
      Students often lose marks when a question is set in an unfamiliar context. The underlying concept may be exactly the same, but they struggle to recognise what the question is asking. That usually means their understanding of the topic isn't secure enough yet to apply it to a new scenario.

      This is why practising exam questions is so important. It teaches students to apply their knowledge, rather than just memorise facts.
  - q: "How should students revise for three sciences at once?"
    a: |
      The key is balance. Focus on weaker topics while keeping the stronger ones topped up, and stay on top of everything by using the specification as a checklist.

      My biggest tip is to practise past papers and exam-style questions. I always tell my students that nothing comes as close to the real exam as a past paper. Try to cover at least the last three years of past papers, as this helps you get familiar with the question styles and build good exam technique. It helps even more when you have a tutor to walk you through the difficult questions.
  - q: "What can parents do at home to help?"
    a: |
      Encourage your child to use active recall, such as flashcards or quizzes, rather than simply rereading their notes. You could also set up small hands-on tasks to reinforce practical techniques or visual concepts. The more engaging and memorable the activity, the better it helps the learning stick.

      It also helps to create a space to revise that's free from distractions, so your child gets used to focusing for longer stretches.
