faqs:
  - q: "Why is Maths so important?"
    a: |
      With all the technology we have now, Maths might not seem as important as it used to. But it teaches something beyond numbers: logic and problem-solving. Being able to work through a problem and organise it into steps is a skill your child will need in all sorts of parts of adult life, and it isn't taught in quite the same way by any other subject.
  - q: "What should parents know about Foundation and Higher tier?"
    a: |
      GCSE Maths is graded from 9 down to 1. A 4 is a standard pass and a 5 is a strong pass. Foundation tier covers grades 1 to 5. Higher tier covers the harder topics and grades 4 to 9, and a student who only just misses a 4 on Higher can be given a 3.

      It helps to know which tier your child is likely to sit by Year 10. There's plenty of overlap between the two, but students heading for Higher need to start the harder topics by the end of Year 10 to get through everything in time.

      The marks needed for a grade 4 are very different on each tier, and they change every year and between exam boards. As a rough guide, a 4 can need around 60% on Foundation and only around 20% on Higher, because the Higher paper is so much harder.
  - q: "What's the biggest misconception about GCSE Maths?"
    a: |
      That Higher is the easier way to get a pass, because the marks needed for a 4 are lower. They're lower because the questions are harder. Higher papers have more questions worth several marks, with multi-step problems that mix more than one topic.
  - q: "Where do students lose the most marks?"
    a: |
      Students get discouraged by questions they don't know how to finish, especially ones worth several marks, and leave them completely blank. But there are method marks on every paper. Knowing even some of the steps can pick up marks, even if the final answer isn't there.

      Showing working where it's needed, and setting it out clearly on the page, adds marks too.
  - q: "How would you suggest students revise for Maths?"
    a: |
      Start by going through all the content. GCSE Maths covers six main areas: number; algebra; ratio, proportion and rates of change; geometry and measures; probability; and statistics.

      After each topic, go back over the main formulas and methods. It's easy to forget them after even a week without a proper review, and just two minutes a day on a new formula makes a big difference.

      Then work through past papers, to get used to how the questions are worded and how the mark schemes work. The last step is sitting full papers under timed conditions, so your child gets used to the pressure and can cope with it on the day.
  - q: "What can parents do at home to help?"
    a: |
      Encourage your child to look over their notes and formulas as often as possible. You can test them with flashcards of the formulas, or suggest a few past-paper questions as extra practice. When it gets closer to the exams, you can help with timed papers by setting the timer for them.

      Make sure they have the right kit, too: a suitable scientific calculator, a protractor and a pair of compasses.
