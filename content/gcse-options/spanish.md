faqs:
  - q: "Why would you pick Spanish for GCSE?"
    a: |
      Spanish is an incredible passport to the world. It's spoken by hundreds of millions of people, which opens up travel and career paths. It's also wonderfully phonetic: words are said the way they're spelled. So once students learn the core building blocks, it clicks into place and it's so rewarding to speak.
  - q: "What does Spanish give students beyond the exam?"
    a: |
      It's a fantastic subject for building real-world problem-solving and thinking skills. And because it works best with regular, bite-sized practice, it helps students build independent study habits and resilience that benefit all their other subjects too.
  - q: "What's the biggest misconception about GCSE Spanish?"
    a: |
      That it's just about memorising endless word lists for an exam. In reality, it's about getting to know a living culture, connecting with different people and finding a whole new way of expressing yourself.
  - q: "What do students find hardest?"
    a: |
      Making the leap from listing facts to juggling different verb tenses. Stepping out of the comfort zone of the present tense and mixing in the past and the future is usually the trickiest hurdle. But once students cross it, their fluency really takes off.
  - q: "How would you suggest students revise for Spanish?"
    a: |
      Keep it active. Flashcards are brilliant for vocabulary, but the secret weapon is speaking out loud, even if it's just narrating what you're doing around the house in Spanish. Pair that with short bursts of creative writing and the grammar starts to stick naturally.
  - q: "What can parents do at home to help?"
    a: |
      Be curious. You don't need to speak a word of Spanish to help. Quizzing them with flashcards, asking them to teach *you* a new rule they've learned, or celebrating their small daily wins makes a big difference to their confidence.
