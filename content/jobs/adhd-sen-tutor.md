## The role

We are taking on ADHD & SEN tutors, and we are fussy about who we say yes to.

Students with ADHD, dyslexia or other additional needs usually do not need easier work. They need the same work taught in a way that fits how they process it.

Joe speaks to the family before you ever hear a name, so you start knowing the predicted grade, the mock that came back lower than everyone hoped, and what the school has already tried. You are not opening an email from a stranger and guessing.

## What the teaching looks like

- Adapting explanation and pacing to how the student actually learns
- Teaching exam subjects to full standard rather than reducing expectations
- Working with access arrangements where they are in place
- Building routines and strategies that hold up outside sessions

## Who we take on

You have real experience with additional needs, not only patience. We ask about specific approaches at interview.

Beyond that, two years of teaching or tutoring behind you, real experience with ADHD & SEN, and an enhanced DBS. We check the DBS before you meet a student, so it is worth having it to hand. You will also need the right to work in the UK, somewhere quiet to teach from and a connection that does not drop halfway through a session.

## How it works week to week

You tell us when you are free and how many students you want. Nothing lands in your diary that you have not agreed to take, you are not advertising yourself anywhere, and you are not chasing families for money. If a student goes quiet or something awkward comes up, you ring us and we deal with it.

## Talk to us

Harry or Joe will interview you before anything else happens. We turn down most people who apply, so expect a real conversation: what you know, how you teach ADHD & SEN, and what you do with a student who has decided they are no good at it.

Message us on WhatsApp or send an email telling us what you have taught. That is the whole application.
