I'm Harry, and I run The Degree Gap with Joe Clark. We've known each other since we were kids, and we were both tutored at school.

I had two tutors growing up. One was excellent and one was only okay. Same student, very different results, and that contrast is behind most of how we run the company now. My maths tutor taught me strategies, and far more of the curriculum than I was meant to know at that age. Somewhere along the way I fell in love with learning, and the grades followed. My school named me as one of the students who made the greatest progress, and I finished 18 grades above my targets.

At A-Level I was predicted lower than the A\*AA I came out with, and I give a lot of the credit for that to my Economics tutor.

I went on to study Economics at the LSE, and it was there I got back into tutoring, this time as the tutor. Before The Degree Gap I ran an undergraduate tutoring agency, helping university economics students fill the gaps their lectures left. After a spell in finance and recruitment, I came back to education full time.

## What I do now

A lot of my week is spent on consultation calls with parents. Every family speaks to Joe or me before we suggest a tutor, because the match is the whole thing. A good tutor for one child can be the wrong tutor for another.

Joe and I interview every tutor before they join the platform. Subject knowledge is the easy part to check. What we're really listening for is how they explain things, and how they'd get on with a teenager who isn't sure they want a tutor at all.

During the day I'm often in schools running revision workshops, and four times a year we run free webinars for parents. I also write most of the articles on this site. They come out of the questions parents ask us on calls, and I try to answer them the way I would if you rang.

Forbes and The Independent have both featured me. Nice, but it's not why we do it. We do it because the right tutor changed how Joe and I felt about learning, and we'd like more children to have that.
