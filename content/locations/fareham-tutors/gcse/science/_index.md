faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Science for Fareham students?"
    a: "The six-mark extended response questions, the required practicals, and the calculation work in Chemistry and Physics. The long answers are hardest because they are marked in levels rather than points, so a student who writes down everything they know can still land in the bottom band. The practicals are examined as recall of variables, apparatus and sources of error rather than as an activity done a year ago. And moles, concentration and rearranging equations lose marks from students who understand the science perfectly well. All three are narrow enough to fix in a term."
  - q: "Does the exam board make a difference in GCSE Science?"
    a: "Yes, more than in most subjects. AQA, Edexcel and OCR cover overlapping content but differ in how the required practicals are examined, how the six-mark questions are marked and which formulae are given rather than learned. The practical questions in particular are board-specific in shape and worth preparing for as their own thing. We confirm the board before suggesting a tutor, and where a student has moved school mid-course we look for someone who has taught the subject on both boards involved."
  - q: "What actually happens in a GCSE Science tutoring session?"
    a: "The first session is a diagnostic that splits the three sciences apart, because a single Science grade on a report hides which one is dragging. After that a typical hour is real exam questions worked on the shared whiteboard with the method written out rather than described, since in Science the marks are in the working. Long answers get planned before they are written, which is the habit most students are missing. The tutor sets a small amount of specific practice at the end so it lines up with what was just taught."

We tell you which of these a tutor is before you meet them."
