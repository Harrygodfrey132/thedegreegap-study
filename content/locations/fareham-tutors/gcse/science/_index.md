angle_body_1: "Bright children lose marks in Science in one place more than any other: the long answers. They're marked differently from the rest of the paper. Short questions are marked in points: say the right thing, get the mark. Six-mark questions are marked in levels. The examiner reads the whole answer and puts it in a level, depending on how well the ideas link up and whether it really answers the question. So a child who thinks Science is about remembering facts writes down every fact they know, in no particular order. And lands in the bottom level, having written more than anyone else in the room."
angle_body_2: "The good news is that it's a technique problem, and it usually improves quickly because the knowledge is already there. What changes is the shape. Your child learns to read what the question's really asking and to plan three or four linked points before writing. Then to put them in an order that builds, using the scientific words the mark scheme looks for instead of everyday ones. Half an hour of that, with a marked example on screen, usually shows a difference. The other two common leaks are Chemistry calculations and rearranging equations in Physics. And the Science grades count after Year 11 too. Fareham College and the colleges towards Portsmouth and Southampton each publish their own Science requirements."

faqs:
  - q: "Where do children in Fareham drop the most marks in GCSE Science?"
    a: "Mostly in three places: the six-mark long answers, the required practicals, and the calculations in Chemistry and Physics. The long answers are hardest, because they're marked in levels, not points. Your child can write everything they know and still land in the bottom level. The practical questions ask your child to remember what was changed and measured, the equipment and what might go wrong. That's hard if the experiment was a year ago. And moles, concentration and rearranging equations cost marks even when the science is well understood. All three are narrow enough to fix in a term."
  - q: "Does it matter which exam board my child's school uses for Science?"
    a: "Yes, it can. AQA, Edexcel and OCR cover a lot of the same science, but they differ in how practical work is examined and in the style of the questions. The practical questions especially come in a different shape on each board, so they're worth preparing for on their own. We'll check the board before suggesting anyone. If your child has moved school partway through the course, we'll look for a tutor who's taught both boards."
