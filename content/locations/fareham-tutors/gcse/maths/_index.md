  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Higher tier caps at grade 9 and floors at a 4, sometimes a 3. Foundation caps at 5 and floors at 1. A student sitting around a 4 or 5 in Year 10 mocks is usually better served by higher tier with targeted work on the early and middle questions, because that is where grades 4 to 6 live and the hardest questions can be left alone entirely. Where a student is well below that, foundation with the aim of securing a strong 5 is often the better plan. Either way the decision should follow a diagnostic rather than a single mock."
  - q: "How do you work out which Maths topics my child is actually missing?"
    a: "By reading the working rather than the score. The first session sorts recent questions into two groups on the shared whiteboard, the ones your child could not start and the ones they started and stalled on. Those mean opposite things. A blank is almost always a topic never properly taught or missed, and it rebuilds quickly once named because nothing has to be unlearned. A stall is method confidence, which needs practice rather than teaching. Most students have some of both, and the balance decides how the term is spent."
