faqs:
  - q: "Which GCSE English grade do Cheltenham sixth forms look for?"
    a: "There's no single number. Pate's, Balcarras and Bournside each set their own, and it also depends which A-Levels your child is after, so check the published requirements, not a rule of thumb. For an essay-based A-Level such as History, Politics or English Literature, expect a higher English grade to be asked for than on a science course. And if your child's in Year 10, it's worth knowing that getting from a 5 to a 7 in English often takes a term or two of structured work, not a whole year. We can't promise it, though."
  - q: "Why is English going badly when my child's such a keen reader?"
    a: "Mostly because planning an essay is its own skill, and reading at home doesn't teach it. It usually started before Year 11. Around Year 8 or 9, your child learnt to start writing and hope the argument would turn up. Often it does, in paragraph four, after three paragraphs of description that scored very little. Spotting that pattern is the tutor's first job."
  - q: "What actually happens in an hour of GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "It's sixty minutes, usually in three parts. The first fifteen go on last week's writing, marked and handed back so your child can see where marks were given and where they weren't. The middle thirty work on one technique, such as choosing evidence, structuring a comparison or tackling an unseen poem. The last fifteen are written to time. English marks are won under time pressure, and practising without a clock trains the wrong habit."
  - q: "Are the English tutors trained teachers or recent graduates?"
    a: "Both are on our platform. Some are qualified secondary English teachers, and others are Russell Group graduates who took these same exams fairly recently. Which suits your child depends on what's missing. A child who needs structure and confidence built from scratch often does best with an experienced teacher. One who's near the top of the band and needs the fine print of the current mark scheme often gets more from a recent high-flying graduate."
  - q: "How do we know if English tutoring is worth it for our child?"
    a: "It depends on the gap, and we'd rather tell you on the call than after you've paid for a term. English often responds well to tutoring, because so much of what separates the grades is technique that can be taught directly, not content that takes ages to learn. But if your child's already writing securely at their target grade, we'll often suggest some focused practice before the exam instead of weekly lessons. The free call is there to give you that answer."
