faqs:
  - q: "What grade do Cheltenham sixth forms ask for in GCSE English?"
    a: "It varies by sixth form and by the A-Levels being applied for, so the honest answer is to read the published requirements rather than trust a rule of thumb. Pate's, Balcarras and Bournside each set their own, and essay-based A-Levels such as English Literature, History and Politics usually ask for a stronger English grade than science routes do. What we would say is that the gap between a 5 and a 7 in English is a term of structured work rather than a year, which is worth knowing in Year 10."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read a lot?"
    a: "Because reading and writing to a mark scheme are separate skills, and only one of them gets practised at home. The usual moment it went wrong is earlier than Year 11: somewhere around Year 8 or 9 the student was never explicitly taught to plan an essay, so they learned to start writing and hope the argument arrives. It often does arrive, in paragraph four, after three paragraphs of description that scored very little. Finding that pattern is the tutor's first job."
  - q: "What does an hour of GCSE English tutoring actually look like?"
    a: "Sixty minutes, usually in three parts. The first fifteen go on last week's writing, marked and handed back with the assessment objectives visible so the student can see where marks were and were not awarded. The middle thirty work one technique, which might be evidence selection, comparative structure or an unseen poetry approach. The last fifteen are written to time, because English marks are decided under time pressure and practising without a clock trains the wrong thing."
  - q: "Are your GCSE English tutors qualified teachers or university students?"
    a: "Both, and which one suits your child depends on what is missing. The network includes qualified secondary English teachers, Russell Group graduates and current undergraduates who sat these exact papers recently. For a student who needs confidence and structure rebuilt from the ground up, an experienced teacher is often the better fit. For a student who is close to the top of the band and needs current mark scheme detail, a recent high-achieving graduate frequently has the edge."
  - q: "Will English tutoring pay for itself in this case?"
    a: "It depends on the gap, and we would rather say so on the call than after you have paid for a term. English tends to respond well to tutoring because so much of what separates grade bands is technique that can be taught directly, rather than content that has to be learned slowly. Where a student is already writing securely at their target band, the honest recommendation is often targeted practice before the exam rather than a full weekly programme. The consultation call is built to give that answer."
