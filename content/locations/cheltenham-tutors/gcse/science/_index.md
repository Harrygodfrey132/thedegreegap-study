  - q: "Should my child take combined science or triple science?"
    a: "Combined science awards two GCSEs from three papers of content; triple awards three from more. Triple is not automatically the better choice. It suits students who are secure across all three sciences and are heading towards A-Level Sciences, and it can stretch a student who is shaky in one of the three very thin. If your child is borderline, the useful move is a Year 10 diagnostic to see whether the weak science is a technique gap or a content gap before the entry is fixed."
  - q: "How do we work out where the Science marks are going?"
    a: "Look at a recent mock and read it by question type rather than by topic. If marks are missing on the six-mark answers while the short recall questions are clean, that is structure, not knowledge. If the practical questions are the weak spot, that is method. If the numbers questions are wrong but the working is started sensibly, that is usually unit conversion or rearranging. Three different diagnoses, three different plans, and you can often see which it is in twenty minutes."
  - q: "What grade improvements have your GCSE Science tutors actually delivered?"
    a: "The honest framing is that we describe what is typical rather than promise outcomes. Where the gap is exam technique, which in Science means six-mark structure, practical method and calculation accuracy, a grade band across a term of weekly sessions is a realistic target and the most common one we work towards. Where the gap is genuine missing content from Year 9 or 10, it is slower and usually spans two terms. The first session is designed to tell you which one you are dealing with."
  - q: "Can my child have GCSE Science tutoring during the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and Science is one of the subjects where holiday blocks earn their keep. Half-term and Easter weeks give enough uninterrupted time to work through a whole topic block properly, which is hard to do in a single weekly hour during term. A lot of Year 11 families run one weekly session through the year and add a short intensive block at Easter aimed at whichever paper is weakest. Sessions run online, so scheduling stays flexible."
