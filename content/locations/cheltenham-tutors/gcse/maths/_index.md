faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Maths for Cheltenham students?"
    a: "Two topic blocks account for most of the lost marks we see. The first is algebra used as a tool rather than a topic, where a question is really about ratio or geometry but cannot be finished without confident rearranging. The second is the wordy problem-solving questions at the back of the calculator paper, which ask the student to choose a method rather than apply an obvious one. Neither is a knowledge gap. Both respond quickly to being taught as question types."
  - q: "Which GCSE Maths paper should my child put the revision hours into?"
    a: "Both, because they are weighted equally and the grade comes from the total. That said, they fail differently. The non-calculator paper punishes shaky arithmetic and slow algebraic manipulation. The calculator papers punish method choice and accuracy across multi-step questions. Most students lose more on one than the other, and a first-session diagnostic on a recent paper shows which within about twenty minutes. The plan then starts wherever the bleeding is worst."
  - q: "Does it matter whether my child sits AQA, Edexcel or OCR GCSE Maths?"
    a: "It matters at the top of the paper. The core content is close to identical across all three, so a tutor who knows one can teach the maths of any of them. The difference is question style and how the marks are laid out. OCR tends to ask longer multi-part problems, Edexcel leans on structured scaffolding, and AQA phrases problem-solving questions in a way students need practice reading. We confirm the board on the consultation call and match on it."
