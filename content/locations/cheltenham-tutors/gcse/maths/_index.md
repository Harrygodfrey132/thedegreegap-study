faqs:
  - q: "What do Cheltenham children find hardest in GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Two kinds of question cost the most marks we see. The first uses algebra as a tool: the question is really about ratio or shapes, but your child can't finish it without confident rearranging. The second is the wordy problem-solving at the back of the calculator paper, where they've got to choose a method rather than follow an obvious one. Neither is a knowledge gap. Both often improve quickly once they're taught as types of question, with plenty of practice on each."
  - q: "Which GCSE Maths paper should my child put the revision hours into?"
    a: "All three, because each one counts the same and the grade comes from the total. But they go wrong in different ways. The non-calculator paper catches out shaky arithmetic and slow algebra. The calculator papers catch out method choice and accuracy over several steps. Most children lose more on one kind than the other, and a tutor can usually see which within about twenty minutes of looking at a recent paper. The plan starts wherever the damage is worst."

What's different is how the questions are worded and how the marks are laid out. That's where practice on the right board's past papers pays off. Not sure which board your child is on? Look at the front cover of a recent mock. We'll check it on the call too, and pick tutors who know it."
