faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE Science do Berkhamsted children find hardest?"
    a: "Usually the Chemistry calculations, then the six-mark answers. Moles, concentrations and yield questions take several steps, and a slip early on can cost the marks that follow. The long answers catch out children who know the content but write it as a list, not a linked explanation. Physics equations and unit conversions come a close third. It's different for every child, though. So the first lesson goes through a recent paper to see where your child's marks are really going."
  - q: "Higher or Foundation Science: how do we decide?"
    a: "Look at where your child is working now and where they need to get to. Higher, the harder set of papers, is aimed at grades 4 to 9, while Foundation stops at a 5. If your child's around a 5 in Year 10, Higher is usually the right call, with focused work on the tougher questions. At a 3 or 4, Foundation can be safer. The school makes the final decision, and that's how it should be. A paper the tutor's marked in Year 10 just gives you solid evidence to take into that conversation."
  - q: "Does my child need a tutor, or would more revision do?"
    a: "The working on a recent paper usually tells you. If the method's clean and the marks vanish on the last few questions, it's exam technique, and a tutor is often the quickest way to fix it. If the long questions are blank or crossed out, the method itself isn't secure yet, and revising from notes rarely sorts that on its own. And if your child's already scoring well in all three sciences, some targeted practice may be all they need. We'll say so if it is."
