faq_1_q: "Year 10 or Year 11, when should a Berkhamsted family start GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10, if there is a choice. The content steps up that year, and a gap closed early stops costing marks for the rest of the course. There is a timing point here as well: sixth form applications, including for students staying on at their own school, go in around December of Year 11, and they are read against predicted grades. Starting in Year 11 still works for plenty of families. It just means more papers and technique, and less time to teach anything from scratch."
faq_2_q: "Do your tutors know the boards and iGCSEs local schools use?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and the board is settled on the first call, not left for the tutor to discover. AQA, Edexcel and OCR phrase questions differently and weight topics differently, and iGCSE papers at independent schools change the mix again, with their own paper structure in several subjects. A tutor who already knows your child's specification is useful from the first ten minutes. Tell us the school and, if you know it, the board for each subject."
faq_3_q: "She understands it in class but the mock grade is lower. Why?"
faq_3_a: "Because a mock tests something a lesson does not. In class the topic is named at the top of the page and help is close by. In a hall there is a clock, a mix of topics and nobody to ask. A tutor reads the marked script and looks at where the marks disappear, which usually points at timing, method choice or how answers are set out. Each of those can be practised directly, and they tend to move within a term."
