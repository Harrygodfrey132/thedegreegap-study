faq_1_q: "Year 10 or Year 11: when should we start GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10, if you've got the choice. The content steps up that year, and a gap closed early stops costing marks for the rest of the course. Timing matters here too. Sixth form applications go in around December of Year 11, even if your child's staying at the same school, and they're read against predicted grades. Starting in Year 11 still works for plenty of families. It just means more past papers and exam technique, and less time to teach anything from scratch."
faq_2_q: "Do your tutors know the exam boards and iGCSEs the schools here use?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and we settle the board on the first call rather than leaving the tutor to find out. AQA, Edexcel and OCR each have their own question style and wording. The iGCSE papers at independent schools change things again, with their own structure in several subjects. A tutor who already knows your child's course is useful from the first ten minutes. Just tell us the school and, if you know it, the board for each subject."
faq_3_q: "She understands it in class, but her mock grade is lower. Why?"
faq_3_a: "Because a mock tests something a lesson doesn't. In class, the topic's written at the top of the page and help is close by. In the exam hall there's a clock, a mix of topics and nobody to ask. The tutor reads her marked paper and looks at where the marks disappear. Usually it's timing, choosing the method, or how answers are set out. She can practise each of those directly, and they tend to improve within a term."
