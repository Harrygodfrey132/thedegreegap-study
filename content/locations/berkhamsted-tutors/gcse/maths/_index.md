faqs:
  - q: "How high does my child's Maths grade need to be for sixth form in Berkhamsted?"
    a: "Each A-Level has its own figure, on top of a general bar. At Ashlyns that bar is a grade 4 in English and Maths, and each subject names its own minimum in the sixth form pack. A-Level Maths usually wants more than a 4. Berkhamsted School, Tring School and Kings Langley School set their own figures the same way. The numbers get revised, so look at this year's pack for each course your child's considering. It's the subject figure that counts."
  - q: "Which Maths paper should my child concentrate on?"
    a: "Neither on its own, because the papers count equally. On AQA, Edexcel and OCR there are three, one non-calculator and two calculator. The non-calculator paper leans on quick arithmetic and clean algebra, so small slips add up fast. The calculator papers reward reading a long problem and keeping several steps in order. Most children lose marks in different places on each, and the pattern shows up quickly once a tutor marks one of each. The plan then goes to whichever paper is costing more, while the other gets a steady share."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Are we starting a Maths tutor too soon?"
    a: "No, Year 10 is often the best time. The course steps up that year, and a gap closed now won't trail behind every new topic in Year 11. Year 10 lessons are mostly about understanding, with less exam drilling. That suits a child who's lost confidence as much as one aiming high. It's also easier to keep a weekly slot going before Year 11 fills the calendar. For most families, one hour a week is plenty at this stage."
  - q: "Do you use qualified teachers?"

Every tutor has passed an interview with Harry or Joe that tests how clearly they explain. If your child's rebuilding foundations, patience and experience count most. If they're chasing top marks, knowing the papers well helps."
  - q: "Is a GCSE Maths tutor worth the money?"
