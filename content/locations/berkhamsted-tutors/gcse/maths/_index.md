  - q: "Which GCSE Maths paper should my child concentrate on, calculator or non-calculator?"
    a: "Neither on its own, since all three papers count equally. The non-calculator paper leans on fluent arithmetic and clean algebra, so small slips add up fast. The calculator papers reward reading a long problem and keeping several steps in order. Most students lose marks in different places on each, and the pattern shows up quickly once a tutor marks one of each. The plan then goes to whichever paper is costing more, while the other gets a steady share."
  - q: "My child is in Year 10. Is it too early for GCSE Maths tutoring?"
    a: "No, Year 10 is often the best time. Much of the higher content arrives that year, and a gap closed now does not trail behind every new topic in Year 11. Lessons in Year 10 are mostly about understanding, with less exam drilling, which suits students who have lost confidence as well as those aiming high. The weekly slot is also easier to keep before Year 11 fills the calendar. Most families find one hour a week is plenty at this stage."
