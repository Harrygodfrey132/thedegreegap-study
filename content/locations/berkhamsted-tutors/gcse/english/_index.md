  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read so much?"
    a: "Because the exam rewards a skill that reading alone does not build. Most students have never been taught to plan an argument quickly, pick quotations they can reuse, or write analysis rather than description. Strong readers can be the most frustrated, since they understand the book and still lose marks on structure and time. In the first session a tutor looks at a recent essay to see which of those is the problem, and the plan starts there."
  - q: "How much GCSE English homework should there be between sessions?"
    a: "About an hour, sometimes up to ninety minutes. Usually that is one timed answer, or a plan plus a paragraph, on whatever the lesson worked on, along with a few quotations to learn. More than that tends to give diminishing returns, because in English the marking and feedback matter more than the amount written. The tutor goes through the work at the start of the next lesson, so the homework and the teaching stay connected."

    a: "Yes, and we ask about it on every first call. The whiteboard keeps everything visible and saved. Tell us what support your child already has, including any access arrangements for exams, and we will match a tutor with relevant experience."
