faq_1_q: "What does Ashlyns ask for to join its sixth form?"
faq_1_a: "Grade 4 or above in English and Maths, plus an average of about grade 4 across six other GCSEs, according to its published admissions criteria. Each A-Level then carries its own minimum, set out in the sixth form pack, and those subject minimums are where most families get caught out. The same standard applies to Year 11s already at the school and to students joining from elsewhere, and the application form goes in during December of Year 11. Read the current pack, since requirements get revised."
faq_2_q: "How do the online sessions work?"
faq_2_a: "They run on Lessonspace, a shared whiteboard made for one-to-one teaching. Your child and the tutor both write on it live, so working gets built up together rather than watched through a camera. Each session is recorded, which helps when a method needs replaying the night before a test. Nothing needs installing, most students have it sorted within minutes, and you get a brief note after every lesson on what was covered and what has been set."
faq_3_q: "My child is a long way behind. Is it too late?"
faq_3_a: "Usually not, and we see this often. The tutor starts from your child's own mistakes on a recent paper and works backwards until the understanding stops, which tends to be earlier than the school year suggests. The plan then rebuilds from there while school carries on. That takes longer than polishing exam technique, and we will say plainly on the first call which of the two we think your child needs, and roughly how many weeks it might take."
faq_4_q: "Who do we talk to first?"
faq_4_a: "Harry or Joe, the two co-founders, on a free call. Profiles only go out after that talk. The questions are about the school, the board, how the latest mock went and what your child might study next. You then see two or three tutors and pick one for a free 15-minute video meeting. There is no contract, and you pay nothing before your child has met the tutor and is happy to carry on."
