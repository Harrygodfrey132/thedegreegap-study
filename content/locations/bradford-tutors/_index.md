faq_1_q: "My child's school ends at Year 11. How does that change what we should be doing?"
faq_1_a: "It raises the stakes on the subject grades, because your child is applying somewhere new rather than moving up a corridor. Sixth forms and colleges across the district each publish their own entry requirements and revise them between years, so the sensible first step is reading this year's list for the two or three places on your shortlist. Then we work backwards from that. Tell us the shortlist on the call and we will be honest about which subjects actually need the hours between now and March."
faq_2_q: "Can you help a student who has fallen a long way behind?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it is a good part of what we do. Some students we work with in Bradford are carrying gaps that go back to Year 8, which is further back than most revision plans ever reach. The first session works backwards through their own wrong answers until the understanding runs out, rather than picking up where school left off. Once that point is found the rebuild is usually quicker than parents expect, because the student has met the material before even if it never stuck."

faq_4_q: "Can tutors help with a GCSE resit in November?"
faq_4_a: "Yes, and the approach is different from first-time preparation. A resit has a short runway, so the work starts from the actual script where that is available, or from a full paper sat under timed conditions if it is not. From there we target the two or three question types costing the most marks rather than reteaching the specification. Plenty of Bradford students resit English or Maths alongside a college course, and sessions fit around that timetable because everything runs online."
