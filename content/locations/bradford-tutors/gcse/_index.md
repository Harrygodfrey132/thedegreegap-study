faq_1_q: "Year 9, Year 10 or Year 11: when does GCSE tutoring make sense in Bradford?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10, where you have the choice. The content steps up and the gaps that open are still small enough to close inside a term, whereas left alone for a year they stack and then surface together in a mock. Year 11 still works, and a lot of families start there, but the plan narrows towards exam technique and papers rather than teaching anything properly from the beginning. Year 9 is worth it when a student has already decided they are no good at a subject. That belief hardens fast."
faq_2_q: "My daughter's school and my nephew's use different exam boards. Does the tutor need to know which?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it is the first thing we confirm rather than a detail settled later. Schools across the district use different boards, and they word questions differently, weight topics differently and print different formulae on the paper. A tutor who knows your child's specification is doing useful work inside ten minutes. One who does not spends the first few sessions catching up, at your expense. Bring the board and the tier to the consultation call if you know them, and we will find out if you do not."
faq_3_q: "What can a tutor do about GCSE Maths that more revision cannot?"
faq_3_a: "Revision practises what a student can already do. A tutor finds what they cannot. The pattern we see most in Bradford is a method from Year 8 or 9 that never properly landed, so the student works round it and the working collapses on anything with four steps in it. Fractions inside algebra, negatives, reverse percentages. None of that gets found by another past paper done alone at the kitchen table, because nobody is watching the working. The first hour is spent watching it."
