faq_1_q: "Can a GCSE tutor help my child get ready for the mocks?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, and mocks are one of the most common reasons Northampton families get in touch. The work is mostly past papers, sat under time and then marked the way an examiner marks them, which is a different exercise from reading notes. Tutors look at where the marks go missing rather than at what the student does not know, because those are rarely the same list. The March round matters most, since that is the set of grades sixth forms and colleges tend to see."
faq_2_q: "My child has been put in for foundation tier. Does tutoring still change anything?"
faq_2_a: "It changes quite a lot. Foundation tier has a ceiling, but nearly all of the marks on it sit in topics a student can properly nail with a term of work, so the gap between a shaky pass and a confident one is smaller than it looks. That confident pass is what most Level 3 courses at Northampton College and most apprenticeship schemes are asking for. If your child is close to the boundary in Year 10, a tutor can also help the school decide the tier on evidence rather than a hunch."
faq_3_q: "Is English Language a different job from English Literature?"
faq_3_a: "Two exams, two sets of skills, and students often struggle with one and not the other. Language rewards reading an unseen source quickly and writing to order under time. Literature rewards knowing texts well enough to argue with them and picking quotations that earn something. A tutor who treats them as one subject will spend half the sessions in the wrong place. We ask which paper the marks are going missing on before matching anyone, and plenty of students only need work on one of the two."
