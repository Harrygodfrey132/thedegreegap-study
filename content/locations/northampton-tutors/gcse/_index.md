faq_1_q: "Can a GCSE tutor help my child get ready for the mocks?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, and the mocks are one of the main reasons Northampton families get in touch. Most of the work is past papers, done against the clock and marked the way an examiner would. That's very different from reading over notes. The tutor looks at where your child's marks go missing, not just at what they don't know. Those are rarely the same list. If your child's school runs a second round in the spring, that's often the set of grades sixth forms and colleges see."
faq_2_q: "My child's been entered for foundation tier. Is tutoring still worth it?"
faq_2_a: "Often more than you'd expect. Foundation is the easier of the two tiers of paper, and it tops out at a grade 5. But most of its marks come from topics your child can get properly secure with a term of steady work. So the gap between a shaky pass and a confident one is smaller than it looks. And a confident pass is what most Level 3 courses at Northampton College and most apprenticeship schemes ask for. If your child's near the line between the tiers in Year 10, a tutor's view gives you and the school more to go on."
faq_3_q: "Are English Language and English Literature really that different?"
faq_3_a: "Very different. They're two separate GCSEs that test different skills, and it's common to struggle with one and not the other. Language is about reading a passage they've never seen, quickly, and writing well against the clock. Literature is about knowing the set texts well enough to argue about them, with quotations that actually earn marks. Treat them as one subject and half the lessons end up in the wrong place. So we'll ask which one is losing your child marks before we match anyone. Plenty only need help with one."
