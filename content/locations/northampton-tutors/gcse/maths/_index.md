  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Maths tutoring session?"
    a: "An hour, split roughly three ways. The first stretch goes on last week's work, marked together, so misconceptions surface while they are still small. The middle is the topic bleeding the most marks, taught properly and then practised rather than explained and moved on from. The last stretch is real exam questions on that topic, timed, with the mark scheme open afterwards so the student can see where the marks actually sit. Homework is set from whatever went wrong."
  - q: "How do I tell whether my child needs a Maths tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at their working on a recent paper, not the grade. Neat, correct method that runs out of time or drops marks at the back of the paper is an exam technique problem, and that is the quickest thing to fix. Blank space or crossed-out attempts on multi-step questions is a method confidence problem, which needs teaching rather than practice. A student who scores well across mocks and wants more is a third case again, and usually needs a short block rather than a full programme."
