faq_1_q: "My child is starting Year 12 at a different school from the one they've been at. Is now a good time for a tutor?"
faq_1_a: "Often it's the best time. The step from GCSE to A-Level is big wherever your child goes, and getting used to a new sixth form at the same moment adds to it. A tutor in the first term can catch a shaky topic early. That's important, because teachers lean mostly on Year 12 work when they predict grades, and those predictions are sent with UCAS applications in the autumn of Year 13. Starting in Year 13 still helps with the final exams. There's just less time before the predictions are made."
faq_2_q: "Can a tutor help with the EPQ?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, within what the rules allow. The Cooper School offers the Extended Project alongside A-Levels, and plenty of other sixth forms do too. A tutor can help your child narrow a big idea into a question they can actually answer, and plan their time so it doesn't all land at once. What a tutor can't do is read or correct a draft, because the rules say the project has to be your child's own work. We'll match your child with someone who knows the area they've chosen."
