  - q: "Is it the non-calculator paper that's costing my child marks?"
    a: "Often, though it's worth checking. On AQA, Edexcel and OCR your child sits three papers of equal weight, and one of them's done without a calculator. That one rewards quick, accurate work by hand, like fractions, negative numbers and rearranging. The other two lean on longer problems, where a calculator helps with the arithmetic but not with knowing where to start. A single marked mock usually shows which way the marks are going. If it's the non-calculator paper, the tutor can open each lesson with a few minutes of arithmetic by hand."
  - q: "Higher or Foundation: how do we know which tier is right?"
    a: "Your child's school makes the decision, but it helps to know how the two work. Foundation papers top out at a grade 5. Higher papers start at a 4, with a 3 awarded for a narrow miss, and go up to a 9. So a child getting 5s in mocks would often be entered for Higher, while one sitting around a 3 might do better on Foundation, where more of the questions are within reach. If you're unsure, a tutor's view after a few lessons is useful to take along to parents' evening."
