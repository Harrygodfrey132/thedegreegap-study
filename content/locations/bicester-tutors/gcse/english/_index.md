faqs:
  - q: "Do English lessons happen online, wherever we live around Bicester?"
    a: "Yes, every lesson. Your child and the tutor share an online whiteboard, so the tutor can underline a quotation or rework a sentence while your child watches, and each lesson is recorded. Whether you're on Elmsbrook, Kingsmere or out in Chesterton, you get the same choice of tutors, picked for your child's set texts rather than for living nearby. That matters in English, where the best match is often the tutor who's taught the exact play and poems your child is studying."
  - q: "Does it matter which exam board my child's school uses for English?"
    a: "It does, mostly for Literature. AQA, Edexcel and OCR each publish their own list of set texts and lay out their papers differently, so a child who's moved between boards can face a new structure as well as new books. The Language papers overlap more in the skills they test, though each board words and marks its questions its own way. We'll check the board and the texts on the call. If you're not sure of the board, it's usually printed on the front of a mock paper."
  - q: "Will my child have English to do between lessons, on top of school homework?"
    a: "An hour or so a week is usually plenty. That might mean learning five quotations from the new play, or writing one timed paragraph for the tutor to look at next time. Ten minutes on most evenings tends to stick better than a weekend cram, especially with texts your child's meeting for the first time. Tutors set it with school homework in mind, so the week doesn't overflow. You'll see what was set in the tutor's note after each lesson."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia. Can an English tutor still help?"
    a: "It can. Many tutors on our platform work with dyslexic children, and we'll ask about it on the call so we suggest the right people. In English that often means reading in shorter chunks and laying essay plans out visually on the whiteboard. One-to-one, there's no worry about reading aloud in front of a class."
  - q: "What if my child falls short in English Language this summer?"
    a: "Your child can sit it again in November, which isn't true of most GCSEs. English Language isn't tiered, so there's no Foundation or Higher choice to make. Your child sits both papers again, a few months on. The writing sections are often where marks come back, because planning and leaving time to check can lift a whole answer. If your child is starting at a Bicester sixth form or college in September, ask how the resit will work alongside their new courses. Lessons can stop once it's done, as there's no contract."
