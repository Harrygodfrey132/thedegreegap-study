faq_1_q: "The college application goes in before Christmas of Year 11. When should tutoring start to count for it?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10, if you have the choice. The form itself is not the hard part. What matters is the grade the school has written down by the autumn of Year 11, because that is what every conversation about courses gets held against, and it comes from Year 10 work and the first mocks. A tutor who starts in Year 10 has two terms to close a gap before it turns into a number on a form. A Year 11 start still helps, and plenty of families begin there, but the work narrows to exam technique and past papers. Either way, the first session is a marked paper, not a lesson."
faq_2_q: "My daughter's school is on a different exam board from her cousin's. Does that change who you suggest?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it is the first thing we confirm. Schools across Stockport sit different boards, and the boards word questions differently, print different formulae and examine practicals in different ways. A tutor who knows your daughter's specification is useful from the first ten minutes. One who does not spends the first few sessions catching up, at your expense. So on the call we ask for the board and the tier, and if you are not sure we tell you where to find it, usually on the school's website or on the front of a recent mock paper."
faq_3_q: "Do the winter mocks matter more in Stockport, with the college application already in?"
faq_3_a: "They matter, but not in the way parents fear. A college place is conditional on the real results in August, so a poor mock does not lose it. What the mock does is show where the marks are going before the exam does. Tutors in our network sit the student through the marked paper question by question, sort the lost marks into content, timing and answer habits, and build the spring around the biggest pile. A mock that lands badly in December and gets read properly is worth more than one that lands fine and gets filed."
faq_4_q: "He has fallen a long way behind. Is GCSE tutoring still worth it this late?"
faq_4_a: "Yes, and the plan is different from the one a student two marks off a grade needs. The tutor goes back to where the understanding stopped, which is often Year 8 or 9 rather than this term, and rebuilds from there instead of racing through the current topic. That sounds slower. It is usually faster, because the student has met the material before and it comes back quickly once the missing step is put in. Grade 4 in Maths and English opens most college courses in the borough, and a focused term of weekly sessions is often enough to get there."
