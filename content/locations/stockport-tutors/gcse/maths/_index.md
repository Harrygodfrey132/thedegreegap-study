  - q: "Is GCSE Maths in Stockport the same standard as in Altrincham and Trafford?"
    a: "It's the same exam. What's different is the school system. Trafford selects at 11, so its grammar schools sit the higher paper with a year group picked by a test, and some Stockport children sit that test and cross the border. Stockport doesn't select, so one Maths class here can hold a child chasing a 4 and a child chasing a 9. So the useful comparison isn't between the two boroughs. It's your child's last paper against the grade their chosen college asks for. Same boards and same mark schemes on both sides of the boundary."
  - q: "Calculator or non-calculator: which paper should my child work on first?"
    a: "Whichever one the marked mock says is leaking, and it's rarely both equally. The three papers carry the same weight, so the grade comes from the total. Paper 1, the non-calculator one, rewards quick arithmetic and clean algebra. It's where children who lean on a calculator drop marks in the first ten questions. Papers 2 and 3 reward method on longer problems, and the losses cluster at the back, in questions written as paragraphs. So the tutor marks the mock paper by paper first, then spends the term on the bigger pile."
  - q: "Should my child sit the higher or foundation Maths paper?"
    a: "It depends on where your child's marks are now, not where anyone hopes they'll finish. Higher goes up to grade 9, but it gives very little below a 4. So a child who's shaky in the middle of the paper can come out with less than foundation would have given them. Foundation stops at a 5, but far more of its marks are within reach. A borderline 5 in Year 10 usually belongs on higher, with a focused term of work. A 3 is usually safer on foundation, where a 4 opens most Stockport college courses. A practice paper with a tutor in Year 10 turns it into a clear decision, made together with school."
