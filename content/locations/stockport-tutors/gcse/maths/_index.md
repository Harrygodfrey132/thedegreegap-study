faqs:
  - q: "Is the GCSE Maths standard in Stockport similar to Altrincham and Trafford?"
    a: "Different systems more than different standards. Trafford selects at 11, so its grammar schools sit the higher paper with a cohort chosen by a test, and some Stockport children sit that test and cross the border. Stockport does not select, so a single Maths class here can hold a student chasing a grade 4 and a student chasing a 9, and results across the borough sit close to the national picture. What a Stockport parent should compare is not the two boroughs but their child's last paper against the line their chosen college publishes. Same exam, same boards, same mark schemes on both sides of the boundary."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or the non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Whichever one the marked mock says is leaking, and it is rarely both equally. The three papers carry the same weight, so the grade is decided by the total. Paper 1, the non-calculator one, rewards arithmetic fluency and clean algebra, and it is where students who lean on the calculator lose marks in the first ten questions. Papers 2 and 3 reward multi-step method, and the losses cluster at the back, in the problems written as paragraphs. A tutor's first job is to mark the mock by paper and by question type, then spend the term on the bigger pile rather than on Maths in general."
  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Maths?"
    a: "It depends on where the marks sit now, not where anyone hopes to finish. Higher tier runs up to grade 9 but scores very little below a 4, so a student who is shaky in the middle of the paper can come out with less than foundation would have given them. Foundation stops at a 5 and makes far more of its marks reachable. A borderline 5 in Year 10 is often right on higher with a focused term. A 3 is usually safer on foundation, where a 4 opens most Stockport college courses. A diagnostic paper in Year 10 turns that into a decision, with school in the conversation."
