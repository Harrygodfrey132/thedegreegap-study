faq_1_q: "My child wants to take Sciences at Andover College. Why does their Maths grade matter so much?"
faq_1_a: "Because the science A-Levels there all ask for it. Andover College's current pages want a 6 in Higher Maths for Biology, along with a 5 in English Language, and a 6 in Maths for Chemistry and for Physics, where students take A-Level Maths alongside too. Peter Symonds builds Maths into its science requirements as well. So if Maths is the subject furthest from the line, it gets the first few weeks. Check the lists again in Year 10, as they can change."
faq_2_q: "Is Year 10 too early to start GCSE tutoring?"
faq_2_a: "Not at all, and it's often the calmest time to start. There's room to go back over a shaky Year 8 or 9 topic before Year 11 adds more on top, and nobody's revising for anything yet. Choices are made in Year 9 and 10 too: Harrow Way, for one, invites some Year 9s onto Triple Science. Starting in Year 11 still works. The lessons just lean more on past papers and timing."
faq_3_q: "How does a GCSE tutor fit in with what my child does at school?"
faq_3_a: "The tutor follows the school, not the other way round. In the first lesson they'll look at recent tests or exercise books to see what's been taught and where the marks are going. After that, lessons either back up the topic your child is on in class or go back to fill an older gap, whichever is costing more. Past paper questions come in once the method is secure. Your child's teachers remain in charge. The tutor just gives them more time on the thing that's stuck."
