    a: "Each course sets its own. At Peter Symonds, A-Level Biology asks for 6, 6 and 5 in any order across Biology, another science and Maths, or across Combined Science and Maths. Chemistry and Physics ask for 7, 6 and 6 in the same way. At Andover College, Chemistry wants a 6 in Maths and in Chemistry, and Biology a 6 in Higher Maths plus a 5 in English Language. Lists change, so check them in Year 10, then plan back from the numbers."
  - q: "Should my child sit Higher or Foundation in GCSE Science?"
    a: "If Science A-Levels are a possibility, Higher, because Foundation caps the grade below what those courses ask for. Combined Science Foundation tops out at a 5-5. If your child is landing around 4-4 on Year 10 tests, Higher is usually realistic with some targeted work on the six-mark questions and the maths. If they're well below that, Foundation can be the safer way to a solid pass. Schools decide entries with you, and a tutor's view of where the marks are going helps that conversation."
  - q: "Will my child need a different tutor for each of the three sciences?"
    a: "Not usually, at GCSE. Plenty of tutors on our platform teach all three to Combined Science students. For Triple, where each science is a full GCSE of its own, some families prefer a separate tutor for the one causing most trouble, often Physics or Chemistry. And if Maths is shaky too, a tutor who covers both Maths and Physics can tie the calculations together. We'll talk through which makes sense on the call."

On Lessonspace those plans sit on a shared whiteboard, and every lesson can be replayed, which helps with learning practical methods. If your child has extra time or a reader in exams, the tutor can practise with that in mind."
