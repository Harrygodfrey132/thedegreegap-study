faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE Maths trips up Bromley children most?"
    a: "The last third of each paper, and not because the topics are unusual. Algebraic proof, circle theorems with reasons, and the long ratio and rates questions on the calculator papers all reward setting-out over speed. If your child's been quick at Maths since the 11+, that's a strange trade. Earlier in the paper, the marks we see dropped most are on rearranging formulae, bounds (the biggest and smallest a rounded number could be) and surds (square roots left as roots), where a right answer with no working can score half. It isn't a knowledge gap either way. Both usually improve with a few weeks of marking against the real mark scheme."
  - q: "Is it the calculator or non-calculator paper my child should practise more?"
    a: "Whichever one is losing more marks, and the first lesson finds out. All three papers are worth the same, and two of them allow a calculator. The non-calculator paper rewards quick arithmetic and algebra, and it catches out children who take shortcuts. The two calculator papers reward multi-step problem solving and accuracy, and most marks go missing at the back, on the wordy questions. Children who work fast in their heads tend to lose more on the non-calculator paper. Careful but slow ones tend to run out of time on the last calculator paper. A marked paper usually tells us which your child is within ten minutes."
