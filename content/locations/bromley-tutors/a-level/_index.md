faq_1_q: "He has just started Year 12 at a new sixth form. Should we wait for the first report before getting a tutor?"
faq_1_a: "No, and the reason is the calendar. The first report tends to arrive after the autumn half term, the Year 12 mocks follow in the spring, and those mocks are what the school uses to write the predicted grades that reach UCAS a few months later. A student who changed school at 16 is also carrying a set of topics taught one way at the old school and assumed another way at the new one. A tutor finds those joins in the first hour or two. If you wait for the report, you find them after the first mark has been recorded."
faq_2_q: "The prediction goes on the UCAS form in the autumn of Year 13. How much can move before then?"
faq_2_a: "A band, sometimes two, if the work starts in Year 12. Predictions rest on Year 12 mocks and the first weeks of Year 13, so tutoring that lands in the spring and summer of Year 12 counts twice: once in the number the school writes down, and again in the exam that has to meet it. From a Year 13 start the plan narrows to the two or three question types costing most marks, which still moves the final grade. It just rarely moves the prediction, because that has usually been written by then."
faq_3_q: "Everything runs online. Does that actually work for a subject like Chemistry or Maths at A-Level?"
faq_3_a: "It works better at A-Level than at any other stage, because the whole subject is written down. Sessions run one-to-one over Lessonspace, a shared whiteboard, so a mechanism or a multi-step integration gets built line by line on screen with the tutor watching each step rather than checking the answer at the end. faq_4_q: "She got top grades at GCSE and her first A-Level mock came back a D. What happened?"
faq_4_a: "Usually nothing to do with effort. A-Level questions stop announcing which method they want, the essays are marked on evaluation rather than on knowing things, and the pace assumes students revise between lessons without being told to. A student who coasted through GCSE on quick recall meets all three at once, and the first mock is where they show. The fix is specific: find which of the three is doing the damage on her script, then rebuild that one. A D in November is recoverable. A D that nobody has read the script for is the problem."
