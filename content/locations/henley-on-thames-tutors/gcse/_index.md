faq_1_q: "Is Year 10 too soon to start, if the big choice is in Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "Not at all. By the autumn of Year 11 your child may well be looking round The Henley College or a sixth form over the river, and predicted grades will be part of that conversation. Year 10 gives a tutor a whole year to deal with the topic your child quietly avoids, whether that's algebra or a poetry anthology, before it turns up in a mock. Lessons can be lighter too: one a week, with no cramming."
faq_2_q: "Year 11 mocks are coming up. Is it worth starting a tutor now?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, if one subject keeps letting your child down. A few weeks is enough to work through a past paper or two together, see where the marks leak, and practise that part against the clock. Mock results tend to shape what teachers predict, and a prediction is often the first thing a college or sixth form sees. Don't expect a whole subject to be rebuilt by then, though. The real target is the summer."

faq_3_a: "Yes. At The Henley College the T Level route asks for 5s in English, Maths and Science, plus two more 5s, so Science is named there in a way it isn't for the A-Level route. A tutor can work on whichever subject is the sticking point for your child, which is often Maths or Science. Requirements can change, so it's worth checking this year's course page first."
