faq_1_q: "When's the best time to start GCSE tutoring: Year 9, Year 10 or Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "None of them is wrong, though Year 10 often gives the most room. In Year 9 there's time to fix an old gap slowly, before GCSE content piles on top of it. Year 10 leaves space for both the gap and the new topics. And in Year 11 the work gets sharper: past papers, timing and the subjects furthest from where they need to be, ideally well before mocks start feeding into sixth form applications."
faq_2_q: "My child has fallen a long way behind in Year 10. Can a tutor still help?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, as long as the tutor starts in the right place. That usually means going backwards first: a recent test, a few homework sheets, and the topic underneath where things went wrong, which might date from Year 8. Racing to catch up with the class rarely works when the foundations aren't there. Rebuilding them takes a few weeks, and then the newer topics start making sense on their own."
faq_3_q: "How does a GCSE tutor fit around what my child is doing at school?"
faq_3_a: "The tutor works from your child's own schoolwork, so lessons add to what the teacher's doing rather than pulling another way. Most lessons mix two things: the topic costing the most marks, and whatever the class is on this week if your child's stuck with it. Past paper questions come in once a method is secure. If there's a test coming up, the plan for that week can change."
