Holy Cross currently asks for a 7 for A-Level Maths, and students who come in with a 7 also sit a bridging assessment at the start of Year 12. Further Maths there needs an 8. Coombe Sixth Form, Richard Challoner and Tiffin publish their own lists, which can change each year. So check the course guides in Year 10, then plan the Maths back from the grade your child needs."
  - q: "Is the non-calculator paper where my child should be putting the work in?"
    a: "It depends where their marks are going. All three papers count equally, so the two calculator papers carry two-thirds of the grade between them. The non-calculator paper tests fluent arithmetic and algebra: fractions, negative numbers, expanding brackets. The calculator papers reward longer problems done accurately. Most children drop more marks early on the non-calculator paper and near the end of Paper 3. A tutor's first lesson works out which applies to your child."
  - q: "Why can my child do it in class, then get stuck on the homework?"
    a: "Often because the method hasn't quite set. In class the steps are on the board. At home your child has to remember them, and if they half-remember one version from school and another from you or a weekend class, they stall. Sometimes it goes back further, to a Year 8 topic like negative numbers or rearranging that never went in. The tutor finds which it is, then practises one clear method until your child can do it unprompted."
