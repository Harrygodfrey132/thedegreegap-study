faq_1_q: "Should A-Level tutoring start in Year 12, or can it wait until Year 13?"
faq_1_a: "Earlier is usually easier. Year 12 is when the gaps from the GCSE-to-A-Level jump open up, and the end-of-year exams often feed into the predicted grades on your child's UCAS form. Starting in Year 13 still helps, mostly with timing and past papers. But by the January mocks there's much less room to rebuild anything from scratch."
faq_2_q: "My child got good GCSEs. Why are they struggling now?"
faq_2_a: "Because A-Level is a different kind of course, not a harder version of the same one. GCSE mostly rewards knowing things. A-Level asks your child to use them on questions they haven't seen, and to write at length about why. Plenty of bright students have never had to struggle before, so the first low mark knocks their confidence more than it deserves. A tutor works on the method, and the confidence usually follows."
