  - q: "My child reads all the time. Why are their English grades stuck?"
    a: "Because reading well and writing an exam answer well are two different skills. Keen readers often understand a text deeply. But planning an essay, picking a quotation that proves the point and writing about the writer's methods rather than the story all take practice. With a whole class to teach, a teacher rarely gets to sit with each child as they write. Some children lose marks on the writing tasks instead, by starting without a plan and running out of time. The tutor's first job is working out which it is, from a recent piece of marked work."
  - q: "Does my child need a tutor, or just more revision?"
    a: "The marked essays usually tell you. If your child's answers are clear and well planned and they just need more quotations learnt, focused revision may well be enough. If the essays drift, run short or keep getting the same comment about analysis, more revision rarely fixes it, because the problem's in how the answer is built. That's where one-to-one help moves things quickly. And if we think revision's all your child needs, we'll say so on the call."
  - q: "How do you pick the English tutors you suggest to Marlow families?"
    a: "Around 3% of the people who apply to tutor on our platform get through. References come last. With English tutors we look hard at how they give feedback on writing. A comment like 'add more analysis' helps nobody. A good tutor shows your child what the better paragraph looks like."
  - q: "Can I sit in on my child's first English session?"
