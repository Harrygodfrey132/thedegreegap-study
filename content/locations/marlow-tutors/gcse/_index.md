faq_1_q: "When should a Marlow family think about GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Ideally in Year 10, when the content gets heavier and a gap can be closed without the pressure of applications. Year 11 starts are common here too and still work, because the sixth-form forms go in during the autumn while the grades that confirm a place come the following August. What changes with a later start is the shape of the plan. It leans on past papers and timing rather than teaching a topic again from the beginning."
faq_2_q: "Can tutoring help with the grade 4 in English or Maths?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it is one of the most common reasons families get in touch. A grade 4 in each keeps doors open that a 3 would close. Some courses let a student resit alongside their other studies, but The Henley College does not allow a resit next to an A-Level programme. The tutor works out which paper is losing the most marks and spends the hours there. For many students it is one paper rather than the whole subject."
faq_3_q: "My daughter works hard in class but the mocks do not show it. Why?"
faq_3_a: "Usually because the mock tests something class does not: recall without notes, under a clock, across a whole specification at once. A student who understands a topic on Tuesday may not be able to produce it two months later in an exam hall. The tutor looks at the actual script to see whether the marks went on knowledge, timing or the way answers were written. Each of those has its own fix, and the timing one often moves fastest."
