faq_1_q: "When's a good time to bring in a GCSE tutor?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10 is ideal, when the work gets heavier and a gap can be closed before applications add any pressure. But plenty of Marlow families start in Year 11, and it still works, because places aren't confirmed until results day in August. What changes with a later start is the plan. There's more past-paper practice and timing, and less teaching a topic again from scratch."
faq_2_q: "My child needs a 4 in English or Maths. Can tutoring help?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and we hear it a lot. A 4 in each keeps doors open that a 3 would close. Some courses let your child resit alongside their other studies, but The Henley College doesn't allow one next to an A-Level programme. So the tutor works out which paper is losing the most marks and spends the time there. Often it's one paper, not the whole subject."
faq_3_q: "My daughter works hard in class, but her mocks don't show it. Why?"
faq_3_a: "Usually because a mock tests something lessons don't. She has to remember everything without notes, against the clock, across the whole course at once. Understanding a topic on a Tuesday isn't the same as producing it two months later in an exam hall. So the tutor looks at your daughter's actual paper. Did the marks go on knowledge, on timing or on how the answers were written? Each has its own fix, and timing is often the quickest to sort."
