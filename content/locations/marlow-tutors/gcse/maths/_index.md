  - q: "Is the non-calculator paper the one to worry about?"
    a: "Not on its own. On AQA, Edexcel and OCR there are three papers worth the same, one without a calculator and two with, so the calculator papers carry two-thirds of the grade between them. The non-calculator paper shows up shaky arithmetic and algebra, because there's nowhere to hide a slip with fractions or negatives. Lots of children drop marks near the end of the non-calculator paper, and in the wordier questions at the back of the last calculator paper. The tutor compares your child's marked papers from each before deciding where the first weeks go."
  - q: "My child's school is talking about Foundation tier. Should we worry?"
    a: "Not necessarily, but it's worth understanding. Foundation tops out at a 5. Higher goes from 9 down to 4, and a near miss on Higher can still earn a 3. So if your child's hoping for Borlase's sixth form, which asks for a 5 in Maths, Foundation leaves no room to spare. If they're on 5s or 6s in Year 10 mocks, Higher is usually right. On 3s and 4s, Foundation can feel less risky. In the end it's the school's call, usually made during Year 11. A tutor's first few lessons give you evidence to take into that conversation."
