  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Both count equally, so the answer depends on where the marks are going. The non-calculator paper exposes weak arithmetic and algebra, because there is nowhere to hide a slip with fractions or negatives. The calculator papers reward careful setting out on long, multi-step problems. Many students drop more marks on the non-calculator paper near the end, and on the final calculator paper in the wordier problem-solving questions. A tutor compares scripts from each to decide where the first few weeks go."
  - q: "Should my child sit foundation or higher tier?"
    a: "It depends on where they are now and how much time is left. Higher tier gives access to grades 4 to 9, with a small safety net at 3. Foundation tops out at a 5. A student sitting on a 5 or 6 in Year 10 mocks is usually right for higher, with focused work on the harder topics. A student on a 3 or 4 may be safer on foundation. The school makes the final call, but a tutor's diagnostic gives you evidence to bring to that conversation."
  - q: "Are your GCSE Maths tutors teachers or students?"
    a: "Both, depending on what suits your child. Tutors on our platform include qualified teachers, graduates from strong universities and some who sat these exact GCSE papers only a few years ago. A student who needs basics rebuilt with patience may do best with an experienced teacher. A student chasing the top grades often clicks with a recent graduate who remembers the mark scheme detail. We ask about your child's learning style on the call and suggest profiles to match."
  - q: "What if my child misses the grade they needed in Maths?"
    a: "There is still a clear way forward. Students who miss a grade 4 can resit Maths in November, and some colleges build a resit into Year 12. An eight-week block in September and October is often enough to move from a 3 to a 4 when the gap is specific. If the issue is a sixth-form requirement rather than a pass, it is worth talking to the school straight away, because some will discuss options. We can help you plan either route."
