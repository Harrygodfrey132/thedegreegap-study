    a: "Chemistry calculations top the list, with six-mark answers and Physics equations close behind. Mole and concentration questions trip up children who are otherwise strong. In all three sciences, a six-mark answer earns its marks by joining the ideas up. A list of correct facts often scores lower than your child expects. In Physics, rearranging and units cost more marks than the ideas themselves. The required practicals are the fourth regular problem. Most children remember doing them, but not the reasoning behind each step."
  - q: "Do the exam boards really differ much for GCSE Science?"

OCR has two separate courses, Gateway and Twenty First Century, organised quite differently from each other, never mind from AQA. If the tutor already knows your child's course, no lesson time goes on catching up. We'll confirm the board when we first speak, before suggesting anyone."
