faq_1_q: "Our child's school finishes at 16. When should we start thinking about grades for college?"
faq_1_a: "Earlier than most parents expect, and Year 10 is ideal. If your child is at Nuneaton Academy, Hartshill or another school that stops at 16, the application to King Edward VI College or NWSLC happens during Year 11, and King Edward's asks for predicted grades on it. Knowing what a course wants by the end of Year 10 gives a tutor well over a year to work on the weakest subject, rather than a few months squeezed around mocks."
faq_2_q: "My child got a 3 in their Maths mock. Will they have to resit at college?"
faq_2_a: "Only if they finish Year 11 below a grade 4. Then they carry on with Maths after 16, which is a government rule, and both NWSLC and King Edward VI College run courses for it. A mock grade isn't the final one, though. A tutor goes through the paper, finds where the more reachable marks went, often number work and simpler algebra near the start, and begins there."
faq_3_q: "At home my child knows it all, then goes blank in the exam hall. Can a tutor help?"
faq_3_a: "Often, yes, because it's usually a practice problem more than a nerves problem. Revising at the kitchen table with notes nearby is very different from a timed paper in a silent hall. A tutor builds up timed questions gradually, a few at a time, then whole sections, and talks through what to do when a question looks unfamiliar. The hall starts to feel like something your child has rehearsed, even if the nerves never vanish completely."
