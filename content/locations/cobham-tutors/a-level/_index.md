faq_1_q: "Our daughter has just started Year 12. Is it too soon to think about a tutor?"
faq_1_a: "Not too soon, though there's no rush in the first few weeks. New subjects and a new routine take a little getting used to, especially after a move to a different sixth form. The first marked tests tell you far more, and most arrive during the autumn term. If one subject comes back well below the others, that's a good moment to call us. It leaves most of Year 12 to work with."
faq_2_q: "How much do Year 12 grades really count for university?"
faq_2_a: "Quite a lot, as it turns out. Universities make offers based on predicted grades, and teachers base those mostly on Year 12 work, often including end-of-year exams. The predictions are sent with UCAS applications in the autumn of Year 13. Most offers still depend on the real results the following summer. A tutor has no say in the predicted grade itself. What they can change is the work your child produces before it's written."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help with coursework or the EPQ?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, with the skills behind it, though there are limits. JCQ rules, which the main exam boards share, restrict the help anyone outside school can give on coursework drafts, so the tutor won't read, mark or correct your child's draft. What they can do is teach the methods it depends on: planning an investigation, judging sources, building an argument, referencing properly. The writing itself has to be your child's own work. We'll go through what's allowed on the call."
faq_4_q: "My son's in Year 13 and hasn't done much work. Have we left it too late?"
faq_4_a: "For the final exams, no, though his predicted grades are probably settled by now, since they usually reach UCAS in the autumn. The tutor starts with the topics carrying the most marks and the ones he's most likely to drop, rather than going back to page one of the course. Past papers come in early, because timed practice tends to be where a late starter picks up marks fastest. It takes a weekly lesson and some work in between. Any mocks still to come will show how it's going."
