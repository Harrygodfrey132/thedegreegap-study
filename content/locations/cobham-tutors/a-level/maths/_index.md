faqs:
  - q: "Which bits of A-Level Maths trip up Cobham sixth formers most?"
    a: "Usually not one topic, but the moment a question stops saying what to do. In the first year that tends to show up in differentiation problems and in Mechanics questions with two connected objects. Statistics catches people out with its wording, since one word can change how a hypothesis test is set up. Under all of it, algebra often wobbles, and a slip in line two spoils everything after. Whichever sixth form your child is at, these sticking points are much the same, so a tutor can usually spot the pattern from one marked test."
  - q: "My child's sixth form uses a different exam board from their GCSE. Does that matter?"
    a: "A little, and it's worth planning for. The topics are much the same on every board, but the papers aren't. AQA, Edexcel and OCR word their questions differently and lay their papers out in their own way. Each has its own large data set for Statistics too. Your child might know the Maths and still trip over an unfamiliar style of question. We'll confirm the board on the call, and every profile we send will be a tutor who teaches it, so practice comes from the right papers from the first lesson."
  - q: "Will there be Maths to do between lessons, and how much?"
    a: "Yes, a little, and it matters more at A-Level than it did at GCSE. An hour or so a week is about right for most sixth formers: a set of questions on that week's topic, done without peeking at the worked examples. Nearer the exams it shifts towards mixed past paper questions, since the real papers don't say which topic each question comes from. The tutor marks it and starts the next lesson with whatever went wrong. If your child's timetable is already packed, tell us and we'll keep it realistic."
