faqs:
  - q: "Do Cobham sixth forms look at English Language or English Literature?"
    a: "Language is the one that comes up in the entry rules here, so it's worth knowing early how it's going. Cobham Free School, for instance, asks children joining from other schools for five GCSEs at grade 5 or above, and English Language and Maths must both be among them. Literature still counts, and a strong grade helps if your child wants to take English at A-Level. Entry rules can change from one year to the next, so look at the latest requirements for every sixth form your child is considering."
  - q: "Why is GCSE English so hard for my child when they read all the time?"
    a: "Because enjoying a book and answering exam questions about one are quite different skills. The exam wants your child to pick out short quotations and explain what the writer's choices do to the reader, and nobody picks that up just by reading more. The papers are tightly timed too, so a careful, thoughtful reader can run out of minutes. The tutor's first job is to find whether it's the analysis or the pace that's costing marks. Then they practise that one thing, a little each week, until it sticks."
  - q: "Can one English tutor cover Language and Literature?"
    a: "Usually, yes. Plenty of English tutors teach both, because the skills overlap a lot, from close reading to picking the right short quotation. Literature adds the set texts, so it helps if the tutor already knows your child's novel, play and poems, and we'll look for that. If one side is much weaker, say the writing task on the Language papers, lessons can lean that way for a while. There's no need for two tutors, or two lots of fees."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes, and we'll ask about it on the call so the match takes it into account. Long extracts and timed essays can wear out a child with dyslexia quickly, and keeping a whole essay plan in mind can be hard with ADHD. A tutor might colour-code a plan on the whiteboard, or break one big question into three small ones. It's just your child and the tutor, so nobody's racing ahead. And every lesson is recorded, so the method's there to watch again. If something has worked well at school, tell us and we'll look for a tutor who works that way."
  - q: "What if my child misses a 4 in English Language this summer?"
    a: "English Language can be resat in November, and it's one of only two GCSEs that can be (Maths is the other). Whether your child sits it then or the following summer is usually up to the new sixth form, in Cobham or Thames Ditton, so ask which it'll be. At Esher Sixth Form College, anyone without a 4 retakes it at the college. The tutor works on the parts of the paper that went wrong, with timed practice building up to the day. It happens to plenty of children, and it's nothing to be embarrassed about."
