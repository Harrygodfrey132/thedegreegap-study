  - q: "My child says it's just silly mistakes. How can I tell where the Maths marks are really going?"
    a: "One marked paper will usually tell you. Go through the lost marks with your child and ask them to put each one right. If they can fix it on their own, straight away, it was a slip: a misread question, a sign gone missing, a sum done in their head. Those are the easier kind to sort. If that's all it is, your child may not need a tutor, and we'll say so on the free call. If they still can't do it with time to think, that's a method that hasn't settled yet. Another evening of revision rarely moves that. That's when a tutor's worth having."
  - q: "Does every St Albans school use the same GCSE Maths exam?"
    a: "It varies from school to school. Each one picks its own exam board, so two children on the same road can be revising for different papers. The Maths doesn't change, because every board covers the same topics. The papers do. AQA, Edexcel and OCR set three papers of an hour and a half, each worth a third of the grade, with a calculator allowed in two. Eduqas sets two longer papers, one without a calculator and one with. Each board has its own way of wording questions, too, and that matters most near the top grades. So we'll ask which board your child's on before suggesting a tutor."
