  - q: "What do St Albans children find hardest in GCSE Science?"
    a: "The practicals and the maths, most of the time. Your child does each required practical once in a lab, then gets asked about it months later, and by then the method's faded even if the facts haven't. The maths shows up as moles and concentration in Chemistry, and Physics questions with a unit change tucked into the wording. Neither goes wrong in one obvious place. They cost a mark here and a mark there, right across the paper."
  - q: "Does my child need one tutor for all three sciences, or just the weak one?"
    a: "It depends where the marks are going. For Combined Science, one tutor for all three usually makes sense. Plenty of tutors are happy across Biology and Chemistry, or Physics and Maths. That second pairing suits a child doing Triple alongside higher tier Maths (the harder of the two exam levels), because the calculations carry straight over. But if your child is solid in two sciences and really stuck in the third, a tutor who knows that one subject inside out tends to get things moving faster."

The last 15 minutes are exam questions on the same thing, then the mark scheme, so your child sees exactly what earned marks and what didn't."
