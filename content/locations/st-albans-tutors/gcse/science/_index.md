faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Science for St Albans students?"
    a: "Two areas account for most of the lost marks we see. The first is the required practicals, because students carry out the experiment once and then answer questions on it many months later, so the method has faded even where the content has not. The second is the quantitative work, mainly moles and concentration in Chemistry and multi-step calculations in Physics where a unit change is buried in the question. Both leak marks quietly across a whole paper rather than failing in one obvious place."
  - q: "Should one tutor handle all three sciences, or just the weak one?"
    a: "It depends which way the marks are falling. For combined science, one tutor covering all three is often the practical choice. Plenty of tutors are comfortable across Biology and Chemistry, or across Physics and Maths, and that second pairing suits triple science students taking higher-tier Maths because the calculation skills carry directly across. Where a student is secure in two sciences and genuinely stuck in the third, a specialist in that single subject usually produces faster movement than one tutor trying to hold all three at the required depth."
  - q: "How is an hour of Science tutoring usually structured?"
    a: "Sixty minutes in roughly three parts. The first fifteen review the work set last week, marked, with any misconceptions surfaced while they are still small. The middle thirty go at one topic or one technique, which might be a required practical method, the structure of a long-answer response, or a block of calculations. The last fifteen are spent on exam questions from that topic with the mark scheme read through afterwards, so the student sees exactly what earned credit and what did not."
