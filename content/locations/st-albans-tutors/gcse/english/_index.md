faqs:
  - q: "What GCSE English grade do St Albans sixth forms ask for?"
    a: "Each sixth form publishes its own, and it varies by the A-Levels being applied for rather than being a single number across the board. Sandringham, Verulam, Beaumont and Loreto College all set their own entry requirements, and essay-based subjects such as English Literature, History and Politics usually ask for a stronger English grade than science and maths routes do. The practical advice is to read the requirements for the specific subjects your child wants in Year 10, which leaves a full year to act on whatever gap that reveals."
  - q: "When should we start GCSE English tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "Between September and February, and ideally the earlier end. The March mocks are what sixth-form predicted grades are built from, so work completed before them counts for more than the same work done afterwards. English also needs runway, because changing how a student plans and paces an essay takes several weeks to become automatic and then needs practising under timed conditions. Starting in April is still useful, but the plan narrows to the two or three highest-yield techniques rather than a full rebuild."
  - q: "Why is my child's English predicted grade lower than expected?"
    a: "Predictions come from mock performance plus teacher judgement, and in English the gap parents notice most is between how well a child discusses a text out loud and how well they argue about it in writing under time pressure. Those are separate skills and only the written one is assessed. Reading recent mock scripts is the fastest way to see where it goes wrong, and it is usually one of three places: the plan, the choice of evidence, or the clock running out before the strongest point was made."
  - q: "Would my child be better off with a teacher or a recent graduate for English?"
    a: "Neither is automatically the stronger option, and the right call depends on the student rather than on seniority. The network includes qualified secondary English teachers, Russell Group graduates and current undergraduates who sat these papers recently. A student who needs structure rebuilt from scratch often does best with an experienced teacher. A student writing securely who needs the last band usually gains more from a recent high-achieving graduate who remembers precisely what examiners rewarded on this specification."
  - q: "Can I sit in on the English sessions?"
    a: "Yes, and most tutors are happy for you to join the first one so you can see how the hour is structured. Most parents step back afterwards, because students tend to be more willing to share writing they are unsure about when there is no audience, and English depends on that willingness more than most subjects. You will still see what was covered through the notes the tutor writes up after each session, so stepping back does not mean losing visibility."
