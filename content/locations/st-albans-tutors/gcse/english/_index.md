  - q: "When's the best time to start GCSE English tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "Between September and February, and sooner is better. The March mocks often feed into sixth-form predictions, so work done before them counts for more. English also takes a while to shift. A new way of planning and pacing an essay needs a few weeks to become a habit, then more practice against the clock. Starting in April still helps. The tutor just narrows the plan to the two or three things likely to win back the most marks."
  - q: "My child's English prediction is lower than I expected. Why?"
    a: "Usually because talking about a book and writing about it against the clock are different skills, and only the writing gets marked. Predictions come from mock results plus the teacher's judgement. Plenty of children can discuss a text brilliantly over dinner and still lose marks on paper. A recent mock essay shows why faster than anything. It's nearly always one of three things: the plan, the quotations picked, or time running out before the best point was made."
  - q: "Would my child be better off with a teacher or a recent graduate for English?"
    a: "It depends on your child more than on how senior the tutor is. Tutors on our platform include qualified English teachers and recent Russell Group graduates who sat these papers not long ago. If your child's essay structure needs rebuilding from scratch, an experienced teacher is often the better fit. If they're already writing well and after the top grades, a recent graduate who remembers what examiners rewarded can be the one. We'll talk it through with you on the call."
  - q: "Can I sit in on my child's English lessons?"
    a: "Yes. Most tutors are happy for you to join the first one, so you can see how the hour works. After that, most parents step back. Children tend to share writing they're unsure about more easily without an audience, and English relies on that more than most subjects. You won't be left guessing, though. The tutor writes up notes after each lesson, so you'll still know what was covered."
