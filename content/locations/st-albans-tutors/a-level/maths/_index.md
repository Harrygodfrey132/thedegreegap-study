angle_body_1: "Pure maths feels like the real subject, so that's where the revision goes. Meanwhile the applied side quietly decides the grade. Mechanics shows it most clearly. Connected particles, two objects joined by a string, need a proper force diagram before any algebra, and a teenager who skips the diagram drops marks on questions they could do. Moments questions, which are about turning forces, need a sensible point to take moments about. That's a choice rather than a technique, and most students have never thought of it that way. Projectiles need the horizontal and vertical motion kept apart, and mixing them up is one of the commonest slips on the paper. Then there's hypothesis testing in statistics, checking whether some data backs up a claim. The sums are easy. But the conclusion has to be written a set way, in context, to score."
angle_body_2: "So the lessons aren't glamorous, but they're where the marks are. Conclusions to hypothesis tests get written out in full and marked, because a right answer with a woolly conclusion still loses marks. Why does it matter so much here?
