  - q: "Should we worry most about the non-calculator paper?"
    a: "It's often where marks go first, but on AQA, Edexcel and OCR it's one of three papers worth the same. That leaves the two calculator papers holding two-thirds of the grade, so they can't be neglected either. Eduqas, for the record, has two papers rather than three. Without a calculator, slow arithmetic and shaky algebra cost the most time. With one, long multi-step problems are what trip children up, usually towards the end of the last paper. Look at where the crossings-out sit on two or three recent papers and your child's pattern often jumps out."
  - q: "Higher or Foundation: which tier suits my child?"
    a: "It depends where your child is now and what they want to do after 16. Higher papers lead to grades 4 to 9, and a 3 can be given to someone who just misses a 4. Foundation can't go above a 5. That matters here, because A-Level Maths asks for a 6 at Long Road and a 7 at Knights Templar and Hills Road, so Foundation rules it out. The school decides the entry, so talk to the Maths teacher early, and the tutor's notes can help you ask the right questions."
