faq_1_q: "Can a GCSE tutor help my child get into Hills Road or Long Road?"
faq_1_a: "A tutor can't touch the admissions side, but they can help with the grades it rests on. Hills Road works out an admissions score from predicted GCSE grades, and at the moment asks for at least eight GCSEs: four at grade 5, English and Maths included, and four at grade 6. Long Road currently asks for five grade 5s, English and Maths among them, for its A-Level courses. Hills Road also gives priority to its Cambridgeshire partner schools. These rules can change, so read this year's guides with your child before deciding."
faq_2_q: "Is Year 10 too soon to start, or should we wait for Year 11?"
faq_2_a: "Year 10 is a good time, and in Royston there's a practical reason too. Applications are made partway through Year 11, so the grades predicted on them come from the work your child is doing now. Starting in Year 10 gives a tutor time to fix an old gap, like rearranging formulae or planning an essay, before Year 11 piles on. Plenty of families start in Year 11 instead, often after the mocks, and it still helps. The tutor just spends less time rebuilding and more on getting answers down in time."
