  - q: "Why is my child finding GCSE English hard when they love reading?"
    a: "Because loving a book and analysing it in a timed exam are two separate skills. A keen reader often knows exactly what a passage is doing but hasn't been shown how to plan an answer, so the best idea turns up in the last paragraph, or never. Others retell the story because it feels safer than arguing a point. A tutor's first job is spotting which of these it is, from a recent mock or homework answer. Then the lessons work on that habit, a little every week."
  - q: "How do I know whether my child needs a tutor or just more practice?"
    a: "A recent marked answer usually tells you. If the ideas are good but the marks stop short of the top bands, it's technique, and a tutor can often help with that quickly. If the answer drifts into summary or stops halfway, your child needs a method first, and revising harder won't supply one. And if they're already doing well across their mocks, some focused practice at home may be enough. We'll say so on the call if we think tutoring isn't needed yet."
  - q: "My son has ADHD and finds timed writing really hard. Can an English tutor help?"
