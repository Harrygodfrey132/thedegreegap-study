faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Welwyn Garden City students?"
    a: "Answering the question that was asked rather than the one that was easier to answer. Almost every script we read contains at least one paragraph of confident, accurate retelling that scores very little, because the question wanted analysis of how something is written. After that it is timing: a strong first answer followed by two visibly rushed ones. Set texts vary between schools, so the particular book matters less than those two patterns, which turn up regardless."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually because the writing has never been taught as a separate skill from the reading. Schools cover the texts carefully, then set an essay with its own rules, and the gap between knowing a book and scoring on a question about it is where marks disappear. The familiar sign is a student who talks well about a text and writes a description of it. It is a habit rather than a knowledge gap, which is good news, because habits shift faster."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs an English tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Read one marked essay. If it is accurate about the text and still scoring badly, more revision will not help, because the problem is what the writing is pointed at rather than how much is known. That is a tutor job and a well-defined one. If the essay shows the right kind of analysis but runs out halfway, that is timing, which also responds to tutoring. If your child has not read the texts at all, start there before paying anyone."
