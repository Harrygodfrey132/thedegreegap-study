    a: "Because lessons and exams test different things. In class the topic is named, an example is nearby and help is available. In a hall none of that is true and a clock is running, so a student who can follow a method when it is being driven may be unable to select it themselves. It is not a contradiction and it is not the school being wrong. It is a different skill that needs practising rather than explaining."
  - q: "What happens in a typical A-Level Maths tutoring session?"

The middle half hour is the student working mixed questions while the tutor watches where they hesitate, because hesitation locates the gap better than a wrong answer. The last fifteen minutes are fresh questions done alone against a clock, then marked together."
