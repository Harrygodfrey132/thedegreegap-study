  - q: "Is a 6 in GCSE Maths enough for A-Level Maths at Strode's?"
    a: "It's enough to get in. For September 2026, Strode's asked for a 6 in Maths for A-Level Maths, and a 7 for Further Maths. Whether it feels enough is another question. A-Level Maths leans hard on the algebra at the top end of the Higher paper, so a child who only just made the 6 often finds the first term of Year 12 a shock. If your child is set on it, the summer after Year 11 is a good time to firm up surds, quadratics and algebraic fractions, and a tutor can do that in a handful of lessons."
  - q: "My child is on the Foundation tier. Can they still get the grade they need?"
    a: "Often, yes, but it depends which grade. Foundation papers top out at a 5, so they suit a child aiming for a solid 4 or 5, with less risk on the day. If your child needs a 6 for a course at Strode's or elsewhere, they'll need Higher, which runs from 4 to 9, with a 3 possible as a safety net. Schools usually settle tiers after the Year 11 mocks. A tutor can help you see whether Higher is realistic, and if it is, cover the extra topics it brings."
  - q: "How many lessons before my child's Maths improves?"
    a: "For a single block of topics, often six to eight weekly lessons, as long as your child practises in between. Moving a whole grade band usually takes longer, around eight to twelve, and nobody can promise it. Where the gap is matters too. If it's technique, like rushing the last questions, things can shift quickly. If it's algebra from Year 8 that never settled, the tutor rebuilds that first, which feels slow for a few weeks before the rest speeds up."
