faqs:
  - q: "What's the hardest bit of GCSE Maths for children in Hemel Hempstead?"
    a: "Algebra, mostly, because it hides inside everything else. Rearranging an equation, simplifying an algebraic fraction or solving two equations at once turns up in geometry, in ratio and in the wordy problems near the end. So when algebra's shaky, marks go in questions your child wouldn't call algebra. Circle theorems come next, then iterative methods, where each answer gets fed back in to find a closer one. The quiet one is arithmetic on the non-calculator paper. It looks like carelessness, but it's usually an older gap."
  - q: "Roughly how many lessons before my child's Maths grade moves?"
    a: "Usually eight to twelve weekly lessons for one grade, as long as there's some practice in between. It's quicker when the problem is exam technique, because habits change fast once your child sees exactly how a mark scheme treats their own paper. It's slower when the gap goes back two or three years, because that has to be rebuilt before the marks follow. The first lesson tells you which it is. Nobody can promise a grade, and a straight answer about the gap beats a number that just sounds good."

A child who's only practised one board's papers can be thrown by another's wording while knowing exactly the same Maths. So we check your child's board on the call, and we only suggest tutors who know it well."
