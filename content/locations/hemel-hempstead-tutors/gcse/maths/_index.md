faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest topic in GCSE Maths for Hemel Hempstead students?"
    a: "Algebra, understood as the thread running through everything rather than one topic. Rearranging equations, algebraic fractions and simultaneous equations appear inside geometry, ratio and the wordy problems at the back, so weakness there costs marks in places that do not look like algebra. Circle theorems and iterative methods come next, both method rather than understanding. The other quiet cost is arithmetic on the non-calculator paper, which looks like carelessness and is usually an older gap."
  - q: "How many GCSE Maths tutoring sessions does it take to improve a grade?"
    a: "Eight to twelve weekly sessions for a band, provided practice happens in between. It is faster where the gap is exam technique, because habits shift quickly once a student watches a mark scheme applied to their own paper. It is slower where the gap traces back two or three years, since the foundation has to be rebuilt before the marks follow. The first session is a diagnostic and the plan says which of the two you are in, rather than quoting a number that sounds good."
  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE Maths, and does it matter for tutoring?"
    a: "It matters more than parents expect. The core content is nearly identical, but the boards differ in how questions are worded, how multi-step problems are built and which formulae are printed rather than memorised. A student drilled on one board's past papers can be thrown by another's phrasing while knowing the same maths. We confirm the board on the consultation call and match on it, because knowing a board's habits is worth a surprising amount in the last two terms."
