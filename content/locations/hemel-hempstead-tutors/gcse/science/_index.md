  - q: "How can I tell which parts of GCSE Science my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Take a marked paper and sort the lost marks into three piles: straight recall, data and graph questions, and the longer application or six-mark answers. Almost every student is clearly worse at one of the three. Losses on recall mean content gaps. Losses on data mean a method nobody taught. Losses concentrated on unfamiliar-context questions mean the content is there but is not transferring, which is the most commonly missed diagnosis and the quickest of the three to improve."
  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Science tutoring session?"
    a: "An hour in three parts. The first fifteen minutes go on last week's questions, marked properly so misconceptions surface. The middle half hour is teaching, aimed at whatever the diagnostic pointed at, with the method written on the shared whiteboard rather than described. The last fifteen minutes are fresh questions on that topic, deliberately including ones set in unfamiliar contexts, marked against the real scheme while your child watches so the reasoning is visible."
