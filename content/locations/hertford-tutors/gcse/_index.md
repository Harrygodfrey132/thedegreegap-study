For 2027 entry, Richard Hale works out an average across all your child's full-course GCSEs: 5.0 with a 5 in English and Maths for A-Levels only, 4.5 for a mix of A-Levels and vocational courses, and 4.0 for vocational courses. The Sele School asks for five grade 5s, including a 5 in each subject your child continues. Simon Balle and Chauncy publish their own rules, which change from year to year. So yes, the subject your child has given up on still counts."
faq_2_q: "My child is only in Year 10. Isn't it a bit early for a tutor in one subject?"
faq_2_a: "No, and in some ways it's the easiest year to start. In Year 10 a shaky subject is still a small job: one or two older topics to rebuild before Year 11 piles new content on top. The lessons can be calmer, too, because there's no mock next month. Plenty of families start with one lesson a week in the weakest subject, then decide in the summer whether to carry on, add another subject or stop."
faq_3_q: "What actually happens in my child's first lesson?"
faq_3_a: "Mostly, the tutor gets to know your child and finds where the marks are going. They'll look through a recent test or some homework, ask your child to talk through a couple of questions, and try a short task together on the shared whiteboard. By the end, they'll have a good idea whether the trouble is an older topic, exam technique or nerves. You'll hear what they found, and the next lessons are planned from it."
