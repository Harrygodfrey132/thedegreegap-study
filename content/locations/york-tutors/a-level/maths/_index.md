faqs:
  - q: "Our child's only in Year 12. Is it too soon for a Maths tutor in York?"
    a: "No, it's usually the best time, and the difference between Year 12 and Year 13 is bigger than most families expect. Predicted grades come out of Year 12 work and reach universities early in Year 13, so anything your child improves then counts for the application as well as the exam. Starting in Year 13 still lifts the final grade, and plenty of families do it. But by then the offers have usually been made against a number set earlier."
  - q: "Why has my child been predicted a lower Maths grade than I expected?"
    a: "Predictions rest on mocks and the teacher's judgement. At A-Level the usual gap is between following a method and choosing one without being told. Your child can be fluent in lessons and lost in a paper where nothing announces the topic. How do you tell which it is? Look at the marked paper itself, not just the grade. It also makes a conversation with school far more useful, and much less tense."
  - q: "My child's seventeen and doesn't want a tutor. Should we push?"
    a: "Not hard, no. At seventeen their view deserves proper weight, because they're close to running their own work and being overruled rarely helps. Most resistance comes before they've met anyone, and it's often about what accepting help seems to say. The free 15-minute video call lets your child make up their own mind, and turn down one tutor without turning down the whole idea. If they still don't want it, pause."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia or ADHD. Can a Maths tutor still help?"
    a: "Yes, and a number of our tutors do this regularly. Most of the help is about lightening the load. A consistent layout, so long working doesn't get lost. Long questions broken into named stages. One method practised until it's automatic. Where exam conditions are the hard part, timed practice comes in gradually. Tell us on the call what school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements."
  - q: "What do York teenagers find hardest about A-Level Maths?"
    a: "The step at the start of Year 12, when questions stop saying which method they want and your child has to choose. Strong GCSE performers often find it hardest of all. Spotting the pattern carried them a long way, and suddenly there's nothing to match. After that, the marks tend to go in whichever part gets neglected, usually mechanics or statistics, depending on the teenager."
