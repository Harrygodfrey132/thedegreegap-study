  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for York students?"
    a: "The unseen material on the Language papers, followed by the comparison question. Unseen work is hard because there is nothing to revise, so a diligent student who has learned every quotation still feels ambushed by it. Comparison is hard because it wants a genuinely different paragraph shape, one that holds two texts at once rather than walking through them in turn. Both are technique problems rather than knowledge problems, which is why they move faster than people expect."
  - q: "How can I tell which English topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Compare a Literature paper and a Language paper side by side rather than looking at either alone. Strong Literature marks with weak Language marks is a preparation imbalance, and it is the most common pattern we see. Marks lost evenly across both, on the extended responses, is a structure problem. And an answer that thins out badly in its final paragraphs is a timing problem wearing an analysis problem's clothes."
  - q: "How do you vet your English tutors?"
    a: "Roughly three in every hundred applicants make it onto the platform. Written application, a subject knowledge stage, then a founder-led interview that is mostly about explaining something difficult clearly while someone is stuck. References are checked before any match. For English that last stage is the one that matters, because knowing why a paragraph sits in a lower band and being able to say it in a way a fifteen-year-old will act on are not the same skill."
  - q: "What if my child does not want a tutor?"
    a: "Very common, and worth handling gently rather than pushing through. The free 15-minute meeting exists mostly for this. If they genuinely do not want to carry on after a session or two, we pause. That is their call."
