faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English gives Rickmansworth Year 11s the most trouble?"
    a: "The poetry anthology catches a lot of children out. On AQA's Literature paper, one poem from the cluster is printed and your child has to pick a second from memory to compare it with, so knowing a few favourites isn't enough. A tutor helps your child sort the poems into pairs that work on shared themes, then practise planning a comparison quickly. It rewards steady work through Years 10 and 11 much more than a last-minute cram."
  - q: "Does the exam board make a difference for English?"
    a: "It does, in a few ways. The question types and timings change from board to board, and in Literature each Rickmansworth school picks its texts from its own board's list. So a tutor who knows AQA's Language Paper 1 inside out still needs to know which play and which novel your child is studying. Both get checked on the call, before a single profile comes your way."
  - q: "The English teacher says my child is capable. Why doesn't the prediction show it?"
    a: "Usually because the exam asks for something narrower than being good at English: answering a set question, in a set time, with evidence and explanation. Capable children often lose marks by drifting from the question, or by running out of time before the writing task. A tutor marks a recent answer with your child against the board's mark scheme, then practises the one habit that cost the most."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia. Will an English tutor know how to help?"
    a: "Yes. Mention it when we speak, and the profiles we send will be tutors who've taught children who learn differently. In English that might mean planning answers visually on the shared whiteboard, or building up timed writing in shorter bursts. Each lesson's recording helps too, because your child can replay a method at their own pace, as many times as they need."
  - q: "What if my child doesn't get the English grade they need?"
    a: "There's a second chance sooner than most people realise. Along with Maths, English Language has a November sitting each year, so your child wouldn't have to wait until the following summer to try again. Sixth forms and colleges each have their own rules about resits alongside other courses, so it's worth asking them early. Either way, a tutor can help your child get ready."
