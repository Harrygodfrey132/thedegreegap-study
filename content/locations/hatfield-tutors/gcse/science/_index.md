faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE Science do Hatfield children usually find hardest?"
    a: "Usually it isn't a topic at all. Three things come up far more often. First, the six-mark answers, which want a chain of reasoning, not a list of true facts. Then the data and graph questions, which need their own step-by-step method that rarely gets practised on its own. And the equations, where time and accuracy go on choosing the right one and rearranging it. All three are about how to answer, not what to know. That's good news, because they tend to improve faster than gaps in content."
  - q: "Why does my child find GCSE Science so difficult?"
    a: "Most often it's because Science wants a particular style of writing that's easy to miss in a busy class. Your child learns the content, then loses marks on longer answers for listing three right points with nothing linking them. Or they describe something when the question said explain. The other common cause is the calculations, where a Maths gap shows up looking like a Science one. The first lesson works out which of the two you're dealing with."

Then it's fifteen minutes of fresh exam questions, marked there and then while your child watches."
