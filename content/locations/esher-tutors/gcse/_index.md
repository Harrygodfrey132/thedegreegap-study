faq_1_q: "Esher High doesn't have a sixth form. Does that change how we plan Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "A little, yes. Your child will be applying for a new place around the time of the mocks, so it helps to find out early which grades matter for the courses they like. Esher Sixth Form College, for example, currently asks for at least a 6 in the relevant GCSE for A-Level Maths or the sciences, and restricts the choice for anyone without a 4 in English Language. Each place publishes its own guide, so it's worth checking this year's. If your child hasn't decided yet, start with English Language and Maths, because nearly every option here looks at those."
faq_2_q: "Our daughter is behind in a few subjects already. Can a GCSE tutor still help?"
faq_2_a: "It's rarely too late, though the plan depends on the time left. The tutor starts by finding where things first went wrong, often a topic from Year 8 or 9 rather than anything recent, and rebuilds from there instead of racing through the syllabus. With a year to go there's room to fill gaps properly. With a few months, the focus moves to the topics worth the most marks. Either way, your child gets one person's full attention, which a class of thirty can't always give."
faq_3_q: "Do exam boards really make that much difference?"
faq_3_a: "More than most parents expect. In Maths, AQA, Edexcel and OCR each set three papers, one of them without a calculator, while Eduqas sets two. Every board also words and marks its questions in its own way. In Science, AQA talks about 'required practicals' and Edexcel about 'core practicals'. A single school can mix boards too, using one for Maths and another for English. So we check the board for each subject your child wants help with, and the tutors we suggest will know it."
