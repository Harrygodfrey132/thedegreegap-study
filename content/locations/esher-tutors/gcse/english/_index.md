faqs:
  - q: "Which English grade will sixth forms and colleges around Esher look at?"
    a: "English Language is the one they name. Esher Sixth Form College says that without a 4 in English Language, far fewer of its courses are open, and a student who arrives without one carries on studying it there. Hinchley Wood wants seven GCSEs at 4 or above for its sixth form, and English has to be one of them. At Three Rivers Academy it's five, with English Language included. A-Level English courses may want more. Each place can change its guide from year to year, so check the current one before your child applies."
  - q: "Two friends at different Esher schools can have totally different English books. Does that matter for tutoring?"
    a: "It does. Schools choose their own Literature texts from the exam board's list, so your child might be revising Macbeth while a friend down the road has Romeo and Juliet. The Language papers differ too: AQA, Edexcel, OCR and Eduqas each set their own, with their own extracts and timings. Even the Shakespeare question can look quite different from one board to the next. So we look for someone who already knows your child's board, and ideally the same books, rather than a tutor who'd be reading them for the first time alongside your child."
  - q: "She's always reading. Why isn't that showing in her English mocks?"
    a: "Reading a lot builds vocabulary and confidence, and that does help. But the exam marks something narrower. It wants your child to explain why a writer chose a word or a structure, and a keen reader often writes about the characters as if they're real people instead. Essays can wander, too, because there's so much to say and no plan for what to leave out. The tutor goes through a recent mock essay with your child, picks out the habit that's costing marks, and practises the fix in short timed answers."
  - q: "How do you choose the English tutors?"
    a: "Harry or Joe interview every tutor themselves, and most applicants don't get through. For English, we look closely at how a tutor responds to a teenager's writing. Do they spot what's working before what isn't? Can they say exactly how to improve a paragraph? Some of our English tutors are qualified teachers. Others are graduates or still at university, many of them at Russell Group universities. And any English tutor we suggest has been tutoring for at least two years before they ever reach you."
  - q: "Could I sit in on the first English lesson?"
    a: "Yes, if you'd like to, and tutors are used to it. After that, most parents step back. Teenagers tend to share their writing more freely without a parent at their shoulder, especially in English, where every sentence feels a little personal. You won't be in the dark, though. The tutor sends a short note after each lesson about what they covered and what comes next. And if anything worries you, you can ring us."
