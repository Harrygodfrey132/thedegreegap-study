faq_1_q: "My son moved to a grammar sixth form at 16 and Year 12 has been a shock. Is that the move or the subject?"
faq_1_a: "Usually both, and the move matters more than families expect. His new class was taught from several different GCSE courses, on different boards, and the teacher has to pitch Year 12 at the middle of that. If his old school did not cover a topic the new course assumes, nobody at the new place knows, because nobody there marked his GCSE work. The first session goes back through the Year 12 topics that are not landing and finds which GCSE ideas sit underneath them. It's a known pattern, and it closes faster than the shock suggests."
faq_2_q: "Do we start A-Level tutoring now in Year 12, or wait for the January mocks?"
faq_2_a: "Now, if there is already a subject that feels different from how it felt at GCSE. The predicted grades that go to universities and to JP Morgan's degree apprenticeship applications come out of Year 12 work and the first mocks of Year 13, so work landing this term counts twice: once in the prediction and again in the exam. If you wait for the January mock to confirm what you already suspect, it usually costs a term. A Year 13 start still moves the final grade, and plenty of families do it, but the offers have often been made against a number set earlier."
