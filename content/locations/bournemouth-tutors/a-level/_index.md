faq_1_q: "My son moved to a grammar sixth form at 16 and Year 12 has been a shock. Is that the move or the subject?"
faq_1_a: "Usually a bit of both, and the move often matters more than families expect. His new class has come from several schools, sometimes on different exam boards, so the teacher has to pitch Year 12 at the middle. And if a GCSE topic the new course leans on is shaky for him, nobody there knows yet, because nobody there marked his GCSE work. So the first session goes through the Year 12 topics that aren't landing and finds the GCSE ideas underneath them. We see it a lot. It's more fixable than that first term makes it feel."
faq_2_q: "Do we start A-Level tutoring now in Year 12, or wait for the January mocks?"
faq_2_a: "Now, if one subject already feels different from how it did at GCSE. The predicted grades that universities and employers like JP Morgan see are built on Year 12 work and the start of Year 13. So work that lands this term counts twice: once in the prediction, and again in the exam. If you wait for a January mock to confirm what you already suspect, it usually costs a term. Plenty of families start in Year 13 too, and it still helps with the final grade. But by then, the predicted grade on the application is often already set."
