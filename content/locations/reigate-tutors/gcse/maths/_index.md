  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Neither on its own, since all three papers count equally, but the diagnostic usually shows one bleeding more. The non-calculator paper exposes weak arithmetic and algebra, especially fractions, surds and rearranging. The calculator papers expose multi-step problem solving and careless accuracy, often in the last third of the paper. A tutor marks one of each and compares where the marks went. Most students need both, just in different proportions, and the plan follows those proportions."
  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at where on the paper the marks go, not just the total. Early questions test routine skills, the middle tests multi-step methods, and the final questions mix topics and hide what is being asked. Lots of lost marks early points to gaps in content or arithmetic. Marks lost mostly at the end point to problem solving and exam technique. A tutor can read that pattern from one mock script in about ten minutes and it shapes the first month."
  - q: "Are your GCSE Maths tutors qualified teachers or students?"
    a: "Both, and we match on which fits. The platform includes qualified teachers, Russell Group graduates and some postgraduates who sat the same specification recently. A teacher with years of classroom Maths is often the best fit for a student rebuilding confidence from the ground up. A recent graduate with a Maths or engineering degree often suits a student chasing the last few marks on the hardest questions. We ask which kind of help your child needs before suggesting anyone."
  - q: "Is GCSE Maths tutoring worth the money?"
