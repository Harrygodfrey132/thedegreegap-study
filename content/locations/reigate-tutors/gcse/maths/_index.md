  - q: "Should my child put more work into the calculator papers or the non-calculator one?"
    a: "Neither on its own. There are three papers, one without a calculator and two with, and each counts the same. But one kind usually costs your child more. The non-calculator paper shows up shaky arithmetic and algebra, above all fractions, square roots and rearranging. The calculator papers show up long, multi-step problems and small slips, often in the last third. The tutor marks one of each and compares where the marks went. Most children need both, just in different amounts, and the plan follows that."
  - q: "How do I work out where my child's losing marks in Maths?"
    a: "Look at where the marks went on the paper, not just the total. The early questions test routine skills. The middle ones take a few steps. And the last few mix topics together without saying what's being asked. If your child drops lots of marks early, it's usually gaps in content or arithmetic. If they mostly lose them near the end, it's problem solving and exam technique. A tutor can spot that pattern on one mock paper in about ten minutes, and it shapes the first month."
