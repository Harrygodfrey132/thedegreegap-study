angle_heading: "Why Gravitational and Electric Fields Stump Children Who Think in Pictures"
angle_body_1: "Lots of children get through mechanics and electricity by building a picture in their head. It works, because those topics describe things you can imagine. Fields don't play along. A gravitational field doesn't look like anything. Potential is measured from a zero point that's infinitely far away. And the signs go against instinct, with potential energy negative and rising towards zero. If your child has always relied on picturing things, this is where the method stops working. It usually happens partway through Year 13, when there's not much time left to learn another way."
angle_body_2: "The answer is to work from definitions and relationships instead of pictures. That means knowing exactly how each quantity is defined. It means knowing which graph links to which, through its gradient or the area under it. And trusting the algebra when it disagrees with instinct. It's a real change of method. It helps to call it that, rather than treat your child as suddenly weaker. The first lesson looks at how they tackle an unfamiliar topic now, so the tutor knows which habit to change."
