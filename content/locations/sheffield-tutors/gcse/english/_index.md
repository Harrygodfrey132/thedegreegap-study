faqs:
  - q: "How does GCSE English attainment in Sheffield compare to the national picture?"
    a: "Sheffield sits below the England average at local authority level, but the city average tells you very little about any individual student. The spread inside Sheffield is wide, and the schools sending students to competitive sixth forms sit well above that figure. What matters for your child is the gap between their current English grade and the number the course they want actually asks for. That's a much smaller and more workable distance than a city-wide statistic suggests."
  - q: "When is the best time to start English tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "September to February, before the mocks that shape sixth-form predicted grades. English rewards an early start more than most subjects because the improvements come from habits, planning, structure, writing to time, and habits need weeks of repetition rather than a fortnight of cramming. Starting in April still helps and we take those families on, but the work then narrows to exam technique on the papers rather than rebuilding how a student approaches an essay."
  - q: "Why is my child's English predicted grade lower than we expected?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from mock performance and teacher judgement, and in English the gap parents notice is usually between what a child can discuss out loud and what they produce in 45 minutes on paper. A student who talks well about a text often assumes that's the skill being assessed. It isn't. A tutor's first job is reading recent mock answers to find where the two come apart, then rebuilding performance under timed conditions until the written answer matches what the student actually knows."
  - q: "Are your English tutors qualified teachers or university students?"
    a: "Both, and which one suits depends on the student. Our network includes qualified secondary teachers, Russell Group graduates and postgraduates who sat these papers recently enough to remember the mark scheme in detail. For a student who needs the mark scheme decoded, a recent high-achieving graduate is often sharper on current examiner expectations. For a student who has decided they're bad at English, an experienced teacher usually handles the rebuild better."
  - q: "Can we do English tutoring on a budget?"
    a: "Yes, and the honest advice is usually to spread it differently rather than to buy less of it. One weekly hour through Year 10, with a second added only in the eight weeks before mocks, costs less over the year than two sessions a week throughout and tends to produce more. The other option is a short intensive block, six to eight sessions aimed at one paper, which suits a student who is close to the grade they need and losing marks in one predictable place."
