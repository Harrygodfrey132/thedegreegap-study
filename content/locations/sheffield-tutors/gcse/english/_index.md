What matters is the gap between your child's current English grade and the grade their chosen course asks for. That's usually a much smaller, more workable distance than a city statistic makes it sound. And in English, a lot of that distance is technique, which is very teachable."
  - q: "Is September too early to start English tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "No, September's about right, and anything up to February is good. That's before the mocks that shape sixth-form predicted grades. English rewards an early start more than most subjects, because the gains come from habits: planning, structure, writing to time. Habits take weeks of repetition, not a fortnight of cramming. If you're reading this in April, it still helps and we'll gladly start. The work just narrows to exam technique on the papers, rather than rebuilding how your child approaches an essay."
  - q: "The school's predicted English grade is lower than we hoped. Why?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from mock results and teacher judgement. In English, the gap parents notice is usually between what your child can say out loud and what they write in 45 minutes. A teenager who talks well about a text often assumes that's what's being marked. It isn't. The tutor starts by reading recent mock answers to find where the two come apart. Then they rebuild your child's timed writing until the answer on paper matches what they actually know."
