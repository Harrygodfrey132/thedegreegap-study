faqs:
  - q: "My child wants to do A-Level Maths. What grade will a Sheffield sixth form want?"
    a: "It depends on the sixth form, and it can change from one year to the next, so check this year's page for the one your child's applying to. Don't go on what a friend's older child needed. King Edward VII, Silverdale and High Storrs each publish their own entry grades. A-Level Maths usually comes with a Maths grade of its own on top, and it's often higher than the essay subjects ask for. The bit that catches families out is that Maths gets judged on its own. Strong results elsewhere won't make up for it."
  - q: "How can I see which bits of Maths are costing my child marks?"
    a: "Look at the question numbers on a recent mock, not the total. Higher tier papers get harder as they go. So marks lost early on usually point at arithmetic and method, while marks lost from around question 17 onwards point more at exam technique than knowledge. If the paper's mostly right until the last third and then falls away, it's often timing and stamina. That's a quicker fix than most parents expect."
  - q: "Does my child need a Maths tutor, or would more revision sort it?"
    a: "Their working on a recent past paper will tell you, far more than the hours they've put in. Clean, correct working that still drops marks near the end is an exam technique gap, and that's the quickest thing a tutor fixes. Blank space or crossed-out attempts on multi-step questions are different. That's a lack of confidence with the method, and revision alone rarely shifts it, because it's natural to skip the questions you most need to practise. And if your child's already scoring above 80% in mocks? Then it's targeted practice, not a full programme."
