  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Maths?"
    a: "It turns on where your child is sitting now rather than on where anyone hopes they will finish. Higher tier reaches the top grades but pays very little below its floor, while foundation caps lower and makes the marks available far more reachable. A student near the boundary in Year 10 is often right on higher with focused preparation on the specific topics that decide it. A student well below usually does better on foundation, where questions are winnable and confidence builds. A Year 10 diagnostic makes that a decision instead of a guess, and school should be part of it."
  - q: "Why is my child's Maths predicted grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from mock performance plus a teacher's judgement, and the gap parents notice is usually between what a student can do in a lesson and what they produce in a hall with a clock running. A student who understands every method can still lose two grades to timing, to not showing working, or to freezing on the wordy questions at the back. The first thing a tutor does is read the actual script rather than the grade, because those three causes look identical from the outside and need completely different work."
  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and a number of tutors in the network do this regularly. Timed practice is introduced gradually where exam conditions are the difficulty. Tell us on the consultation call what school has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we match accordingly."
