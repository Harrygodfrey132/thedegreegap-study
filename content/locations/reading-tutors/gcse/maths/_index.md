  - q: "Should my child sit foundation or higher tier Maths?"
    a: "It depends where your child is now, not where everyone hopes they'll finish. Higher tier goes up to a 9, but fall too far below a 4 and there's no grade at all. Foundation stops at a 5, and its marks are far easier to reach. Near the borderline in Year 10, higher is often right, with focused work on the topics that decide it. Well below, foundation usually goes better, because the questions are winnable and confidence builds. A tutor's look at a Year 10 paper turns it into a decision, not a guess. It's worth bringing your child's Maths teacher in on it too."
  - q: "Why does my child's predicted Maths grade look so low?"
    a: "Usually it's the gap between what your child can do in a lesson and what comes out in an exam hall with a clock running. School bases the prediction on the mocks plus the teacher's own judgement, so that gap shows. A child who understands every method can still lose two grades to timing, to not showing working, or to freezing on the wordy questions at the end. From the outside those three look the same. So the tutor reads the actual paper, not the grade, because each one needs different work."
