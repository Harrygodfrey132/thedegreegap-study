faq_1_q: "We're hoping for a sixth-form place at Reading School or Kendrick. How can a GCSE tutor help?"
faq_1_a: "Mostly by working on the question types that cost your child marks, like the longer Science answers or unseen poetry against the clock. Those are hard to drill properly in a class of thirty. It's worth checking this year's booklet first, though, because Reading School and Kendrick each set their own points totals and subject grades, and they change by course. Then the tutor's aiming at the real target, not a guess."
faq_2_q: "Will the tutor know the exam board my child's school has picked?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and we check it before we suggest anyone. AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC each write their own papers, so a tutor who knows your child's board can go straight to the right content from the first lesson. Schools in Reading often pick a different board for each subject, so we match the tutor to the board for that subject, not just the subject itself."
faq_3_q: "My son has quietly switched off from a subject he used to be fine at. Can a GCSE tutor help with that, not just the grade?"
faq_3_a: "Often, yes, and it's usually where the work starts. When a subject gets avoided, it's rarely laziness. Something stopped making sense, the marks dipped, and it felt easier to look away. A tutor finds that moment, rebuilds from it one-to-one, and gives him the small wins that make the next question feel worth a try."
faq_4_q: "Is Year 10 too early to start GCSE tutoring, or Year 11 too late?"
faq_4_a: "Neither. Year 10 gives your child time to rebuild a shaky topic without the clock ticking, which is the calmer way if the gap is in the content. Year 11 works too, and lots of Reading families get in touch after the autumn mocks come back lower than hoped, before the spring papers that firm up the predicted grade. Starting earlier just means less of a rush later."
