angle_body_1: "Sequences and series are often the first place your child meets a question that runs in reverse. The forward version's simple enough: here's the first term and the common difference, now find the twentieth. But the exam prefers it the other way round. It gives the sum of the first ten terms and the sum of the first twenty, then asks for the common difference. That means forming two equations and solving them together. Geometric series add a catch, since a sum to infinity only exists inside a certain range, and that range has to be stated and checked. And the wordy questions wrap it all up in savings or salaries, so your child has to spot the formula before they can use it."

  - q: "How can I find out which Maths topics are losing my child marks?"
    a: "Put their textbook work next to their exam answers on the same topic. If the textbook's fine and the past paper isn't, the gap is reading the question, not the method, and that's a very common one. If the working starts and falls apart halfway, it's the algebra.
