  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE English for most Nottingham teenagers?"
    a: "Usually the 19th-century extract, and it's a reading problem more than an analysis one. The sentences are longer than almost anything else your child reads, the vocabulary is unfamiliar and the punctuation does different jobs. So working out what it says eats the time the questions needed. Unseen poetry comes a close second, for a similar reason. There's nothing to revise, so a hard-working child can feel ambushed. Both get easier with practice alongside someone who reads with them."
  - q: "What's usually behind my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "There are two usual reasons. One is that nobody ever showed them what an exam paragraph should look like, and reading more won't teach that. The other is reading stamina: your child can analyse well but runs out of time because the text itself took too long. On a school report the two look the same, but they need opposite kinds of work. The first lesson is how the tutor tells them apart. Neither is about effort. And with Nottingham families we see the second far more often than people expect."
