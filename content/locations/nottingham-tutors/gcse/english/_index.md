  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Nottingham students?"
    a: "The 19th-century extract, and it is a reading problem rather than an analysis one. The sentences are longer than anything most students meet elsewhere, the vocabulary is unfamiliar and the punctuation does different work, so decoding it eats the time the questions needed. Unseen poetry runs it close for the same reason: there is nothing to revise, so a hard-working student feels ambushed. Both respond to practice with someone reading alongside them."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Two causes account for most of it. The first is that nobody ever taught them the shape of an examinable paragraph, and reading more does not supply it. The second is reading stamina, where a student can analyse well but runs out of time because the text itself took too long. They look identical on a report and need opposite work, which is what the first session is for. Neither is about effort, and in this city we see the second far more often than people expect."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about learning differences when matching rather than waiting for a parent to raise it. Several tutors work regularly with neurodivergent students. Online helps more than families expect: the whiteboard keeps text visible instead of wiping it away, passages can be worked through in chunks at the student's own pace, and because every session is recorded nothing has to be held in memory first time round."
  - q: "What if my child does not want an English tutor?"
    a: "Common, and worth handling gently. With English the resistance is often about the subject rather than the tutor, because it is the one place effort has not obviously paid off. That usually shifts within two or three sessions once someone shows there is a method rather than a knack. The free 15-minute meeting lets your child meet the tutor with nothing committed. If they still do not want to carry on after a session or two, we pause. That is their call."
