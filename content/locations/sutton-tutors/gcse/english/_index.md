An average across dozens of schools says nothing about one student on one paper, and in English the spread inside a single classroom is wide. What helps more is the comparison between your child's last marked answer and the band descriptors for the paper they are entered for. That is what a first session does, and unlike a borough figure it hands you something to act on."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read all the time?"
    a: "Reading builds the raw material and very little of the technique, which is why strong readers still write thin answers. The moment it slips is usually earlier than Year 10, somewhere around Year 8, when essays stop being marked for what you know and start being marked for the argument you build, and no class stops to teach the difference. A student who never learned to plan writes everything they have in the order it occurred to them. Week one works out which of those is happening."
  - q: "Why is my child's English predicted grade lower than we expected?"
    a: "Predictions come from mock performance and teacher judgement, and in English the gap is nearly always between what a student understands in discussion and what reaches the page in ninety minutes. Handwriting speed, planning time, an opening paragraph that circles before it argues: all of it costs marks that knowledge never gets back. Ask school for the marked script rather than the number. It shows where the answer stopped scoring, which makes the next conversation a useful one."
