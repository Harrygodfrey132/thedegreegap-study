faqs:
  - q: "What grade do you need for A-Level Maths at Sutton sixth forms?"
    a: "It varies by school and it moves between years, so read this year's criteria for the sixth form your child actually wants. The pattern here is that Maths asks for more than most subjects, often a grade near the top end, and several of the borough's sixth forms also rank external applicants on overall GCSE points. Sutton Grammar School, Nonsuch and Greenshaw each publish their own version. If the current grade is a way off, twelve to sixteen weeks of weekly work is a realistic window."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or the non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Both decide the grade and they carry equal weight. Paper 1 rewards arithmetic fluency and clean algebra, which is why it exposes a method that was never fully drilled. The calculator papers reward accuracy held across several steps and reading a long question properly, and that is where marks drain at the back of Paper 3. Nearly every student is clearly worse at one of the two. A marked paper shows which in about ten minutes, and the term follows the marks."
  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Maths?"
    a: "That depends on where your child is sitting now, not on where anyone hopes they land. The higher paper carries the top grades and gives very little away underneath its floor. Foundation stops lower but puts far more of the marks within reach, which for some students is the difference between a grade and nothing at all. Schools normally review entry during Year 10. A diagnostic in that window turns it into a decision rather than a guess, and the Maths department should be part of it."
  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about it while matching rather than waiting for it to come up. Much of the practical help is about load: one layout for multi-step working so nothing goes missing, long questions broken into named stages, one method drilled until it is automatic instead of three alternatives offered at once. Lessonspace helps, because the working stays on screen and the recording is there afterwards. Tell us what school has in place already, including any exam access arrangements."
  - q: "Is GCSE Maths tutoring in Sutton actually worth the money?"

Sometimes the honest suggestion is that tutoring is not the thing your child needs right now."
