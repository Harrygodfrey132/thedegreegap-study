    a: "Hertfordshire as a whole sits above the national average, but a county figure hides a lot and Stevenage families sometimes read it and feel their child is further behind than they are. English also tends to be one of the more movable subjects, because a large share of what separates grade 4 from grade 6 is technique that can be taught directly rather than knowledge that has to accumulate slowly."
  - q: "Is Year 10 too early to bring in an English tutor?"
    a: "Year 10 is a good time to start, and for English it may be the best time. The skills that decide the grade, which are planning, evidence selection and writing to time, need repetition before they become automatic, and Year 10 has the space for that in a way Year 11 does not. Year 10 sessions are also less exam-shaped, with more time spent on how to read and structure an argument, which tends to make the subject feel less like a chore by the time Year 11 starts."
  - q: "Does my child need an English tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "For English, more revision is often the least useful answer, because you cannot revise a skill you have not been taught. The test is to look at a recent marked essay. If the student clearly knows the text but the essay describes rather than argues, or stops halfway, that is technique and a tutor is the fastest route. If they cannot recall the plot or the key quotations at all, that is a knowledge gap and some of it can be closed at home. Most students we see are the first kind."
  - q: "How selective are you about who teaches English on the platform?"
    a: "About one in eight applicants makes it onto the platform. The stages are a written application, a subject knowledge test, a structured interview and reference checks. Every tutor has spoken with Harry or Joe before they are put in front of a family."
  - q: "What if my child does not want an English tutor?"
    a: "Very common, and often strongest in English, because handing over your own writing to be marked by a stranger feels more exposing than getting a maths question wrong. Most students settle within two or three sessions once they realise nobody else is watching and the tutor is not marking them against a class.
