faqs:
  - q: "Does the Science grade matter for an apprenticeship at GSK, Airbus or MBDA?"
    a: "For some routes, yes, mostly the technical ones. GSK's research campus, Airbus Defence and Space and MBDA all run apprenticeships in Stevenage, alongside other engineering and life sciences employers, and each publishes its GCSE requirements. Maths and English are on nearly all of them, but Science isn't always. Find the requirements for the route your child wants, then work back from those grades. A tutor can aim at a named grade far more easily than at general improvement."
  - q: "How do I work out where my child's Science marks are going?"
    a: "Look at a recent mock by type of question rather than by topic. If the short recall questions are right but the six-mark answers score two or three, the knowledge is there and the organising isn't. If the practical questions are blank, the method's faded since the lesson in the lab. A sensible start on a calculation and a wrong answer usually means units or rearranging. Each has a different fix and takes a different amount of time. Separating them is most of what the first lesson is for."
  - q: "Should my child be doing extra Science between lessons?"
    a: "Yes, roughly 60 to 90 minutes across the week, spent on questions rather than notes. Two or three sets of past-paper questions on that lesson's topic is about right. Making colourful revision notes is one of the commonest ways to spend hours on Science without the grade moving, so it's worth gently steering your child away from it. The tutor sets specific work each week and marks it at the start of the next lesson. That keeps it honest without you having to police it."
  - q: "How much can a Science tutor realistically move the grade?"
    a: "Where the problem is technique, about one grade band over a term of weekly lessons is a fair target. By technique we mean practical method, how long answers are put together and accuracy in calculations. Where topics from Year 9 and 10 are really missing, it's closer to two terms, because the content has to be rebuilt before technique has anything to work on. We'd rather tell you what's normal than quote a headline number, and nobody can promise a grade. The first lesson shows which of the two you're looking at."
  - q: "What happens if my child misses the Science grade on results day?"
    a: "Talk to the college or sixth form first, then to us. Unlike Maths and English, GCSE Science has no November resit, so the next chance to resit it is the following summer. If a place depended on the Science grade, ring early, because some will talk through the options with you. We work with families through results day and after, and we'll help you decide whether a resit is worth it. If it is, weekly lessons can build steadily towards the summer exams, aiming at the 4 or 5 that many courses and apprenticeships name."
