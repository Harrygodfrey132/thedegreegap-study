faqs:
  - q: "Can a Science tutor help my child reach the grade an apprenticeship asks for?"
    a: "Often yes, though it is worth checking exactly which grades a scheme names, because Science is not always one of them. The engineering, manufacturing and life sciences employers around Stevenage and the wider area run apprenticeship routes with published GCSE requirements, and Maths and English appear on nearly all of them while Science appears on some. Find the specific requirements for the route your child wants, then work backwards from those numbers. A tutor can target a named grade far more efficiently than a general improvement."
  - q: "How can I tell which Science topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Read a recent mock by question type rather than by topic. Short recall questions answered correctly while six-mark responses score two or three points means the knowledge is there and the organisation is not. Blank practical questions mean the method faded after the lab. Calculation questions with a sensible start and a wrong answer usually mean units or rearranging. Each of those has a different fix and a different timescale, and separating them is most of what the first session is for."
  - q: "How much Science work should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Roughly 60 to 90 minutes across the week, spent on questions rather than on notes. Two or three past-paper question sets on the topic from the lesson is the right shape. Making colourful revision resources is the most common way students spend hours on Science without moving the grade, and it is worth gently redirecting. The tutor sets specific work each week and marks it at the start of the next session, which keeps it honest without you having to police it."
  - q: "What grade improvements do your Science tutors typically achieve?"
    a: "We would rather describe what is normal than quote a headline number. Where the issue is technique, so practical method, long-answer structure and calculation accuracy, a grade band over a term of weekly sessions is a realistic target. Where topics from Year 9 and 10 are genuinely missing, it is closer to two terms, because the content has to be rebuilt before technique has anything to work with. The diagnostic in the first session is what tells you which of those you are looking at."
  - q: "What happens if my child misses the Science grade on results day?"
    a: "We work with families through results day and into the November resit window, and this comes up every year, so it is a well-worn path rather than a crisis. An eight-week block from September usually targets a move from grade 3 to 4 or from 4 to 5, which are the thresholds most college courses and apprenticeship routes name. If a sixth-form or college place is conditional on the grade, it is worth calling them early, because plenty will hold a place with a plan in writing."
