  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE Maths when they work so hard?"
    a: "Usually because Maths builds on itself, and hard work can't fill a missing layer. Something didn't quite land a few years back, most often fractions, negative numbers or rearranging. Since then your child's been learning each new topic as steps to memorise, not something that makes sense. That holds up until the questions get long enough to mix topics together. Finding the first gap is the tutor's first job. And most children are relieved to hear it was never about being \"no good at Maths\"."
  - q: "Foundation or higher tier: which will get my child the better grade?"
    a: "If your child's working at around a 3, foundation usually does. GCSE Maths comes in two tiers, or levels of paper. Foundation covers grades 1 to 5, and higher covers 4 to 9, with a 3 possible if a child just misses a 4. On foundation far more of the marks are within reach, and a strong foundation result beats a thin higher one. At a borderline 4 or 5 it's a closer call, and it depends whether the gap is exam technique or older topics. A first lesson in Year 10 gives you something solid to decide with."
