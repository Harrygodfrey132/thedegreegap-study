  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help my child get onto an apprenticeship in Stevenage?"

  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE Maths when they work hard?"
    a: "Because effort cannot compensate for a missing foundation in a subject that builds on itself. The usual pattern is that something did not land properly a few years earlier, most often fractions, negative numbers or rearranging, and every topic since has been learned as a separate procedure to memorise rather than something that makes sense. That works until the questions get long enough to combine topics. Finding the original gap is the tutor's first job, and it is usually a relief for the student to hear it is not a lack of ability."
  - q: "Foundation or higher tier, which gives my child the better result?"
    a: "Foundation runs from grade 1 to 5, higher from 4 to 9. If your child is working around a grade 3, foundation usually produces the better outcome, because the paper is pitched so that the marks are genuinely reachable and a strong foundation result beats a thin higher one. At a borderline 4 or 5 the decision is closer and depends on whether the gap is technique or foundations. A Year 10 diagnostic gives you something concrete to base the decision on rather than a guess."

For Maths in particular, the shared whiteboard helps, since methods stay visible on screen rather than being held in working memory, steps can be broken down as far as they need to be, and the student can go back over the session notes afterwards."
