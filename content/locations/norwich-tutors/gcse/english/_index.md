  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE English for most children in Norwich?"
    a: "Writing enough good answers inside the time, more than understanding the texts. Both Language papers and the Literature papers ask for several long answers in one sitting. The pattern we see most is a strong first answer, then each one after it a little shorter. Schools across Norwich and Norfolk teach different set texts, but that pattern turns up whatever the book. Next hardest is arguing a point rather than describing what happens. That's a habit, not a gap in what your child knows."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Is that too early for English tutoring?"
    a: "No, it's the best time for English, more than for most subjects. The exam tests stamina as well as reading, and stamina comes from writing regularly over months, not from a big push in the spring of Year 11. Starting now means your child walks into the mocks with a paragraph structure that's automatic, not one they're still thinking about. The lessons are calmer too, because nothing's looming. That suits a child who's started to dread English."
