faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Norwich students?"
    a: "Producing enough good writing inside the time, more than understanding the texts. Both Language papers and the Literature papers ask for several developed answers in one sitting, and the commonest script pattern is a strong first answer followed by ones that get visibly shorter. Set texts vary between schools across Norwich and Norfolk, so the particular book matters less than that pattern, which turns up regardless. The second hardest part is writing an argument rather than a description, which is a habit rather than a gap in knowledge."
  - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the best time for English, more so than for most subjects. The exam is an endurance task as well as an academic one, and stamina builds through regular writing over months rather than through intensive work in the spring of Year 11. Starting in Year 10 also means a student arrives at mocks with a paragraph structure that is automatic rather than something they are still thinking about. The sessions are calmer too, because nothing is imminent, which suits students who have begun to dread the subject."
  - q: "How long is each GCSE English tutoring session?"
    a: "Most are sixty minutes, which fits English well: long enough to plan an answer, write it to time, and have it marked in the same sitting, which is where the learning actually happens. Closer to exams some families book ninety minutes so a full paper can be attempted under conditions. For Year 10 students, or where concentration is the limiting factor, forty-five minutes sometimes works better and still does the job if it happens every week. It is worth discussing on the consultation call rather than defaulting."
