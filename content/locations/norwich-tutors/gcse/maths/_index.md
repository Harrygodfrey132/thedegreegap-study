faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE Maths do Norwich teenagers find hardest?"
    a: "Algebra, and not as one topic but as the thread running through everything else. Rearranging equations, algebraic fractions and simultaneous equations turn up inside geometry questions, ratio questions and the wordy problems near the end of a paper. So a weakness there costs marks in places that don't look like algebra at all. Circle theorems and iterative methods come next, and both are more about method than understanding. The fourth, which hardly anyone mentions, is not knowing which formulae are given. It quietly costs time and accuracy on every paper."
  - q: "What's the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE Maths, and does it matter?"
    a: "It matters more than most parents expect, though not because of what's taught. The content is set nationally, so it's much the same whichever board your child sits. What changes is how the questions are worded and how the multi-step problems are put together. A child who's practised on one board's past papers can be thrown by another's phrasing while knowing exactly the same maths. So we confirm the board on the free call before suggesting anyone, and match tutors to it. Knowing a board's habits is worth a surprising amount in the last two terms."
  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Maths lesson?"
    a: "Most lessons are an hour, split three ways. The opening quarter of an hour is last week's work, marked properly so any mistakes come out rather than getting skimmed over. Then about thirty minutes of teaching, aimed at whatever the first lesson's check turned up, with every step written on the shared whiteboard. The last part is exam questions on that topic, marked against the real scheme with your child watching, so they can see where each mark comes from. Next week's work gets set at the end, so it follows straight on."
