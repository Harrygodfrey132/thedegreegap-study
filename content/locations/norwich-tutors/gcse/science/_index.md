faqs:
  - q: "Which bits of GCSE Science trip Norwich children up most?"
    a: "Usually the required practicals first, then the six-mark answers. Practical questions are precise, and a child who didn't really do the experiment has very little to draw on. Reading about it won't fix that. The six-mark answers want a chain of reasoning, one step leading to the next, but most children write a list of facts. After those, rearranging equations in Physics and moles in Chemistry account for most of the other lost marks. All four are about method, not knowledge, which is why they tend to improve fairly quickly."
  - q: "If my child's in Year 11, when should Science tutoring start?"
    a: "Ideally in the autumn term, and certainly before February. The winter mocks feed into predicted grades, and those are what sixth forms and colleges look at when they make offers. So anything fixed before the mocks helps twice: once in the mock, and again in the summer. Starting later still helps, and lots of families do, but the plan narrows to the topics worth the most marks rather than a proper rebuild. Science has one more reason to start early. The practicals are still being taught, and going over them as they happen beats piecing them together later."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Is that too early for a Science tutor?"
    a: "No. In Science it's a better time to start than in most subjects, because the required practicals are spread over both years. With a tutor in Year 10, your child can go over each one soon after doing it at school, before the details blur. Year 10 lessons are calmer too. It's rebuilding method, not drilling papers, which suits a child who's started to decide Science isn't for them. Nothing's looming yet, so the pressure's lower."
  - q: "How do you choose your Science tutors?"
    a: "Harry or Joe interviews every one, and roughly 3% of applicants get through. We check subject knowledge, but that's rarely the sticking point, since most Science applicants know their subject well. What we're really testing is whether they can explain an idea to someone who doesn't get it yet, and tell when it's landed. We watch them do it live. And we take up references before any tutor works with a family."
  - q: "Can my child have Science lessons in the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and a week off school can be the most productive week of the term. Once lessons aren't squeezed in between homework, clubs and everything else, a block over half-term or Easter can go through the practicals, or one weak science, across several days running. Science suits that, because it's taught in chunks. It's all online, so the timing stays flexible, and if you're away from home your child can still keep their normal slot."
