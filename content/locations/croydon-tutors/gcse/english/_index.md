  - q: "What GCSE grades will my child need for A-Level English at a Croydon sixth form?"
    a: "Usually a set of 5s plus a 6 in English, though each sixth form sets its own. The Harris sixth forms ask for seven 5s with a 6 in the subject. Archbishop Tenison's wants 5s in English Language and Maths, with a 6 in each subject your child takes on. Coulsdon asks for 4s in English and Maths plus its own course grade, and Whitgift and Trinity sit higher, at six 7s. They all change their numbers between years, so check the current ones. Going from a 5 to a secure 6 usually takes twelve to sixteen weeks of marked essays. That's why the autumn matters."
  - q: "My child's a keen reader. Why is English still such a struggle?"
    a: "Because reading isn't what's being marked. The exam rewards a certain kind of writing. The shift usually comes in Year 8 or 9, when class work moves from 'find the quote' to 'argue a point'. A planned argument takes a lot of practice with feedback, and in a full class there's rarely time to go through it one-to-one. So a keen reader can understand a text completely and still hand in three sentences that retell it. On the mark scheme that's a 'some' answer, however much they've read. The tutor's first job is finding which step of the argument is missing, and it's often the same one."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia or ADHD. Can a GCSE English tutor still help?"

Timed writing comes in short pieces before a full answer. Tell us what school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements like extra time, and we'll match with that in mind."
