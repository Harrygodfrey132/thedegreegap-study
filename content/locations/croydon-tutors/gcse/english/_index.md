faqs:
  - q: "What grade do you need for A-Level English at Croydon sixth forms?"
    a: "The common pattern across the borough is a general bar of grade 5s plus a 6 in English. The Harris sixth forms at South Norwood and Invictus ask for seven 5s with a 6 in the relevant subject, and Archbishop Tenison's expects 5s in English Language and Maths with a 6 in each subject taken on. Coulsdon sets grade 4s in English and Maths plus its own course requirement, and Whitgift and Trinity sit higher again, at six 7s. Every one revises its numbers between years. From a 5 to a secure 6 is usually twelve to sixteen weeks of marked essays, which is why the autumn matters."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read all the time?"
    a: "Because reading isn't what's being marked. The exam rewards a specific kind of writing, and the moment it usually went missing is Year 8 or 9, when the class moved from 'find the quote' to 'argue a point' without anyone teaching what a planned argument looks like. A keen reader can understand a text completely and still hand in three sentences that retell it. On the descriptor that's a 'some' answer however much they've read. The tutor's first job is finding which step of the argument never got taught, and it's nearly always the same one."
  - q: "Can a GCSE English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and a number of tutors on the platform work with neurodivergent students most weeks. We ask about it when we match. In English the practical help is structure: a fixed paragraph shape written on the shared whiteboard so the plan doesn't have to be held in working memory, one band word worked on at a time, and timed writing introduced in short pieces before a full answer. Lessonspace suits this because the plan stays visible on screen. Tell us on the call what school has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we match to that."
