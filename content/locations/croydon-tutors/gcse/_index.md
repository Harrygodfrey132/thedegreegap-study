faq_1_q: "My daughter is in Year 10 at a school with its own sixth form. Is there any point starting now?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10 is the better time, and having a sixth form upstairs doesn't change that. Students who stay on still meet the published bar, and the subject grade is usually the harder half of it. In Year 10 the gaps are small enough to close properly, because the Year 11 content hasn't stacked on top yet. Start in Year 11 and the work narrows to past papers and technique, which still helps but teaches less from the beginning. And a Year 10 mock is the first honest look at which subject needs the hour."
faq_2_q: "The gap is in two subjects and the mock is in November. Which one gets the hour?"
faq_2_a: "The one that gates the next step. If the sixth form wants a 6 in Chemistry and Chemistry is on a 4, that's the hour, and English can wait a term unless it's under the general bar too. We work this out on the call, with the prospectus open. Sometimes the answer is a short block in one subject and then a switch. What we won't do is split the hour in half, because thirty minutes a week moves neither grade and leaves everyone frustrated by Christmas."
faq_3_q: "Everything is online. Does that actually work for a fifteen-year-old?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and for most of them it works better than being driven across Croydon on a school night. Sessions run over Lessonspace, our shared whiteboard: the tutor and your child write on the same screen, the working stays visible, and the whole hour is recorded so the method can be replayed before a mock. There's no one else in the room, which is the point. A student who won't ask a question in a class of thirty will usually ask it here by week two."
