faq_1_q: "My daughter is in Year 10 at a school with its own sixth form. Is there any point starting now?"
faq_1_a: "Yes. Year 10's the better time, and a sixth form upstairs doesn't change that. Children who stay on still have to meet the published bar, and the subject grade is usually the harder half of it. In Year 10 the gaps are small enough to close properly, because the Year 11 content hasn't piled on top yet. Start in Year 11 and the work narrows to past papers and technique, which still helps but can't rebuild as much. And a Year 10 mock is the first honest look at which subject needs the hour."
faq_2_q: "The gap is in two subjects and the mock is in November. Which one gets the hour?"
faq_2_a: "The one that decides the next step. If the sixth form wants a 6 in Chemistry and Chemistry's on a 4, that's the hour, and English can wait a term unless it's under the general bar too. We'll work this out together on the call, with the prospectus open. Sometimes the answer's a short block in one subject and then a switch. What we won't do is split the hour in half. Thirty minutes a week moves neither grade and leaves everyone frustrated by Christmas."
