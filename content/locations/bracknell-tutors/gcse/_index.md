faq_1_q: "When do Bracknell Year 11s apply for sixth form, and what goes on the form?"
faq_1_a: "In the autumn term, well before the exams. Ranelagh's 2027 guide asks its Year 11s to apply by 2 December, and Edgbarrow opens applications for pupils from other schools in October, asking their current school to confirm predicted grades. Offers are conditional, so the real results still decide it in August. But the prediction shapes which courses your child is offered, which is why the summer of Year 10 is a sensible time to start."
faq_2_q: "My child is at Sandhurst School, which stops at 16. Does that change how we plan?"
faq_2_a: "A little. Your child will apply as an outside student wherever they go, which usually means sending predicted grades confirmed by Sandhurst and, for Ranelagh, a school reference too. Edgbarrow, for example, meets outside applicants in the spring term before making conditional offers, and Bracknell and Wokingham College is another route. We'd suggest picking two or three likely sixth forms early in Year 11, checking what each asks for in your child's subjects, and letting the tutor work back from there."
faq_3_q: "My child fell behind after changing schools in Year 9. Can they still catch up before their GCSEs?"
faq_3_a: "Usually, yes, if you start soon. The tutor won't race through the whole course again. They'll look at your child's books and recent tests, find the topics that were taught before they arrived, and rebuild those first, because later work often depends on them. Once the foundations are back, lessons move on to exam questions under time. Starting in Year 10 gives the most room, but Year 11 still leaves enough time for a lot of progress."
