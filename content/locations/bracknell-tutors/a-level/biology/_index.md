  - q: "What's the hardest part of A-Level Biology for Bracknell sixth formers?"
    a: "For most, it's the answers that link topics, more than any single topic. On AQA, which Ranelagh teaches, Paper 3 includes an essay drawing on the whole course, and the longer questions on the other papers reward the same joined-up thinking. After that, students tend to struggle with the statistics and the required practical questions. Topics like photosynthesis and the nervous system are dense, but they're learnable. Linking them under time is the harder skill."
  - q: "How can I tell which A-Level Biology topics my Bracknell sixth former is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at a recent marked paper, ideally a mock, and see where the marks went. If short recall questions are fine but long answers score half, it's linking and structure, not knowledge. If the trouble sits in data and graph questions, it's the maths and the practical skills. And if marks are scattered everywhere, some content needs re-teaching. A tutor does this sort in the first lesson, so the plan starts from evidence rather than guesswork."
