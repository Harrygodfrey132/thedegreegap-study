faqs:
  - q: "Which parts of A-Level Economics trip up Bracknell students who've never studied it before?"
    a: "Mostly the things GCSE never asked for. Diagrams come first: knowing which curve shifts, which way, and labelling it all, every time. Then evaluation, which means weighing a point up instead of just making it, and that's where much of the mark sits in the long essays. Data questions catch out students who describe the figures without explaining them. At Ranelagh, which sits Edexcel, Paper 3 brings micro and macro together, so any gaps from Year 12 come back in Year 13."
  - q: "Why is my child's predicted Economics grade lower than we hoped?"
    a: "Usually it's timed essays rather than understanding. Predicted grades lean on tests and mocks, and plenty of students who follow every lesson run out of time, or never get past analysis, once the clock is running. A tutor reads two or three of your child's marked essays to see which it is. Then they practise planning in five minutes, and writing evaluation that answers 'how much' and 'for whom', a little every week."
  - q: "How much Economics work should my Bracknell sixth former do between lessons?"

Past that, extra hours tend to help less and tire your child more. The tutor marks the work at the start of the next lesson, so your child sees straight away what earned marks and what didn't."
