faq_1_q: "Ranelagh sits Edexcel for A-Level Maths but AQA for the sciences. Does the exam board really matter for tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, more than most parents expect. The core content overlaps, but each board words its questions differently, weights topics differently and rewards a slightly different style of answer, and that shows most in the long questions. Practising another board's papers isn't wasted, but it's slower. So we ask about each subject separately on the call and only suggest tutors who know that board. If you're not sure, the board is printed on the front of your child's mock papers."
faq_2_q: "How do Year 12 results feed into my child's UCAS predicted grades?"
faq_2_a: "Fairly directly. Teachers usually write predicted grades early in Year 13, before UCAS applications go off, and they lean heavily on Year 12 tests and the end-of-year exams. So a shaky Year 12 can leave a prediction below the offers your child wants to apply for. A tutor can't change a prediction, but they can work on the performance it's based on, which is why a lot of families start in the spring of Year 12 rather than waiting for Year 13."
