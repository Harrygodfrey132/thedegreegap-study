faqs:
  - q: "Which bit of GCSE English trips up Walton-on-Thames students most?"
    a: "Poetry, more often than not. On AQA Literature, which Three Rivers teaches, your child has to compare two poems from the anthology, then write about a poem they've never seen. Both reward close reading under time pressure, and both fall apart without a plan. The other common one is the creative writing at the end of Language Paper 1, which needs proper time but usually gets the leftovers. A tutor can practise both until the routine is automatic."
  - q: "Does it matter whether my child's English is AQA or another board?"
    a: "Yes, mostly for Literature. Boards set different poetry and arrange the papers differently, so the comparison your child practises depends on the board. AQA students compare poems from a cluster such as Love and Relationships, while an iGCSE or Edexcel student will have a different anthology and different question styles. The skills overlap, but the details win marks. We confirm the board and the set texts on the first call."
  - q: "My child reads loads but still gets 4s and 5s in English. Why?"
    a: "Because reading widely helps, but the exam rewards something narrower. Literature marks come from quotations known by heart and analysed closely, and from answering the exact question asked. Language marks depend a lot on timing and on writing that's planned. Plenty of keen readers write long, thoughtful answers that drift away from the question. A tutor shows your child what each mark band looks for, then practises it against the clock."
