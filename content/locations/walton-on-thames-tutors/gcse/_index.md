faq_1_q: "Should we start now, or wait and see how the November mocks go?"
faq_1_a: "Start before, if you can. A few lessons ahead of the mocks give the tutor a picture of how your child works, so the result tells you something instead of coming as a shock. Starting straight afterwards works well too, because a marked mock is the best map a tutor can have: it shows which questions cost the most. A January start, with spring mocks only weeks away, leaves less time to fix older gaps. Even then, the tutor would put the time into the few topics worth the most marks."
faq_2_q: "Our children's schools use different exam boards. Does the tutor need to know which?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it's one of the first things we ask. Most of the content overlaps. The papers don't. English Literature boards set different poems, so a Three Rivers pupil comparing poems from the AQA Love and Relationships cluster needs a tutor who knows that cluster well. AQA and Edexcel also lay out their Science and Maths papers differently. So each child gets a tutor matched to their own board and tier, even when a brother and sister are on different ones."
faq_3_q: "What should my child actually do in the weeks before a mock?"
faq_3_a: "Past paper questions, done properly, more than rereading notes. Two or three short timed sets a week on the topics losing most marks, then marked against the mark scheme, is plenty alongside school homework. Flashcards help with definitions and quotations. Long evenings copying out a textbook mostly don't. The tutor sets that practice between lessons and goes through it at the start of the next one, so mistakes get caught while they're still fresh."
faq_4_q: "My daughter's nervous about a stranger on a screen. How does the first meeting work?"
faq_4_a: "Gently. Before anything is booked, she has a free 15-minute video call with the tutor, and you're welcome to sit beside her. It's a chance to say hello and ask a question or two, nothing more. Plenty of quieter children find one adult on a screen easier than a classroom, because nobody else is listening.
