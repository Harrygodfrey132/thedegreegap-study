faq_1_q: "My child's at Three Rivers. Do the end-of-Year-12 exams really matter that much?"
faq_1_a: "Yes. Three Rivers' course guides for Maths, Biology, Chemistry and Physics all say students are assessed at the end of the first year to check they're ready for the second. What happens after a weak result is the school's call, so it's worth asking your child's teachers how they use it. Those same exams often inform the predicted grades written in Year 13 as well. So the useful time to start is the autumn or early spring of Year 12, while there's room to rebuild rather than cram."
faq_2_q: "Three Rivers and Esher use different exam boards. Does that change the tutoring?"
faq_2_a: "For some subjects it does. A-Level Maths is Pearson Edexcel at both, and Physics is AQA at both. But Chemistry is AQA at Three Rivers and OCR at Esher Sixth Form College, while Biology and Economics are AQA at one and Edexcel at the other. The science and the economics underneath are much the same. The papers, the practicals and the long questions aren't. We confirm your child's board on the first call and match the tutor to it."
faq_3_q: "Our son's predicted grades go on his UCAS form next autumn. What can we do before then?"
faq_3_a: "Use Year 12. Predicted grades usually draw on the end-of-year exams, early Year 13 work and the teacher's view of your son, so steady progress over the next few months counts. A tutor reads his recent tests to find the topics losing most marks, then works on those and on answering against the clock. If the gap is in one subject, one lesson a week is often enough. Help with the personal statement is there too, when he's ready to start writing it."
faq_4_q: "We're not sure our daughter needs a tutor at all. How would we know?"
faq_4_a: "Look at a recent marked test rather than the report. If her working is clear but marks slip away on the longer questions, it's often technique, and that can move fairly quickly. If whole questions are blank or crossed out, there's a gap in understanding worth tackling soon. And if she's comfortably on target, she may just need a revision plan, which we'd tell you on the free call. If you do go ahead, lessons are from £37 an hour with no contract."
