faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Tamworth students?"
    a: "The unseen texts on the Language papers, and the analysis paragraph in Literature. Unseen extracts remove the safety net of revision, so a student who has learned quotes thoroughly can still stall when the material is new, and the fix is a repeatable reading method rather than more content. In Literature the marks sit in the two sentences after the quotation, where a student is expected to say what the writer's choice does rather than what it is. Both are technique, which is why they move faster than parents expect once they are named."
  - q: "How do you work out what is going wrong in English?"
    a: "By reading the student's own writing rather than the report. The first session is a diagnostic: an essay written or brought in, then marked line by line against the actual band descriptors while the student watches. Ideas that never get developed, quotations dropped without comment, an argument that changes direction halfway through and a conclusion that repeats the introduction are four different problems with four different fixes, and a single grade hides all of them. From session two the plan is built around whichever one is costing the most."
  - q: "How much homework should my child do between English sessions?"
    a: "One piece of writing a week, and it does not need to be a full essay. A single well-built analysis paragraph that gets marked properly is worth more than three essays nobody reads, because the improvement comes from seeing what a mark scheme does with your own sentences. In Literature we usually add short quotation work, five minutes a day rather than an hour on a Sunday. Most tutors set the work at the end of the session so it lines up with what was just taught."
