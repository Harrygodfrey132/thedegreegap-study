faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English costs Burton upon Trent teenagers the most marks?"
    a: "Timing, more than any single question. Students spend too long on the reading section of the Language papers and rush the writing at the end, which on AQA carries as many marks as the reading. In Literature, the unseen poem and remembering quotations for a closed-book exam cause the most worry. A tutor works on all of it, but usually starts with timing, because it's the quickest thing to improve."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read so much at home?"
    a: "Because reading well and writing an exam answer are different skills. Plenty of keen readers have never been shown how to make a point and back it with a quotation in a few minutes. Others write beautifully but drift away from the question. The tutor's first job is reading some of your child's marked work to see which it is, then teaching that one skill directly, with short timed practice each week."
  - q: "Between English lessons, how much should my child be practising?"
    a: "About an hour, sometimes a little more. Usually that's one timed piece, a Literature paragraph or a short piece of writing, which the tutor marks before the next lesson. Learning a handful of quotations each week helps too. Much beyond 90 minutes tends to crowd out other subjects without adding a great deal."
  - q: "My son has dyslexia. Can a GCSE English tutor help him?"
    a: "Yes. Mention it on the call, and the tutors we suggest will be ones who've taught dyslexic teenagers before. One-to-one, the tutor can break a long answer into small steps, use planning frames on the whiteboard and give him time to get ideas down without a room watching. Each lesson is recorded, so he can rewatch a method rather than rely on notes. It's worth asking school about exam access arrangements as well."
  - q: "My child didn't get a grade 4 in English. Can a tutor help with the resit?"
    a: "Yes, and plenty of students pass the second time with the right focus. There's a November resit window for English Language as well as the summer exams, and a block of weekly lessons in the autumn can work towards it. The tutor starts with the paper itself, finding where the marks slipped, often the writing section, rather than reteaching the whole course. If your child has started college, lessons fit around that timetable."
