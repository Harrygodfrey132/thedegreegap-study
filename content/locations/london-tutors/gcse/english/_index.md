faqs:
  - q: "Where do London children usually lose marks in GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually the unseen poem and the comparison question, more than the set texts. Both ask your child to build an argument about something they can't revise in advance, so a method helps far more than memory. The other big loss is the writing paper, where marks for accuracy get treated as an afterthought. Set texts vary a lot between London schools. But those three sore spots turn up almost everywhere."
  - q: "When's the best time in Year 11 to start English tutoring?"
    a: "Between September and February, before the spring mocks, which many sixth forms look at when they make offers. English needs a method that's practised over and over, not learnt once. So the earlier the work on structure starts, the more papers your child writes before it counts. Starting in April or May still helps. The plan just narrows to exam technique and timing, rather than rebuilding how essays are put together."
  - q: "Can the same tutor help with both Language and Literature?"
    a: "Usually, yes, and most families prefer it that way. The two share skills but test different things. Language is about handling a passage your child's never seen, under time pressure. Literature is about arguing from evidence they've learnt. A tutor covering both will normally keep them in separate parts of the lesson rather than blending them. And if your child's much weaker in one, we'd rather put more of the hours there than split them evenly for the sake of it."
  - q: "Who are your English tutors, and how are they picked?"
    a: "Every tutor goes through an interview with Harry or Joe, and only about 3% of people who apply get through. Knowing English well gets them in the door. What decides it is whether they can explain why one paragraph scores and a very similar one doesn't, clearly, to a tired teenager on a Tuesday evening. All of them are graduates of top Russell Group universities, and many are qualified teachers."
  - q: "Is English tutoring really worth the money?"
    a: "It depends on the gap, and we'll say which it is on the call. If your child understands the texts but can't turn that into marks, the work is technique, and it tends to move fairly quickly. If they're already writing well and sitting at their target, the gain will be smaller. The free call with Harry or Joe is there to give you that answer. Sometimes it's that tutoring isn't what your child needs right now."
