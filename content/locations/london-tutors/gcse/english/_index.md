faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for London students?"
    a: "Unseen poetry and the comparison question, more often than the set texts. Both ask a student to build an argument on material they cannot revise in advance, which rewards a method rather than recall. The other consistent loss is the writing paper, where AO6 marks for accuracy get treated as an afterthought. Across London the set texts vary a lot between schools, but those three pressure points come up almost everywhere."
  - q: "When is the best time to start GCSE English tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "September to February, before the March mocks that most sixth forms use for conditional offers. English rewards a method that has to be practised repeatedly rather than learned once, so the earlier the structure work starts, the more papers a student writes before it counts. An April or May start still helps, though the plan narrows to exam technique and timing rather than rebuilding how essays are put together."
  - q: "Can one tutor cover both English Language and English Literature?"
    a: "Usually yes, and most families want that. The two share skills but test different things: Language is about handling an unseen source under time pressure, Literature about arguing from memorised evidence. A tutor covering both will normally split the sessions rather than blend them. Where a student is much weaker in one, we would rather weight the hours towards it than split them evenly for the sake of it."
  - q: "How do you vet your GCSE English tutors?"

What decides it is whether someone can explain why one paragraph scores and a similar-looking one does not, clearly, to a tired teenager on a Tuesday evening. Tutors are graduates of top Russell Group universities, and many are qualified teachers."
  - q: "Is GCSE English tutoring actually worth the money?"
    a: "It depends on the gap, and we will say so on the call. Where a student understands the texts but cannot convert that into marks, the work is technique and the movement tends to come quickly. Where a student is already writing well and sitting at their target, the honest answer is that the marginal gain is smaller. The consultation with Harry or Joe is built to give you that answer, including when tutoring is not what your child needs."
