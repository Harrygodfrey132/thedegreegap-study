angle_body_1: "Literature is the one your child can prepare for. The texts are known, the quotes can be learned, and the hours they put in show up on the paper. Language is different. The extracts are ones they've never seen, so there's no text to learn in advance. And their own writing is marked on how well it's written, not on what it's about. So the two reward different children. The pattern we see most in Southampton is a child who's secure enough on the set texts. Then they lose ground on Language: reading a long non-fiction extract at speed, answering the comparison question, and writing something good in whatever time is left."

  - q: "Why won't my child's English grade move?"
    a: "Usually it's the essay plan, or the lack of one. Your child starts writing and hopes the argument turns up. It normally does, a few paragraphs in, after a stretch of retelling the story that scores very little. On the Language paper the same thing shows up in the reading. Your child reads the extract as if for pleasure, runs short of time and writes the answer in a rush. Both are fixable. Neither is about ability."
  - q: "Is it too late to start English tutoring partway through Year 11?"
    a: "No, there's no cliff edge, though it helps less after February. The spring mocks are where many sixth-form and college predicted grades come from, so work done before them counts for more. English also needs a longer run-up than most subjects. Changing how your child plans, reads and paces takes a few weeks to settle, and then it has to be practised against the clock. Starting in April or May is still worth it. The plan just narrows to the few techniques that pay back fastest."
