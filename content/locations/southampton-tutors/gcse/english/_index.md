    a: "Southampton as a local authority has historically sat a little below the national average at GCSE, with a wide spread across the city. That figure describes an area, not a child, and it is worth being careful with it, because it tells you nothing about what any individual student can reach. English is also one of the subjects that moves most once technique is worked on directly, since a large share of what separates a grade 4 from a grade 6 is method rather than accumulated knowledge."
  - q: "What is usually behind a stuck English grade?"
    a: "The most common cause we find is that nobody explicitly taught them how to build an essay, so they learned to start writing and hope the argument arrives. It usually does arrive, several paragraphs in, after a stretch of description that scored very little. On the Language paper the equivalent problem is reading strategy: the student reads the extract as though reading for pleasure, runs short of time, and writes the answer under pressure. Both are fixable and neither is about ability."
  - q: "Is there a point in Year 11 after which English tutoring stops helping?"
    a: "There is no cliff edge, but the return shrinks noticeably after February. The spring mocks are what sixth-form and college predicted grades come from, so work done before them counts for more. English needs more runway than most subjects, because changing how a student plans, reads and paces takes a few weeks to bed in and then has to be practised against a clock. A start in April or May is still worth doing, but the plan narrows to the few techniques with the highest yield."
  - q: "Do you match differently for a student with dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes, and it is worth telling us during the consultation call so we can match on it properly rather than treating it as a detail. A number of tutors on the platform work regularly with neurodivergent students and some specialise in it. English is often where processing and organisation differences are most exposed, because the subject demands sustained writing under time pressure. The shared whiteboard helps, since structures can stay on screen as scaffolds and the session notes can be revisited afterwards."
  - q: "What if my child does not get the English grade they needed?"
    a: "We work with families through results day and the November resit window, so this is familiar ground rather than an emergency. English and Maths are the two subjects where resits are most common, and an eight-week block from September typically targets the move from a grade 3 to a 4 or a 4 to a 5. Where a college or sixth-form place was conditional on the grade, it is worth ringing them quickly, since many will hold a place if there is a plan in place."
