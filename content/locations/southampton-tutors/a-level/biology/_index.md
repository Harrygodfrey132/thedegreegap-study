faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of A-Level Biology for Southampton students?"
    a: "Two things, and it is nearly always the same two. The first is the extended synoptic questions, which ask students to connect topics studied a year apart, so a strong grasp of each individual module is not enough on its own. The second is the application questions built on unfamiliar contexts, where the biology is known but the student has never seen the organism or the experiment before. Both reward practice on unseen material rather than more revision of the specification, which is the opposite of most students' instinct."
  - q: "Why is my child's A-Level Biology predicted grade lower than expected?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from internal assessments and teacher judgement, and in Biology the usual gap is between how much a student knows and how well they perform on unseen application questions under time pressure. A student can revise conscientiously, know the content genuinely well, and still write answers that describe rather than explain. Reading recent scripts is the fastest way to find it, and the pattern is often visible in the command words rather than the biology itself."
  - q: "How long is each A-Level Biology session?"
    a: "Most run to 60 minutes, which suits weekly work through Year 12. In Year 13 a lot of students move to 90 minutes, because the synoptic questions take longer to work through properly and stopping halfway through a long answer is unhelpful. Some families run a 60-minute session most weeks and a longer one before mocks. It is agreed with the tutor rather than fixed by us, and can change during the year as the pressure shifts."
  - q: "What grade improvements have your A-Level Biology tutors delivered?"
    a: "We describe what is typical rather than promise a number, which matters more at A-Level where families are often working towards a conditional offer. Where the gap is application and exam technique, a grade band across two terms of weekly sessions is a realistic target, and a two-band move is possible where the starting point is well below the student's actual understanding. Where content is genuinely missing across modules, it takes longer and we will say so at the consultation rather than after a term."
  - q: "Is A-Level Biology tutoring actually worth the money?"
