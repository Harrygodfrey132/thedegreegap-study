faqs:
  - q: "Where does A-Level Biology usually go wrong for Southampton teenagers?"
    a: "Two things, and it's nearly always the same two. The first is the long synoptic questions, which ask your child to connect topics studied a year apart, so knowing each topic well isn't enough on its own. The second is questions that put familiar biology in an unfamiliar setting. Your child knows the biology but has never seen that organism or that experiment before. Both reward practice on unseen material, not more rereading of the course. That's the opposite of what most teenagers instinctively do."
  - q: "Why is my child's predicted Biology grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Usually because of the gap between what your child knows and how they do on unseen questions against the clock. Predicted grades come from school tests and teachers' judgement. A child can revise carefully, really know the content, and still write answers that describe rather than explain. A tutor reading a few recent tests is the quickest way to find it. Often the pattern shows up in the command words, not the biology itself."
  - q: "How long is each lesson?"
    a: "Most lessons are an hour, which suits weekly work through Year 12. In Year 13 plenty of families move to 90 minutes, because the synoptic questions take longer to work through properly, and stopping halfway through a long answer doesn't help. Some have an hour most weeks and a longer lesson before mocks. You agree it with the tutor, not us, and it can change through the year as the pressure shifts."
