It's a real help for Maths, Physics, Engineering or Computer Science degrees, and some universities ask for it. But most courses don't need it, and a strong grade in three subjects is usually worth more than a weaker one in four."
  - q: "My child was top of the class in GCSE Maths. Why is the A-Level prediction so low?"
    a: "Because A-Level Maths rewards different things. GCSE questions mostly tell your child what to do; A-Level ones often don't, and the algebra has to stay quick and accurate across a whole page. Predicted grades come from timed assessments, so a student who follows everything in class can still score lower against the clock. The tutor's first step is a recent paper, to see whether the marks went on choosing a method, on algebra slips, or on time."

And if your child is already scoring highly, a few lessons on proof and the hardest questions can be plenty."
