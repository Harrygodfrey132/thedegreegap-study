faq_1_q: "She's already getting A grades. Would a tutor actually add anything?"
faq_1_a: "Less than for someone two grades lower, and sometimes it isn't worth it. It earns its place when the offer really does need an A*. That step is about judgement, not knowledge, and a busy class rarely has time for line-by-line feedback on it. For someone already doing well, a short block before the mocks usually beats a whole year of lessons. We'll tell you on the call, even if the answer is that she doesn't need us."
faq_2_q: "Can my child's predicted grade be changed once it's set?"
faq_2_a: "Sometimes, but only before the school sends off the UCAS reference. There's no formal appeal, because it's the school's professional judgement, not an exam result. What can work is a calm conversation with the subject teacher, backed by evidence: a recent paper that scored higher, or proof that a weakness from the mock has gone. What doesn't work is arguing about effort, or about the university your child has set their heart on."
faq_3_q: "Our school picked particular modules. Will the tutor know them?"
faq_3_a: "Yes. We'll ask about them before we suggest anyone. At A-Level, two people taking the same subject can sit different option papers, so the subject name on its own doesn't tell us enough. Bring the board and the options to the first call. If a tutor starts on the wrong option, the first couple of weeks go on catching up, and you'd be paying for those weeks."
