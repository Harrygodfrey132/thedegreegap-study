faq_1_q: "She is already sitting on A grades. Would a tutor add anything?"
faq_1_a: "Honestly, less than for a student two bands below, and sometimes it is not worth it. It earns its place when the target really does need the top band, since A to A* turns on judgement rather than knowledge, and judgement is the thing a classroom almost never gives feedback on. For a strong student, a tight block before mocks usually beats a programme stretched over a year. We will say so on the call, including if the answer is that she does not need us."
faq_2_q: "Can a predicted grade be changed?"
faq_2_a: "Sometimes, and only before the UCAS reference is submitted. There is no formal appeal, because it is a professional judgement by the school rather than a result. What works is a narrow, evidenced conversation with the subject teacher: a recent paper marked at a higher standard, a specific weakness in the mock that demonstrably is not there now. What does not work is an argument about effort or about the university your child has set their heart on."
faq_3_q: "Do you cover the specific modules the school teaches?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and we check them before matching anyone. At A-Level two students on the same subject can sit different option papers, so the subject name alone is not enough. Bring the board and the option choices to the first call. A tutor working from the wrong option spends the first fortnight catching up, and that is a fortnight you have paid for."
