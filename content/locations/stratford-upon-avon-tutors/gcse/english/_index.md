faqs:
  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE English for children in Stratford-upon-Avon?"
    a: "The Shakespeare question, and usually not for the reason you'd expect. Children here tend to know the plays, and knowing them well leads to retelling, which earns almost nothing. The examiner wants an argument about how the language works in the printed extract, linked to where that idea sits in the play as a whole. Next comes the unseen poem. Reading more won't prepare your child for it. It needs a method, practised every week."
  - q: "Where exactly is my child dropping marks in English, and how do I find out?"
    a: "Ask to see the marks question by question, not just the overall grade, because English hides its problems in the total. Your child might be strong on the extract questions and lose most of the writing section to timing, and the two average out into a middling grade that tells you nothing. One marked paper usually shows the pattern. If the last section of every paper is thin or unfinished, that's timing, not ability. And it's one of the quicker things to fix."
  - q: "How much English should my child be doing between lessons?"
    a: "About sixty to ninety minutes a week, split up rather than done in one go. Most tutors set one timed essay, which needs a quiet stretch, plus ten minutes of quotation practice on most other days. The split matters. The essay builds the shape of an answer and the daily practice builds recall, and cramming both into a Sunday afternoon usually does neither. Beyond about ninety minutes a week, the extra effort stops showing in the marks."
