faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Stratford-upon-Avon students?"
    a: "The Shakespeare question, and usually for the opposite reason parents expect. Students in this town tend to know the plays, and knowing them encourages retelling, which the mark scheme pays almost nothing for. The examiner wants an argument about how the language works in the printed extract, tied to where that idea sits in the play as a whole. Behind it comes the unseen poetry question, which cannot be prepared for by reading and needs a method practised weekly instead."
  - q: "How can I tell which English topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Ask for the question-by-question marks rather than the overall grade, because English hides its problems in the total. A student can be strong on the extract questions and lose the whole writing section to timing, and the two averages out into a middling grade that explains nothing. The pattern is usually visible on one marked paper. If the last section of every paper is thin or unfinished, that is timing, not ability, and it is one of the quicker things to fix."
  - q: "How much English should be happening between the weekly sessions?"
    a: "Sixty to ninety minutes, split rather than done in one sitting. Most tutors set one timed essay, which wants a clear stretch of quiet, plus daily quotation work in ten-minute pieces across the rest of the week. The split is the point. The essay builds the answer shape and the daily repetition builds recall, and putting both into a Sunday afternoon usually delivers neither. Past about ninety minutes a week the extra effort stops appearing in the marks."
