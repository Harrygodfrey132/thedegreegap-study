faq_1_q: "My daughter's mid-table at a grammar school. Should I be worried?"
faq_1_a: "Often not. Her GCSE grades are measured against the grade boundaries, not against her classmates, so where she sits in the year tells you less than it feels like. What matters is the grade the course she wants next asks for, which is a number, not a ranking. Ask her school which questions she loses most marks on, and how close her last mock was to the next grade up. Those two answers will tell you whether there's anything worth doing."
faq_2_q: "Would Year 10 be too soon to start?"
faq_2_a: "No. If your child has a particular grade in mind, Year 10 is the better year to start. There's still time to build things up steadily, and lessons are calmer, more about understanding than exam technique. It's also often when schools decide between the higher and foundation papers (the two tiers) in Maths and the Sciences. Having a tutor look properly at a paper before that decision is worth an hour on its own."
faq_3_q: "Can the same tutor cover two subjects?"
faq_3_a: "Sometimes, if the subjects sit close together. Maths with Physics or Computer Science works well, and so do English Language with Literature, and Biology with Chemistry. Maths alongside History or Geography usually means two tutors, and that's better than stretching one person too thin. Most children only need help in one subject anyway. Starting there and adding another later costs less than the other way round."
