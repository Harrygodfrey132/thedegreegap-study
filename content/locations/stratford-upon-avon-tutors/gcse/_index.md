faq_1_q: "My daughter is mid-table at a selective school. Is that actually a problem?"
faq_1_a: "Often not, and it is worth separating two things. Mid-table in a selective cohort is usually a strong absolute position, and the exam marks the absolute one. What matters is the specific requirement for the course she wants next, which is a number rather than a ranking. Ask her school which question types she loses most marks on and how close the last mock was to the next boundary. Those two answers tell you whether there is anything worth doing."
faq_2_q: "Is Year 10 too early to start?"
faq_2_a: "Year 10 is the better year if the aim is a specific band rather than a pass. There is still time for the work that compounds, sessions are calmer and more about understanding than exam management, and it is the point where the higher and foundation tier decision gets made in Maths and the Sciences. Having someone look properly at a paper before that call is worth an hour on its own."
faq_3_q: "Can the same tutor cover two subjects?"
faq_3_a: "Where the subjects sit next to each other, yes. Maths with Physics or Computer Science works well, as does English Language with Literature, and Biology with Chemistry. Maths paired with a humanity usually means two specialists and it is better to accept that than stretch one person. Most students only need one subject anyway, and starting there and adding later is cheaper than the reverse."
