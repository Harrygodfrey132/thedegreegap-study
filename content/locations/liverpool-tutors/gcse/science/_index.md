faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Science for Liverpool students?"
    a: "The extended-response questions, by some distance. The six and nine-mark items ask a student to organise an explanation in a logical order, and most lose marks not because they don't know the science but because they write everything they can recall and hope the marks are in there somewhere. After that it's the required practicals, which students often meet once in a lab and never revisit, and the maths embedded in Physics. Those three account for most of the gap between a grade 4 and a grade 6 in our experience."
  - q: "Can one tutor cover all three Sciences, or do we need separate specialists?"
    a: "It depends where the marks are going. For a student who is broadly steady across Combined Science and wants coordination, one strong tutor across all three works well and keeps things simple. But when one subject is clearly the weak spot, we match a specialist for it, someone who knows that specification's practicals and long-answer questions cold, rather than spreading one tutor thin. Plenty of Liverpool families start with one subject and add a second later."
  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Science tutoring session?"
    a: "Most run 60 minutes in three parts. The first fifteen reviews the week's homework and surfaces the misconceptions the marking threw up. The middle thirty works on whichever topic is bleeding the most marks, often a required practical or a six-mark explanation, with the method broken down step by step. The last fifteen puts it straight into exam-paper practice with a mark-scheme walkthrough, so the student sees exactly where each mark is awarded and why."
  - q: "Are your Science tutors qualified teachers or university students?"
    a: "Both, and the right answer depends on the student. Our network includes qualified secondary teachers, Russell Group graduates and postgraduates who sat these papers recently enough to remember the mark scheme in detail. For a student pushing at the top of the band, a recent high-achieving graduate often has the edge on current specification detail. For a student who has lost confidence entirely, an experienced teacher usually handles that better. We match on which of those two problems you actually have."
