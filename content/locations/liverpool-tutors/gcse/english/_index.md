faqs:
  - q: "What grade does my child need in English to take it at A-Level in Liverpool?"
    a: "It depends on the sixth form, and it's usually higher than the general entry bar. St Edward's College, Liverpool Blue Coat and Calderstones each publish their own requirements, and they change by course and from year to year. So check this year's page for the school your child's applying to. The thing to know is that the English grade gets looked at on its own.   - q: "My child reads all the time. Why are they struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Because reading a lot and writing for a mark scheme are two different skills, and the second one rarely gets taught step by step. Keen readers often lose marks by writing about what happens instead of how the writer made it happen. Or they've never learnt to plan an answer in the first four minutes. In a first lesson we usually find your child knows far more about the text than their marks show. The gap is getting it onto the page in the shape the examiner wants."
  - q: "How much English work should we expect between lessons?"
    a: "About 60 to 90 minutes a week. For English, that's usually one full essay or two timed exam questions, not more reading. Writing against the clock is the part that needs practice, because knowing a text doesn't mean you can write about it in 45 minutes. Much beyond 90 minutes and it stops helping. One essay that gets properly marked is worth more than three that don't."
  - q: "Can a GCSE English tutor help if my child has dyslexia or processes things differently?"
    a: "Yes. We'll ask about it on the call, so the tutors we suggest are picked with it in mind. Plenty of tutors in our network work with neurodivergent children regularly. Clear structure helps a lot in English. Sentence starters, the same planning method every time, a paragraph shape your child can lean on when the blank page feels like the hardest bit. And in Lessonspace a plan can be laid out visually and saved for the next lesson."
  - q: "My child doesn't want a tutor at all. What then?"
