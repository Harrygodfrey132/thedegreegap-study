faq_1_q: "My son's A-Level essays list points instead of weighing them up, and his grade won't budge. What can a tutor do?"
faq_1_a: "This is one of the things a tutor can help with most, because the knowledge usually isn't the problem. What's missing is the habit of setting one point against another and coming to a view, which is exactly what examiners look for. His tutor works on that with him using real exam questions, marking each attempt and then the rewrite, until it becomes second nature. Once his writing starts arguing rather than describing, the grade tends to follow."
faq_2_q: "His predicted grade is below what the University of Liverpool asks for. How can a tutor help close that?"
faq_2_a: "By working on the mocks and class tests his predicted grade comes from. The tutor finds where the marks are going, then builds the exam technique that wins them back. Starting in Year 12 or early Year 13 gives him the most room before predictions go off with his UCAS form. And even after that, it's the summer exams that decide whether he meets the offer."
