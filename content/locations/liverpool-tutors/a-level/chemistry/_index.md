faqs:
  - q: "Which bits of Year 13 Chemistry lose the most marks in Liverpool?"
    a: "Three second-year topics, mostly. Transition metals come first, because they read like content to learn but they're examined as reasoning about colour, shape and ligand swaps. Electrochemistry is next: your child has to combine electrode potentials and predict which way a reaction goes, not just recall a definition. Spectroscopy is the least forgiving. A mass spectrum and an NMR trace have to become a structure, and knowing the theory on its own earns very little."
  - q: "How many lessons before we see the grade move?"
    a: "Usually around eight to twelve weekly lessons for a grade, as long as your child is practising questions in between. Interpretation suits that rhythm, because it's about spotting patterns, and that comes from doing it again and again, not from being told. It takes longer if Year 12 basics like moles and bonding are shaky, since they need rebuilding while school moves on. The first lesson shows where your child is, and the honest estimate comes after it."
