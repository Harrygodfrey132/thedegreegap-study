Once your child can build them, the trouble moves to accuracy, with signs and units costing single marks right across a paper. Rate equations come a close second. Working out an order from a table of data takes a lot of practice, and a busy lesson with a full class doesn't leave much time for it."
  - q: "How can I tell if my child's problem is the method or the arithmetic?"
    a: "Look at the working on a marked paper, not the score. If the answer goes wrong from the first line, your child needs the method taught again. If the setup's right and the final number isn't, it's care with the arithmetic, and that needs regular practice instead. If the calculations are fine but the explain-why questions lose marks, the gap is in how precisely they write. One marked paper usually shows which in about ten minutes."
