  - q: "My child's Maths prediction is lower than we hoped. Why?"
    a: "Usually it's the gap between what your child can do with time and what they manage cold in a two-hour paper. Predicted grades come from mock results plus the teacher's judgement, so that gap shows up. In Year 12 it's often functions, where the notation itself gets in the way and your child can't see how to start. For a Year 13 on a B, it's usually the longer questions, where each method is known but the route between them isn't. Both look the same on a report. They need quite different work."

A Year 13 already on a B can still get a lot from a shorter, harder run of lessons on mixed questions. But predicted grades usually go to UCAS in the autumn of Year 13, so if the prediction matters, sooner's better."
