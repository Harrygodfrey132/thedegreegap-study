  - q: "Which bits of GCSE Maths do Manchester children find hardest?"
    a: "Usually three things, and they decide most grades. There's algebra hidden inside questions worth several marks. There are circle theorem proofs and iteration, among the hardest questions on the higher paper. And there's the wordy problem-solving at the back of Paper 3. Lower down, a wobbly grasp of fractions or negative numbers from earlier years quietly undoes the harder work on top of it. So the tutor's first job is marking a recent paper to see which of these costs your child the most. That's what gets worked on first."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper for GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Both, since each of the three papers is worth the same. Without a calculator, it's about quick, confident arithmetic and clean algebra. With one, it's about staying accurate through long, wordy problems, and that's where plenty of the Manchester children we see slip up towards the end. So in lesson one the tutor looks at one of each, side by side. Whichever is doing more damage gets the early lessons."
  - q: "How do I work out where my child is losing Maths marks?"
    a: "Ignore the total for a minute and look at the question numbers on a recent mock. On a higher AQA or Edexcel paper, the questions get harder as they go, and the last third is where circle theorems, iteration and long problem-solving tend to sit. If your child keeps losing marks there, it's usually exam technique rather than missing knowledge. That often moves quickly with focused practice. If the marks go early on, it's more likely something older underneath, and a tutor can tell you which within a lesson."
