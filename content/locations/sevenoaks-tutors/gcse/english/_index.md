  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Most often because nobody has taught the writing directly. Students are taught the texts thoroughly and then asked to produce a kind of essay that has its own rules, and the gap between knowing a book and scoring on a question about it is where most marks go. The classic symptom is a student who talks brilliantly about a text and writes a description of it. The other frequent cause is timing: a strong opening paragraph followed by two rushed ones because the clock ran out. Both are fixable, and both are faster to fix than a reading problem would be."
  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "Twenty to forty minutes, and what it is matters much more than how long it takes. One planned essay paragraph written to time beats an hour of re-reading notes, because English marks are awarded for how an argument is built rather than for how much of the text a student can recall. Where timing is the issue, short timed bursts two or three times a week do more than one long sitting. Most tutors set the task at the end of the hour so it points directly at what was just worked on, and mark it before the next session."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"

Extra time only helps if a student knows what to do with it, which is often where the real work sits.
