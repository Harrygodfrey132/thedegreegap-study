  - q: "How does GCSE Science performance around Sevenoaks compare to the national average?"
    a: "Kent sits above the national picture on headline measures, but that figure is shaped by a selective system and tells you very little about any individual child. Two students at neighbouring schools can be on different Science routes with different amounts of laboratory time behind them, and a county average smooths all of that away. The comparison worth making is your child's most recent paper against the paper they are going to sit. That is what the first session looks at, and unlike a published average it gives you something you can act on this term."
  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE Science, and does it matter for tutoring?"
    a: "Yes, and it matters more in Science than in most subjects. The content overlaps but the examining does not: the boards assess the required practicals differently, mark the six-mark answers to different structures, and print different amounts of equation support in the paper. A student who learned a practical one way can meet it framed another way and drop marks on material they genuinely understand. We confirm the board on the consultation call, and for a student who changed schools mid-course we look for a tutor who has taught both specifications."
  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Science?"
    a: "It depends on where your child actually sits in Year 10 rather than on where anyone hopes they will finish. Higher tier reaches the top grades but scores very little below its floor, while foundation caps lower and makes the available marks considerably more reachable. A student close to the boundary is often right on higher with focused work on the handful of topics that decide it. A student some way below usually does better on foundation, where questions are winnable and confidence builds. A Year 10 diagnostic turns that into a decision rather than a guess, and school should be in the conversation too."
