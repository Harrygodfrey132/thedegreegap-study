  - q: "How does GCSE Science in Sevenoaks compare with the rest of the country?"
    a: "Kent's headline results usually sit around or a little above the national picture, but the grammar system shapes that figure, and it says very little about your child. Two teenagers at neighbouring schools can be on different Science routes, with very different amounts of lab time behind them, and a county average smooths all of that out. The comparison that helps is your child's latest paper against the one they'll actually sit. That's what the first lesson looks at. And unlike an average, it gives you something to do about it this term."
  - q: "Does my child's exam board change what the tutor does?"
    a: "Yes, quite a bit in Science. The content overlaps, but AQA, Edexcel and OCR don't examine it the same way. They ask about the required practicals differently, and they mark the six-mark answers to different structures. So your child can meet a practical they know, framed in a way they don't, and lose marks on it. We confirm the board on the call. If your child changed schools partway through the course, we'll look for a tutor who's taught both."
  - q: "Foundation or higher tier: which should my child sit?"
    a: "Go by where your child is in Year 10, not where anyone hopes they'll finish. Higher tier, the harder of the two sets of papers, leads to the top grades but gives very little credit if your child falls below its lowest grade. Foundation tops out lower, but far more of its marks are within reach. A child near the boundary is often fine on higher, with focused work on the handful of topics that decide it. A child well below usually does better on foundation, where they can win questions and get their confidence back. It's worth deciding with school, and a tutor who's seen your child's recent papers can give you something concrete to take into that conversation."
  - q: "What sort of Science improvement can we realistically expect?"
    a: "We won't put a number on it in advance, because nobody can promise a grade. What we can tell you is what usually happens. If the problem turns out to be one topic block that got rushed, or how your child writes the long answers, a full grade band over a term of weekly lessons is common, because the fix is narrow. If the gap goes back two or three years, the same term usually brings clear progress without the full band, and a good tutor will tell you that early. The reviews on this page are real, and we don't edit them."
