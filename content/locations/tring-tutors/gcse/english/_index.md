  - q: "My child is good at Maths and Science. Why is English such a struggle?"
    a: "Because English rewards a different kind of thinking, and nobody has shown them how to break it into steps. Maths has a method; an extract question seems to have none. Often the gap goes back to Year 7 or 8, when analysing a writer's choices first came up and got skimmed over. A tutor gives your child a repeatable structure for those answers, which tends to suit exactly the child who likes rules."
  - q: "Will one tutor handle both English Language and English Literature?"
    a: "Yes, nearly always. The two GCSEs share the same reading and writing skills, though they're examined differently. Language is built around extracts and your child's own writing, while Literature tests the set texts and poetry, some of it unseen. A good tutor plans lessons to cover both, often switching week by week as mocks approach. If your child's texts are less common ones, we'll match on those too."
  - q: "My child is dyslexic. Can an English tutor still help?"

If your child has extra time or a reader in exams, the tutor practises under the same conditions so the real thing feels familiar."
  - q: "Do you offer English tutoring in the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes. Holidays suit English well, because there's room to read a set text properly or write a full timed essay without homework in the way.
