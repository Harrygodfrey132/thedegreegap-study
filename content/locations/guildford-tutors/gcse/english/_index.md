angle_body_1: "Guildford's secondaries are large, and English classes here are the same size as English classes anywhere. That is not a criticism of the schools, it is arithmetic. A teacher with a hundred and fifty students cannot mark a full essay from every one of them every week, so most students write far more than gets read closely, and they get a grade and a comment rather than a line-by-line account of where the marks went. In every other subject that is survivable. In English it is the whole problem, because English improves through the loop of writing, being shown exactly what a mark scheme did with your sentences, and writing it again."
angle_body_2: "So the work is not usually about learning more. Most students we see already know the text, already have ideas worth having, and lose marks in the two sentences after the quotation, where the examiner is waiting to be told what the writer's choice actually does. That is a technique gap and it is the fastest thing in English to move, because the thinking is already there and only the shape of the paragraph has to change. It applies the same way at a grade 3 and at a grade 8. The paragraph is the unit either way, and the difference is only how much of it is already in place."

    a: "The first session is a diagnostic. Your child writes, or brings something they have written, and the tutor marks it line by line against the real band descriptors while they watch, which is usually the first time a student has seen that happen. After that a typical hour is roughly a third looking at last week's marked work, a third on a specific skill such as building an analysis paragraph or planning an unseen response, and a third writing under something close to exam conditions. The writing is always taken away and marked before the next session."
  - q: "Does my child need a tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "In English, revision alone rarely moves a grade, and that surprises parents. Learning more quotations helps a student who does not know the text, but most students who are stuck do know it. What they cannot do is turn what they know into the shape the mark scheme rewards, and you cannot revise your way into that because there is nothing to memorise. It needs writing, marking and rewriting with someone who can tell you precisely which sentence lost the mark. That is the case for a tutor in English rather than in most subjects."
