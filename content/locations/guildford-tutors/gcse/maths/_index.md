  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Higher tier caps at grade 9 and floors at a 4, sometimes a 3. Foundation caps at 5 and floors at 1. A student sitting around a 4 or 5 in Year 10 mocks is usually better served by higher tier with targeted work on the early and middle questions, because that is where grades 4 to 6 actually live and the hardest questions on the paper can be left alone. Where a set placement made years earlier is driving the tier decision, it is worth getting an independent read on what the student can do now before it is fixed."
  - q: "Does my child need a calculator paper tutor or a non-calculator one?"
    a: "The same tutor, but the two fail for different reasons and a good diagnostic separates them. Marks lost on the non-calculator paper are usually arithmetic fluency and algebraic manipulation, which need short daily practice rather than teaching. Marks lost on the calculator papers are more often multi-step reasoning, where the student knows every step and cannot see the order to put them in. Bring both papers to the first session if you have them, because a student strong on one and struggling on the other tells us more than an overall grade does."

We tell you which of these a tutor is before you meet them, and why we think that background fits your child rather than another."
