faq_1_q: "My daughter is predicted a 5 but wants to do A-Level Chemistry. What can a tutor do?"
faq_1_a: "Quite a lot, if you've got a year to work with. Sixth forms usually want a decent grade in Chemistry itself before they'll take her onto the A-Level, so the work is narrow, not general revision. The tutor finds which parts of the Chemistry papers are costing her marks, often the calculations or the six-mark answers, and practises those. That's a much smaller job than lifting every subject at once."
faq_2_q: "Do you cover the exam boards Wolverhampton schools actually use?"
faq_2_a: "Yes. It's the first thing we check. Between them, schools across the city use AQA, Edexcel and OCR, sometimes a different board for different subjects in the same school. A tutor teaching from the wrong course can explain things beautifully, and your child will still drop marks on wording and structure. So we match on board, and on tier (the Foundation or Higher paper), before anything else."
faq_3_q: "He's fallen a long way behind. Is it too late?"
faq_3_a: "Hardly ever, though the lessons will look different. If your son's well behind, racing into this term's topics only widens the gap. So the tutor goes back to the point where things stopped making sense and rebuilds from there. Only then do they start pulling him towards where the class is. Getting that order right matters more than the number of hours."
