faq_1_q: "My daughter is predicted a 5 but wants to do A-Level Chemistry. What can a tutor do?"
faq_1_a: "Quite a lot, if there is a year to work with. Sixth forms usually ask for a grade in the subject itself for A-Level entry, so the work is narrow and specific rather than general revision. A tutor finds which parts of the Chemistry paper are costing the marks, usually the calculations or the six-mark answers, and drills those. That is a much smaller job than lifting every subject at once."
faq_2_q: "Do you cover the exam boards Wolverhampton schools actually use?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it is the first thing we ask. Schools across the city run AQA, Edexcel and OCR between them, sometimes different boards for different subjects in the same school. A tutor working from the wrong specification will teach good content and still lose marks on wording and structure, so we match on board and tier before we look at anything else."
faq_3_q: "He has fallen a long way behind. Is it too late?"
faq_3_a: "Rarely, though what the sessions look like changes. When a student is well back, pushing forward into current content just widens the gap. The tutor traces the work back to the point where it stopped making sense, rebuilds from there, and only then starts pulling towards where the class is. That order matters more than the number of hours you buy."
