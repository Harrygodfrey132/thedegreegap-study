faq_1_q: "Will my child need particular GCSE grades to get into the J6th?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, for some A-Levels. On top of its general entry expectations, the J6th asks for a particular grade in certain subjects: a 6 in GCSE Maths for A-Level Maths, for example. Longcroft's sixth form and Wyke Sixth Form College in Hull publish their own requirements. These get updated, so it's best to read the latest guide together in Year 10, while there's still time to shore up a weaker subject."
faq_2_q: "My child's fallen a long way behind. Is it too late for GCSE tutoring?"
faq_2_a: "No, though the plan looks different. A tutor won't race through this term's topics. They'll go back to where things stopped making sense, sometimes a year or two earlier, and rebuild from there. For some children the aim is a grade 4 in both English and Maths, which means neither has to be studied again after Year 11. For others it's getting back onto Higher papers. Either way, the first lesson starts by finding where things went wrong."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help before the Year 11 mocks?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and the autumn is a good time to start. Mocks often feed into predicted grades and sixth form conversations, so it's worth having a few weeks of focused work behind your child before they sit them. In the run-up, the tutor mixes timed past-paper questions with the few topics that keep costing marks. If the mocks have already happened, the marked papers are the best place to begin."
faq_4_q: "Can one tutor help with both English and Maths?"
faq_4_a: "Sometimes. A few tutors on our platform teach both at GCSE, and if your child would rather get used to one person, that can work well. More often we'd suggest a separate tutor for each, because the skills are so different: one's about methods and accuracy, the other about reading closely and writing to time. On the call, tell us which subject worries you more and we'll take it from there."
