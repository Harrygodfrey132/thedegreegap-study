faq_1_q: "How does tutoring affect my child's predicted grades?"
faq_1_a: "Not directly, because predicted grades are the teachers' call. What a tutor changes is the work they're judging, and most of that is Year 12: the class tests and the end-of-year exams especially. Predictions travel with the UCAS application in the autumn of Year 13, which is why Year 12 work counts for so much. And whatever the prediction says, the summer exams in Year 13 are a fresh chance."
faq_2_q: "My child's thinking about a degree apprenticeship. Can a tutor help with that?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, with the A-Level side, and Harry or Joe can talk the options through with you on the call. Degree apprenticeships usually start after Year 13, and schemes near Beverley, like the ones at the University of Hull and BAE Systems at Brough, each set their own grades and application window. Some close in late winter for a September start, so it's worth looking early. The tutor's job is the A-Level work those grades depend on."
faq_3_q: "My child's started at a new sixth form. Will the tutor know their course?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, because we check before we suggest anyone. On the call we'll ask about each subject's exam board and how the first few weeks have gone, then find tutors who've taught those exact courses. It matters most when your child has moved, say to Wyke Sixth Form College in Hull after GCSEs somewhere else, because the board can change between GCSE and A-Level even in the same subject."
faq_4_q: "What if my child wants to drop one of their A-Levels?"
faq_4_a: "Talk to us before they decide, if you can. Often the subject itself isn't the problem. It's one part of it, like the mechanics in Maths or the essays in History, that's dragging the marks down, and a tutor can usually tell fairly quickly whether that's fixable. If it really is the wrong subject, the sixth form will have its own rules and deadline for changing, so it's worth asking them early too."
