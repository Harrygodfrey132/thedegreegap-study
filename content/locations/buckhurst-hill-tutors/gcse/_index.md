faq_1_q: "My child regrets one of their GCSE options. Can a tutor help, or should they drop it?"
faq_1_a: "Talk to the school first, because each one handles switches differently and many only allow them in the first weeks of Year 10, if at all. But a struggling option is rarely a lost cause. Usually your child has missed two or three topics that everything since has leaned on. A tutor finds them from a recent test and works on them alongside the class, and within a few weeks you'll both know far more about whether the subject can work."
faq_2_q: "Does every GCSE count towards sixth form, or only the subjects my child wants to carry on with?"
faq_2_a: "Often more of them than parents expect. Woodford County High looks at a GCSE average across your child's best eight subjects as well as the grades for their chosen A-Levels, and Roding Valley ranks sixth form applicants by a total point score from their GCSE grades. So an option your child has no plans to continue can still move the total. A tutor can't fix everything at once, so we'd usually start with whichever grade sits furthest below the rest."
faq_3_q: "My child's school uses a different exam board from their cousin's in Woodford. Does that matter for tutoring?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, which is why we match tutors to the board, not just the subject. AQA, Edexcel and OCR cover much of the same content, but the questions are worded differently and the mark schemes reward slightly different things, especially on the longer answers. Tell us the board on the call. If you're not sure, it's usually on the school website or printed on the front of a mock paper."
