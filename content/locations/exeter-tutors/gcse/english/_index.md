faqs:
  - q: "What grade does Exeter College need in GCSE English?"
    a: "Most Level 3 courses ask for grade 4 in English as a minimum, with some asking for a 5 and A-Level English asking for more again in the subject itself. It varies course by course and changes between years, so read the current entry requirements for the exact course rather than assuming. The point families miss is that this applies well beyond the essay subjects. Engineering, health and digital courses carry an English requirement too, which is why the grade matters to students who never intend to write another essay."
  - q: "How many English sessions does it take to move a grade?"
    a: "Eight to twelve weekly hours for one band is the usual range, with the practice between lessons happening. Moving from a 3 to a 4, or a 4 to a 5, is often at the faster end, because the marks being missed are structural rather than interpretive and answer shapes can be taught quickly. The top bands take longer. A first-session diagnostic on a marked mock should give you an honest timescale, and if a tutor promises a band in three weeks, be sceptical."
  - q: "When should we start English tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "September to February, before the mocks that shape predicted grades and college applications. That window gives enough weeks for answer shapes to become automatic under timed conditions, which is the part that needs repetition rather than explanation. Starting in April still helps, though the plan narrows to the two or three highest-yield question types rather than covering the paper. If your child is in Year 10 now, that is better again, especially where the writing marks are the problem."
  - q: "Will my child get a qualified teacher or a university student?"
    a: "Both, and which one fits comes down to the student. The network includes qualified secondary English teachers, Russell Group graduates and current undergraduates. For a reluctant student who has decided the subject is not for them, a tutor closer to their own age often gets further in the first month, simply because the resistance is lower. For a student rebuilding written accuracy from a long way back, an experienced teacher who has taught that ground many times is usually the better match."
