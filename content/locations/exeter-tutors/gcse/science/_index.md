angle_body_1: "Watch your child revise Science and you'll probably see a lot of Biology. It reads like a story, so it gets revised like one, and it feels like progress while they're doing it. Then the paper asks about a plant or animal they've never come across, or an experiment they've never run. It wants them to use what they know, not recite it. That's where the Biology grade gets decided, and another read of the textbook won't touch it. Chemistry and Physics get the opposite treatment. They look hard, so they get less time. Moles, concentration and equations with a kilo or a milli hiding in the units go unpractised until March."

The Met Office's technical and data routes and the Royal Devon trust's healthcare apprenticeships often read Science alongside Maths and English. Exeter College sets its own minimum grades for each course, so check the Science ones your child likes the look of."

  - q: "Does it make a difference whether the school uses AQA, Edexcel or OCR for Science?"
    a: "A little, and mostly near the top of the paper. The Science is mostly the same whichever board sets it. What changes is the list of required practicals (the experiments every student does in class and can be asked about), how the long answers are worded and how the calculation questions are set out. If your child has been practising another board's practicals, that's one place they'll get caught out. We'll check the board on the call, and the tutor we suggest will know it."

Most tutors set a bit of each. Past about ninety minutes a week, the extra effort tends to stop showing up in the marks."
