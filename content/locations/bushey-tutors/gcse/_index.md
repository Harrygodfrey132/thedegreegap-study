faq_1_q: "When do mocks start to matter for a Bushey Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "Sooner than a lot of parents expect. Sixth form applications go in during Year 11, months before results day, and Queens' took its 2026 applications between December and April. So the mocks are often the latest grades on the table when your child applies. Starting in the autumn of Year 11 leaves room to fix a topic before the mocks rather than after them, and Year 10 gives more room again. Other sixth forms and colleges keep their own timetables, so check the ones your child likes."
faq_2_q: "Does my child need a different tutor for each science?"
faq_2_a: "Not always. On Combined Science, which gives a double grade like 6-5, one tutor who's comfortable with all three is often the simplest route. On separate sciences, each with its own grade, it depends where the marks are going. If it's Physics and the other two are fine, a Physics tutor makes more sense than a general one. And if Maths is shaky as well, do mention it, because a lot of Physics marks turn out to be Maths marks. We'll suggest what fits after the call."
faq_3_q: "My child's school teaches some iGCSEs. Can a tutor help with those?"
faq_3_a: "Yes. Some independent schools nearby, Immanuel College among them, teach a mix of GCSEs and iGCSEs, and the two can feel quite different on the day. The content overlaps a lot, but the papers and the question styles aren't the same. So we'll ask which qualification and which board your child is on for each subject, and match a tutor who knows that exact paper. If you're not sure, your child's subject teacher will know, or we can work it out together on the call."
faq_4_q: "One subject has slipped badly. Where does a tutor even begin?"
faq_4_a: "By going back before going forward. Racing through this term's topics on top of a shaky base rarely works, so the first lessons look for the earliest thing that didn't go in, whether that's fractions, balancing equations or how to build a paragraph. That gets rebuilt properly, and the newer topics follow in order. It can feel slow for a few weeks. It isn't wasted time, though, and the note you get after every lesson shows exactly what's been covered."
