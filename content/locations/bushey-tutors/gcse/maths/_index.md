faqs:
  - q: "Which GCSE Maths topics catch Bushey teenagers out most?"
    a: "The ones that ask your child to choose a method rather than follow one. Ratio and proportion written as a story, 'show that' questions, algebraic proof and the longer problems near the end of each paper come up again and again. None of this is special to Bushey schools. It's how the papers are built. A tutor finds which of these is costing your child most from a recent mock."
  - q: "Should my child practise more for the non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Probably, if that's where the marks go, but check first. On AQA, Edexcel and OCR there are three papers, and all three count equally: one without a calculator and two with. So the calculator papers carry two-thirds of the grade between them. Eduqas sets two papers instead. The non-calculator paper rewards quick, accurate arithmetic and algebra, while the calculator papers reward longer problem-solving. Your child's last mock will show which is costing more, and the tutor plans around that."
  - q: "Higher or Foundation: which tier is right for my child in Maths?"
    a: "The tier decides which grades are possible, so it's worth getting right. Foundation tops out at a 5. Higher starts at a 4, or a 3 for a near miss, and runs up to a 9. A child who's anxious and scraping a 4 on Higher papers might do better aiming for a solid 5 on Foundation. A child getting 5s on Foundation might be ready to move up. Schools decide the entries, usually after the Year 11 mocks, and a tutor's view of your child's papers is useful to bring to them."
