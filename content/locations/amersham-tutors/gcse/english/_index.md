  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they love reading?"
    a: "Because the exam rewards a different skill. Reading widely builds vocabulary and instinct, but GCSE answers need a student to name a method, quote briefly and explain its effect, then connect that to the question, all against a clock. Many keen readers have never been taught to plan, so their best idea turns up in the final paragraph. The tutor's first job is to find which step is missing and rebuild it, usually starting with planning."
  - q: "How much English homework should there be between sessions?"
    a: "Around an hour, rarely more than ninety minutes. Usually that is one timed answer on the question type covered in the lesson, plus a short set of quotations to learn. A single piece marked carefully does more than three written in a hurry, because the feedback is where the improvement happens. In the run-up to mocks the tutor may add one full paper under timed conditions, but the weekly load stays modest so it fits around other subjects."
