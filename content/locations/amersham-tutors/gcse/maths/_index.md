  - q: "Should my child sit Higher or Foundation tier?"
    a: "It turns on where the mocks sit and what comes next. Foundation tops out at grade 5, so a student who needs a 6 or above for a sixth form course has to be on Higher. Higher gives access to grades 4 to 9, with a narrow allowed 3 below that, which makes it risky for a student hovering around a 3 or 4. A tutor's Year 10 diagnostic gives the school better evidence to make that call, and it is worth settling before Year 11 begins."
  - q: "How can I tell which Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at where on the paper the marks go, not just the total. Early questions test routine methods, the middle mixes ratio, geometry and algebra, and the final third is multi-step problem solving and proof. A student dropping marks early has a content gap. One who scores well until the last third usually has the methods but struggles to choose between them or to set out the reasoning. One marked mock paper is enough to tell the two apart."
  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help a child with dyslexia, dyscalculia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about it when we match. For dyscalculia the work slows down and leans on visual models, number lines and bar diagrams, before moving to abstract methods. For ADHD, shorter tasks with a clear finish help, as does a whiteboard that keeps every step visible instead of spoken once. For dyslexia, the wordy problem questions get broken into labelled parts. Let us know about any access arrangements such as extra time, because timed practice should mirror them."
