faq_1_q: "Is Year 12 or Year 13 the better time to start?"
faq_1_a: "Year 12, and the gap between the two is bigger than most families expect. Predicted grades come out of Year 12 work and the first part of Year 13, and they reach universities in the autumn, so an hour a week starting in October counts twice over. Year 13 starts still move the final grade and plenty of families begin there. But by then the offers have usually been made against a number set earlier, and the work turns into repair rather than build."
faq_2_q: "How does tutoring help with the predicted grade UCAS sees?"
faq_2_a: "By moving the evidence the prediction rests on, which is mock performance plus teacher judgement. A tutor reads the actual script instead of the grade, finds where the marks went, and works on that under timed conditions until the next assessment looks different. It also makes the conversation with school more useful, because you are asking about specific questions rather than asking for a better number. None of this happens in a fortnight, so autumn of Year 13 is the outside edge of useful."
faq_3_q: "She got a grade 8 at GCSE and is struggling now. What happened?"
faq_3_a: "Nothing went wrong with your daughter. GCSE rewards recall and recognising the question type, and strong students get very good at both. A-Level stops signposting. The question no longer tells you which method it wants, the marks sit in explanation and evaluation rather than the answer line, and a paper can run to two and a half hours. Students who coasted on pattern-matching feel that first and hardest. The fix is teaching the choosing, not more content."
