faq_1_q: "Should we start tutoring in Year 12, or wait until Year 13?"
faq_1_a: "Year 12 if you can. It makes a bigger difference than you'd think. Predicted grades are built mostly on Year 12 work and the first weeks of Year 13, and they go to universities in the autumn. So an hour a week from October of Year 12 helps your child twice: once with the prediction, and again with the final exams. Starting in Year 13 still helps the final grade, and plenty of families do. But by then the prediction's mostly set, and the tutor's patching things up rather than building."
faq_2_q: "Will tutoring help with the predicted grade that goes on the UCAS form?"
faq_2_a: "It can, by changing what the prediction's based on, which is mock results plus the teacher's view of your child's work. The tutor reads the marked mock itself, not just the grade, finds where the marks went and practises those questions against the clock until the next assessment looks different. It helps when you talk to school, too, because you can ask about particular questions rather than asking for a higher number. None of it happens in a fortnight, though. The autumn of Year 13 is about the last point where it'll help the prediction."
faq_3_q: "She got 8s at GCSE and she's struggling now. What's gone wrong?"
faq_3_a: "Probably nothing. We hear this a lot. GCSE rewards remembering things and spotting the type of question, and bright teenagers get very good at both. A-Level stops giving those clues. The question doesn't say which method it wants, more of the marks are for explaining and weighing things up than for the final answer, and a paper can run to two and a half hours. The children who leaned hardest on spotting patterns tend to feel it first. So the tutor teaches the choosing, rather than piling on more content."
