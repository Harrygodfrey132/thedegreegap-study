faq_1_q: "Year 10 or Year 11, when does GCSE tutoring make sense in Woking?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10 if you have the choice. The content steps up that year and a gap closed then stops doing damage for another eighteen months. There is a local reason too: college and sixth form applications open in the autumn of Year 11, and the mock sitting behind the predicted grade on that form happens soon after. Plenty of families begin in Year 11 and it still works, but by then the plan narrows towards papers and technique rather than teaching anything properly from the beginning."
faq_2_q: "Do your tutors cover the boards Woking schools teach?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and it is the first thing we pin down rather than a detail left until later. AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC word questions differently, examine practicals differently and print different formulae, so a tutor who knows your child's specification is useful inside ten minutes. We confirm the board on the consultation call, before any profile goes out. If your child is on an iGCSE at an independent school, say so, because the paper mix changes again."
faq_3_q: "He is fine in class and drops marks in mocks. What can a tutor actually do about that?"
faq_3_a: "Read the script, which is the bit nobody at school has time for. Lost marks cluster, and where they cluster tells you whether this is content, timing or the way answers get written down. A student who works calmly at home and loses the last twenty minutes of a paper needs drilling under a clock, not more revision. That work is hard to do alone and it moves quickly, usually inside a term of weekly hours."
