faq_1_q: "Year 10 or Year 11, when does GCSE tutoring make sense in Woking?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10, if you've got the choice. The content steps up that year, and a gap closed then won't keep costing marks for the next eighteen months. There's a Woking reason too. College and sixth form applications open in the autumn of Year 11, and the mock behind the predicted grade on that form comes soon after. Lots of families start in Year 11, and that's fine too. The plan just narrows to past papers and technique, rather than going back over topics from the start."
faq_2_q: "Do your tutors know the exam boards Woking schools use?"
faq_2_a: "Yes. We find out which boards your child's sitting before a single profile goes out. AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC Eduqas each word their questions their own way and lay their papers out differently. So a tutor who already knows your child's board won't be learning it on your time. If your child's doing iGCSEs at an independent school, mention it, because those papers are different again."
faq_3_q: "He's fine in class but drops marks in mocks. What can a tutor actually do?"
faq_3_a: "Go through his actual mock paper, which there's rarely time to do one-to-one in a full class. Look at where the marks went missing and you can usually tell whether it's content, timing or the way answers get written down. If he works calmly at home and loses the last twenty minutes of a paper, he needs practice against the clock, not more revision. That's hard to do alone. So the tutor sets timed sections each week and goes through where the minutes went."
