faq_1_q: "What does Woking College ask for at sixteen?"
faq_1_a: "It publishes its requirements course by course. To start an A-Level programme the college asks for at least five GCSE passes at grade 4 to 9, with subject requirements on top of that, and the final ask is settled at interview. Its A-Level Maths course names at least a grade 6 in GCSE Maths, Further Maths a 7, and Physics also asks for a 6 in Maths. Requirements get reviewed between years, so work from this year's course page rather than what an older sibling remembers."
faq_2_q: "What platform do the sessions actually run on?"
faq_2_a: "Lessonspace. It is a shared whiteboard built for one-to-one teaching, so your child and the tutor write on the same page rather than one of them talking at a camera. Every session is recorded, which matters more than parents expect when revision starts. Nothing to install. Most Woking students have worked it out inside ten minutes, and you get a short note afterwards saying what was covered and what was set."
faq_3_q: "Can you help a student who has fallen a long way behind?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and a fair bit of our work looks like this. Where the gap goes back a year or more, the tutor works backwards through your child's own wrong answers until the understanding runs out, and that point is usually earlier than anyone guessed. The plan then builds forward alongside school rather than repeating it. It takes longer than fixing exam technique, and we will tell you which of the two we think you are dealing with."
faq_4_q: "Do we speak to anyone before a tutor is suggested?"
faq_4_a: "Always. Harry or Joe take the first call themselves and no profile goes out before that conversation has happened. We ask about the board, the year group, what the last mock looked like and which course your child is working towards. Two or three profiles follow, then a free 15-minute video meeting with whoever you like the look of. Nothing is paid until your child has met them."

Parents tend to hear the change before they see it, somewhere around week four, in how their child talks about the subject at dinner."
