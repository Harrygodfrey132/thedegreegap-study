faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English do Aylesbury children struggle with most?"
    a: "Two things come up every year. First, the unseen poetry comparison, because your child has to build an argument about poems they've never seen, against the clock, with nothing to revise beforehand. Second, the long writing task on the Language paper, which carries a big share of the marks and often gets whatever minutes are left. Neither is about how much your child knows. Both improve quickly once they're taught as a method, which is why we usually start there."
  - q: "Is there a best point in Year 11 to start English tutoring?"
    a: "Between September and February, and ideally before the mocks that shape sixth form predicted grades. English rewards a run of weeks more than a burst, because essays get better through repetition and feedback, not revision. Starting in April is still worth it. But the work narrows to technique and timing, rather than building the analysis that lifts a band. The autumn leaves room for both."

A tutor who knows your child's board knows which phrasing earns marks and which just sounds good. We check the board and the set texts with you on the call before we suggest anyone."
