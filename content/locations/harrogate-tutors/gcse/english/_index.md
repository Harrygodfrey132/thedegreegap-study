  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Harrogate students?"
    a: "Unseen poetry and the comparison question, in that order. Unseen poetry is hard because there is nothing to revise, so students who have worked hard feel ambushed by it. The comparison is hard because it asks for a genuinely different shape of paragraph, one that holds two texts at once instead of walking through them in turn. Both are learnable, and both move faster than people expect, because they are technique problems rather than knowledge problems."
  - q: "How can I tell which English topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at the mark breakdown rather than the total, then look at where in the answer the marks stopped. A student who scores well on the shorter reading questions and then drops on the extended response has a structure problem. A student marked down across the board on AO2 is describing rather than analysing. And an answer that thins out badly in its final paragraphs is a timing problem wearing an analysis problem's clothes. Bring a marked paper to the consultation and the pattern is usually visible in a few minutes."
  - q: "How do you vet your English tutors?"
    a: "Roughly three in every hundred applicants make it onto the platform. There is a written application, a subject knowledge stage, and then a founder-led interview that is mostly about explaining something difficult clearly while someone is stuck. That last part is the filter that matters for English, because knowing why a paragraph is a band 4 and being able to say it in a way a fifteen-year-old will act on are not the same skill. References get checked before anyone is matched."
  - q: "What if my child does not want a tutor?"
