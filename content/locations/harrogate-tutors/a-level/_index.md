faq_1_q: "Can we get a predicted grade changed?"
faq_1_a: "Sometimes, and only before the UCAS reference is submitted. There is no formal appeal, because a prediction is a professional judgement by the school rather than an exam result. What works is a narrow, evidenced conversation with the subject teacher: a recent paper marked at a higher standard, a specific topic that was weak in the mock and demonstrably is not now. What does not work is an argument about how hard your child is working, or about the university they have set their heart on."
faq_2_q: "My son's predicted grade seems harsh compared with what he can do."
faq_2_a: "It might be, and it is worth knowing that the research points the other way. Work by Murphy and Wyness published in 2020, using three years of UCAS data, found only about 16% of applicants were predicted accurately across their best three A-Levels, and roughly 75% were predicted too high rather than too low. So a prediction that feels harsh is sometimes just an unusually honest one. The way to find out is a recent paper and an outside opinion on where the marks are going."
faq_3_q: "Is a short block before mocks enough, or does it need to be a full year?"
faq_3_a: "For a student already close to the target, a short block of six to eight sessions aimed at one thing usually does more than a year-long programme, and costs a fraction. A full year makes sense where a subject needs genuine rebuilding rather than sharpening. The consultation call is where we work out which of the two you have, and we would rather recommend the smaller option and be right than sell the bigger one."
faq_4_q: "How does online A-Level tutoring work in practice?"
faq_4_a: "Sessions run on Lessonspace, our shared whiteboard, where a proof, a mechanism or an essay plan stays on screen while it is worked through. Every session is recorded, which matters more at A-Level than at GCSE because a method explained in October is exactly what a student wants back in May. Online also means matching on specification rather than on who lives within driving distance of Harrogate, which is the constraint that actually limits quality."
