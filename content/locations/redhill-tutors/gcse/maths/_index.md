  - q: "Should my child sit Foundation or Higher Maths?"
    a: "It depends on where they're working now, and the school makes the final call. Higher gives a shot at grades 4 to 9 but assumes the Foundation basics are secure. Foundation is safer for a child on a 3, but it can't go above a 5. If your child is on a borderline 4 or 5, a few months of focused work can make Higher a sensible choice, and a tutor's marked papers give you real evidence to take to the school."
  - q: "How much Maths should my child practise between lessons?"
    a: "About an hour a week, split into two or three short goes. Usually it's a set of exam questions on that lesson's topic, plus a few from older topics so they don't fade. More than an hour and a half tends to turn into copying out worked examples, which doesn't help much. The tutor marks it before the next lesson, so mistakes get caught while your child still remembers making them."
  - q: "My child has dyscalculia or ADHD. Can a Maths tutor still help?"
    a: "Yes. Tell us on the call, and we'll suggest a tutor who has worked that way before. In practice it means shorter chunks, the same layout for every type of question, and lots of drawing on the whiteboard instead of explaining out loud. If your child has exam access arrangements at school, such as extra time, the tutor can practise papers with those in mind so nothing feels new on the day."
  - q: "My child says they hate Maths and won't want a tutor. Is it worth trying?"
    a: "Very often, yes. A lot of children who say they hate Maths really mean they hate getting it wrong in front of people, and a one-to-one lesson takes that away. Your child meets the tutor on a free video call first and can say no. Most give it two or three lessons and start to relax once a few things go right. If they genuinely don't want to carry on, you can stop at any point."
