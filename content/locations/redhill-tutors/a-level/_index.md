faq_1_q: "Is Year 12 too soon for A-Level tutoring, or should we hold off until Year 13?"
faq_1_a: "Year 12 is rarely too soon, because that's the year most predictions are based on. A gap that opens in October of Year 12, say in Chemistry calculations or Economics diagrams, tends to show up again in the summer exams, and those results feed the UCAS form. A Year 13 start is still worth it for the final papers. It just leaves less room to change the prediction that universities see first."
faq_2_q: "My daughter has picked two A-Levels she's never studied before. Will she be behind?"
faq_2_a: "Probably not, because most of her class will be new to them too. St Bede's, for one, only teaches Economics in the sixth form, and subjects like Politics or Sociology are new to plenty of Year 12s wherever they study. What tends to catch new students out is the writing: diagrams that need to be exact, and long answers that weigh up both sides instead of describing one. A tutor can build those habits from the first term, before the first big essay comes back with a disappointing mark."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help with the EPQ?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, with one firm rule: the project stays your child's own work. St Bede's offers the Extended Project Qualification alongside A-Levels, and a tutor can help your child narrow a title, plan the research, and learn how to argue from sources instead of summarising them. They won't write sections or edit the final report. Plenty of universities like to see an EPQ, and the habits it builds help in essay subjects too."
