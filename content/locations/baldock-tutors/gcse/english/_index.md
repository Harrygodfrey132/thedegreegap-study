  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Most often because the writing has not been taught as a separate skill from the reading. Schools cover the texts properly and then ask for an essay that follows its own rules, and the distance between understanding a book and scoring on a question about it is where the marks disappear. The familiar sign is a student who talks well about a text and writes a description of it. In Literature the other common cause is evidence: quotations learned because they are famous rather than because they can be argued with. Both are quicker to fix than a reading problem."
  - q: "Why is my child's English predicted grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from mock performance plus a teacher's judgement, and in English the gap parents notice is usually between what a student can discuss and what they produce on paper under time. A student who is articulate in class can write an essay that reads as description, and the mark scheme rewards argument. The first thing a tutor does is read the actual mock script rather than the grade, because the script shows whether the problem is structure, evidence or simply running out of clock. Those three need different terms."
