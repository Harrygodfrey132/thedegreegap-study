faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE Science do children around Baldock find hardest?"
    a: "The long answers, more than any single topic. Six-mark questions turn up in all three sciences, and they reward a chain of reasoning rather than a list of right facts. That's not how most teenagers revise. After that, three topics come up again and again: rearranging equations in Physics, moles and concentration in Chemistry, and genetics in Biology, where the working has to be set out the way the mark scheme expects. All four are about method rather than knowledge. So they tend to respond quickly."
  - q: "Do the Science exam boards really differ that much?"
    a: "Yes, and it shows more in Science than in most subjects. The content is similar, but the exams aren't. AQA, Edexcel and OCR each ask about the practicals in their own way, and they mark six-mark answers to different patterns. If your child learnt a practical one board's way, the same experiment can look unfamiliar on another board's paper, and they'll drop marks on something they know. We check the board on the call. If your child has moved schools, we'll look for a tutor who's taught both."
  - q: "Should my child be doing foundation or higher tier Science?"
    a: "It depends on where your child is in Year 10, not where anyone hopes they'll end up. Higher tier (the harder set of papers) is the only route to the top grades, but it has very little safety net underneath. Foundation caps lower, and its marks are much easier to reach. If your child is close to the line, higher often works, with focused practice on the few topics that decide it. If they're well below, foundation is usually kinder, and confidence builds. A tutor looking at your child's work in Year 10 turns it into a decision rather than a guess. School should be part of that conversation too."
