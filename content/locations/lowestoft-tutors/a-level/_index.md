faq_1_a: "It's the third paper in OCR's A-Level Chemistry and Physics, and it's designed to pull the whole course together, so questions mix Year 12 and Year 13 topics and there's nowhere for an old gap to hide. At the college it's worth 26% of each of those A-Levels. In practice, Year 12 work needs revisiting all through Year 13 rather than being left behind, and a tutor can build that revisiting into the weekly lessons."
faq_2_q: "Should my child start A-Level tutoring in Year 12, or wait until Year 13?"
faq_2_a: "Earlier is easier. In Year 12 a shaky topic is only a few weeks old, and the tutor can fix it before the next one is built on top. Plenty of families still start in Year 13, often after a mock that didn't go well, and then the lessons lean towards past papers and the topics losing most marks. If a university offer matters, try to start before predicted grades are settled in the autumn of Year 13."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor still help before my child's UCAS predicted grades are set?"
faq_3_a: "Most sixth forms set UCAS predictions in the autumn of Year 13, based largely on Year 12 assessments and early Year 13 work, though each college has its own timetable. Tutoring can't change a prediction directly. What it can do is lift the work the prediction rests on, so the earlier your child starts, the more of that evidence improves. Once predictions are sent, the goal becomes meeting the offer in the summer, and the tutor's focus shifts to exam technique."
