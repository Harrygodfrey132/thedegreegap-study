faq_2_q: "Is one-to-one online tutoring really better than a group revision class?"
faq_2_a: "For most students, yes, because the hour is about their gap and nobody else's. A group class moves at the pace of the group, which is the same problem as school with fewer people in it. One-to-one over Lessonspace means the tutor watches the working line by line, stops at the exact step where it goes wrong and doesn't move on until it holds. For a student who won't ask a question in front of others, and plenty of Kingston teenagers won't, that's usually the whole difference."
faq_3_q: "What does an online session actually run on?"
faq_3_a: "Lessonspace, a shared interactive whiteboard built for one-to-one teaching. Your child and the tutor see the same page, write on it together, and the session is recorded so a worked example can be replayed the night before a test. There's no software to install and most students are comfortable with it inside the first ten minutes. It also lets the tutor see the working as it happens, which is where most Maths and Science marks are actually won or lost."
