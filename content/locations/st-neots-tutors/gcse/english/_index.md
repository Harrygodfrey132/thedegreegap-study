faqs:
  - q: "If my child wants Maths and Science A-Levels in St Neots, does the English grade matter?"
    a: "Yes, quite a lot. At Longsands Sixth Form, every A-Level course currently needs a 5 in English Language as one of five grade 5s, whichever subjects your child picks, and vocational courses need a 4. Hinchingbrooke and other sixth forms nearby have their own rules, which can change, so check each one's current guide. A 4 in English Language could hold up a teenager heading for Physics and Further Maths. The Language papers reward method, though, and method can be taught. That's where a tutor starts with a numbers-minded child."
  - q: "Why does my child find English so much harder than Maths?"
    a: "Because the marks work differently, and in a class of thirty there isn't always time to show each child how. In Maths an answer is right or wrong. In English the mark scheme rewards how clearly your child explains a point and backs it up, so 'I knew what to say' and a high mark aren't the same thing. Children who like rules often feel there aren't any. There are. They're just less obvious. A tutor shows your child what a strong answer to each question type looks like, then gives them a pattern to practise until it feels natural."
  - q: "Do children at different St Neots schools study the same English texts?"
    a: "Not always. Each school picks its own exam board, whether that's AQA, Edexcel, OCR or Eduqas, and the Language papers differ from board to board. For Literature, every board sets a Shakespeare play, a 19th-century novel, a modern text and poetry, but schools choose which ones from the board's list. So two cousins in Year 11, one at Longsands and one at Ernulf, could be revising different books. That's why we ask for the board and the set texts when you call, then suggest tutors who've taught them. Your child's mock papers usually have the board printed on the front."
  - q: "My son is autistic and struggles to revise on his own. Can an English tutor help?"
    a: "Yes, and it's one of the most useful things one-to-one lessons can do. We'll ask about autism, ADHD, dyslexia or anything else on the call, and suggest tutors who've worked with it before. The tutor can give each lesson the same shape, set a small amount of work in between, and mark it together with your son the following week, so revision stops being something he faces alone. Lessons are recorded too, so he can rewatch a tricky explanation as often as he likes."
  - q: "Do we have to book a set number of English lessons?"
    a: "No. There's no block to buy and no contract. You're only charged for lessons that actually happen, so you can pause or stop whenever you like. Some families book a term of weekly English lessons before the mocks, stop for a while, then come back for a few weeks before the summer exams. Others keep going right through Year 11."
