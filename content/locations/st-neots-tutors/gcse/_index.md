faq_2_q: "My child's only in Year 10. Is that too soon for a tutor?"
faq_2_a: "No, and it can be the easier year to start. There's less riding on Year 10 work, so a tutor can spend several weeks on one shaky topic, like algebraic fractions or how to structure a long answer, without a mock looming. Year 11 gets busier. Plenty of families start in the autumn of that year instead, once the mocks are on the way, and that works too. By spring, most lessons are past papers against the clock."
faq_3_q: "Won't a tutor confuse things by doing it differently from school?"
faq_3_a: "It shouldn't, because the tutor works from what your child's class is covering and from their own mock papers, so lessons go on the gaps school hasn't had time to revisit. It's the same syllabus, not a new one. If there's a topic test coming up, that can take priority for a week. Tutoring isn't a replacement for school. It's the one-to-one time that's hard to find in a full classroom, and the tutor's note after each lesson means you'll always know what was covered."
