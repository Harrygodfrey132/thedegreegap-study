  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Portsmouth students?"
    a: "Turning a good spoken point into a scoring written one. After that, unseen poetry and the comparison question. Unseen poetry is hard because there is nothing to revise, so a hard-working student feels ambushed. Comparison is hard because it wants a genuinely different paragraph shape, one that holds two texts at once instead of walking through them in turn. All three are technique rather than knowledge, and all three move faster than people expect."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "If they are articulate in class and disappointing on the page, the cause is almost always that speech does work writing has to do explicitly. Out loud, tone carries the argument. On paper only what is written counts, and the mark scheme credits nothing it cannot see. The other common cause is timing, where marks are lost to the clock rather than to understanding. Both look like weak analysis on a report and neither is."
