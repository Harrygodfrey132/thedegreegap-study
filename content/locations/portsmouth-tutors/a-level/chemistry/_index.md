    a: "Redox, more often than not, and it's sneaky because a question rarely says redox on it. Oxidation numbers are taught early as a set of rules, then sit underneath transition metals, electrochemistry and redox titrations for the rest of the course. So if your child's unsure of them, marks go in topics that look unrelated. Building and combining half-equations is the biggest single loss. There are several steps, and one mistake usually costs the whole equation."
  - q: "How do I find out where my child is really losing marks in Chemistry?"
    a: "Look at where the marks went on a marked paper, not just how many. If they're spread across lots of topics, check redox first, because it hides inside other content. If calculations go wrong everywhere, the gap is usually the moles work from Year 12, known as amount of substance. If the maths is fine but written explanations score badly, it's the precision of the wording. All three can give the same grade and need very different plans. One marked paper is usually enough for a tutor to tell which."
