faq_1_q: "Redborne's GCSE courses start in June of Year 9. What does that mean by Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "A long course, close to two full years, which is good for depth and hard on memory. Topics from that first summer can feel brand new again by the mocks. So a tutor builds short, regular returns to older units into the weekly lessons, and Year 11 revision becomes a refresher rather than relearning. If your child is still in Year 9, a few lessons before June also give their Maths and English basics a chance to settle before the course begins."
faq_2_q: "My child is doing Biology, Chemistry and Physics as separate GCSEs. One tutor or three?"
faq_2_a: "Often one, sometimes two. Plenty of tutors on our platform teach all three sciences at GCSE, and one person seeing the whole picture helps when the same skills, like rearranging equations or writing six-mark answers, come up in each. If one science is far behind the other two, a tutor who knows that subject inside out may be the better choice. We'll suggest what makes sense once we've heard about your child on the call."
faq_3_q: "Will a tutor just go over the same things my child does in class?"
faq_3_a: "No, though they'll keep an eye on it. The first lesson looks at recent tests and homework to find what's actually costing marks, and that sets the plan. Some weeks the tutor works alongside the class topic, which helps when homework is due. Other weeks they go back to something older or practise exam questions in timed conditions, which a class of thirty rarely has time for. You'll always know which, and why."
