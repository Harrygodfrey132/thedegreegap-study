  - q: "My child panics on the non-calculator paper. Should we focus there?"
    a: "At first, probably, but not only there. Your child sits three papers that all count equally, one without a calculator and two with, so the calculator papers make up two-thirds of the grade. The non-calculator paper shows up shaky arithmetic and fractions straight away, which is often where an older gap is hiding. The calculator papers reward steady, multi-step working on the longer questions. A tutor looks at your child's recent papers and splits the practice to match where the marks are going."
  - q: "Higher or Foundation: which tier should my child sit?"
    a: "It depends on where your child is in Year 10 and how quickly they're moving. Higher runs from grade 4 up to 9, with a 3 allowed in some cases, and Foundation covers grades 1 to 5. A child working around a 5 usually suits Higher, with some focused work on the harder topics. One around a 3 or 4 may be safer on Foundation. The school makes the final decision, but a tutor's view of your child's papers gives you something solid to discuss with them."
