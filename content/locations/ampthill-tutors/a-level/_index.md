faq_1_q: "Results day didn't go to plan. Can a tutor help my child retake an A-Level?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, and a retake works best when it doesn't start from page one again. A-Level exams only run in the summer, so there's a full year, and the first job is finding out what actually went wrong. Was it one paper, one type of question, or running out of time? If the school can get the marked scripts back, they're the best place to look. The tutor then spends the year on those patterns, alongside whatever your child is doing for work or study."
faq_2_q: "My child did well in GCSE Maths, so why is A-Level Maths suddenly such a struggle?"
faq_2_a: "Because in many ways it's a different subject, and plenty of strong students feel the same in their first term. GCSE questions usually point to the method. A-Level ones don't, and they lean on fast, accurate algebra in every line. So a child who was comfortable at GCSE can find themselves slow and unsure. A tutor checks the algebra first, then practises choosing a method from a blank start, which is the skill the exam rewards."
faq_3_q: "When do predicted grades get decided, and can tutoring still make a difference before they go to UCAS?"
faq_3_a: "Teachers usually set them towards the end of Year 12 or early in Year 13, from mock and class results, and they go off with the UCAS application in the autumn. Tutoring doesn't change the number directly. What it changes is the work your child produces in those assessments. So starting in Year 12 gives the most room, and a strong Year 13 mock can still help if a school revisits its predictions."
