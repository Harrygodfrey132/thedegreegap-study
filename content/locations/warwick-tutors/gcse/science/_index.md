The comparison worth making is your child's most recent paper against the paper they will sit. That is what the first session does, and unlike an average it produces something to work on this term."
  - q: "How can I tell which parts of GCSE Science my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Take a marked paper and sort the lost marks into three piles: recall questions, data and graph questions, and the extended six-mark answers. Almost every student is clearly worse at one of the three, and each needs different work. Losses on recall mean content gaps. Losses on data mean a method that has never been taught. Losses on the long answers usually mean the content is there but the chain of reasoning is not being written down. Five minutes sorting them is worth more than a term of general revision."
  - q: "How much Science homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "Twenty to thirty minutes, and it should be exam questions rather than reading. Answering cold and then marking against the scheme builds what the paper actually tests, while re-reading notes builds the feeling of knowing, which does not transfer. Where data questions are the gap, two or three short graph questions a week does more than one long session. Most tutors set the work at the end of the hour so it follows directly from what was taught, and mark it before you next meet."
