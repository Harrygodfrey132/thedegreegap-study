  - q: "When should we start GCSE Maths tutoring in Year 11?"
    a: "Any time from September to February, and sooner is better. The winter mocks feed into the predicted grades that sixth forms and colleges look at when they make offers, so anything that improves before the mocks helps twice: once in the prediction, and again in the summer. Starting after them still helps, and plenty of families do. But the plan has to narrow to the topics worth the most marks, rather than rebuilding properly. If the gap goes back more than a year, the autumn's really the last point where it can be fixed rather than worked around."
  - q: "How do I work out which Maths topics are costing my child marks?"
    a: "Look at a marked paper and read the question numbers, not the total. Questions get harder as the paper goes on. So if the lost marks bunch up at the end, it's usually a few topics, like algebra, circle theorems or the problems with lots of steps. If they're spread evenly, it's more often accuracy or speed. Blank answers and half-finished ones tell you different things, too. A blank means your child didn't know where to start. Working that stops halfway means the method ran out. Five minutes with the paper tells you more than a term of reports."
  - q: "Who are the tutors? Teachers, or university students?"
    a: "Both, and we'd choose between them with your child in mind. Some are qualified secondary teachers, some are Russell Group graduates, and some are undergraduates who sat these same papers only recently. If a topic needs rebuilding from the ground up, a qualified teacher who's taught it many times is often the better choice. If your child knows the method but falls apart under exam conditions, a recent graduate who remembers the pressure can suit them better. We suggest tutors based on what your child's work shows, not on a house preference."
  - q: "Is a GCSE Maths tutor actually worth the money?"
