faq_1_q: "Should we start GCSE tutoring before the Year 11 mocks or wait for the results?"
faq_1_a: "Before, if you can. The winter mock is the last big piece of evidence a school has when it writes the predicted grades that sixth forms and MidKent College read, so work that lands before it counts twice: once in the mock and again in the number on the form. After the mock the job doesn't vanish, it narrows. There's less time to teach a topic from the beginning, so sessions lean on past papers and technique instead. Both are worth doing. The earlier one just has more room in it."
faq_2_q: "My daughter does triple science. Is that one tutor or three?"
faq_2_a: "Usually one, and now and then two. Most Science tutors on our platform cover Biology, Chemistry and Physics at GCSE, and a single tutor is often better placed to spot that the same weakness, rearranging an equation say, is costing marks in two of the three. Where a student is strong in two sciences and adrift in one, we match on that one subject only. Triple and combined are handled the same way. Tell us which of the three feels worst and whether that's new or has been true since Year 9."
faq_3_q: "He's a long way behind in Maths and it isn't one topic. Where does a tutor even start?"
faq_3_a: "With a marked paper, not the syllabus. The tutor works back through the wrong answers to find where the understanding actually stops, which for a lot of Maidstone Year 10s and 11s is a Year 8 idea like fractions or negative numbers rather than anything from this term. That point is where the rebuild begins, and it's usually further back than the student expects and closer to the surface than the parent fears. From there the plan runs alongside school rather than repeating it. Six to eight weeks in, the current lessons start making sense again."
