faq_1_q: "Should we start GCSE tutoring before the Year 11 mocks or wait for the results?"
faq_1_a: "Before, if you can. The winter mock is the last big piece of evidence your child's school has when it writes the predicted grades that sixth forms and MidKent College read. So work done before it counts twice: once in the mock, and again in the grade on the form. After the mock, the job doesn't disappear. It narrows. There's less time to teach a topic from scratch, so lessons lean on past papers and technique instead. Both are worth doing. An earlier start just gives the tutor more room."
faq_2_q: "My daughter does triple Science. Is that one tutor or three?"
faq_2_a: "Usually one, sometimes two. Most of the Science tutors on our platform teach Biology, Chemistry and Physics at GCSE. And one tutor is often better placed to notice the same weakness, rearranging an equation say, costing her marks in two of the three. If she's strong in two sciences and adrift in one, we'd match a tutor for that one only. It works the same way for combined Science. On the call, tell us which of the three feels worst, and whether that's new or has been true since Year 9."
faq_3_q: "He's a long way behind in Maths and it isn't one topic. Where does a tutor even start?"
faq_3_a: "With a marked paper rather than the syllabus. The tutor goes through his wrong answers one by one until they find where his understanding actually runs out. For a lot of Maidstone Year 10s and 11s that's a Year 8 idea, like fractions or negative numbers, not anything from this term. It's usually further back than he'd expect, and easier to fix than you might fear. From there the plan runs alongside school rather than repeating it, and this term's lessons tend to make more sense as the old gaps close."
faq_4_q: "What does an online GCSE session look like from our side?"
faq_4_a: "A laptop at the kitchen table, a quiet hour and the last bit of marked work. Lessons happen in Lessonspace, an online classroom where your child and the tutor write on the same whiteboard, so the tutor sees the working rather than hearing it described. There's a replay of every lesson, which means an explanation from November can be watched again before the summer exams. You'll get a short note after each one on what was covered and what's set for the week. And there's no drive across Maidstone to fit around, which is a big part of why the weekly hour actually keeps happening."
