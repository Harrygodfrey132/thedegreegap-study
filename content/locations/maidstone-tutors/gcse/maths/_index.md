faqs:
  - q: "Is the GCSE Maths standard in Maidstone similar to Tonbridge and Sevenoaks?"
    a: "The exam is identical. Every Maidstone student sits the same three papers as a student at Judd, Tonbridge Grammar or Weald of Kent, on the same boards, marked to the same grade boundaries. What differs is the mix of schools a child might be in. The Tonbridge grammars tend to fill their places on Kent Test score rather than on distance, so their intakes come from across west Kent, while Maidstone's four grammars sit alongside a wider spread of other secondaries in one town. For a parent that mostly changes the comparison in the classroom, not the grade on the certificate. The paper doesn't know which town it was sat in."
  - q: "How many GCSE Maths tutoring sessions does it usually take to move a grade?"
    a: "Eight to twelve weekly sessions for one grade band, as long as there's practice between them. It's quicker when the problem is exam technique, like a Maidstone Year 11 who knows the method but loses the marks for working on the multi-step questions. It's slower when the gap is a foundation one, a Year 8 fractions idea that never held, because something has to be built before it can be practised. The first session is a diagnostic on a marked paper, and it's what tells us which of the two we're looking at. The plan from session two is shaped by it."
  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at the question numbers on the last mock, not the total. Each paper runs roughly from the shortest questions to the longest, so where the marks disappear tells you what kind of problem it is. Losses spread across the early questions point to arithmetic and basic algebra. Losses that start around the middle, in ratio, geometry and the first multi-step questions, point to method. Losses only at the back, on the long problem-solving questions, are usually exam technique rather than knowledge. A Maidstone GCSE Maths tutor does this sort in the first twenty minutes, and it decides the whole term."
  - q: "Are your GCSE Maths tutors qualified teachers or university students?"
    a: "Both, and the match depends on the problem. A student who needs Maths taught again from the Year 8 foundations up often suits a qualified teacher who has done exactly that many times. A student who knows the content and is losing marks on the higher paper's problem-solving questions often does better with a recent graduate who sat the current papers and knows the mark scheme closely. All tutors on our platform are graduates, most from Russell Group universities, and each passes a founder-led interview on how clearly they explain a method, not only on whether they know it."
  - q: "Can my child have GCSE Maths tutoring during the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and the holidays are often where the biggest jumps happen. A short daily block at October half term or over Easter, on the one topic a mock has just exposed, can do what a term of weekly hours does, because nothing else is competing for the student's attention. For a Maidstone Year 11 the Easter block is the last real chance to fix a method before the summer papers. Sessions run online over Lessonspace, so a holiday away doesn't stop them. We'll say honestly if a student needs the break more than the sessions."
