faq_2_q: "My child got the 6 they needed for A-Level Maths, and Year 12 already feels like a different subject. Is that normal?"
faq_2_a: "It's very common, and it doesn't mean they chose wrong. A-Level Maths assumes GCSE algebra is automatic, so a child who just made the 6 can follow the new ideas and still lose marks in the working: factorising, surds, rearranging. A tutor finds which GCSE methods are slowing them down and practises those alongside the Year 12 topics. If it still isn't working by the spring, Harry or Joe can talk through the options with you, including whether to keep the subject."

faq_3_a: "Year 12, if you can. Teachers usually base UCAS predicted grades on work and exams from Year 12 and the start of Year 13, so the months before that are when a tutor helps most. Starting in the autumn also gives time to close the gap from GCSE before the first big assessments. Year 13 isn't too late. The lessons just lean more on past papers and timing than on rebuilding."
