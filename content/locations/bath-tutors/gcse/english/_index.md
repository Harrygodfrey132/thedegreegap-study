faqs:
  - q: "What do Bath children find hardest in GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually the comparison, then the unseen poem. The comparison question asks for two texts held together in one argument. Most Bath children we meet write well about the first poem and tack the second one on at the end, and that caps the mark however good the analysis is. The unseen poem has no revised quotations to lean on, so a child who's treated English as something to memorise has little to say. The same habit fixes both: decide what the writer's doing before writing anything, then pick the evidence for that. The writing task comes a distant third, and it's mostly about planning."
  - q: "Is there a way to see which questions are costing my child marks?"
    a: "Yes: look at the mark breakdown from the last mock, not just the grade, because each question tests something different. Marks lost on the language and structure questions usually mean spotting features without an argument. Marks lost on the long evaluation or comparison questions point to essays that describe rather than judge. Marks lost on the writing task are usually planning and time, not vocabulary. Ten minutes with the marked paper tells a tutor which it is, and each needs different work. If you haven't got the breakdown, ask the school. Most English departments can give you one."
  - q: "Will revising harder fix my child's English grade?"
    a: "Probably not on its own. Revision helps with remembering things, and English has less to remember than most subjects, which is why it so rarely moves the grade. Read a marked essay instead. If the ideas are sensible, the quotations fit and the marks still aren't there, the problem is how the answer's built. That's a habit someone needs to show and correct week after week, which is a tutor's job. The same goes if your child talks about a text far better than they write about it. But if the essays already argue and only the timing slips, timed practice marked against the mark scheme might be enough."

For English we also look at how a tutor marks, since marking is most of the job."
