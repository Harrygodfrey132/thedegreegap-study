faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Bath students?"
    a: "The comparison and the unseen poem, in that order. The comparison question asks for two texts held in one argument, and most Bath students we meet write about the first poem well and bolt the second on at the end, which caps the band whatever the quality of the analysis. The unseen poem has no revised quotations to fall back on, so a student who has learned English as recall has nothing to say. Both are fixed by the same habit: decide what the writer is doing before writing anything, then choose the evidence for that. The writing task is a distant third, and mostly a planning problem."
  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE English questions my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at the mark breakdown on the last mock rather than the grade, because each question tests a different skill. Marks lost on the language and structure questions point to feature-spotting without an argument. Marks lost on the long evaluation or comparison questions point to essays that describe rather than judge. Marks lost on the writing task are usually planning and time, not vocabulary. Ten minutes with the marked paper tells a tutor which of those it is, and each one needs a different term of work. If you don't have the breakdown, ask the school for it. Every English department keeps it."
  - q: "Does my child need an English tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Read a marked essay, not the grade. If the ideas are sensible, the quotations are apt and the marks still aren't there, the gap is how the answer is built, and that is a tutor's job, because it is a habit somebody has to model and correct week after week. If your child discusses a text far better than they write about it, the same applies. If the essays already argue and the marks only slip on timing, a run of timed practice marked against the scheme might be enough. Revision helps recall, and in English there is very little to recall, which is why it so rarely moves the grade on its own."
  - q: "How do you vet your GCSE English tutors?"

That interview removes most people, because knowing a text and teaching someone to argue about it are different skills. Joe and Harry run the interviews themselves, and references are taken at the end. For English we also look at how the tutor marks, since marking is most of the work."
  - q: "What happens if my child gets their GCSE English results and didn't hit the grade?"
    a: "We work through results day with families and into the November resit window. For English Language the most common resit target is a 3 into a 4, and an eight-week block in September and October, built around the writing task and the structure question, usually gets there. For a Bath student who needed a 6 for an essay A-Level and landed a 5, we'd talk to the sixth form first, because several will take a student on a plan, and then work the specific question type that fell short. Nothing about August is final for English."
