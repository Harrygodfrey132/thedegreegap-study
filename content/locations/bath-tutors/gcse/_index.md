faq_1_q: "The Year 11 mocks are a few weeks away. Can a tutor do anything useful in that time?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, but the job changes. With a few weeks left the tutor stops teaching new content and works the papers: one timed past paper a week, marked the way the board marks it, then the two or three question types where the marks left worked until they hold. That alone often moves a grade, because a lot of lost marks are method and timing rather than knowledge. What it can't do is rebuild a Year 9 topic from scratch in a fortnight. That work starts after the mock, with the marked paper as the map."
faq_2_q: "My daughter has slipped in two subjects at once. Where does a tutor even start?"
faq_2_a: "With whichever one gates the next step. If she wants an A-Level in one of them, or a sixth form publishes a grade for it, that subject gets the first hour. Often the two slips share a root: a Maths method that is also costing marks in Physics, or essay planning that is failing in both English and History. The first session reads a recent paper in each and says so honestly. Sometimes the answer is one tutor for both. Sometimes it is two specialists, one after the other, and we'd rather tell you that than sell two sets of hours."
faq_3_q: "How does online GCSE tutoring work for a Bath family?"
faq_3_a: "Every session runs one-to-one over Lessonspace, our shared whiteboard. Your child and the tutor see the same page, the working gets written out on screen rather than done in a head, and the hour is recorded so it can be watched back before the mock. There's nothing to drive to, which in Bath is a real saving. The bigger gain is the match: a tutor who knows your child's exact specification can be in Leeds or Cardiff, and for a student in Larkhall or Combe Down that changes nothing."
