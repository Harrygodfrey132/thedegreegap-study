faq_1_q: "The mocks are only a few weeks off. Is it still worth starting?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, though the job changes. With a few weeks left, the tutor stops teaching new topics and switches to papers. Your child sits one timed past paper a week at home, and the tutor marks it the way the exam board would. Then the lessons go on the two or three question types losing the most marks. That alone can move a grade, because plenty of lost marks come down to method and timing, not knowledge. What it can't do is rebuild a Year 9 topic in a fortnight. That starts after the mock, with the marked paper as the map."
faq_2_q: "My daughter's slipping in two subjects. Which one gets the tutor first?"
faq_2_a: "The one that decides what she does next. If she wants to take it at A-Level, or her sixth form has set a grade for it, that subject gets the first hour. Quite often the two slips have the same root, like a Maths method that's also losing marks in Physics, or essay planning that's letting her down in English and History alike. The first lesson looks at a recent paper in each, and the tutor will tell you straight. Sometimes one tutor can cover both. Sometimes it's two tutors, one after the other, and we'd rather say so than sell you two lots of hours."
faq_3_q: "How do online lessons actually work for a Bath family?"
faq_3_a: "Your child and the tutor meet one-to-one in the online classroom Lessonspace. They write on the same whiteboard, so the working goes down on screen where the tutor can see it. Every lesson's recorded, too, so your child can watch a method again the night before the mock. There's nothing to drive to, which in Bath counts for a lot. But the bigger win is the match. The tutor who knows your child's exact course might live in Leeds or Cardiff, and for a family in Larkhall or Combe Down that makes no difference at all."
