faq_1_q: "The predicted grades go off in the autumn and one is a grade short. Can tutoring change it in time?"
faq_1_a: "Sometimes, and it depends on what the school is willing to look at. Predictions are the teacher's judgement, so the tutor's job is to give that teacher a reason: a run of marked past-paper questions at the higher grade, produced under time, in the weeks before the prediction is finalised. That is a narrow, honest piece of work and it can be done in a term. If the school has already sent the number, the plan switches to meeting the offer in the summer, which is a longer job but a clearer one."
faq_2_q: "My son is applying to Airbus and DE&S degree apprenticeships as well as UCAS. Does a tutor help with both?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, because both read the same predicted grades and the same subjects. The Filton and Abbey Wood schemes name Maths and a Science in their published requirements, so the tutoring sits in those subjects and the work is identical whichever route wins. What differs is the calendar. The apprenticeship windows often close earlier than UCAS and include online tests and an assessment day, so we plan the term around the earlier deadline. Harry and Joe can also talk through the application itself, since they've walked plenty of families through it."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help with the EPQ without it stopping being his own work?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, within a clear line. The EPQ is marked on the process as much as the product, so a tutor helps him choose a question that is actually answerable, plan the research, and structure the write-up so the evaluation is visible. What the tutor doesn't do is write a paragraph of it. At Oldfield, where the EPQ sits inside the sixth-form offer, and at the other Bath sixth forms that run it, the students who score well are the ones who kept an honest log, and that is a habit a weekly hour can build."
