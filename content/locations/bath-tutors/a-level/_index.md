faq_1_q: "The predicted grades go off in the autumn and one is a grade short. Can tutoring change it in time?"
faq_1_a: "Sometimes, and it depends on what your child's school is willing to look at. Predictions are the teacher's call. So the aim is to give that teacher a reason. That means a run of timed, marked past-paper answers at the higher grade, which your child can show them before the prediction's finalised. It's a narrow, honest piece of work, and it can be done in a term. If the school's already sent the number, the plan switches to meeting the offer in the summer. That's a longer job, but a clearer one."
faq_2_q: "My son's applying to Airbus and DE&S apprenticeships as well as through UCAS. Can a tutor help with both?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, because both look at the same predicted grades in the same subjects. Airbus and DE&S each name Maths in their requirements, and DE&S asks for a Science too, so that's where the tutoring goes. It's the same work whichever route he ends up taking. What's different is the calendar. Apprenticeship windows often close earlier than UCAS, and many include online tests and an assessment day, so we'd plan the term around the earlier deadline. Harry and Joe can talk you through the application itself as well, since they've helped plenty of families with it."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help with his EPQ and still keep it his own work?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, with a clear line. The EPQ (the Extended Project Qualification) is marked on the process as much as the finished piece. So the tutor helps him pick a question he can actually answer, plan the research and lay out the write-up so his evaluation shows. What the tutor won't do is write a single paragraph of it. Oldfield has the EPQ as part of its sixth-form offer, and other Bath sixth forms run it too. Students who do well on it tend to keep an honest log as they go, and that's a habit a weekly hour can build."
