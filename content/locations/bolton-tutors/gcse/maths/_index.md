  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Read the question numbers on a marked paper, not the total. Marks lost in clusters near the front usually mean arithmetic or a method that was never secure. Marks lost only at the back tend to be about question style rather than topic knowledge. Blank working on multi-step questions says confidence went before the maths did. Ten minutes with a real script tells you more than a term of general revision, and it is the first thing a tutor asks for."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or the non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Both decide the grade and they carry the same weight, so the honest answer is whichever one is bleeding more marks. The non-calculator paper rewards fluent arithmetic, fractions and algebraic manipulation, and slow number work costs time there as well as marks. The calculator papers reward accuracy across multi-step problems and careful reading of long questions. A first session marks one of each and settles the question instead of guessing at it."
