faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of A-Level Maths for Bolton students?"
    a: "The step at the start of Year 12, where a question stops naming its topic and the student has to pick the method unprompted. Strong GCSE performers often find that part hardest, because pattern-matching carried them a long way and now there is nothing to match. After that, losses gather in whichever applied strand gets the least attention, Mechanics for students who dislike the modelling, Statistics for students who never open the data set. Proof is the third place marks go quietly."
  - q: "Does the exam board matter for A-Level Maths tutoring?"
    a: "Yes, more than it does at GCSE. Core content overlaps heavily, but boards differ in how they divide Pure from the applied content, how much formula support is printed in the booklet and how the longer questions are worded, and those differences decide marks at the top of a paper. The large data set behind the Statistics work varies as well. We confirm the board and the applied modules on the consultation call before anyone is suggested."
  - q: "How long is each A-Level Maths session?"
    a: "Most run an hour, once a week. Some Year 13 students move to ninety minutes in the run-up to mocks, because a full past paper plus the marking of it will not fit into sixty, though that is a decision for later rather than for the start. Regular and consistent beats long blocks crammed into a holiday. We will suggest a shape on the first call and change it when the term asks for something different."
  - q: "Can an A-Level Maths tutor help a student with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and several tutors do this work regularly. In practice it is about reducing load: a consistent layout so multi-step working does not get lost halfway down a page, long questions broken into named stages, and one method drilled until it runs without thought. Timed practice comes in gradually where exam conditions are the difficulty rather than the maths itself. Tell us on the call what school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements."
  - q: "Can we do A-Level Maths tutoring on a budget?"
    a: "Yes, mostly by being deliberate about when the hours land rather than by buying more of them. One session a week through Year 12 achieves more than two a week begun in the spring of Year 13, because the prediction has already gone by then. The other route is a short block, six to eight sessions aimed at the one strand a mock says is doing the damage, then stopping. Plenty of Bolton families work exactly that way, paying one session at a time."
