faq_1_q: "My child's well short of the grade their course at Farnborough Sixth Form College asks for. Is it worth trying?"
faq_1_a: "Often it is, but it depends what's behind the gap. The tutor's first lesson usually makes that clear, as the two of them mark a recent paper together. A handful of missing topics is one kind of gap. An older one, like algebra from Year 8 that never quite settled, needs more groundwork before the exam practice starts. And if the grade looks out of reach this year, the tutor will tell you plainly, so you can look at other courses with your child too."
faq_2_q: "Can my child have a tutor for just the one science they want to take at A-Level?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and if your child's set on one A-Level, it often makes sense. Say it's Chemistry. If your child sits Chemistry as a GCSE of its own, we'd suggest tutors who teach it on their board, and the lessons stay on it. Combined Science is different, though. It gives one double grade across all three sciences, with no Chemistry grade on its own. So check how the college words its requirement for Combined Science. If the double grade is what counts, the tutor can bring in Biology and Physics as well."
faq_3_q: "There's already plenty of homework from school. Will a tutor add more?"
faq_3_a: "Some, yes, though the tutor won't pile it on. It's usually a couple of sets of practice questions on the topic from that lesson, so it doubles as revision, and it shouldn't add up to more than 90 minutes or so a week. If school has already set homework on the same topic, the tutor may use that as the practice instead. They'll talk it through rather than do it, though, so the work stays your child's own."
faq_4_q: "If my child's course asks for a 7, not a 5, what does the tutor do differently?"
faq_4_a: "Mostly, more of the time goes on harder questions. On the higher Maths papers, a 5 is largely won in the earlier and middle questions, so those get made secure first. A 7 needs a fair share of the later ones too, often multi-step problems set out in a way your child hasn't met before. In the Sciences, it's the longer answers and the unfamiliar ones. And fewer slips on easy marks, so checking and timing get practised as well."
