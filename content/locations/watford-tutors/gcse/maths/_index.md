  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper for GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Both, because they carry equal weight and decide the grade together. The non-calculator paper rewards arithmetic fluency and clean algebra. The two calculator papers reward accurate multi-step problem solving, and that is where most Watford students we see lose the larger share, at the back of Paper 3 where the questions turn wordy. The first session marks one of each so lessons start on whichever paper is costing more."
  - q: "Why is my child's GCSE Maths predicted grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from mock performance plus teacher judgement, and the gap parents notice most is between what a child can do in class and what they can produce in 90 minutes under timed conditions. Those are different skills. A tutor's first job is reading the actual mock script, not the grade on the front, to see whether marks were lost to time, to method, or to a topic that was never secure. Then the work rebuilds performance under pressure."
  - q: "Are your GCSE Maths tutors qualified teachers or university students?"

  - q: "Can I do GCSE Maths tutoring on a budget?"
