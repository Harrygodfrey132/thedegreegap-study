  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between lessons?"
    a: "Somewhere between 60 and 90 minutes a week is plenty, and in English what they do matters more than how long it takes. One planned essay written to time teaches more than reading the set text three times. Most Watford children we work with do one timed piece, plus a short task gathering quotations on that week's topic. Beyond 90 minutes it stops helping much. That's truer in English than most subjects, because tiredness shows straight away in the writing."
  - q: "Is it a tutor my child needs, or just more revision?"
    a: "A recent marked essay will usually tell you, more than the grade will. If the essays are well organised but stuck in the middle band, it's the depth of analysis, and that's something a tutor can move fairly quickly. If the answers tail off or the last question's half done, it's planning and timing, which is also quick to work on. But if your child's already in the top band across their mocks, some focused practice on the weaker paper will do more than weekly lessons. We'll tell you that on the call."
