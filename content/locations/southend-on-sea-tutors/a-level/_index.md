faq_1_q: "When does a predicted grade actually get decided?"
faq_1_a: "Earlier than most families expect. Predictions come out of Year 12 performance and the first assessments of Year 13, and they reach UCAS in the autumn, so offers get made against a number largely settled before Christmas. That is why we push families towards a Year 12 start rather than a spring one in Year 13. Work done then counts twice over: once in the prediction the sixth form writes, once in the paper your child sits in June."
faq_2_q: "She did well at GCSE and is struggling now. What changed?"
faq_2_a: "The subject changed and nobody announced it. A GCSE question usually names the topic it belongs to, so a strong student can pattern-match a long way. A-Level questions do not do that, and the marks shift from getting the right answer to showing a method somebody else can follow. Students who never had to work before often have no study habit to fall back on either. The fix is not more hours. It is different hours, with someone watching the working rather than the answer."
faq_3_q: "Can tutoring help with a degree apprenticeship application?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and the calendar is different from UCAS. Ford's Dunton campus at Laindon runs a four-year degree engineering apprenticeship, and Mid and South Essex NHS Foundation Trust takes apprentices as far as Level 7, with each scheme publishing its own requirements and shifting them between intakes. Applications often close before the UCAS deadline and nearly all of them add a written stage and an assessment day. Tutors work on the grades the scheme screens against and, where it helps, on the writing."
faq_4_q: "Does an online session really work for A-Level depth work?"
faq_4_a: "Better than at GCSE, from what we see. The subjects are narrow, so the value sits in finding the one person who genuinely knows your child's specification and module set, and that person rarely lives in Essex. Sessions run on Lessonspace, a shared whiteboard where tutor and student write on the same page rather than talk at each other. Every session is recorded, which matters when a proof or a mechanism needs watching twice. Most students settle into it inside the first lesson."
