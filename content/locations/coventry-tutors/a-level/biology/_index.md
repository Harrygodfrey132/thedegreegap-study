angle_heading: "Reading the Cardiac Cycle Graph, Not Just Labelling the Heart"
angle_body_1: "In the exam, the heart often turns up as a graph rather than a diagram: three pressure lines, for the atrium, the ventricle and the aorta, on the same axes. Then the question asks when a valve closes, and why. The answer is a comparison of two pressures. The valve between the atrium and the ventricle shuts because the pressure in the ventricle has risen above the pressure in the atrium. Write 'to stop blood flowing backwards' and it sounds right to your child, but that's what the valve is for, not what makes it shut. That gap is the mark."
angle_body_2: "Lessons keep the graph in front of your child, not the diagram. Your child marks each point where a valve opens or closes, and says out loud which two pressures explain it, until the cause comes before the purpose without thinking. Then the numbers: reading times off the axis carefully, and working out a heart rate from the length of one cycle. And in Coventry the grade counts for a lot. If your child has a health or biomedical course at Warwick or Coventry University in mind, that route starts with the predicted grade."
