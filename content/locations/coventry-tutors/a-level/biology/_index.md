angle_heading: "Reading the Cardiac Cycle Graph, Not Just Labelling the Heart"
angle_body_1: "Labelling the heart is rarely where the marks go. The exam shows the cardiac cycle as a graph instead: three pressure lines, for the atrium, the ventricle and the aorta, on the same axes. Then it asks when a valve closes, and why. The answer is a comparison of two pressures. The valve between the atrium and the ventricle shuts because the pressure in the ventricle has risen above the pressure in the atrium. Write 'to stop blood flowing backwards' and it sounds right to your child, but that's what the valve is for, not what makes it shut. That gap is the mark."
angle_body_2: "Lessons keep the graph in front of your child, not the diagram. Your child marks each point where a valve opens or closes, and says out loud which two pressures explain it, until the cause comes before the purpose without thinking. Then the numbers: reading times off the axis carefully, and working out a heart rate from the length of one cycle. It matters in Coventry, where healthcare and biomedical courses at the local universities publish their own entry standards, and the hospital trust recruits from exactly those courses. Every one of those routes starts with the predicted grade."
