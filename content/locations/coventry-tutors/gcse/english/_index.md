  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Coventry students?"
    a: "Unseen poetry and the comparison question. Unseen poetry is hard because there is nothing to revise, which is exactly the problem this subject has generally, so students who work hard elsewhere feel ambushed by it. Comparison is hard because it wants a genuinely different paragraph shape, one that holds two texts at once rather than walking through them in turn. Both are technique rather than knowledge, and both move faster than people expect once they are taught as their own thing."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Most often because nobody has shown them what to practise. Every other subject hands a student a list; English appears not to, so the hours go elsewhere and the grade stays put. Underneath that the usual specifics are a paragraph that names a technique without saying what it does, quotations dropped in without being taken apart, and timing, where the last question gets nine minutes because the first got forty. All three are concrete and all three are teachable."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about learning differences when matching rather than waiting for you to raise it. Several tutors work regularly with neurodivergent students. Online suits this well: the whiteboard keeps the working visible instead of wiping it off, explanations can be broken into steps at the student's own pace, and because every session is recorded nothing has to be held in memory the first time round. Mention it on the consultation call and it becomes part of the matching."
  - q: "What if my child does not want an English tutor?"
    a: "Very common, and worth handling gently rather than pushing through. With English the resistance is usually about the subject rather than the person, because it has been the one place effort did not visibly pay. That tends to shift within two or three sessions once there is a method to follow. The free 15-minute meeting lets your child meet the tutor with nothing committed, and if they genuinely do not want to carry on after a session or two, we pause."
