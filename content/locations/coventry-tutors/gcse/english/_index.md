  - q: "How do we stop last year's English texts going cold?"
    a: "Little and often. Coming back to each text every few weeks keeps it fresher than one big push in the Easter holidays. A tutor can plan that rotation around your child's texts, so the play from Year 10 gets a quick outing alongside whatever the class is on now. At home, short quotation checks help too: ten minutes on one text, five quotations, then one paragraph that uses them. It sounds small. Done every week, it adds up."
  - q: "When will we start to notice a change in English?"
    a: "You'll often notice your child's attitude shift first, sometimes within a few lessons, once there's a plan. Marks take longer. If the gap is mostly exam technique, like timing or how to set out a comparison, a few weeks of practice can show up in the next mock. If it's something deeper, like texts that have gone cold or essay planning that hasn't clicked yet, allow a term or so. Nobody can promise a grade, but the tutor will keep you posted on what they're seeing."
