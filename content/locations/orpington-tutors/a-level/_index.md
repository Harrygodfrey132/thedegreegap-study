faq_1_q: "My daughter picked Chemistry because she wants to do Medicine, and now it's her weakest subject. Is that common?"
faq_1_a: "Very, and it doesn't mean she chose wrongly. Chemistry often gets picked for where it leads, and the jump from GCSE is steep, with moles, equilibrium and organic mechanisms arriving close together. The tutor looks at her recent tests, finds which of those is costing the most marks, and starts there. Medical schools set their own subject and grade requirements, so check the current ones for the universities she likes and plan back from them."
faq_2_q: "Is Year 12 too soon for A-Level tutoring, or should we wait for the mocks?"
faq_2_a: "Year 12 is usually the better time. A-Levels are examined at the end of Year 13, but the predicted grades on your child's UCAS form tend to be set around the end of Year 12 or early in Year 13, from the work done before then. A gap spotted in the spring of Year 12 has months to close. One spotted after the Year 13 mocks has weeks. Starting later still helps, with a tighter plan."
