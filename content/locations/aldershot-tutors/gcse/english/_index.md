faqs:
  - q: "We've moved to Aldershot and the new school does different English set texts. Where do we start?"
    a: "With the texts your child will be examined on now, because the exam only asks about those. Their old school's novel or play won't come up. After that it's time and method: getting to know the new text properly, learning quotations in themes, and writing practice essays on it from the first few weeks. It's slower than for a child who's had the book for two years. But it's very doable, and a term of weekly lessons goes a long way."
  - q: "The new school uses a different exam board for English. Does that matter?"
    a: "It can, so tell us both boards on the call. Most of the skills carry over. But each board lays its papers out differently, words its questions its own way and marks the longer answers against its own scheme. The 19th-century extract, for example, sits on a different Language paper depending on the board. So a question your child practised at the old school might not appear at all, and a new one might. A tutor who knows the new board can spot which habits to keep and which to change."
  - q: "My child missed the poetry cluster the new class studied. Do they need all of it?"
    a: "Mostly, yes. The exam can print any poem from the cluster, and your child will usually have to write about a second one from memory as well. So every poem needs to feel familiar. They don't all need the same depth, though. A tutor will often help your child get to know a handful really well, ready to bring in, and the rest well enough to handle whichever one turns up on the day."
  - q: "How do you choose the English tutors?"
    a: "Harry or Joe interview every applicant themselves, and around 3% end up on the platform. All of them studied at top Russell Group universities, and most have tutored one-to-one for at least two years. The interview is where most people fall short. We want to see someone make a hard idea simple for a teenager who's struggling. For a child who's just changed school, that patience matters as much as knowing the texts. We check references too."
  - q: "What happens to the English lessons if we move away from Aldershot?"
    a: "Nothing has to change. Everything happens online, so a new postcode doesn't mean a new tutor, whether you end up in Fleet, Farnham or the other end of the country. If the next school teaches different texts or uses another board, tell us and the tutor will adjust the plan. And there's no contract, so you're never tied in if the timing stops working. For a child who's had to start over a few times, one familiar face each week can be a real comfort."
