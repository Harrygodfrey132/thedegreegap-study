  - q: "My child changed schools and is studying different set texts. What now?"
    a: "It is a real problem and a solvable one. The exam does not care which texts a student studied, only that they can write about the ones they are entered for, so the first job is confirming exactly what those are. From there the work is building usable knowledge of a text met late, which means quotations grouped by theme rather than learned as lists, and essay practice on that text from early rather than at the end. It is slower than for a student who has had two years, and it is very doable in a term."
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Aldershot students?"
    a: "Closed-book quotation recall, especially for anyone who met a text late, followed by the comparison question. Recall is a memory problem wearing an analysis problem's clothes, and students who understand a play completely can still freeze without it in front of them. Comparison is hard because it wants a paragraph shape that holds two texts at once rather than walking through them in turn. Both respond quickly to targeted work."
  - q: "How do you vet your English tutors?"
    a: "Roughly three in every hundred applicants make it onto the platform. Written application, a subject knowledge stage, then a founder-led interview that is mostly about explaining something difficult clearly while someone is stuck. References are checked before any match. For English that last stage matters most, because knowing why a paragraph sits in a lower band and saying it in a way a fifteen-year-old will act on are not the same skill."
  - q: "What if my child does not want a tutor?"
    a: "Common, and worth handling gently rather than pushing through. After a school move it is often less about tutoring and more about not wanting one more new adult, which is fair. The free 15-minute meeting exists for exactly that: your child meets the tutor with nothing committed. Most engage within two or three sessions once it is clear this is one person paying attention rather than more school. If they genuinely do not want to carry on, we pause."
