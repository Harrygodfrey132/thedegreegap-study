  - q: "Which part of GCSE English do Beckenham students find hardest?"
    a: "Usually the writing questions, because they start from nothing. Reading answers have an extract to lean on, but a story or an argument needs your child to plan and write against the clock, and on AQA's Language papers those two questions carry half the marks. Unseen poetry comes a close second in Literature, for much the same reason: no notes, no revision guide, just your child and a poem they've never read before."
  - q: "How much English work should my child do between lessons?"
    a: "About an hour a week is usually right. That might be one timed piece of writing, or learning six short quotations and writing a line on what each one shows. More than that tends to get rushed, and the point is for the tutor to mark something your child did on their own. If the week is packed with other homework, a short piece done well beats a long one finished at eleven at night."
  - q: "My son has dyslexia. Can a GCSE English tutor help with that?"
    a: "It can, and it's something we ask about on every first call. Some tutors on our platform have worked with lots of dyslexic teenagers, and they tend to teach English differently: planning out loud before anything's written, colour-coding the structure of a paragraph, and keeping quotation sets short. If your son has extra time or a reader in exams, his practice papers are done the same way, so the real thing feels familiar."
  - q: "My daughter doesn't want an English tutor. Is it worth trying anyway?"
