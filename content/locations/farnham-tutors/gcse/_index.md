faq_1_q: "Does applying to college change when we should start GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "It helps to start before the busiest stretch of Year 11. College applications go in during Year 11, and The Sixth Form College Farnborough talks applicants through their options at interview. The final word comes in August, when the GCSE results decide which courses your child can begin. Start in Year 10, or early in Year 11, and the tutor's got time to fix a weak subject properly rather than in a rush."

faq_2_a: "It depends on the plan, but Language usually comes first. Godalming College and The Sixth Form College Farnborough both warn that missing a 4 in English Language narrows the level 3 courses on offer. Literature matters more if English is on the A-Level list: Farnham College currently wants a 6 in English for its English Language and Literature A-Level. The two GCSEs reward different habits, so a tutor can split the time between them. We'll talk it through on the call."
faq_3_q: "My child is a long way behind. Is it too late for GCSE tutoring?"
faq_3_a: "No, it isn't. When a child is well behind, racing through this year's topics doesn't help. The tutor finds where things first stopped making sense, often something from Year 8 or 9, and builds up from there. Progress can feel slow at first, and it'll usually show in homework before it shows in a mock. And if the grades still fall short, there's a route at 16 too. Farnham College's STEP course is for young people with mostly 3s who want to retake their GCSEs full time."
faq_4_q: "What if the results aren't what my child needed?"
faq_4_a: "It happens, and there's usually a way forward. English Language and Maths are the only GCSEs that can be resat in November, and they're the two that college courses here ask about most. All Hallows, for example, helps sixth formers who missed a 4 in either keep going with it alongside their other courses. A tutor can work towards the November resit from September, at whatever pace your child needs. The sooner you get in touch after results day, the more time there is."
