  - q: "Can't my child just revise for English on their own?"
    a: "Some of it, yes, like learning quotations for Literature. But the Language papers are built around extracts your child hasn't seen, so there's nothing to memorise, and rereading notes won't move the grade much. What helps is writing answers and getting feedback on them, line by line. That's hard to get in a busy class, and hard for a parent to give without it turning into a row. A tutor reads what your child writes as they write it, points to the sentence where the marks stopped, and sets one short piece to try before next week."
  - q: "What actually happens in an English lesson?"
    a: "Most follow a simple shape. First, a look at what your child wrote since last time, marked together so they can see why. Then the main part: one skill, like opening a story well or choosing the right quotation, taught and then tried on a fresh extract. The last stretch is often timed, so the new habit gets tested under a bit of pressure. And every lesson's recorded, so your child can go back to an explanation that didn't quite land the first time."
