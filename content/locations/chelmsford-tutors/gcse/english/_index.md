The more useful point is that Language and Literature results often differ within the same student, and a combined impression hides that.   - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the better starting point, especially where Language is the weaker of the two. Language rewards stamina and a repeatable approach to unseen material, and both build through regular writing across months rather than through intensive work in the spring of Year 11. Starting in Year 10 also means arriving at mocks with a paragraph structure that is automatic. The sessions are calmer, since nothing is imminent, which suits students who have started to dread the subject rather than just find it hard."
  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "Twenty to forty minutes, and what it is matters far more than how long it takes. One planned paragraph written to time beats an hour of re-reading notes, because marks go to how an argument is built rather than to how much of the text is recalled. For Language, one timed response to an unseen extract each week is worth more than any amount of revision, since the skill is the response itself. Most tutors set the work at the end of the hour and mark it before you next meet."
  - q: "How do you vet your GCSE English tutors?"
