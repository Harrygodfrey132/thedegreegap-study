  - q: "Is Year 10 too early to start English tutoring?"
    a: "No. For English, Year 10 is often a better start than Year 11, and more so if Language is the weaker one. Language needs stamina and a dependable way into an extract your child has never read. Those come from months of steady practice, and a cram late in Year 11 can't build them. By the mocks, a Year 10 starter has a paragraph structure they don't have to think about. Lessons are calmer too, with nothing looming. That helps a child who's begun to dread English rather than simply find it hard."
  - q: "How much English homework will my child have between lessons?"
    a: "Usually between twenty and forty minutes. The kind of work counts for more than the time spent. A single paragraph, planned and written against the clock, does more good than an hour of rereading notes. That's because the marks reward how an argument is put together, not how much of the book your child can recall. For Language, the best homework is one timed answer to an unseen extract each week. Writing that answer is the skill. Tutors usually set it as the lesson ends and have it marked by the next one."
