  - q: "How many lessons will it take before my child's Maths grade moves?"
    a: "For one grade, eight to twelve weekly lessons is the usual guide, as long as your child's practising in between. It's quicker when the gap is exam technique, because habits change fast once your child has seen a mark scheme applied to their own work. It's slower when the gap goes back two or three years, since that has to be rebuilt before the marks follow. The first lesson is mostly about finding the gap, and the plan from lesson two follows what it finds, not a fixed programme. Nobody can promise a grade, but that's the rough shape of it."
  - q: "Is it a Maths tutor my child needs, or just more revision?"
    a: "The best sign is whether the effort's already there without results. If your child isn't revising at all, they don't need a tutor yet. They need a routine, and we'll say so on the call. If they've really put the hours in across two terms and the grade hasn't moved, that's a method problem, not a motivation one. And that's the kind of problem one-to-one lessons are good at fixing. It matters, because a tutor on top of no revision tends to give you one good hour a week and not much else."
  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help if my child has dyslexia, ADHD or a processing difference?"
    a: "Yes. Quite a few of the tutors we work with teach children who learn differently. In Maths, most of the help is about lightening the load. Your child sets out every question the same way, so no step gets lost. Longer questions get broken into stages, each with its own name. And one method gets practised until it's automatic, rather than three squeezed into an hour. If exams themselves are the hard part, timed work comes in slowly. On the call, tell us what school already does for your child, including any exam access arrangements, and we'll match with that in mind."
