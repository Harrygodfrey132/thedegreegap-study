  - q: "How do I work out which Maths topics are losing my child marks?"
    a: "Look at where the lost marks fall on a recent mock. Papers roughly get harder as they go. So marks dropped in the first third usually mean a basic method is missing, while marks dropped only near the end point to problem-solving and reading the question. Blank spaces tell you something different from crossed-out working, too. In the first lesson a tutor sorts the paper into those groups. Then they work on whichever group is costing most, rather than going through the textbook in order."
  - q: "Which paper should my child spend more time on?"
    a: "Start with whichever one is losing more marks, since the papers count the same. If your child's on AQA, Edexcel or OCR, that's three papers: one without a calculator and two with. The non-calculator paper tests arithmetic and algebra with nowhere to hide, so a child who leans on the calculator often loses more there. The calculator papers reward careful, checked working over several steps, and that's where rushing costs marks. The tutor compares the papers in the first lesson, then evens things out as the exam gets closer."
