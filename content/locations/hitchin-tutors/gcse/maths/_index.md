  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at where the lost marks fall on a recent mock. Papers roughly climb in difficulty, so marks dropped in the first third usually mean a basic method is missing, while marks dropped only near the end point to problem-solving and reading the question. Blank spaces tell you something different from crossed-out working. A tutor sorts the script into those groups in the first session, then targets the group that is costing the most rather than working through the textbook."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Neither on its own, because all three papers count equally. The non-calculator paper tests arithmetic and algebra with nowhere to hide, so students who lean on the calculator often lose more there. The calculator papers reward careful multi-step working and checking, which is where rushing costs marks. The tutor compares your child's scores across the papers in the first session and starts with the one losing most, before evening them out as the exam approaches."
