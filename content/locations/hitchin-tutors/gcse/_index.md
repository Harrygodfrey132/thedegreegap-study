faq_1_q: "When should a Hitchin student start GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10 is the easier start, because the harder content arrives that year and a gap fixed early stops costing marks across the whole of Year 11. In Hitchin there is a timing reason as well. Sixth form choices are made during Year 11, and the mock results that shape them come round quickly after the autumn. A Year 11 start still works for plenty of families. It simply means a tighter plan built around past papers rather than reteaching topics from scratch."
faq_2_q: "Do your tutors know the exam boards Hitchin schools use?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and the board is settled on the consultation call, before any profile reaches you. AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC phrase questions differently, set practicals differently and supply different formula sheets, so a tutor who knows your child's exact specification is useful from the first ten minutes. Schools do not always use the same board across subjects either. Bring a recent paper or the name on the front of a mock and we can confirm it in seconds."
faq_3_q: "She understands it in lessons but not in the exam. Can a tutor fix that?"
faq_3_a: "Often, and faster than a content gap. The tutor reads a marked script closely, the part a busy teacher rarely has time for, and looks at where the marks fall away. If the first half of the paper is strong and the last third is thin, that is timing. If answers are right but short of the marks, that is how they are written. Both respond to timed practice under exam conditions, usually within a term of weekly lessons."
