faq_1_q: "Is Year 11 leaving it late, with sixth form choices coming up?"
faq_1_a: "It's not too late, though Year 10 is the easier start. The harder content arrives in Year 10, and a gap fixed then stops costing marks right through Year 11. In Hitchin there's a timing reason too: sixth form choices are made during Year 11, and the mocks that shape them come round soon after the autumn. Starting in Year 11 still works for lots of families. The plan's just tighter, built around past papers rather than reteaching topics from scratch."
faq_2_q: "Hitchin's schools don't all use the same exam boards. Will the tutor know my child's?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, and we sort it out on the first call, before any profiles reach you. AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC word their questions differently and lay their papers out differently, so a tutor who knows your child's exact course is useful from the first ten minutes. Schools don't always use the same board for every subject, either. Have a recent mock paper handy on the call and we can confirm it in seconds."
faq_3_q: "She understands it in lessons but not in the exam. Can a tutor fix that?"
faq_3_a: "Often, yes. And it's usually quicker to fix than a gap in what she knows. The tutor goes through a marked paper closely, which a busy teacher rarely has time to do, and looks at where the marks fall away. If the first half is strong and the last third is thin, it's timing. If her answers are right but short of the marks, it's how they're written. Both improve with timed practice, usually within a term of weekly lessons."
