faqs:
  - q: "What do Hitchin students find hardest in GCSE English?"
    a: "Unseen material and the comparison questions, more than the set texts. Literature can be revised, however heavy the memory load, but unseen poetry and the Language extracts reward method alone. The other common casualty is the comparison task, where students write about each source in turn and forget to link them. Timing sits underneath all of it. Most Hitchin students we see understand the texts well enough. The marks go on structure and the last twenty minutes."
  - q: "How much English work should my child do between lessons?"
    a: "About an hour, rarely more than ninety minutes. In English that usually means one timed answer, written and handed in for marking, plus ten minutes on a short quotation list. Quality matters more than volume, because an essay the tutor marks line by line teaches more than three that nobody reads closely. In the final weeks before exams we sometimes set a second answer. Tutors adjust the load around coursework deadlines and mock weeks, so it sits alongside school work rather than competing with it."
  - q: "Does my child need a tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at what a recent essay shows. If the ideas are good but the answer runs out of time or wanders off the question, that is technique, and a tutor fixes technique quickly. If your child cannot say what a poem is about or remembers no quotations, more revision may be the first step, with a tutor setting its shape. If mocks are already comfortably where they need to be, a few focused sessions on one paper usually beat a full term."
  - q: "Can a tutor support a child with dyslexia or ADHD in English?"
    a: "Yes, and several tutors on our platform do this often. For English the useful changes are practical: quotations learned in small, repeated sets, essay plans built on a fixed template so the structure is not a fresh decision each time, and extracts broken into chunks on the whiteboard. Recorded lessons help a student who loses track mid-explanation. Let us know about any exam access arrangements, such as extra time or a reader, and we will match and plan around them."
  - q: "What if the tutor turns out not to suit my child?"
    a: "Then we change tutor, at no cost and without awkwardness. The free 15-minute video meeting is there to catch most mismatches before a paid lesson, and after that a quick message to us is enough. Harry or Joe will suggest someone else, usually within a day. It happens now and then, and it is better said early than after weeks of lessons that are not landing. Your child's comfort with the tutor affects how much they write, which in English is most of the point."
