faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English do Hitchin children find hardest?"
    a: "Unseen texts and the comparison questions, more than the set texts. Literature can be revised, however much there is to remember. Unseen poetry and the Language extracts only reward method. The other common casualty is comparison, where your child writes about each source in turn and forgets to link them. And timing's underneath all of it. Most Hitchin children we work with understand the texts well enough. The marks go on structure and on the last twenty minutes."
  - q: "How much English homework will the tutor set?"
    a: "About an hour a week, and rarely more than ninety minutes. In English that usually means one timed answer, written and sent over for marking, plus ten minutes on a short quotation list. It's quality that counts, not volume. One essay the tutor marks line by line teaches more than three nobody reads closely. In the last few weeks before the exams, the tutor might set a second one. And tutors lighten the load around school deadlines and mock weeks, so it sits alongside school work instead of competing with it."
  - q: "How do I know whether my child needs a tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at what a recent essay shows. If the ideas are good but the answer runs out of time or drifts off the question, that's technique, and a tutor can sort technique fairly quickly. If your child can't say what a poem's about or remembers no quotations, more revision may come first, with a tutor giving it a shape. And if mocks are already comfortably where they need to be, a few focused lessons on one paper usually beat a full term."
  - q: "Will English lessons work for a child with dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes, and it's something several tutors on our platform do often. For English the useful changes are practical. Quotations learnt in small sets and repeated. Essay plans built on a fixed template, so the structure isn't a fresh decision every time. Extracts broken into chunks on the whiteboard. The lesson replay helps too, if your child loses track halfway through an explanation. Tell us about any exam access arrangements, like extra time or a reader, and we'll plan around them."
  - q: "What if my child and the tutor just don't hit it off?"
    a: "We'll change tutor, at no cost and with no awkwardness. The free 15-minute video meeting catches most mismatches before a paid lesson, and after that a quick message to us is enough. Harry or Joe will suggest someone else, usually within a day. It happens now and then, and it's better said early than after weeks of lessons that aren't landing. How comfortable your child feels with the tutor affects how much they write. In English, that's most of the point."
