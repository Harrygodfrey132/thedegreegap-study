faq_1_q: "How early should we start: Year 12 or Year 13?"
faq_1_a: "Year 12 for most families, ideally before the first round of internal assessments. Predicted grades lean heavily on Year 12 and early Year 13, and universities see them in the autumn. So work in the first two terms of Year 12 counts for the application as much as the exam. Starting in Year 13 still helps your child's final result. But by then the prediction's usually been sent, and the tutor's playing catch-up instead of building."
faq_2_q: "Can tutoring change the predicted grade my child's school sends to UCAS?"
faq_2_a: "Not directly. The prediction comes from mock results and teacher judgement, and tutoring can change the work those are based on. The tutor goes through your child's recent marked work to see where the marks leaked, then practises those questions against the clock until the next assessment reads differently. You'll also have something specific to bring up at parents' evening: particular questions, not a hoped-for grade. That takes a few months, so for the prediction, the autumn of Year 13 is about as late as tutoring can help."
faq_3_q: "He did well at GCSE. Why is A-Level suddenly so hard?"
faq_3_a: "Because the two exams reward different habits. GCSE papers mostly tell him which technique a question wants, and a bright teenager gets quick at spotting it. A-Level questions leave that choice to him, put more marks on reasoning and evaluation than on the final answer, and run for longer. Clever children who relied on spotting the pattern tend to feel this most. It doesn't mean he picked the wrong subject. It's a skill, and a tutor can teach it."
faq_4_q: "My son wants to apply to MBDA as well as to university. Can you help with both?"
faq_4_a: "Yes. Schemes like MBDA's in Stevenage set their own grade requirements and often close before the UCAS deadline, with online tests and interviews along the way. The tutor's job is the grades that get your son's application read. Mention both routes on the first call. Harry or Joe will talk you through the timings and the written answers, and how to keep the university application going alongside so neither door closes early."
