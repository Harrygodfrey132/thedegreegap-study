faq_1_q: "When is the right time to start A-Level tutoring, Year 12 or Year 13?"
faq_1_a: "Year 12 for most students, and ideally before the first set of internal assessments. Predicted grades lean heavily on Year 12 performance and the early part of Year 13, and universities see them in the autumn, so work done in the first two terms counts for the application as much as the exam. Starting in Year 13 still lifts the final result. By then, though, the prediction has usually been sent and the tutoring is catching up rather than building."
faq_2_q: "Can tutoring change the predicted grade my child's school sends to UCAS?"
faq_2_a: "It can change what the prediction is based on, which is mock results and teacher judgement. The tutor goes through recent marked work to see where the marks actually leaked, then practises that under timed conditions until the next assessment reads differently. It also gives you something concrete to raise at parents' evening, about particular questions rather than a hoped-for grade. That needs a few months, so the autumn of Year 13 is about as late as it helps."
faq_3_q: "He did well at GCSE. Why is A-Level suddenly so hard?"
faq_3_a: "Because the two exams reward different habits. GCSE papers mostly tell a student which technique a question wants, and a strong student learns to spot that quickly. A-Level questions leave the choice to them, put more marks on reasoning and evaluation than on the final answer, and run for longer. Clever students who relied on spotting the pattern tend to feel this most. It is not a sign he chose the wrong subject. It is a skill a tutor can teach."

faq_4_a: "Yes. Schemes like MBDA's in Stevenage set their own grade requirements and often close before the UCAS deadline, with online tests and interviews along the way.
