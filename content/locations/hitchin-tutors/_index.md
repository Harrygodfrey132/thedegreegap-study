faq_1_q: "How does the Hitchin sixth form consortium work?"
faq_1_a: "Hitchin Boys', Hitchin Girls' and The Priory plan their sixth form subjects together, so your child can take a course that's only taught at one or two of the three. Classes mix young people from all three schools. The consortium's standard entry requirement is five GCSEs at grades 9 to 4, including English and Maths at grade 4 or above, with an average points score of at least 4. Most courses add their own requirement on top. It's worth checking this year's course list, not last year's."
faq_2_q: "Is everything really online, and what do the lessons run on?"
faq_2_a: "Yes, all of them. Your child and the tutor meet in Lessonspace, an online classroom, and write on the same whiteboard, so the tutor watches the working appear line by line. Every lesson's recorded for watching back before a test. You won't need to download anything. A laptop's enough. Families in Hitchin, Letchworth and the villages around all use it, and after each lesson you'll get a short note on what was covered."
faq_3_q: "My child's quite far behind. Is it too late to help?"
faq_3_a: "Almost never, though it changes what the first term looks like. When a gap goes back a year or two, the tutor traces your child's mistakes backwards until they find the point where the understanding ran out. That's often further back than the school report suggests. The plan builds forward from there, alongside school rather than repeating it. It takes more weeks than polishing exam technique does, and we'll tell you which of the two we think is going on."
faq_4_q: "What will it cost us, and is there a contract?"
faq_4_a: "No contract, and lessons start from £37 an hour, depending on how experienced the tutor is. We agree the rate with you before anything's booked. It covers the live online lesson, the tutor's preparation, the practice set afterwards and the note home. There's no joining fee or materials charge either. You pay one lesson at a time, so you can pause for a holiday, stop once a grade feels secure, or come back before the mocks."
