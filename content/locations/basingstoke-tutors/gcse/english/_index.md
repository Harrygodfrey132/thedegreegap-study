  - q: "How much English work should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Sixty to ninety minutes, and it should be writing rather than reading. One timed paragraph or one full question, done under the clock, is worth more than an evening spent re-reading notes and highlighting quotations. Re-reading feels productive and changes very little, which is exactly why a student can revise for hours and watch the grade stay put. Beyond ninety minutes the returns fall away sharply."
  - q: "How can I tell which English topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at the mark breakdown rather than the total, then at where in the answer the marks stopped. Strong scores on the shorter reading questions followed by a drop on the extended response is a structure problem. Marked down across the board on AO2 means describing rather than analysing. And an answer that thins out badly in its final paragraphs is a timing problem wearing an analysis problem's clothes. Bring a marked paper and the pattern is usually visible in minutes."
