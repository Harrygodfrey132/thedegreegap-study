faq_1_q: "My child sits a mix of GCSEs and IGCSEs. Will the tutor know which is which?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, and we check before suggesting anyone. Some Hampstead independents teach International GCSEs, often called IGCSEs, in a few subjects and ordinary GCSEs in the rest, and the papers aren't the same. Edexcel's International GCSE in Maths, for example, allows a calculator on both papers and includes some differentiation, which the UK GCSE doesn't. If you can find the board and the course code on a timetable or a past paper, we'll match to that."
faq_2_q: "Should we start before the Year 11 mocks, or wait to see the results?"
faq_2_a: "Before, if you can, and Year 10 or the start of Year 11 is ideal. A tutor who starts in September can work on the weakest topics before the mocks, and the mock then shows what's left to do. Starting after the results still helps, because the marked papers make a very clear plan. It just means the grades a sixth form reads over the winter will reflect the mock as it went."
faq_3_q: "How does a tutor fit in without piling more work onto my child?"
faq_3_a: "By replacing some of the revision rather than adding to it. The tutor looks at your child's homework and recent tests, finds the topic losing the most marks, and spends the hour there. Practice between lessons is usually about half an hour, aimed at that one topic. Most children find it cuts the time they spend revising things they already know. If weekdays are full, a weekend lesson often fits better."
