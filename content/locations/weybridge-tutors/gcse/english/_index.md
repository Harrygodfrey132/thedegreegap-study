faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Weybridge students?"
    a: "The comparison question in Language Paper 2 and the unseen poetry in Literature catch the most students. Comparison needs a clear view of how two writers differ in attitude and method, written as one argument. Unseen poetry leaves nothing to revise, only a method to apply under time pressure. Closed-book Literature adds the memory load of quotations. All three improve with timed practice and honest marking, which is where a tutor makes the quickest gains."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read a lot?"
    a: "Because reading well and writing exam answers are separate skills. Many keen readers never learned how to plan an essay, so their ideas come out in the order they thought of them rather than as an argument. Others know the text well but spend half the answer retelling it. A tutor's first job is to read a recent essay and find the gap, whether that is planning, analysis of language or timing, then work on that one thing until it improves."
  - q: "How much GCSE English homework should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Around an hour to an hour and a half a week is plenty. Usually that means one timed essay or one Language question on the skill covered in the lesson, plus a few quotations to learn. More than that tends to produce tired writing rather than better writing. The tutor marks the work and goes through it at the start of the next session, so every piece of homework feeds straight into the next lesson."
