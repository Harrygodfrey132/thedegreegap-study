faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English do Weybridge teenagers find hardest?"
    a: "Usually the comparison question on Language Paper 2 and the unseen poetry in Literature. Comparing needs a clear view of how two writers differ in attitude and method, written as one argument. Unseen poetry leaves nothing to revise, just a method your child has to use under time pressure. And the Literature exams are closed book, so the quotations have to come from memory. All three get better with timed practice and honest marking, which is where a tutor makes the quickest gains."
  - q: "My child's always got a book on the go. Why is GCSE English so hard for them?"
    a: "Because being a good reader and writing a good exam answer don't always go together, at least not straight away. Lots of keen readers haven't yet got a way of planning an essay, so their ideas come out in the order they thought of them rather than as an argument. Sorting that out takes one-to-one time, and a busy classroom doesn't have much of it. Other children know the text well but spend half the answer retelling it. The tutor reads a recent essay, works out whether it's planning, analysis or timing, and sticks with that one thing until it improves."
  - q: "Will the tutor set homework, and how much?"
    a: "Yes, and around an hour to an hour and a half a week is plenty. Usually that's one timed essay or one Language question on the skill from the lesson, plus a few quotations to learn. Much more than that tends to produce tired writing, not better writing. The tutor marks it and goes through it at the start of the next lesson, so everything your child does feeds straight into the next hour."
