faqs:
  - q: "Does it matter whether my child's on AQA, Edexcel or OCR for A-Level Maths?"
    a: "Yes, more than it looks. The maths itself is almost the same. What changes is how questions are worded, how much working earns the method marks, which results are printed in the formula booklet, and how the large data set is used in Statistics. That's a set of real figures each board picks for its own Statistics questions. So a child who's only practised one board's papers can drop marks on another board's wording while doing the same maths. We'll confirm the board before suggesting anyone."
  - q: "My child's doing Further Maths too. Can one tutor cover both?"
    a: "Usually, yes, and it's often the better set-up. The two courses overlap, so one tutor can plan them side by side rather than covering things twice, and your child isn't juggling two ways of explaining the same idea. It does narrow the choice, because not every A-Level Maths tutor is comfortable at Further Maths level. So mention it on the call. It changes who we'd suggest, not just what gets covered."
  - q: "Can I watch one of my child's Maths lessons?"
    a: "Of course. Some parents join the first one to see how it works. After that, most A-Level students work better without an audience. It matters more than usual in Maths, because a child who's being watched won't risk getting stuck, and being stuck is where the useful work happens. Every lesson has a replay, so you can see any of it later. The tutor also sends a short note on what was covered and what was set."
  - q: "My child has ADHD, dyslexia or a processing difference. Can an A-Level Maths tutor still help?"
    a: "Yes. It's something plenty of the tutors on our platform do regularly. At A-Level it's mostly about lightening the load. The tutor keeps a steady layout so long working doesn't get lost, splits long questions into named stages, and sticks with one method until it's automatic rather than rushing three in an hour. Timed practice comes in gradually if exam conditions are the hard part. It helps to tell us on the call what support school already gives."
  - q: "Which part of A-Level Maths do Banbury sixth formers find hardest?"
    a: "The start of Year 12 catches most of them out. GCSE rewards recognising a type of question. A-Level asks your child to pick a method when nothing says which one. After that, the lost marks pile up in whichever area they've neglected. Usually that's Mechanics for those who don't like drawing a diagram of the situation, or Statistics for those who skim the wording. Both are habits, not ability, and that's why they improve."
