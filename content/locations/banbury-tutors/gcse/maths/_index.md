faqs:
  - q: "What part of GCSE Maths do Banbury children find hardest?"
    a: "The non-calculator paper, more often than any one topic. It sits underneath everything else. If your child is unsure with fractions or negatives, they'll lose marks inside geometry, ratio and algebra questions, even though those topics aren't the real problem. After that, it's usually algebra, circle theorems and the long problem-solving questions at the back of each paper. They're all about method more than understanding. That's why they tend to come on faster than parents expect, once someone works on them properly."
  - q: "Does it matter whether my child's school uses AQA, Edexcel or OCR?"
    a: "Yes, more than most parents expect. The Maths itself is almost the same, but the boards word their questions differently and build their longer problems in different ways. A child who's practised on one board's papers can be thrown by another's wording, while knowing exactly the same Maths. So we check your child's board on the call before suggesting anyone, and match on it. Knowing a board's habits is worth a lot in the last two terms before the exams."
  - q: "How can I tell if my child needs a tutor rather than more practice?"
    a: "Try this quick test. Give your child a past-paper question they got wrong, with no help, and watch the first thirty seconds. If they make a start, go wrong partway and can roughly see where, that's a practice problem, and more marked papers may sort it. If they stare at it, or ask which topic it is, the method isn't there to practise yet. Then a tutor is by far the quicker route, because repetition on its own rarely fixes it."
  - q: "Will we get a qualified teacher or a recent graduate?"

If your child needs arithmetic and algebra rebuilt from the ground up, a qualified teacher who's taught it in that order many times is usually the better fit.   - q: "What if my child's results don't reach the grade they needed?"
