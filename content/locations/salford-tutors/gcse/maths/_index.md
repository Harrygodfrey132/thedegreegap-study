faqs:
  - q: "What GCSE Maths grade does my child need for A-Level Maths at Pendleton?"
    a: "A 6, according to the college's own course page, alongside five GCSEs at grade 5 or above including English Language and what the college calls a G-Score of 6. The same 6 in Maths appears on its Biology, Chemistry and Physics pages, so Maths matters even for a child set on a science. Pendleton also runs a Maths assessment in the first few weeks of Year 12. Requirements can change, so check the course page the year your child applies."
  - q: "Calculator or non-calculator: which Maths paper should my child work on first?"
    a: "Neither on its own, because all three papers count equally: one non-calculator and two calculator, so the calculator papers carry two-thirds of the grade between them. The non-calculator paper rewards quick, accurate arithmetic and confident algebra. The calculator papers reward working through multi-step problems without losing your place. Most students drop more marks early on the non-calculator paper and late on the final calculator one, and a tutor's first lesson shows which is true for your child."
  - q: "How many Maths lessons does it usually take to move up a grade?"
    a: "Often around 8 to 12 weekly lessons for one grade band, as long as your child does some practice between them. It can be quicker when the problem is exam technique, like rushing or not showing working. It takes longer when the gap goes back to Year 8 topics such as negative numbers or fractions, because those need rebuilding first. The first lesson works out which it is, and the plan from week two follows from that."
  - q: "Is the Maths tutor likely to be a teacher, or someone who sat their GCSEs more recently?"
    a: "Both, depending on what suits your child. Whoever it is, they'll have at least two years of tutoring behind them and will have sat an interview with Harry or Joe. For a child who's lost confidence we'd lean towards someone calm and patient; for a child chasing a 6, someone who knows the harder questions inside out."
