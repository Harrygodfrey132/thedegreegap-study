faq_1_q: "My son's predicted 4s, and the Pendleton course he wants asks for 5s. Can that gap close in time?"
faq_1_a: "Often it can, because a 4 and a 5 are sometimes only a few marks apart, and those marks tend to sit in a few repeatable places. Nobody can guarantee a grade. But a tutor who goes through his mocks question by question can usually point to where the missing marks are and practise exactly those. Check the course page first, too: Economics at Pendleton asks for 5s in English and Maths but accepts 4s elsewhere, while Biology and Maths want five 5s and a 6 in Maths on top."
faq_2_q: "Which exam board does my child sit, and does the tutor need to know?"
faq_2_a: "It matters more than most parents expect. Salford schools choose their own boards subject by subject, so your child might sit AQA for English and Edexcel for Maths, and the papers word their questions differently. English Literature set texts differ from school to school as well. If you're not sure, a recent mock paper usually says the board on the front. We confirm it on the first call and match the tutor to it."
