faqs:
  - q: "Where do Salford students tend to lose the most marks in GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually the long writing questions, because they carry so many marks and they're the ones most often rushed at the end. Next comes the comparison question in English Language, where two sources get summarised but never compared. In Literature it's the essay that retells the plot instead of making a point about it. A tutor can see which of these is costing your child most from one marked mock, and that's where the lessons start."
  - q: "My child reads all the time. Why are they struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Because reading well and writing a timed exam answer are different skills, and the second one is rarely taught step by step. A lot of keen readers have never been shown how to plan an essay, so they write everything they notice and run out of time before reaching a point. Others answer the question they expected rather than the one on the paper. The tutor's first job is finding which of those is happening."
  - q: "Does it matter whether my child sits AQA or Edexcel for English Language?"
    a: "Yes, because the papers are built differently. On AQA, Paper 1 is modern fiction with creative writing and Paper 2 pairs two non-fiction sources with a piece of viewpoint writing. Edexcel's Paper 1 uses a 19th-century fiction extract with imaginative writing, and its Paper 2 is non-fiction with transactional writing such as a letter or speech. The skills overlap, but the question styles don't. We'll check the board on the first call, and the tutor we suggest will know that paper."
  - q: "My son has dyslexia. Can an English tutor help with the writing papers?"
    a: "Yes. Say so on the call and we'll only put forward tutors who've taught dyslexic teenagers before. If your son has extra time or a reader in exams, that's arranged by his school, but the tutor can practise with exactly the same conditions so nothing feels new on the day."
  - q: "What if my child says they don't want an English tutor?"
