faq_1_q: "Is Year 9 too soon to think about GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Not if a subject's already slipping. Year 9 is usually when options are chosen. A child who's decided they're bad at something can drop it for the wrong reason, or keep it and dread it for two years. A few months of steady help on the shaky part can change how that subject feels before the GCSE content starts piling up. And if nothing's wrong yet, there's no need to start early."
faq_2_q: "When do the Year 11 mocks happen, and should we start before them?"
faq_2_a: "It varies, so ask your child's school for the dates, though plenty run Year 11 mocks in the winter. Starting a term or so before gives the tutor time to find what's costing marks and practise full papers against the clock, instead of cramming in the last week. If the mocks have already happened, that's useful in its own way: the marked papers are the best place to begin."
faq_3_q: "Triple science means three grades. Do we need three tutors?"
faq_3_a: "Usually not. Some tutors are strong in all three sciences, but if one is clearly slipping, a Physics or Chemistry tutor for that one often makes more sense. With triple science each grade stands alone, so a weak Chemistry grade can't hide behind a good Biology one. Combined Science is different: it's a double award, graded something like 6-5, and one tutor usually covers it well."
