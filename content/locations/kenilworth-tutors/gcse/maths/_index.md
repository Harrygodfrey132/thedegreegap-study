faqs:
  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE Maths for a Kenilworth Year 11?"
    a: "It depends on the child, but a few topics come up again and again. On Higher papers it's often algebraic fractions and the worded problems near the end, where the maths is hidden inside a paragraph. On Foundation it's more often fractions and ratio. Whichever board Kenilworth School or your child's school uses, the topic list is much the same; the difference is how questions are worded and marked."
  - q: "Is the non-calculator paper where my child should start?"
    a: "Probably, at first. AQA, Edexcel and OCR all set three papers, one without a calculator and two with, and all three papers count equally. Most children lose more marks early on the non-calculator paper, on arithmetic and algebra they rush. The calculator papers reward careful working through longer problems instead. A tutor looks at your child's last mock to see which paper is costing more, and starts there."
  - q: "Why does my child find Maths so hard when they try so hard?"
    a: "Usually because something from earlier never settled. Maths stacks up: a shaky grip on negative numbers in Year 7 turns into trouble with algebra in Year 9 and with quadratics in Year 11. Working harder at this year's topic won't fix last year's gap, which is why effort alone can feel so unrewarding. The tutor's first job is finding the oldest wobbly idea and rebuilding from there."

  - q: "Can a Kenilworth family do GCSE Maths tutoring on a tight budget?"
