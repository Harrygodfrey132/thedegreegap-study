  - q: "My son is at Southborough and wants A-Level Economics. Why does his English grade matter?"
    a: "Because Southborough currently asks for a 6 in GCSE English before A-Level Economics, and prefers a 5 in Maths. Economics is an essay subject, so the school wants to see he can build an argument in writing. If his English sits around a 5, that's the grade to work on in Year 11. A tutor would focus on the longer answers in Language and the Literature essays, where most of the marks are. Check the school's current course list, as it can change."
  - q: "How many quotations does my child actually need to learn for English Literature?"
    a: "Fewer than most children think. Eight to ten short quotations per text, plus a line or two from each poem, is usually plenty, as long as they're flexible. A quotation about guilt that also shows power can serve three different essay questions. Short ones are easier to remember and easier to analyse, often down to a single word. The tutor helps your child choose them, then tests them in small amounts each week so they're still there in May."
  - q: "What happens in an online English lesson?"
    a: "Usually three parts. The tutor starts with last week's work, often a paragraph your child wrote, and talks through what earned marks and what didn't. Then it's the skill that needs the most time, such as analysing language or planning a creative piece. The last part is writing to time, straight onto the shared whiteboard, so the tutor can mark it there and then. Every lesson is recorded, so your child can replay the explanation before the next essay."
