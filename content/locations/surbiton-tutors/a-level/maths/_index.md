  - q: "Which calculator should my child have for A-Level Maths?"
    a: "A scientific calculator that handles statistical distributions, and most sixth forms will name one. Exam rules don't allow calculators that do algebra symbolically, such as solving or differentiating with letters, so a computer algebra model is out. Whatever your child uses, they should practise with it all year rather than meet its statistics menus a week before the exam. A tutor can show which functions save time, and which questions still need every step written out."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs an A-Level Maths tutor rather than more practice?"
    a: "Look at where the marks went on a recent test. If the method is right and the slips are arithmetic, more practice and better checking may be enough. If whole questions are blank, or the working stops halfway through a proof or an integral, that's understanding, and repeating papers tends to repeat the gap. A tutor finds the missing step in the first lesson. And if your child is near the top already, targeted work on the hardest questions can be plenty."
