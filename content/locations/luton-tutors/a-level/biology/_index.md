faqs:
  - q: "Is there one part of A-Level Biology that Luton sixth formers find harder than the rest?"
    a: "The questions that join topics up, more than any one topic. Your child can know cell division, inheritance and ecology separately and still freeze when a single question needs all three, which is exactly what the synoptic long answers do. Close behind are the statistics questions, which turn up in the middle of a genetics or ecology paper and ask your child to choose a test, run it and say what the result means. For anyone who started the course on the grade 6 in GCSE Maths that Luton Sixth Form College asks for, that's often where the first mock goes wrong."
  - q: "My child's Biology mock came back low. How do we find out why?"
    a: "Go through the paper question by question rather than looking at the grade. Short recall questions dropped means content to relearn. Marks lost on the longer written answers usually means your child knows the Biology but writes it out of order or misses the key terms. Blank statistics questions point to maths. Questions on the practicals, AQA's required practicals or OCR's PAGs, point to method and evaluation. Bring the paper to the first lesson and the tutor will build the plan from what it shows."
  - q: "Why has my child's predicted Biology grade come out lower than we hoped?"
    a: "Because a prediction is built on timed work, and Biology rewards precise writing under time more than most subjects. Your child can understand a lesson and still lose marks for writing that energy is made rather than released, or for running out of time before the long answer. The end-of-Year 12 exams usually carry a lot of weight too, because predictions for UCAS get written soon after them. A tutor reads the recent papers, finds which of these is costing marks, and works on it before the next assessment the prediction draws on."
  - q: "Can an A-Level Biology tutor help a child with dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes. Tell us on the call and we'll suggest tutors who've worked with neurodivergent sixth formers before. Biology has a lot of reading and a lot of names, so the tutor tends to turn pathways into flowcharts on the whiteboard, break long answers into labelled steps and keep a short list of the exact words mark schemes want. Every lesson is recorded, so an explanation can be watched again the night before a test. If your child has extra time or a reader in exams, practice papers can be done the same way."
  - q: "Can Biology lessons carry on over the summer before Year 13?"
    a: "Yes, and for Biology that summer is worth using. Year 13 content leans hard on Year 12, so a few weeks back on respiration, inheritance or the statistics in August means September starts on firmer ground. Some families keep one lesson a week going through the holidays, while others book a short block before term starts. You agree times with the tutor directly, so lessons can fit around family plans and any summer job. Easter of Year 13 is the other busy point, usually for full past papers."
