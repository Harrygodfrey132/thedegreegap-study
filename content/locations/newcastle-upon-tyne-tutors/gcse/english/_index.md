Newcastle Sixth Form College sets a requirement for every A-Level it offers, and Gosforth Academy publishes them course by course. And from the winter mock to the summer exam is roughly sixteen weeks, which is time to work on a grade if you start now."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Should we wait?"
    a: "No, and English rewards an early start more than most subjects. The question types in Year 10 are the same ones your child will meet in the exam. So if they practise them over two years, the structure's automatic by Year 11, and that whole year is free for the texts. Year 10 lessons are calmer too. One question type a week, marked and redrafted, rather than the rush through past papers a Year 11 start often turns into. The set texts usually start in Year 10 as well, and a tutor who knows them from the first chapter is worth having."
  - q: "How much time will English homework take each week?"
    a: "About an hour, and rarely more than ninety minutes. Usually that's one timed answer to the question type from that week's lesson, written against the clock rather than spread over an evening. Plus one redraft of the answer the tutor marked. The timing is the point. An answer that takes forty minutes at home, when the exam only leaves time for twelve, hasn't really been practised. Beyond ninety minutes it stops helping, and your child starts to resent English. That costs more than the extra practice gains."
  - q: "Is the tutor likely to be a teacher or someone who's just left university?"
    a: "Either, depending on what's going wrong. If a text has never really opened up for your child, a qualified teacher who's taught it a dozen times knows where children lose the thread. If the ideas are there but the marks aren't, a recent graduate with the current mark scheme fresh in their mind is often the better match. Some of our tutors are still at university, too. Everyone on our platform has tutored for at least two years and was interviewed on how clearly they explain as well as what they know. And you'll see two or three profiles and choose."
  - q: "What if my child's English result in August isn't what they needed?"
    a: "There's a November resit for English Language, and we help Newcastle upon Tyne families from results day right through to it. An eight-week block in September and October is usually aimed at lifting a 3 to a 4, or a 4 to a 5. The work is narrow: the same question types, timed, marked and redrafted. Newcastle Sixth Form College even runs a Year 12 Access course for anyone who needs to lift their GCSE results before starting A-Levels. That shows how common this is. And if your child needed a 6 for an A-Level English place and got a 5, we'll talk to the sixth form with you about a plan."
