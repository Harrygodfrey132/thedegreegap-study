  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Maths for Newcastle upon Tyne students?"
    a: "The questions where nothing announces the method. Ratio and proportion chains, forming an equation from a worded problem, and on the higher paper the circle theorem proofs and the algebraic proof near the back are where most Newcastle scripts go quiet after the first line. On foundation the same thing happens on the five-mark problem-solving questions, and it costs more there because the grade 4 boundary is decided on them. In a city where results sit below the national picture, the tier decision matters as much as any topic, and it is made in Year 10, so it is worth making on purpose."
  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at where the marks stop on a marked mock, not at the total. If the first line of the long questions is there and the rest is blank, the problem is carrying a method through, which is exactly what the first session works on. If whole questions are untouched, that is a topic gap, and the question numbers tell you which topic. If the working runs to the end and the answer is wrong, it is accuracy and checking, which is the quickest fix of the three. Bring the paper to the call and we will read it with you."
  - q: "Does my child need a GCSE Maths tutor, or just more practice?"
    a: "The working answers that. Clean working that loses marks at the top end of the paper is a technique gap, and a tutor fixes it fastest because the student already has the Maths. Blank or crossed-out working on the multi-step questions is a method gap, and practice on your own tends to practise the wrong thing, so that is where a tutor is essential. A student already scoring highly across mocks needs timed papers and a marker, not a full programme. Say on the call which of those you are seeing and we will be honest about which it is."

Tell us on the consultation call what school has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we match with that in mind."
