  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Loughborough students?"
    a: "Unseen poetry and the comparison question, especially for students whose strengths are elsewhere. Unseen poetry is hard because there is nothing to revise, so a hard-working student feels ambushed by it. Comparison is hard because it wants a genuinely different paragraph shape, one that holds two texts at once rather than walking through them in turn. Both are learnable and both move faster than people expect, because they are technique problems rather than knowledge problems."
  - q: "How can I tell which English topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at the mark breakdown rather than the total, then at where in the answer the marks stopped. Strong scores on the shorter reading questions followed by a drop on the extended response is a structure problem. Marked down across the board on AO2 means describing rather than analysing. An answer that thins out badly in its final paragraphs is a timing problem wearing an analysis problem's clothes. Bring a marked paper to the consultation and the pattern is usually visible in minutes."
