faqs:
  - q: "Which bits of GCSE English trip up a Maths-minded Loughborough teenager?"
    a: "Usually the questions with no single right answer. On AQA's first Language paper, that's the evaluation question, where your child weighs up a statement about how the writer creates an effect. In Literature, it's the essays, which want an argument that runs from the first line to the last. Both feel open-ended, and open-ended is exactly what a logical child dislikes. But each has a shape that can be learnt and practised. Once your child can see it, they tend to relax."
  - q: "What should I look for on a marked mock?"
    a: "The mark for each question, rather than the total. Good marks on the short reading questions, then a drop on the longest one, usually means structure: the ideas are there, but the answer isn't built to score. Low marks on every analysis question often means your child is describing what happens rather than how the writer does it. If the last answer is noticeably thinner, that's timing. The tutor will go through a marked mock in the first lesson and tell you what they find."
