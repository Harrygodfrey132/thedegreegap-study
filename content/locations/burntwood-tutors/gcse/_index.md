faq_1_q: "Chase Terrace and Erasmus Darwin use different boards for some GCSEs. Does that matter for tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "It does, more in English and Science than in Maths. Both schools sit Edexcel GCSE Maths, but Erasmus Darwin takes AQA English and Edexcel Science, while Chase Terrace takes Edexcel English and AQA Science. The choice of set texts, the wording of questions and the way marks are split all change with the board. So we ask which board each subject uses and match the tutor to it. If you're not sure, the front of a recent mock paper will say."
faq_2_q: "My child is only in Year 9. Is that too soon for a GCSE tutor?"
faq_2_a: "Not if something's already wobbling. Both Burntwood schools choose GCSE options in the spring term of Year 9, and at Chase Terrace, separate Biology, Chemistry and Physics is for pupils who've done well right through Year 9. A few months spent on a shaky Maths or Science topic at that point can keep the door open. For most children, though, Year 10 is plenty of time, and Year 11 still works with a sharper plan."
faq_3_q: "What happens if my child misses the grade their sixth form asked for?"
faq_3_a: "Talk to the sixth form straight away, because some will look again at a near miss or suggest a different mix of subjects. If it's English or Maths below a 4, your child will carry on studying it after 16 and resit, with the first chance in November. A tutor can start on that resit in September, working on the questions that cost them marks in the summer, so the November paper isn't a repeat of June."
