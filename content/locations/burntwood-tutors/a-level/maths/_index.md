faqs:
  - q: "Which parts of A-Level Maths do Burntwood students find hardest?"
    a: "Mechanics surprises the most people, because it asks your child to model a situation before calculating anything, and GCSE rarely did that. Proof is the other one: writing a clear argument rather than finding a number. On AQA both turn up across the three papers, alongside calculus that mixes several rules in a single question. A tutor works mostly on setting the problem up, which is where the marks usually go, and less on the arithmetic at the end."
  - q: "Year 12 or Year 13: when should my child's A-Level Maths lessons begin?"
    a: "Year 12 if you can, because the topics build on each other and a shaky first term makes everything after it harder. Erasmus Darwin runs formal assessment weeks twice a year, and how your child does in Year 12 shapes what teachers predict for UCAS. Starting in Year 13 still helps, especially for full-paper practice before the mocks. After about Christmas of Year 13 it becomes a shorter, sharper revision plan."
  - q: "My child does hours of Maths practice. Do they really need a tutor?"
    a: "Maybe not, and it's worth checking before you spend anything. Get out a recent paper and look at the working. If questions are left blank or abandoned halfway, a tutor helps far more, because the problem is getting started. If your child is already scoring well right across the paper, practice alone may be enough."
