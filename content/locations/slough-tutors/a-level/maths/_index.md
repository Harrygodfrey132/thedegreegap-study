  - q: "How much A-Level Maths homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "About an hour to ninety minutes, set by the tutor and marked before the next session. Usually that is two or three exam questions on the topic from the lesson, done under something like timed conditions, plus one question from an older topic so nothing quietly disappears. More than ninety minutes tends to produce tired working rather than better working. The marking is the point. A wrong answer nobody looks at teaches the wrong method twice."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs an A-Level Maths tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at the working on a recent paper, not the score. Clean working that runs out of marks near the end of a question is an exam technique gap, and a tutor is the fastest fix for it. Blank space or crossed-out starts on the multi-step questions is a method confidence gap, and revision alone rarely closes it, because the student does not know which method to revise. A student already scoring well across Pure, Mechanics and Statistics probably needs practice papers and a marker, not a programme, and we will say so."

References are checked afterwards. Nobody joins on a degree alone."
