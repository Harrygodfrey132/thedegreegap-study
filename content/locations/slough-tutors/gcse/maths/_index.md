  - q: "My child was fine at Maths in primary school. Why are they struggling now?"
    a: "Usually because Maths changes shape around Year 8, and in a full class there's rarely time to check the change has landed for every child. Primary Maths rewards arithmetic and speed, which is also what the Slough 11+ tested. GCSE Maths rewards method. Negatives handled properly inside an equation. Fractions treated as numbers, not pictures. A worded ratio question turned into a calculation. Your child can carry a gap in one of those for three years and look fine, right up to the paper that piles them into one question. The tutor's first job is finding which one it was."
  - q: "My child panics without a calculator. Is that the paper to work on?"
    a: "Possibly, but let the last mock decide, because the two kinds of paper lose marks for different reasons. On the non-calculator paper, any wobble in arithmetic or algebra shows straight away, because there's nothing to hide behind. The two calculator papers are about working through long problems accurately, and the marks tend to slip away at the back, where the questions get wordy. All three papers carry the same weight, so neither kind can be left out. Most children are weaker on one kind, and a marked paper shows which in about ten minutes. Splitting practice evenly across both is the slow way round."
