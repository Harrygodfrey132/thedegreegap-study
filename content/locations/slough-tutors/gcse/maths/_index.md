    a: "Because the Maths changed shape somewhere in Year 8 and nobody stopped to check it had landed. Primary Maths rewards arithmetic and speed, which is also what the Slough 11+ tested. GCSE Maths rewards method: negatives handled correctly inside an equation, fractions treated as numbers rather than pictures, a worded ratio question turned into a calculation. A student can carry a gap in one of those for three years and look fine, right up to the paper that stacks them into one question. The tutor's first job is finding which one it was."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper for GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Whichever one the last mock says is losing more, and the two lose marks for different reasons. Paper 1, the non-calculator paper, punishes shaky arithmetic and algebra, because there is nothing to hide behind. Papers 2 and 3 reward working through multi-step problems accurately, and the marks tend to leak at the back, where the questions get wordy. Most students bleed more on one than the other, and a marked paper shows which inside ten minutes. Practice split evenly across both is the slow route."
