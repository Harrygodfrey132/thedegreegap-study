faq_1_q: "The winter mock is a few weeks away. Can a tutor change it in time?"
faq_1_a: "Often, yes, if the problem is technique rather than content. A few weeks is enough to fix how a student reads a six-mark question and where the time goes in a paper, and those two cost more marks than most parents expect. If the gap is content from Year 9, a few weeks will not close it, but the mock will show exactly where it is, and that is what the spring term is for. We will tell you which one you are looking at on the first call."
faq_2_q: "My son's school already runs revision sessions. What does a tutor add?"
faq_2_a: "The bit a room of thirty cannot do: sit with his own wrong answers. School revision goes through the topics in order, and that is useful. A tutor starts from the last paper he sat, finds the two or three question types that keep going wrong and works on those until they hold. Then the school sessions land better, because he is no longer lost in the same place every week. The two run alongside each other. Nobody is replacing school."
faq_3_q: "Slough schools don't all sit the same exam boards. Does that matter for the match?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and we treat it as the first filter rather than the last. Two students on the same subject at different Slough schools can be sitting different boards, and the boards differ in question wording, in what the formula sheet gives you and in how the longer answers are marked. So we confirm the board and the tier on the call and match on both. A tutor who knows your child's specification is useful from the first ten minutes. One who does not spends your money catching up."
faq_4_q: "My daughter is a long way behind in Maths. Where does a tutor even start?"
faq_4_a: "Further back than the topic her class is on, and that is the point. Being a long way behind in Year 10 usually means a method from Year 7 or Year 8 that never settled, and everything since has been built on top of it. The tutor finds that spot on a paper in the first hour, goes back to it without making a thing of it, and rebuilds forward from there. It is slower than revision at first. It is also the only version that lasts past the next test."
