  - q: "How can I tell where my child's losing marks in English?"
    a: "Look at the teacher's comments on a marked essay, not just the grade. 'What's the effect?' or 'develop this' usually means the analysis is vague. A note asking for better evidence points somewhere else. And if the history and background around the text feel bolted on, that's a third problem. If the last answer on the paper is thin, it's pacing, not analysis. Each one needs a different term of work, which is why the tutor works out which it is before any teaching starts."
  - q: "My child's doing well in other subjects. Why is English the one that's stuck?"
    a: "Often it's because the analysis isn't precise enough. Your child understands the text and picks sensible quotations, then describes the effect in words that could fit almost any piece of writing. The mark scheme rewards saying what this particular choice does, and that needs vocabulary they may not have yet. The other common cause is timing. Both are habits, not missing knowledge. And habits shift faster than problems with understanding the text do."
