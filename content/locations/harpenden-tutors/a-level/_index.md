faq_1_q: "Should we wait and see how Year 13 starts?"
faq_1_a: "We'd usually say no, though it's a very common instinct. Predicted grades are built on Year 12 work and go to universities early in Year 13, so a Year 13 mock usually arrives after the number behind the offers has already gone. Starting in Year 12 means the work shows up in the prediction itself. Starting later still helps the final grade. It's just that the application's been made by then."
faq_2_q: "She understands it in lessons and then the mock comes back low. Why?"
faq_2_a: "Because lessons and exams test different things. In class, the teacher's named the topic, there's a worked example on the board and someone to ask. In the exam there's none of that, and a clock's running. At A-Level the gap is usually that your daughter can follow a method when someone's leading her through it, but can't pick it for herself under pressure. That's something you practise rather than something you explain, which is why the lessons are quieter than parents expect."
