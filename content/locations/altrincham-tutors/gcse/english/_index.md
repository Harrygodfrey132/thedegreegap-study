faqs:
  - q: "What do Altrincham teenagers find hardest in GCSE English?"
    a: "Evaluation, by a long way. The reading half of the fiction paper ends with a question asking how far your child agrees with a statement about the writing. Most answer it by retelling what happens and adding 'which is effective'. The marks are really for weighing the writer's choices against the claim, and that's hard to unpick answer by answer in a busy classroom. Close behind is the comparison question on the non-fiction paper, where two viewpoints get summarised one after the other and never really compared. In Literature, it's the step from the printed extract to the rest of the text. All three can be taught. That's the good news."
  - q: "My child's in Year 10 at an Altrincham school. Is that too early for English tutoring?"
    a: "No, and English rewards a Year 10 start, because what's being built is a habit, not a pile of facts. A sentence shape for evaluating. A four-minute plan for the writing task. The reflex of quoting three words, not three lines. Those take months of weekly practice to become automatic, and Year 10 is when there are months to spare. It's calmer too, more about reading the set texts properly than sitting timed papers. And with Altrincham sixth forms looking at every applicant's English Language grade, it's the least stressful way to stop English being the short one."
  - q: "How do I find out which English questions are costing my child marks?"
    a: "Ask for the last mock back with the marks for each question, not just the total, and look at where the numbers thin out. English papers have a shape. Shorter reading questions come first, then the long evaluate or compare question, then the writing task, worth around half the paper. If the short questions score and the long one doesn't, it's technique. If the writing's unfinished, it's timing. If marks are low everywhere, the reading itself needs work, which is rarer in Altrincham than parents fear. Ten minutes with a marked paper usually tells you which of the three it is."
