faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Altrincham students?"
    a: "Evaluation, by some distance. The fiction paper ends with a question that asks how far the student agrees with a statement about the writing, and most Altrincham students answer it by describing what happens and adding 'which is effective'. The marks are for weighing the writer's choices against the claim, and nobody has told them that in so many words. Close behind it is the comparison question on the non-fiction paper, where two viewpoints get summarised one after the other and never actually compared. In Literature it's the move from the printed extract to the rest of the text. All three are teachable, which is the good news."
  - q: "My child is in Year 10. Is it too early for GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "No, and English rewards a Year 10 start, because the thing being built is a habit rather than a set of facts. A sentence shape for evaluation, a four-minute plan for the writing task, the reflex of quoting three words rather than three lines: those take months of weekly repetition to become automatic, and Year 10 is when there are months to spare. Year 10 work is calmer too, more about reading the set texts properly than about timed papers. And in Altrincham, where the sixth forms read the English Language grade from everyone who applies, it's the least stressful way to make sure that grade isn't the short one."
  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE English questions my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Get the last mock back with the marks per question, not just the total, and look at where the numbers thin out. English papers have a shape: shorter retrieval and language questions first, then the long evaluate or compare question, then the writing task worth around half the paper. If the short questions score and the long one doesn't, it's technique. If the writing task is unfinished, it's timing. If the marks are low across the board, the reading itself needs work, which is rarer in Altrincham than parents fear. Ten minutes with a marked paper and the school's mark breakdown usually settles which of the three it is."
