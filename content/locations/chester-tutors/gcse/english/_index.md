  - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the better starting point for English. Producing several developed answers in one sitting is an endurance habit as much as an academic skill, and habits build through repetition across months rather than intensity in the spring of Year 11. Starting in Year 10 also means arriving at mocks with a paragraph structure that is automatic rather than still being worked out, and the sessions are calmer because nothing is imminent."
  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Twenty to forty minutes, and what it is matters far more than how long it takes. One planned paragraph written against a clock beats an hour of re-reading, because marks go to how an argument is built rather than to how much of the text is recalled. For a student who already reads widely, more reading is the least useful thing they can do. Most tutors set the task at the end of the hour and mark it before you next meet."
  - q: "Are your GCSE English tutors qualified teachers or university students?"

  - q: "Can I sit in on my child's English sessions?"
