  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Both count the same, so the question is where more marks are going. The non-calculator paper rewards arithmetic fluency and confident algebra, and it is where a student carrying an older gap tends to suffer, because a shaky method has nowhere to hide. The calculator papers reward accuracy across multi-step problems and careful reading of the wordy questions at the back. Nearly every student is clearly weaker on one, and the first session works out which."
  - q: "Why is my child's Maths predicted grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Predicted grades come from mock performance plus a teacher's judgement, and the gap parents notice is usually between what a student can do in a lesson and what they produce in a hall with a clock running. A student who understands every method can still lose two grades to timing, to not showing working, or to freezing on the wordy questions. A tutor reads the actual script rather than the grade, because those causes look identical from outside."
  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and several tutors in the network do this regularly. In Maths the help is mostly about reducing load: a consistent layout so steps do not get lost, multi-step questions broken into named stages, and one method practised until it is automatic rather than three covered in an hour. Where exam conditions are the difficulty, timed work is introduced gradually. Tell us on the call what school already has in place, including any access arrangements."
  - q: "How do I cancel or pause GCSE Maths tutoring?"
    a: "There is no contract, so nothing formal needs cancelling. Sessions are paid one at a time, and pausing means telling your tutor, ideally a day or two before the next slot so the time is not held empty. Families pause for exam leave, for holidays, or because the thing they came for has been sorted. Starting again later is a message rather than a rejoining process."
