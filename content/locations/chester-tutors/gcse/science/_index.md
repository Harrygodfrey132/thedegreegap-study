What is worth knowing is which Science requirement the specific sixth form your child wants publishes this year, and how far the current grade sits from it."
  - q: "How can I tell which parts of GCSE Science my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Split the three sciences apart first, because the combined grade is exactly what hides the problem. Then within the weakest, sort the lost marks into three piles: straight recall, data and graph questions, and the longer six-mark answers. Almost every student is clearly worse at one of those three, and each needs different work. Ten minutes with a marked paper tells you more than a term of general revision."
  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Science?"
    a: "It depends on where your child sits now rather than where anyone hopes they will finish. Higher tier reaches the top grades but scores very little below its floor, while foundation caps lower and makes the available marks considerably more reachable. A student near the boundary in Year 10 is often right on higher with focused work on the topics that decide it. A Year 10 diagnostic turns that into a decision rather than a guess, and school should be part of the conversation."
