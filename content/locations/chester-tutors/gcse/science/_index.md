And families around Chester use schools in more than one council area, on both sides of the Welsh border, where GCSEs can follow a different course.   - q: "How do I work out where my child's losing marks in Science?"
    a: "Start by splitting the three sciences apart, because the overall grade is exactly what hides the problem. Then, in the weakest one, sort the lost marks into three piles: straight recall, data and graph questions, and the longer six-mark answers. Most children are noticeably weaker in one pile, and each pile needs a different kind of practice. Ten minutes with a marked mock will tell you more than a term of general revision."
  - q: "Should my child be sitting foundation or higher tier Science?"
    a: "It depends on where your child is now, not where everyone hopes they'll finish. Higher tier (the harder papers) is the only route to the top grades, but if your child falls below its lowest grade, there's very little to fall back on. Foundation has a lower ceiling, but your child can get at far more of its marks. If your child's near the borderline in Year 10, higher is often right, with focused work on the topics that decide it. A tutor's check in Year 10 turns that into a decision rather than a guess. And school should be part of that conversation."

References are checked at the end."
