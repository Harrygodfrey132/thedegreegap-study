faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Solihull students?"
    a: "The structure question on the Language paper, by a distance. Students are asked how a writer has ordered a text to interest a reader, and they answer by analysing words instead, which is a different question and scores badly. Close behind it is the writing task, where a good idea loses marks on punctuation and paragraphing that nobody has looked at since Year 9. Neither is a reading problem, and both are among the fastest things to fix in one-to-one work."
  - q: "Does the exam board matter for GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "It matters for Literature more than for Language. The Language papers across AQA, Edexcel and Eduqas test similar skills with differently worded questions, so a tutor who knows one adapts quickly. Literature is another matter, because the set texts differ, the anthology poems differ, and whether the exam is open or closed book differs. A tutor who has taught your child's actual poems is worth a great deal more than one who has taught a different set well. We confirm the board and the texts on the first call."
  - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start English tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the better year for English, more so than for most subjects. The Language paper rewards habits built by repetition, and repetition needs time that Year 11 does not have once mocks and coursework arrive. A Year 10 programme can spend a term purely on unseen extracts and writing accuracy without anything urgent hanging over it. By Year 11 the same work has to compete with revision for nine other subjects, and it usually loses."
