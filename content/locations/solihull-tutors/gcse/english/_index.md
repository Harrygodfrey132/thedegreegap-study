faqs:
  - q: "What part of GCSE English trips up Solihull teenagers most?"
    a: "Usually the structure question on the Language paper. It asks how a writer has ordered a text to interest the reader, and most children answer by analysing individual words instead, which scores badly. Close behind is the writing task, where a good idea loses marks on punctuation and paragraphing once the clock's running. Neither is a reading problem. And both are among the quickest things to fix one-to-one."
  - q: "Does it matter which exam board my child's on for English?"
    a: "More for Literature than for Language. The Language papers from AQA, Edexcel and Eduqas test similar skills with differently worded questions, so a good tutor adapts quickly. Literature's different. The set texts vary by board, and so do the anthology poems. Every board's Literature exam is closed book, too, so your child needs the key quotes from their own texts learnt by heart. A tutor who's taught your child's actual poems is worth far more than one who's taught a different set well. We check the board and the texts on the first call."
  - q: "Is Year 10 too soon to start English tutoring?"

In Year 10, a tutor can spend a whole term on unseen extracts and writing accuracy with nothing urgent hanging over it. In Year 11, the same work competes with revision for nine other subjects, and it usually loses."
