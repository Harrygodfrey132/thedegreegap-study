  - q: "How do Solihull's GCSE Science results compare with the rest of the country?"
    a: "Solihull as a whole does better than the national average, and that changes what tutoring is usually for here. It's rarely about scraping a 4. More often it's a capable child sitting a grade below what the sixth form course or apprenticeship asks for, with no obvious weak spot.   - q: "Triple or Combined Science: which is better for my child?"
    a: "It depends on your child, and neither one closes the door on A-Level Science. Triple gives three grades and more content. Combined gives two grades covering the same three sciences in less depth. Some sixth forms ask for a higher Combined grade than they'd want in the single science, so check the Tudor Grange, Arden or Solihull Sixth Form College course pages before deciding. The best guide is how Year 9 went, and whether the extra content will get revised or resented. A lesson looking at your child's work before the options form goes in beats a gut feeling."
  - q: "What does an hour with the Science tutor look like?"
    a: "An hour, in three parts. It starts with a quarter of an hour on last week's work, with the tutor saying out loud why each mark was or wasn't given. The middle half hour takes one thing and rebuilds it, often a practical drawn out with its variables labelled, or a calculation worked step by step. The last quarter of an hour is exam questions on that same thing, marked against the real scheme there and then, so your child sees what the marks were actually for."
  - q: "How do you choose your Science tutors?"
    a: "Carefully. We turn away far more than we take: about 3% of applicants get onto the platform. There's a written application, a subject knowledge check, an interview built around explaining something clearly under pressure, and references. For Science, the interview leans on the practicals, because a tutor who knows the content but hasn't thought about how practicals are examined will teach the wrong half. Every tutor talks to Harry or Joe before we'd suggest them to a family."
  - q: "Can I sit in on my child's Science lessons?"
    a: "Yes, and most tutors are happy for you to join the first one so you can see how the hour works. Most parents step back after that. Children tend to admit what they don't understand more easily without an audience, and that's the useful part. You'll get notes after each lesson either way, and every lesson has a replay if something in the notes surprises you."
