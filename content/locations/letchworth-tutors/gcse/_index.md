faq_1_q: "My child has stopped trying in one subject. Where does a tutor even start?"
faq_1_a: "With the work itself, not a pep talk. The tutor takes a recent test or piece of homework in that subject and goes through it with your child, question by question, until the first real sticking point shows up. That gives the lessons a starting point your child can see. Children who've switched off usually haven't stopped caring. They've stopped expecting it to make sense, and getting a few questions right for the first time in a while can shift that. After the first lesson, the tutor's note will tell you what they found."
faq_2_q: "Should we start in Year 9 or Year 10, or wait until Year 11?"
faq_2_a: "Any of them can work. Starting earlier mostly buys time: in Year 9 or 10, a tutor can go back over an old topic slowly, with no exam date looming, and spend lessons on why a method works. Starting in Year 11 is common too, often once mock results come home, and then lessons lean more on past papers. Whenever you start, a regular weekly slot usually helps more than lots of lessons squeezed in just before the exams."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help my child get ready for the Year 11 mocks?"
faq_3_a: "Yes. Mocks are often the first time your child sits full papers against the clock, and the results can feed into predicted grades and sixth form applications. The tutor works through past papers from your child's own exam board, showing where the marks are given, and helps with timing so the last questions get a proper go. Mock dates vary from school to school, so it helps to start a term or so before yours."
faq_4_q: "And if my child and the tutor just don't get on?"
faq_4_a: "Then we'll suggest someone else, and it won't cost you anything. Just let Harry or Joe know. Meeting the tutor on a free 15-minute video call first makes a mismatch less likely, but it still happens, and teenagers change: a tutor who suited your child in Year 9 might not be right by Year 11. It's far better to switch early than to keep going with lessons your child dreads."
