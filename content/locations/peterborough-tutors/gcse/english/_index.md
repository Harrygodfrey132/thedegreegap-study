faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Peterborough students?"
    a: "Timing, more often than content. Both Language papers and the Literature papers ask for several developed answers inside a fixed window, and the commonest script we see has a strong opening answer followed by two that were clearly rushed. The second hardest part is writing an argument rather than a description, which is a habit rather than a skill gap. Set texts vary between schools across the city, so the specific text is less predictive than those two things, which show up on nearly every paper regardless of which books were studied."
  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "Twenty to forty minutes, and what it is matters far more than how long it takes. One planned paragraph written against a clock beats an hour of re-reading, because the marks go to how an argument is constructed rather than to how much of the text can be recalled. Where timing is the issue, short timed bursts two or three times a week do more than one long sitting. Most tutors set the task at the end of the hour so it points straight at what was just worked on, and mark it before the next session."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs an English tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at a recent marked essay rather than at the grade. If the writing is organised, the points are sensible and the marks are still not there, the gap is technique and a tutor is the fastest route, because it is about how answers are built rather than what your child knows. If the writing trails off halfway, or the essay describes the text instead of answering the question, that is also a tutor job. If your child is already writing well-structured arguments and simply needs volume, more past papers marked by their teacher may be enough."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and several tutors in the network work this way regularly. In English the help tends to be structural: planning frames that hold an argument together, a fixed paragraph shape so writing does not start from a blank page each time, and breaking long answers into named parts. Where recall is hard, shorter and more flexible quotations are easier to hold and tend to score better anyway. Tell us on the consultation call what school already has in place, including exam access arrangements, and we match on it."
