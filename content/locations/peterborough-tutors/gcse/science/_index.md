  - q: "How does GCSE Science performance in Peterborough compare to the national average?"
    a: "Peterborough sits below the national picture on headline measures, and the spread across the city is wide, which matters more to a parent than the average does. Two students at schools a few miles apart can be on different Science routes with quite different amounts of laboratory time behind them. So the comparison worth making is your child's most recent paper against the paper they are going to sit, which is what the first session does. Unlike a published figure it gives you something specific you can work on this term."
  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Science tutoring session?"
    a: "An hour, usually in three parts. The first fifteen minutes go on the work set last week, marked properly so misconceptions surface rather than staying hidden. The middle half hour is the real teaching, on whichever topic or skill the diagnostic pointed at, with method written out on the shared whiteboard rather than described aloud. The last fifteen minutes are exam questions on that topic, marked against the real scheme in front of your child. Homework is set at the end so it points directly at what was just covered."
  - q: "How long is each GCSE Science tutoring session?"
    a: "Most are sixty minutes, which suits Science well because the content comes in blocks that fit an hour. Some families book ninety minutes in the run-up to exams, especially where a student is doing triple and wants to cover more than one science in a sitting, though an hour a week done properly beats a longer session done tiredly. For younger students, or where concentration is genuinely the limiting factor, forty-five minutes sometimes works better. It is worth raising on the consultation call rather than defaulting."
