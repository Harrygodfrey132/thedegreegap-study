faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest topic in GCSE Science for Tunbridge Wells students?"
    a: "Three come up more than the rest, and they are spread across the three sciences. In Physics it is rearranging equations under time pressure, which is really an algebra problem wearing a Physics hat. In Chemistry it is moles and concentration calculations, where one slip early makes every later line wrong. In Biology it is the genetics and inheritance questions, where students who know the content still lose marks because the working is not set out the way the mark scheme wants it. All three are method problems rather than knowledge problems, which is good news, because method is faster to fix."
  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE Science, and does it matter for tutoring?"
    a: "It matters, and in Science it matters more than in most subjects. The three boards cover overlapping content but differ in how the required practicals are examined, how the extended six-mark answers are marked, and which formulae are given in the paper rather than learned. A student who met a practical one way can find the same practical framed quite differently on another board's paper. We confirm the board on the consultation call before suggesting anyone, and where a student has changed schools mid-course we look for a tutor who has taught both."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE Science?"
    a: "Usually because Science rewards a way of writing that nobody teaches directly. Students learn the content, then lose marks on the six-mark questions for giving three good points with no link between them, or for describing when the command word asked them to explain. The other common cause is a gap further back: a student who never quite got equation rearranging in Year 9 Physics will keep meeting it, because it turns up in every paper. The first hour is spent finding which of those two it is, because the term looks completely different depending on the answer."
  - q: "Are your GCSE Science tutors qualified teachers or university students?"
    a: "Both, and the right answer depends on the student. The network includes qualified secondary teachers, Russell Group graduates and current undergraduates who sat these exact papers recently. For a student who needs a topic rebuilt from the foundations, a qualified teacher who has taught the sequence many times is often the better fit. For a student whose content is broadly there but whose exam answers are not landing, a recent graduate who knows the current mark scheme in detail frequently has the edge. We suggest based on what the diagnostic finds, not on a house preference."
  - q: "Can my child have Science tutoring during the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and holidays are often where the most focused progress happens. Term time means fitting an hour around everything else a Year 10 or Year 11 is carrying, whereas a half-term or Easter block can take one weak science and rebuild it properly across a few consecutive days. Because everything runs online, scheduling stays flexible, and families travelling still keep their slot. Plenty of families use holiday weeks for the required practicals, which are hard to revise alone and easy to work through with someone."
