faqs:
  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE Science for Tunbridge Wells teenagers?"
    a: "Usually one of three things, one in each science. In Physics, it's rearranging equations against the clock, which is really an algebra problem wearing a Physics hat. In Chemistry, it's moles and concentration, where one early slip makes every later line wrong. In Biology, it's genetics and inheritance: your child can know the content and still lose marks because the working isn't set out the way the mark scheme wants. The good news is that all three are about method, not knowledge. And method is quicker to fix."
  - q: "Does the exam board make much difference in Science?"
    a: "Yes, more in Science than in most subjects. The content overlaps a lot, but AQA, Edexcel and OCR examine it differently, above all in how they ask about the required practicals and how they mark the six-mark answers. So a practical your child met one way can look quite different on another board's paper. We check the board on the call before suggesting anyone. And if your child changed schools partway through, we'll look for a tutor who's taught both."
  - q: "Why is Science such a struggle for my child when they're putting the work in?"
    a: "Often because Science exams reward a particular way of writing, and a busy class rarely has time to practise it answer by answer. Your child learns the content, then loses marks on six-mark questions for giving three good points with nothing linking them, or for describing when the question said explain. The other usual cause sits further back. If rearranging equations never clicked in Year 9 Physics, it'll keep coming back, because it's on every paper. The first lesson works out which of the two it is. The term looks very different depending on the answer."
  - q: "Are the tutors qualified teachers?"
    a: "Many are, and every tutor is a graduate of a top Russell Group university. Some are experienced secondary teachers. Others graduated recently and sat these same papers not long ago. Which suits your child depends on what the first lesson finds. If a topic needs rebuilding from the ground up, a teacher who's taught it in order many times is often the better fit. If the content's mostly there but exam answers aren't landing, a recent graduate who knows the current mark scheme closely can have the edge. We suggest tutors on that basis, not out of habit."
  - q: "Can we do Science lessons in the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and holidays are often when the most focused work happens. In term time, an hour of Science has to squeeze in around everything else a Year 10 or 11 is juggling. A half-term or Easter block can take one weak science and rebuild it over a few days in a row. It's all online, so the timing's flexible and you keep your slot if you're away. Lots of families use holiday weeks for the required practicals, which are hard to revise alone and much easier with someone alongside."
