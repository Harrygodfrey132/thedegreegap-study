  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually because the writing has never been taught as its own skill. Schools teach the texts carefully, then set a kind of essay that has rules of its own, and the space between understanding a book and scoring on a question about it is where most marks disappear. The classic sign is a student who discusses a text well out loud and writes a description of it on paper. The other common cause is timing: a strong first paragraph and two rushed ones. Both are fixable, and considerably faster to fix than a reading problem would be."
  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "Twenty to forty minutes, and what it is matters much more than how long it lasts. One planned paragraph written against a clock is worth more than an hour of re-reading notes, because the marks go to how an argument is built rather than to how much of the text can be recalled. Where timing is the problem, short timed bursts a few times a week beat one long sitting. Most tutors set the work at the end of the session so it points at what was just covered, and mark it before you next meet."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and several tutors in the network do this regularly. In English the useful help is structural: planning frames that hold an argument together, a fixed paragraph shape so nothing has to be invented from a blank page each time, and breaking a long answer into named parts. Extra time only helps a student who knows what to do with it, and that is often where the real work is. Tell us on the consultation call what school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we match on it."
  - q: "What if my child does not want a tutor?"
