  - q: "Why is my child finding GCSE English so hard?"
    a: "Mostly because writing an exam answer is a skill of its own, and it takes lots of one-to-one feedback that a full class rarely has time for. Understanding a book and scoring on a question about it aren't the same thing, and the space between them is where most marks go. The classic sign is a child who talks well about a text out loud, then writes a description of it on paper. The other common cause is timing: one strong paragraph and two rushed ones. Both can be fixed, and much faster than a reading problem could be."
  - q: "How long is each GCSE English lesson?"
    a: "Most lessons are an hour. That suits English. It's long enough to plan an answer, write it against the clock and get it marked in one sitting, and that's where the learning happens. Nearer the exams, some families book ninety minutes so a full section of a paper can be done under exam conditions. In Year 10, or if concentration is the real issue, forty-five minutes every week beats an hour done grudgingly. We'll settle it together on the call rather than just picking the default."
  - q: "Are the English tutors qualified teachers?"

If a text needs opening up from the start, a qualified teacher who's taught it many times often works best. If the ideas are sound but the answers aren't scoring, a recent graduate who knows the current mark scheme in detail is often the better fit.
