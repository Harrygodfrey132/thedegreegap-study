  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually because the writing has never been taught as its own skill. Schools teach the texts carefully, then set a kind of essay that has rules of its own, and the space between understanding a book and scoring on a question about it is where most marks disappear. The classic sign is a student who discusses a text well out loud and writes a description of it on paper. The other common cause is timing: a strong first paragraph and two rushed ones. Both are fixable, and considerably faster to fix than a reading problem would be."
  - q: "How long is each GCSE English tutoring session?"
    a: "Most run to sixty minutes, which suits English because it is long enough to plan an answer, write it against a clock and have it marked in the same sitting, which is where the learning happens. Nearer the exams some families book ninety minutes so a whole paper can be attempted under conditions. For Year 10 students, or where concentration is the real limit, forty-five minutes every week beats an hour done reluctantly. Worth settling on the consultation call rather than taking the default."
