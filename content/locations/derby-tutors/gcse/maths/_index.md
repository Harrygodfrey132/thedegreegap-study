  - q: "What Maths grade will my child need for A-Level Maths in Derby?"
    a: "It depends where they apply, because each sixth form sets its own bar and can change it from year to year. Maths usually sits near the top of what any of them asks, since A-Level Maths leans on confident algebra from the first week. Derby College and school sixth forms like Littleover and Allestree Woodlands all publish their own entry grades, and they aren't the same. So check the page for the place your child wants. Where a child's one grade short, twelve to sixteen weeks of weekly lessons is a fair window, so autumn's the time to start."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Are we starting too soon?"
    a: "No, it's often the best time. Year 11 builds straight on top of Year 10, so a gap closed now stops causing trouble for the next eighteen months. Find the same gap in March of Year 11 and it has to be fixed while every other subject wants the same evenings. Year 10 lessons feel different, too. There's more rebuilding of methods and less exam drilling, which is calmer. That tends to suit a child who's started to decide Maths isn't for them."
  - q: "Could we just do more Maths revision at home instead?"
    a: "Sometimes, and the working on a recent paper will tell you, much more than the grade does. If it's clean and organised, with small slips and a few questions unfinished at the end, it's accuracy and pace. More timed practice, marked honestly, might be enough on its own. If the working stops halfway, or questions are left blank, there's a gap in method. That's where a tutor is much quicker than working alone. And if your child's already scoring well across mocks and just wants stretching, targeted practice beats a full programme."
  - q: "Who are the Maths tutors, and how are they picked?"
    a: "Some graduates, some still at university, every one with two years or more of tutoring, and all carefully picked. What we're really testing at interview is whether they can explain under pressure: take an idea apart and rebuild it for a child who's lost, without making them feel silly. Harry and Joe do the interviews themselves, then references are checked. Nobody gets near your child without clearing both."
  - q: "My child's dead against having a tutor. What do we do?"
    a: "That's really common, and it's worth handling gently rather than overruling. Most of the pushback happens before your child has met a single tutor. It's usually about what having a tutor seems to say about them, not the hour itself. That's why there's a free 15-minute video meeting before any paid lesson: your child meets the tutor first and gets a say in who it is.
