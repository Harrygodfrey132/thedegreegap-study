  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper for GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Both are weighted the same, so the question is which one is losing more marks. The non-calculator paper rewards arithmetic fluency and confident algebra, and a student carrying an older gap tends to suffer there because there is nowhere to hide a shaky method. The calculator papers reward accuracy over multi-step problems and careful reading of the wordy questions at the back, which is a different skill altogether. Nearly every student is clearly weaker on one, and the first session works out which, so the early weeks go where the marks are."
  - q: "Should my child take foundation or higher tier GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Higher tier reaches the top grades but pays very little below its floor, while foundation caps lower and makes the marks on the paper much more reachable. The honest answer depends on where your child is sitting in Year 10 rather than on anyone's hopes. A student near the boundary is often right on higher with targeted work on the specific topics that decide it. A student some way below usually does better on foundation, where the questions are winnable and confidence starts to rebuild. A Year 10 diagnostic makes that a decision rather than a guess, and it is worth raising with school as well."
