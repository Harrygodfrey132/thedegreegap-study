  - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start GCSE Maths tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the best point to start rather than too early. Year 11 content builds straight on top of Year 10, so a gap closed now stops causing damage for the next eighteen months, whereas the same gap found in March of Year 11 has to be repaired while every other subject is competing for the same evenings. Year 10 sessions also look different: more rebuilding of method, less exam drilling, which is calmer and tends to suit a student who has started to decide Maths is not for them."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs a Maths tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at the working on a recent paper rather than the grade. Clean, organised working with small slips and unfinished questions at the end points to accuracy and pace, and more timed practice marked honestly may be enough on its own. Working that stops partway through, or questions left blank, points to a method gap, and that is where a tutor is genuinely faster than anything a student can manage alone. If your child is already scoring well across mocks and wants stretch, targeted practice beats a full programme."
  - q: "How do you vet your GCSE Maths tutors?"
    a: "Around 3% of people who apply come onto the platform. References follow. Joe and Harry interview personally rather than delegating it. Someone can be genuinely good at Maths and still be no use across a one-to-one hour, and the interview exists to find that out."
  - q: "What if my child does not want a tutor?"
