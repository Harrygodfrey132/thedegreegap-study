faqs:
  - q: "Which bit of GCSE English trips up Cannock students most often?"
    a: "Poetry, more often than not. The anthology question asks your child to compare two poems from memory, and the unseen poems give them nothing to revise in advance. Close behind is the last question on Language Paper 2, where your child argues their own view in a set form, like a letter or an article. A tutor looks at a recent mock to see which applies to your child, rather than guessing."
  - q: "Does the exam board make much difference for GCSE English?"
    a: "Yes, for Literature especially. The skills overlap, but the set texts, the order of the papers and the way marks are split all differ between boards. Practising with the wrong board's questions can train your child to plan the wrong kind of answer. Cardinal Griffin sits AQA, while other Cannock schools may not, so we confirm the board on the call and match a tutor who knows it."
  - q: "What would my child actually do in an English lesson with a tutor?"
    a: "Usually three parts in an hour. First, the paragraph your child wrote for homework, marked together so they can see where it lost marks. Then one skill, like comparing two poems or making a quotation do more work. Last, a short timed task using that skill, so it's practised under a little pressure. Nearer the exams, timed answers take over most of the hour."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia. Can a tutor help with all the reading and writing?"
    a: "Yes. We ask about learning differences on the first call and suggest tutors who've worked with dyslexic students before. In English that often means shorter chunks of text, quotations learnt out loud before they're written, and plans built on the whiteboard in colour. Your child can replay any explanation later, which helps when reading speed is the issue. It's worth asking school about access arrangements, like extra time, too."
  - q: "Could I watch one of my child's English lessons?"
    a: "You're welcome to, and lots of parents watch the first one. After that most step back, because teenagers tend to admit they're lost more readily without an audience. You won't miss anything, since every lesson is recorded and you can watch it back. And you can ask the tutor, or us, how things are going at any time."
