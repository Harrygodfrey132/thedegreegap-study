faq_1_q: "Kingsmead looks at how many grade 6s my child gets. Should we focus on one subject or spread the lessons?"
faq_1_a: "Usually one or two subjects, chosen carefully. Kingsmead's suggested pathways count GCSEs at grade 6 or above, so a subject sitting just under a 6 often deserves more time than one that's already safe or a long way off. If your child needs a set grade for a particular A-Level, a 7 in Maths for A-Level Maths, say, that subject comes first. Other sixth forms set their bars differently, so bring whatever your child's school has published to the call."
faq_2_q: "How does a GCSE tutor fit around what my child is learning at school?"
faq_2_a: "They start from school, not from a separate course. In the first lesson the tutor looks at exercise books, recent tests and which board your child sits, then works on whatever is costing marks right now. Some weeks that means going back to a Year 8 topic the new work depends on. Other weeks it's past paper questions on what the class has just finished. Lessons should make school easier to follow, never compete with it."
faq_3_q: "How soon would we notice a difference?"
faq_3_a: "Often in how your child talks about the subject, before you see it in a grade. Within a few lessons, homework tends to get started sooner and finish with fewer tears. Test results usually take longer, around a half term of weekly lessons, and longer again if the gap goes back a few years. The tutor will tell you what they're seeing as they go. Nobody can promise a particular grade."
