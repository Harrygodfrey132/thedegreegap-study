The second is the application. Most schemes ask for written answers about why the applicant wants the role, and those are marked by a human who is comparing them. Tutors regularly help students plan and sharpen that writing, which is the same skill the Language paper is testing."
  - q: "When should a Year 11 start English tutoring?"
    a: "Autumn term if the aim is to move a grade band, because that leaves time for a fortnightly cycle of write, mark, rewrite, and it is the rewrite that moves the mark. From January onward the work has to be more selective, usually one paper and two question types rather than everything, and that is still worth doing. English rewards starting early more than most subjects because the improvement comes from repetition rather than from covering material, and repetition needs weeks."
  - q: "How much homework should my child do between English sessions?"
    a: "One piece of writing a week, and it does not need to be a full essay. A single well-built analysis paragraph that gets marked properly is worth more than three essays nobody reads, because the learning happens when a student sees what a mark scheme does with their own sentences. In Literature we usually add short quotation work, five minutes a day rather than an hour on a Sunday. Most tutors set the work at the end of the session so it lines up with what was just taught."
