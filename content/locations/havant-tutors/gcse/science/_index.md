  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE Science, and does it matter for tutoring?"
    a: "It matters, and in Science it matters more than elsewhere. The content overlaps but the examining differs: how required practicals are assessed, how extended answers are marked, and which equations are printed in the paper rather than learned. A student who met a practical under one board's method can face the same practical framed the other way and lose marks on material they know. We confirm the board on the consultation call, and for a student who has moved schools mid-course we look for a tutor who has taught both specifications."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE Science?"
    a: "Usually one of two things, and they need different terms. The first is a writing problem: the content is there, but extended answers score badly because points are listed without being linked, or because the answer describes when the command word asked it to explain. The second sits further back, a skill like equation rearranging that never properly landed and now turns up in every paper. The first hour is spent working out which of the two it is, because the plan that follows looks completely different depending on the answer."
  - q: "Can a Science tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and a number of tutors in the network work with neurodivergent students regularly. In Science most of the practical help is about reducing load: a consistent layout for calculations so steps do not get lost, planning frames for six-mark answers, and learning each required practical as named parts rather than a paragraph of prose. One-to-one fits this better than a classroom because the pace belongs to the student rather than the middle of the room. Tell us on the call what school already has in place, including exam access arrangements, and we match on it."
