faq_1_q: "He got 7s and 8s at GCSE. Why is A-Level suddenly so hard?"
faq_1_a: "Because it's a different kind of test, not because he's stopped trying. GCSE questions often tell you which method to use. A-Level questions expect your child to work that out, then explain it or weigh it up. There's also far more to learn, and much less time in class to go over it twice. A tutor looks at the first marked piece of work, finds where the marks went, and practises exactly that. It's very common, and it's fixable."
faq_2_q: "When do predicted grades get written, and can tutoring still help?"
faq_2_a: "Teachers usually write them early in Year 13, drawing on the Year 12 exams and the work since. That's why the end of Year 12 matters more than many families expect. Tutoring can't change a prediction directly, but it can change how your child performs in the tests the prediction is based on. Starting in the first term of Year 12 gives the most room. A Year 13 start still lifts the final result."
