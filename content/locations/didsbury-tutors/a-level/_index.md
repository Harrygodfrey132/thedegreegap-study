faq_1_q: "He got 7s and 8s at GCSE. Why is Year 12 so hard for him?"
faq_1_a: "Two things change at once, and neither means he's stopped trying. A-Level covers far more ground at a faster pace, and lessons rarely have time to go over anything twice. And the questions change. A GCSE question often hints at the method, but at A-Level he has to pick it himself, then explain it or weigh it up. It's very common. A tutor starts with his first marked piece of work, finds where the marks went, and works on exactly that with him."
faq_2_q: "When do predicted grades get written, and can tutoring still help?"
faq_2_a: "Teachers usually write them early in Year 13, from the Year 12 exams and the work since. That's why the end of Year 12 matters more than many families expect. Tutoring can't change a prediction directly, but it can change how your child performs in the tests it's based on. The first term of Year 12 gives the most room. But even in Year 13, there's time to work on the final exams."
