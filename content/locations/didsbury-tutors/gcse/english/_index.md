faqs:
  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE English for Didsbury students?"
    a: "Unseen poetry and timing come up most. Unseen poems can't be learnt in advance, so a child without a clear way in tends to freeze. Literature essays need quotations from memory and an argument, not a retelling of the plot, and lots of students run out of time on the second question. In Language, the writing task suffers because it comes last. All of these are about technique, which is good news: timed practice moves them fairly quickly."
  - q: "Why is my child's predicted English grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Usually because of the gap between what they know and what they can write in the time. Plenty of children understand the texts well in class discussion, then produce a half-finished essay in a mock. Predicted grades lean on those timed pieces. A tutor reads a recent essay or mock paper, works out whether it's planning, quotations or timing, and practises that one thing each week until it holds under pressure."
  - q: "What actually happens in an English lesson?"
    a: "Most lessons are an hour, in three rough parts. First, a look at the piece your child did during the week, marked together. Then the main bit: a technique like planning an essay or reading an unseen poem, taught using your child's own texts. Finally, a short timed task on it, marked on the spot. Every lesson has a replay, so your child can go back over the explanation later."
  - q: "Can a tutor help if my child has dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes, and English is often where it helps most, because the subject leans so heavily on reading speed and long written answers. Tutors break essays into small, named steps, use visual plans on the whiteboard, and build up timing gradually. Tell us about any diagnosis or exam access arrangements on the consultation call, and we'll pick tutors with that experience."
  - q: "Can we pause or stop the lessons?"
    a: "Yes, whenever you like. There's no contract, so you can pause over half term, stop once a grade has settled, or come back in the spring before the exams. Just let the tutor know ahead of the next lesson. You only ever pay for the lessons you have, from £37 an hour."
