faqs:
  - q: "Which bit of GCSE English catches Didsbury teenagers out most?"
    a: "Unseen poetry and timing, more than anything else. An unseen poem can't be learnt in advance, so a child without a clear way in tends to freeze. Literature essays need quotations from memory and an argument, not a retelling of the plot, and plenty of teenagers run out of time on the second question. In Language, the writing task suffers because it comes last. The good news is that it's all technique, and timed practice moves it fairly quickly."
  - q: "The school's predicted a lower English grade than we hoped. Why?"
    a: "Usually it's the gap between what your child knows and what they can write in the time. Lots of children talk about the texts well in class, then hand in a half-finished essay in a mock. Predicted grades lean on those timed pieces. A tutor reads a recent essay or mock paper, works out whether it's planning, quotations or timing, and practises that one thing each week until it holds under pressure."
