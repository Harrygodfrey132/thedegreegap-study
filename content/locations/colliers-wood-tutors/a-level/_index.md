faq_1_q: "My child just says 'fine' whenever I ask about A-Levels. How would I know if something's wrong?"
faq_1_a: "Look for small signs rather than waiting for a confession. Marked work that never leaves the bag, one subject that's suddenly 'boring', or a first assessment a grade or two below what their GCSEs suggested. Any of those is worth a gentle question. A tutor gives you a second view: after a couple of lessons they'll know which topics are secure and which aren't, and they'll tell you plainly if you ask. Your child keeps some privacy, and you still get a straight answer."
faq_2_q: "Is the summer before Year 12 too early to start A-Level tutoring?"
faq_2_a: "No, and for Maths or the sciences it can be a very good time. St Mark's booklet says final GCSE results and assessments in September help settle which courses each student stays on, and other sixth forms run early checks of their own. A few summer lessons on the GCSE topics A-Level leans on, algebra for Maths and Physics or moles for Chemistry, mean your child starts the year steady. The first half term of Year 12 works too. Leaving it until the Year 13 mocks makes everything tighter."
