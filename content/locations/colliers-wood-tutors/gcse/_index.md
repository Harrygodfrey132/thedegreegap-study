faq_1_q: "I can't follow my child's GCSE Maths homework any more. Is that normal?"
faq_1_a: "Completely, and it doesn't mean you've forgotten everything. GCSE Maths now includes topics plenty of parents never met at school, like iteration or the equation of a circle, and familiar ones are often taught a different way. Helping with your own method can leave your child holding two methods and trusting neither. A tutor teaches the one their school uses. You can still help at home: ask your child to explain a question back to you, and listen."
faq_2_q: "How do GCSE results decide what my child can do at RR6 or Sutton Grammar?"
faq_2_a: "In different ways, which is why it helps to look in Year 10. At RR6, results set how many courses your child starts with: eight or more GCSEs at grades 7 to 9, including English and Maths, open the way to four A-Levels, while five at grade 4 or above, again with English and Maths, lead to three A-Levels or a Level 3 BTEC. Sutton Grammar admits on competitive grades and keeps at least 25 places for students joining from other schools. Both can change their rules, so check the current pages."
