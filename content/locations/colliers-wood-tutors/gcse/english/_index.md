  - q: "Does it matter whether my child's school uses AQA or Edexcel for English?"
    a: "It does, mainly because the papers are arranged differently. On AQA's Literature exam, the Shakespeare play and the 19th-century novel share Paper 1, while the modern text sits with poetry on Paper 2. Edexcel pairs Shakespeare with the modern text, and the novel with poetry. The skills overlap a lot, but the timing and question styles don't. So we check your child's board for both Language and Literature before suggesting anyone."
  - q: "My child says English has no right answers, so there's no point revising. What can a tutor do?"
    a: "Quite a lot, because English has more method in it than it seems. Mark schemes reward the same moves again and again: a clear point, a short quotation, a comment on one word the writer chose, a link back to the question. Once your child sees that pattern, revising stops feeling pointless. The tutor shows it on past questions, then has them practise it in short timed bursts until it's a habit."
