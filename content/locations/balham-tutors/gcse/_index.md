faq_1_q: "My child is only in Year 10. Is that too soon for GCSE tutoring?"
faq_1_a: "Not at all, and it's often the easiest time to begin. Year 10 is when longer answers start showing up in tests, and habits formed now tend to stick. There's room to fix an older gap, an algebra method or how to plan an essay, without a mock looming. It also leaves the autumn of Year 11 clear for sixth form applications, which come early around Balham: Chestnut Grove's close on 1 December. Starting in Year 11 works too, with a tighter plan."
faq_2_q: "My child's English essays are full of quotes but still get low marks. What can a tutor do?"
faq_2_a: "Quite a lot, because it's usually about structure. A quote earns little on its own. The marks come from what your child says about it: the one word that matters, and why the writer might have chosen it. A tutor picks a recent essay, re-marks it with your child against the mark scheme, then they rewrite one paragraph together. After a few weeks of that, long answers in History and Religious Studies often improve too."
