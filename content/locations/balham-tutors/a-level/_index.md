faq_1_q: "My child's predicted grades have already gone off to UCAS. Can tutoring still change anything?"
faq_1_a: "No. Predicted grades get your child the offers, but the offers themselves depend on the results in August. Between now and the exams there's time for real movement, above all in one subject. The January mocks are the first checkpoint, so the tutor usually starts there, with whichever subject the main offer names or the one furthest from its target. Lessons can carry on through the spring and into study leave."
faq_2_q: "My child is looking at degree apprenticeships as well as university. Can A-Level tutoring help with both?"
faq_2_a: "Yes. Most degree apprenticeships still set minimum A-Level grades, so the subject work is the same. The difference is timing: employers open applications on their own calendars, some in the autumn of Year 13, and many add online tests or an assessment day. The tutor keeps the grades moving, and Harry and Joe can talk the applications through with you, alongside UCAS if your child keeps both routes open."
faq_3_q: "Year 13 feels overwhelming for my child. Should a tutor cover all three subjects or just one?"
faq_3_a: "Usually one, at least to begin with. An hour a week split across three A-Levels rarely moves any of them. Pick the subject with the biggest gap to the grade your child needs, or the one their offer names, and give it a steady weekly lesson. If a second subject wobbles later, it can have its own tutor. Much of the overwhelm tends to ease once one subject feels under control."
