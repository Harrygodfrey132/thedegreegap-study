faq_1_q: "My daughter is already getting As. What would a tutor actually add?"
faq_1_a: "Honest answer: less than for a student two bands below, and sometimes it is not worth it. Where it does pay is when the target genuinely needs the top band, because an A to A* move is about judgement rather than knowledge and almost nobody gets individual feedback on judgement in a classroom. A short block before mocks often does more for a strong student than a year-long programme. We will say on the call if we think that is the shape, including if the answer is that she does not need us."
faq_2_q: "How do predicted grades actually get decided?"
faq_2_a: "Mostly from mock performance, adjusted by teacher judgement and moderated so a school's predictions stay consistent. There is no national formula and no appeal, because it is a professional judgement rather than a result. The practical consequence is that the Year 13 autumn mock matters far more than students realise, since it is the paper that gets converted into the number UCAS sees. Anything you want to change is best changed before that, not argued about afterwards."
faq_3_q: "Do you cover the specific modules my child's school teaches?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and we check them before matching. At A-Level two students taking the same subject can sit different option papers, so the subject name on its own is not enough to match on. Tell us the board and the module choices on the consultation call. A tutor working from the wrong option spends the first fortnight catching up, which is a fortnight you have paid for."
