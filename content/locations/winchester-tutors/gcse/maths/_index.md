faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest topic in GCSE Maths for Winchester students?"
    a: "Vectors and algebraic proof, in that order. Vectors go wrong because students treat them as a geometry question and look for a length, when the mark scheme wants the route written in terms of the two vectors given. Algebraic proof goes wrong because a student who can do the algebra has never been shown what a proof is allowed to assume. Both sit near the end of the higher paper, both carry several marks, and both respond fast once they are taught as their own thing."
  - q: "How do we work out where the Maths marks are actually going?"
    a: "Look at question numbers, not the total. On a higher paper the early questions test single topics and the later ones combine them, so a student losing marks evenly across the paper has a content problem, and a student who is clean until question sixteen and then scattered has a technique problem. The second is faster to fix. Bring a marked mock to the consultation call and the pattern is usually visible before anyone has taught anything, which is why we ask Winchester Maths families for the script rather than for the grade."
  - q: "Our daughter is only in Year 10. Is that too early to start Maths tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the better year to start if the target is a grade 8 or 9. There is room to build the harder topics slowly instead of meeting them for the first time in a February mock, and no exam a few weeks away forcing everything into past papers. Year 10 sessions tend to be concept work with proof, vectors and algebraic manipulation built properly. Year 11 sessions are mostly papers. Both help, but the first is more comfortable."
