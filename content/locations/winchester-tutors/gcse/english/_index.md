faqs:
  - q: "What grade do Winchester sixth forms want for A-Level English?"
    a: "A grade 6 in English Literature is a common floor and the busier courses often ask for a 7, with some also wanting a 6 in Language. Peter Symonds takes most of the city's cohort and publishes its own requirement per course, and Kings' Winchester, Henry Beaufort and The Westgate set theirs separately. Requirements shift between years, so work from this year's admissions page. Moving a settled 6 into a secure 7 is usually a term of weekly work when the reading is already there."
  - q: "Would more English revision do the same job as a tutor?"
    a: "In English, more revision often means rereading the text, which rarely moves the grade. The useful test is a marked mock paragraph. If the writing is clear and the marks are still low, the gap is analysis, and no amount of rereading fixes that because the student cannot see what is missing. If the writing is thin or unfinished, the gap is planning or timing. Both are teachable, and both need someone marking the work each week rather than the student marking themselves."
  - q: "How much English homework should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Sixty to ninety minutes, split rather than done in one go. Most tutors set one timed question, which needs a clear 45 minutes and a quiet room, plus short quotation work spread across the week in ten-minute pieces. The split matters. The essay builds the answer shape and the daily repetition builds recall, and doing both in one sitting on a Sunday tends to deliver neither. Beyond about ninety minutes the returns fall away sharply."
  - q: "How are your English tutors chosen?"
    a: "Around one applicant in eight gets through. The stages are a written application, a subject knowledge test, a structured interview about explaining one idea clearly while under pressure, then reference checks. For English the interview leans hard on marking, because a tutor who writes well but cannot explain why a paragraph scored what it scored is no use to a Year 11. Harry or Joe speak to every tutor personally before they are matched with a family."
  - q: "Can we do English tutoring on a smaller budget?"
    a: "Yes, and the usual advice is to concentrate rather than to spread. One weekly hour through Year 10 and a second added only in the eight weeks before mocks costs a good deal less than two hours all year, and lands in much the same place. The other option is a short block, six to eight sessions aimed at one thing, the poetry comparison or the writing question, then stop. English responds well to that because the problems are usually named and finite. Plenty of Winchester families run it that way for two years and never add a second weekly hour at all."
