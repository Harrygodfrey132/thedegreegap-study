faqs:
  - q: "What GCSE English grade will my child need for A-Level English in Winchester?"
    a: "Often a 6 in English Literature, and the busier courses may ask for a 7, sometimes with a 6 in Language as well. Most of the city's Year 11s go on to Peter Symonds, which publishes its own requirement for each course. Other sixth forms set theirs separately. The numbers change between years, so check this year's admissions page. When the reading's already there, moving a settled 6 to a secure 7 is usually a term of weekly work, though nobody can promise it."
  - q: "Would more revision at home do the same job as a tutor?"
    a: "Probably not, because in English more revision often means rereading the book, and that rarely moves the grade. A marked mock paragraph is the best test. If the writing's clear and the marks are still low, the gap is analysis. Rereading can't fix that, because your child can't see what's missing. If the writing's thin or unfinished, the gap is planning or timing. Both can be taught. But both need someone marking the work every week, not your child marking their own."
  - q: "What should my child be doing between English lessons, and how much?"
    a: "Roughly an hour to ninety minutes a week, in a few short pieces. Most weeks there's one timed question to write, which needs 45 minutes and a quiet room. Around it sits quotation practice, ten minutes here and there across the week. Keeping them apart helps. The timed essay trains the shape of an answer and the little daily bits train memory, so one long Sunday session tends to do neither job well. Past an hour and a half, more work rarely helps."
  - q: "How do you choose your English tutors?"
    a: "Very carefully: of everyone who applies, around 3% get onto the platform. There's a written application, a subject knowledge test, a structured interview about explaining an idea clearly under pressure, then reference checks. For English, the interview leans hard on marking. A tutor who writes well but can't explain why a paragraph got the mark it did won't help a Year 11. Every tutor is interviewed personally by Harry or Joe before being matched with a family."
  - q: "Can we do English tutoring without spending a fortune?"
    a: "Yes, and the usual advice is to focus the lessons rather than spread them thin. Think one hour a week through Year 10, with a second added only in the eight weeks before mocks. That costs a lot less than two hours all year, and it gets to much the same place. The other option is a short block of six to eight lessons on one thing, like the poetry comparison or the writing question, and then stop. English suits that, because the problems usually have a name and an end. Plenty of Winchester families work this way for two years and never add a second weekly hour."
