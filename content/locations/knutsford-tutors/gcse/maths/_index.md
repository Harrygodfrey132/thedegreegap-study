faqs:
  - q: "What do Knutsford teenagers find hardest in GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Usually the long problem-solving questions near the end of each paper. Your child may know every topic in them, but the question won't say which one to use, and that's where the marks disappear. Next come algebra inside worded questions and the non-calculator paper, where shaky fractions or negatives from Year 8 cost a mark here and a mark there. If your child's aiming for a 7 or above, it's mostly those multi-step questions. If a 4 or 5 is the goal, it's more often the basics wobbling against the clock."
  - q: "How can I tell which Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Get out a recent mock and look at where on the paper the marks went, not just the total. If the early questions are mostly right and the damage is in the last third, it's usually exam technique: knowing where to start. If marks go missing all the way through, there's probably a gap from an earlier year. The tutor goes through it properly in the first lesson, question by question. But even a quick look together can tell you a lot."
  - q: "Higher or Foundation: which papers should my child sit?"
    a: "It depends where they are now and where they need to be. Higher tier runs from 9 down to 4, with a 3 possible for a near miss, while Foundation tops out at a 5. Knutsford Academy has asked for a 4 in Maths for its sixth form, which your child can get on either. A steady 5 or 6 in mocks usually points to Higher. If they're hovering around a 3 or 4, Foundation can be the safer bet. The school makes the final call, and a tutor's first lesson gives you something solid to take into that conversation."
  - q: "How do you choose your GCSE Maths tutors?"

References come last. That interview is where most people fall down, because being good at Maths and explaining it clearly aren't the same thing."
