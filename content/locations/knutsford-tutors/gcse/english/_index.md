  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read a lot?"
    a: "Because the exam rewards something reading doesn't build on its own: a planned, timed argument about a text. Lots of keen readers understand the books well and still write essays that retell the story instead of analysing it, or run out of time before their best point. Others were never taught to plan, so every essay comes out as it comes. A tutor works out which one it is from a recent essay, then practises that one habit."
  - q: "What actually happens in a GCSE English lesson?"
    a: "Most lessons run for an hour in three rough parts. First the tutor goes through last week's piece, marked, so your child sees which sentences earned marks and which didn't. Then comes the main teaching, on whatever's costing the most, like unseen poetry or the Language writing task. The last part is timed practice on that same thing. Every lesson has a replay, so your child can look back at the tutor's comments before a mock."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia or ADHD. Can a tutor still help with English?"
    a: "Yes, and English is often where it helps most, because the subject leans so much on reading speed and long stretches of writing. Tutors break essays into small named steps, use visual plans on the whiteboard and build up timing gradually rather than all at once. The replay means your child can revisit an explanation instead of relying on notes. Tell us about any diagnosis or exam arrangements on the call and we'll pick tutors with that in mind."
