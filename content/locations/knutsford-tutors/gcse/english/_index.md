  - q: "Why doesn't all that reading show up in my child's English grade?"
    a: "Because the exam rewards something reading doesn't build on its own: a planned, timed argument about a text. Lots of keen readers understand the books well and still write essays that retell the story, or run out of time before their best point. Others haven't found a way to plan yet, so every essay comes out as it comes. A tutor works out which it is from a recent essay, then practises that one habit."
  - q: "What will my child actually do in an English lesson?"
    a: "Most lessons run for an hour, split roughly three ways. The tutor starts with last week's piece, already marked, so your child sees which sentences earned marks and which didn't. Then comes the main teaching, on whatever's costing the most, like unseen poetry or the Language writing task. The last part is timed practice on that same thing. There's a replay of every lesson, so your child can look back at the tutor's comments before a mock."

The replay means your child can revisit an explanation instead of relying on notes. Mention any diagnosis or exam arrangements on the call and we'll pick tutors with that in mind."
