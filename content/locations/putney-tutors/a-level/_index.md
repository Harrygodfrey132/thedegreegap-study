faq_1_q: "My child had a great GCSE tutor. Should they just carry on with them for A-Level?"

Plenty of excellent GCSE tutors don't teach beyond Year 11, and that's no criticism. If so, we can suggest someone for the A-Level subject while your child keeps the person they like for anything else."
faq_2_q: "My child is starting at a new sixth form. Is it better to give them a term to find their feet before bringing in a tutor?"
faq_2_a: "A few weeks is sensible, but a whole term can be too long. Putney High and Ibstock Place both take students from other schools at sixteen, and those newcomers join classmates who already know the teachers and the school's way of doing things. The first marked assessments, usually in the autumn term, show quickly whether a topic was missed in the move. If Maths or Chemistry is the worry, a few lessons in the summer before Year 12 can help, too."
faq_3_q: "Ibstock Place and Putney High both offer the EPQ. Can a tutor help without doing the work for my child?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, within clear limits. The EPQ is your child's own project, and what they submit has to be their work. A tutor can help them narrow a big idea into a question they can actually answer, find and judge sources, and plan a write-up that argues rather than describes. They can also talk through drafts and ask the awkward questions a supervisor might. What they won't do is write any of it. Most families use a handful of lessons at the start and again before the final deadline."
