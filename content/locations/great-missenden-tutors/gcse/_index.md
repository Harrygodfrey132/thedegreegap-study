faq_1_q: "My child's friends at other schools seem to be revising different things. Does the exam board really matter?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, more than most parents expect. AQA, Edexcel and OCR cover much the same content, but the questions are worded differently, the marks are split differently and some topics carry more weight on one board than another. In Maths, OCR even numbers its papers differently from AQA and Edexcel. One school can use different boards for different subjects, too. So we ask about the board for every subject on the call, and match the tutor to it."
faq_2_q: "Why does my child get the homework right and then lose marks in the mock?"
faq_2_a: "Because homework and a mock test different things. At home there's a textbook, an example to copy and no clock, so a shaky method can still produce the right answer. In the exam hall, your child has to remember the method, pick the right one and write it out quickly. A tutor checks which of those is breaking down, then practises it under the same conditions: timed questions, no notes, marked the way an examiner would."
faq_3_q: "How soon would we notice a difference?"
faq_3_a: "Often within a few weeks, though not always in the grade first. What families tend to see early is a child who'll sit down with the subject instead of avoiding it. Marks usually take longer, typically a half term or more of weekly lessons with some practice in between. If the problem is exam technique rather than a gap from earlier years, things can move faster, and the tutor will tell you honestly how it's going."
