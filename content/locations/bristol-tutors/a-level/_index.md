faq_1_q: "My daughter's torn between a Filton degree apprenticeship and a university place. Can a tutor help with both?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, because both start with the same thing: her predicted grades. Airbus, GKN and the schemes linked to UWE look at those before anything else, and so do the University of Bristol and the rest of the Russell Group. A tutor can start in Year 12 on the subjects that matter most for both. That way she can keep both doors open while she makes up her mind."
faq_2_q: "My son says the jump to A-Level Physics is much steeper than GCSE. What does a tutor actually do?"
faq_2_a: "He's right, and it's usually not about effort. A-Level Physics leans hard on the maths inside it, and on multi-step problems GCSE never really asked for. A tutor goes through a past paper with him to see whether it's mechanics, fields or the algebra letting him down. Then they'll practise the methods, and the required practicals, until they feel routine."
faq_3_q: "Can A-Level tutoring actually move a UCAS predicted grade?"
faq_3_a: "Indirectly, yes. Your child's school sets the prediction from their Year 12 and early Year 13 work, so that's the work a tutor goes after. They mark recent essays and past papers the way an examiner would, see where the answers stop short, and practise that before the teacher settles on a number. And the earlier it starts, the more room there is."
faq_4_q: "Is it all online, and does that really work for A-Level?"
faq_4_a: "Yes, every lesson's online, using the platform Lessonspace, where your child and the tutor share a whiteboard. It suits A-Level well. Past-paper marking, essay feedback and worked problems all happen live on screen, and there's a replay of every lesson to watch back. Being online also means your child can be matched with the best tutor or examiner for their course anywhere in the country, not just whoever lives nearby."
