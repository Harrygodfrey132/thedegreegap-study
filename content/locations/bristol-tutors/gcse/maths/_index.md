faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE Maths do Bristol children find hardest?"
    a: "Usually the last third of the higher paper. The topics that separate a 6 from a 7 and above are algebra inside longer questions, circle theorem proofs where every reason has to be written down, iterative methods, and the multi-step problems that fill the end of the last calculator paper. None of those tests a single fact. They test whether your child can hold three steps together against the clock. That's a different skill from knowing the topic. And it's one that gets better with the right practice."
  - q: "Calculator or non-calculator: which paper should my child spend more time on?"
    a: "The one that's losing more marks, since each of the three papers counts the same. Without a calculator, the marks come from quick, sure arithmetic and tidy algebra. With one, they come from staying accurate over several steps, and from the patience to read a long question properly before diving in. The two kinds of paper tend to catch a child out in different ways, so one of the first things a tutor often does is mark one of each and compare where the marks went."
