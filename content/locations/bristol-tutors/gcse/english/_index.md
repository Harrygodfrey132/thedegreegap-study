faqs:
  - q: "What grade do Bristol sixth forms ask for before A-Level English?"
    a: "Each one publishes its own requirement and they vary by course and by year, so read the current page for the school your child is applying to rather than relying on what someone needed two years ago. Cotham, Redland Green and St Mary Redcliffe all set a subject-specific minimum for English that sits above their general entry bar. The point worth knowing is that English is assessed on its own, so a strong overall set of results won't compensate for the English grade itself when places are competitive."
  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE English, and does it matter?"
    a: "It matters more in English than parents expect. The core skills overlap, but the set texts differ school by school, the mark scheme wording differs between boards, and the balance between the reading and writing sections isn't identical. A tutor working to the wrong board can teach a good essay that scores below its worth on the paper your child actually sits. We confirm the board and the set texts on the consultation call before matching anyone, because getting that wrong wastes the first month."
  - q: "How can I tell which parts of GCSE English my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at which questions on a recent mock lost marks rather than the total. If the reading responses hold up and the writing question is weak, that's almost always timing, not ability, and it's a quick fix. If marks fall away across the longer analytical answers, the issue is essay structure and how evidence is used. And if Literature is fine while Language dips, or the other way round, you're looking at two different problems that need to be worked separately."
  - q: "How do you vet your GCSE English tutors?"
    a: "Every tutor clears a founder-led interview that roughly 3% of applicants pass, and Joe and Harry run those personally. Subject knowledge is the first filter, but the harder test is watching someone explain what makes one paragraph score higher than another to a student who can't yet see the difference. That's the part most applicants fail. All our tutors are graduates of top Russell Group universities and most have at least two years of one-to-one experience."
  - q: "What if my child doesn't want a tutor?"
    a: "It's the most common worry we hear and usually the least founded. Students resist the idea of a tutor, then settle within two or three sessions once they find it isn't school-shaped: one adult, full attention, and nobody else in the room to hear them get something wrong. Your child meets the tutor in a free 15-minute video call before anything is booked, so the first paid session isn't also the first introduction. If they still don't want to continue after a session or two, we pause it. That's their call."
