faq_1_q: "Can my child switch to a different sixth form after their GCSEs?"
faq_1_a: "Yes, and plenty do. Each sixth form sets its own entry rules and its own application dates. Owen's, for example, publishes rules asking for five grade 6s, with at least a 5 in English Language and in Maths, plus whatever each A-Level subject needs, and it only has a limited number of places for teenagers joining from elsewhere. Gracewood, Chancellor's and the colleges round about all set their own. Rules get updated most years, so read the latest guides while your child is in Year 10, when there's still time to act on them."
faq_2_q: "How will I know if the tutoring's working?"
faq_2_a: "You'll hear after every lesson. The tutor sends a short note on what they covered and what's next, so you can see the plan moving. Beyond that, look at what your child brings home. Fewer questions left blank is often the first sign, then a better mark in the topic they've been practising. Mocks show it too, though that can take a round or two. If something isn't shifting, tell us, and we'll talk it through with the tutor or find someone new."
faq_3_q: "Can one tutor cover Maths and Science, or do we need two?"
faq_3_a: "Sometimes one is enough. A few tutors on our platform are comfortable with GCSE Maths and Physics, or with all three sciences, and the calculations overlap enough to make that work. For a child who's really struggling in one subject, though, a tutor who knows that subject inside out usually gets further. English nearly always means a separate tutor. Tell us on the call which subjects are worrying you, and how much, and we'll suggest what makes sense for your child."
