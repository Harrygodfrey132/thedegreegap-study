faq_1_q: "My daughter's predicted grade seems harsh compared with what she can do."
faq_1_a: "It might be, and the research points the other way. Murphy and Wyness, publishing in 2020 from three years of UCAS data, found roughly 16% of applicants predicted accurately across their best three A-Levels and about 75% predicted too high. So a prediction that feels harsh is sometimes an unusually honest one. The way to find out is a recent paper and an outside opinion on where the marks are going."
faq_2_q: "Is a short block before mocks enough?"
faq_2_a: "For a student already close to the target, a short block of six to eight sessions aimed at one thing usually does more than a year-long programme and costs a fraction. A longer run only makes sense where the subject needs rebuilding rather than sharpening. The consultation call is where we work out which of the two you have, and we would rather recommend the smaller option and be right than sell the bigger one."
faq_3_q: "How does online A-Level tutoring work in practice?"

Online also means matching on specification rather than on who lives within driving distance, which is the constraint that actually limits quality."
