faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English trips up Chesham children most?"
    a: "Two questions come up again and again. The first is the long evaluation question, where your child's asked how far they agree with a statement about a text. Lots of teenagers describe what happens instead of arguing with it. The second is the poetry comparison, which needs both poems in the same paragraph, not one after the other. Neither is a reading problem. They're both answer shapes, and a shape can be taught."
  - q: "Why is my child's English prediction lower than we expected?"
    a: "Usually because the prediction follows what reached the page, not what your child knows. Predictions come from mocks plus teacher judgement. And the gap parents notice is between what their child can talk about at home and what they can write in 45 minutes with no notes. If your child discusses a novel brilliantly and then writes three paragraphs retelling it, the prediction goes with the writing. So a tutor starts by reading the actual mock script. Chesham Grammar, Chiltern Hills and The Amersham School each run English mocks on their own timetable. It's worth asking when your child sat theirs, as well as what it scored."
  - q: "What goes on in a typical GCSE English lesson?"
    a: "An hour usually splits into three parts. The first fifteen minutes go on last week's writing, marked against the real criteria, with the tutor explaining each mark out loud. The middle half hour rebuilds one skill: an opening that takes a position, a quotation worked into a sentence, or two poems held together. Then the last fifteen minutes are a timed question on that one skill, marked on the spot. Your child sees their own answer get better inside the hour. That does more for their confidence than being told it will."
  - q: "Has English tutoring actually helped other families?"
