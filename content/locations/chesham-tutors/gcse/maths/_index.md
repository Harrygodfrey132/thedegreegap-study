  - q: "Should my child put the revision into the calculator or non-calculator Maths paper?"
    a: "Both, because they carry equal weight and the grade comes from the total. They fail differently, though. The non-calculator paper punishes slow arithmetic and shaky algebraic manipulation, so a student who reaches for a calculator by habit loses marks early. The calculator papers punish method choice on multi-step questions and small accuracy errors late on. A diagnostic on one recent paper shows which is costing more inside twenty minutes, and the plan starts wherever the bleeding is worst."
  - q: "Is this a tutoring problem, or does my child just need to revise harder?"
    a: "Look at the working on a recent mock, not the mark. Clean, complete working with marks lost at the back of the paper points to exam technique, which a tutor fixes quickly. Blank space or crossed-out attempts on multi-step questions points to a method confidence gap, and that is harder to solve alone because more revision of the same kind repeats the same misunderstanding. A student already scoring in the high 80s across mocks usually needs targeted practice, not a full programme. We see both kinds in Chesham, and the Maths ones divide fairly cleanly. Grammar-set students tend to arrive with the technique problem, upper school students more often with the method one."
