  - q: "Where should my child's Maths revision go: the calculator paper or the non-calculator one?"
    a: "Into both. Each of the three papers is worth the same, and the grade comes from the total. But they catch children out differently. The non-calculator paper punishes slow arithmetic and shaky algebra, so a child who reaches for a calculator out of habit loses marks early on. The calculator papers punish picking the wrong method on longer questions, and small slips late in the paper. Twenty minutes with a recent set of mock papers shows which is costing your child more, and the plan starts wherever it hurts most."
  - q: "Is this a tutoring problem, or does my child just need to revise harder?"
    a: "The working on a recent mock will usually tell you, more than the mark will. If it's clean and complete but marks go at the back of the paper, that's exam technique, and a tutor can usually fix it quickly. Blank spaces or crossed-out attempts on the longer questions mean your child isn't confident with the method. That's harder to fix alone, because more of the same revision repeats the same misunderstanding. If they're already scoring in the high 80s per cent across their mocks, a bit of targeted practice beats a full programme. We see both kinds in Chesham, and they split fairly neatly. Children at the grammar schools more often come to us with the technique problem, and those at the upper schools more often with the method one."

If a topic needs rebuilding from Year 8, an experienced teacher who's seen that gap a hundred times is often the better match.
