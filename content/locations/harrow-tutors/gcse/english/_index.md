faqs:
  - q: "Which bit of GCSE English do Harrow teenagers find hardest?"
    a: "The unseen questions, more often than the set texts. Your child can know Macbeth or An Inspector Calls really well and still drop marks on a poem they've never seen, because that question tests a method, not a memory. Close behind comes timing in the Literature papers, where the essay that would've scored well ends up two-thirds written. Across the borough, that's where most of the marks you can win back are hiding. And both respond to weekly practice faster than most parents expect."
  - q: "AQA, Edexcel or OCR: does the exam board really matter for English?"
    a: "It does, and more in English than in most subjects. The core skills overlap, but the boards set different poetry anthologies, weight the comparison question differently and want different things from the Language writing task. A tutor who's taught your child's exact paper knows which paragraph shape the mark scheme rewards and which it quietly doesn't. So we check the board and the set texts on the call, before we suggest any tutor. Getting it wrong wastes a fortnight."
  - q: "Can one tutor cover both English Language and English Literature?"
    a: "Usually, yes, and most families prefer it, because each one helps the other. The close reading your child does in Literature lifts their answers on the Language reading questions, and the writing practice for Language shows up as tighter essays. If your child's solid in one and really struggling in the other, we'll sometimes split them. What we wouldn't do is give one tutor English and Maths just to keep things tidy. Those need two different tutors."
  - q: "My child has dyslexia or ADHD. Can a GCSE English tutor still help?"
    a: "Yes. We'll ask about it on the call, so you don't have to be the one to raise it. A number of tutors on the platform work with neurodivergent children regularly. In English the help is mostly about structure: a planning frame that's the same every time, quotations trimmed to what your child can actually remember, and long questions broken into stages on the shared whiteboard. Tell us what school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we'll match with that in mind."
  - q: "Can we pause lessons, or stop altogether?"
    a: "Just tell us. That's the whole process. There's no contract and no minimum term, and you pay lesson by lesson rather than for a block, so pausing over a holiday or after a good set of mocks costs nothing. We'd ask for a day or two's notice where you can, simply so the tutor isn't left waiting. Some Harrow families stop after the summer exams and come back for a younger child, and that's fine too."
