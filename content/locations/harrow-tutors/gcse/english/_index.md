faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Harrow students?"
    a: "The unseen questions, more often than the set texts. A student can know Macbeth or An Inspector Calls well and still lose marks on a poem they have never met, because that answer tests a method rather than a memory. Close behind it comes timing on the Literature papers, where the essay that would have scored well is only two thirds written. Those two are where most of the recoverable marks sit for students across the borough, and both respond to weekly practice faster than parents expect."
  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE English, and does it matter?"
    a: "It matters, and more in English than in most subjects. The core skills overlap, but the boards set different anthologies, weight the comparison question differently and ask for different things in the Language writing task. A tutor who has taught your child's exact paper knows which paragraph shape the mark scheme rewards and which it quietly does not. We confirm the board and the set texts on the consultation call, before any tutor in Harrow is suggested, because getting it wrong wastes a fortnight."
  - q: "Can the same tutor cover English Language and English Literature?"
    a: "Usually yes, and most families want that, because the two feed each other. The analytical reading built in Literature is what lifts the Language paper 1 response, and the writing technique drilled for Language shows up in essay control. Where a student is solid in one and struggling badly in the other, we sometimes split it. What we would not do is pair English with Maths in the same tutor for the sake of tidiness, since those need genuinely different specialists."
  - q: "Can a GCSE English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about it during matching rather than waiting to be told. A number of tutors on the platform work with neurodivergent students regularly. In English the practical help tends to be structural: a planning frame that stays the same every time, quotations cut down to what can actually be recalled, and long questions broken into named stages on the shared whiteboard. Tell us what the school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we will match accordingly."
  - q: "How do we pause or stop GCSE English tutoring if we need to?"
    a: "You tell us, and that is the whole process. There is no contract and no minimum term, and you pay session by session rather than for a block, so pausing over a holiday or after a good set of mocks costs nothing. We ask for a day or two of notice where possible, simply so the tutor is not sitting waiting. Families in Harrow often stop after the summer exams and come back for the next child, which is fine too."
