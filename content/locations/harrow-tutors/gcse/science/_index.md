  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Science tutoring session for a Harrow student?"
    a: "An hour in three rough parts. The first stretch marks the work set last week, out loud, so misconceptions get named rather than noted. The middle stretch teaches the topic bleeding the most marks, with equations rearranged and practicals rebuilt step by step on the shared whiteboard. The last stretch is exam questions on that same topic, marked against the scheme while your child watches where the marks actually sit. Sessions are recorded, so a method explained quickly can be replayed during revision."
  - q: "How much Science homework should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Around an hour to an hour and a half across the week, and it should be past-paper questions rather than re-reading notes. Two or three question sets on the topic from that lesson is the usual shape, because Science rewards practice at writing answers far more than it rewards highlighting. Past ninety minutes the returns drop off sharply, especially in Year 11 when school is already setting work. What matters more than volume is that it gets marked in the next session."
  - q: "What grade improvements have your Science tutors actually delivered?"

Where a foundation block is genuinely missing, the first month is spent building it and the grade follows afterwards. We do not promise grades, and the consultation call is also where we say if tutoring is not what your child needs right now.   - q: "Can my child have Science tutoring during the school holidays?"
