  - q: "How much English work should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Sixty to ninety minutes, and it should be writing rather than reading. One planned essay or one timed question beats an evening of highlighting a revision guide, because the marks go missing in the production and not in the knowledge. Tutors tend to set one piece written under the clock plus something shorter, a paragraph rebuilt or a set of quotations chosen and justified. Past ninety minutes the returns drop away, and in a Year 11 autumn across Plymouth there are four other subjects wanting the same evening."
  - q: "Why is my child's English predicted grade lower than we expected?"
    a: "Predictions come out of mock performance and teacher judgement, and in English the gap is nearly always between what a student can say about a text and what reaches the page in the time allowed. A thoughtful reader who produces three short paragraphs gets marked on the three paragraphs. Ask to see the marked script rather than the grade, then look for the point where the writing thins out. That point is the thing to work on, and it is a fixable thing rather than a verdict."
