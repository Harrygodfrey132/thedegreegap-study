faq_1_q: "He is in Year 12 and the first term has gone badly. Is it too early for a tutor?"
faq_1_a: "No. Year 12 is the best point in the whole course. Year 13 builds straight onto it, so a gap left now gets more expensive every month, and the predicted grade that goes to UCAS comes out of work done across this year. Sessions this early also have room to rebuild how your child studies, which a Year 13 plan rarely does. Plenty of families start later and still move a grade. It is just tighter, and it costs more hours."
faq_2_q: "My child got a grade 8 at GCSE and is struggling anyway. What changed?"
faq_2_a: "The question did. At GCSE a question mostly announces the method it wants, and strong students get a long way on pattern recognition without ever choosing anything. A-Level questions stop announcing it, so your child has to pick the approach first, and picking is a separate skill nobody sat them down and taught. Early sessions work on unseen questions with no topic heading attached. Uncomfortable for a fortnight, then much less so."
faq_3_q: "Can tutoring help with a degree apprenticeship application?"

Predicted grades have to clear whatever the scheme publishes, and employers like Babcock at Devonport set their own requirements and change them between intakes, so the subject work comes first. Then there is the application itself, which on most engineering routes means written answers and an assessment stage months ahead of the UCAS deadline. Tutors handle the subject side.
