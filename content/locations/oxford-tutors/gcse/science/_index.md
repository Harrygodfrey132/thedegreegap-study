faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Science for Oxford students?"
    a: "Three things, and they are the same across the Oxford schools because they are the same across the specifications. The required practicals, which are examined as recall of variables, apparatus and sources of error rather than as something done in a lab a year earlier. Moles and concentration calculations in Chemistry, where a student who understands the chemistry can lose every mark on the arithmetic. And rearranging equations in Physics, which is really a Maths gap turning up in a Science lesson. All three are narrow, which is why they respond quickly to targeted work."
  - q: "What actually happens in a GCSE Science tutoring session?"
    a: "The first session is a diagnostic that splits the three sciences apart, because a single Science grade on a report hides which one is dragging. After that a typical hour is real exam questions worked through on the shared whiteboard with the method written out rather than described, since in Science the marks are in the working. Required practicals get covered as a set, one or two per session, framed as the questions they become. The tutor sets a small amount of specific practice at the end so it lines up with what was just taught."
