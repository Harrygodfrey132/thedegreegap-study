faqs:
  - q: "Which parts of GCSE Science cost Oxford children the most marks?"
    a: "Three things, and they come up at every Oxford school, whichever exam board it uses. First, the practicals. Each board has its own list, and the exam asks about them as recall of variables, equipment and sources of error, not as something your child did in a lab a year earlier. Second, moles and concentration in Chemistry, where a child who understands the chemistry can lose every mark on the arithmetic. Third, rearranging equations in Physics, which is really a Maths gap showing up in a Science lesson. All three are narrow, so they're usually quick to improve."
  - q: "What does a Science lesson with a tutor actually look like?"
    a: "The first lesson pulls the three sciences apart, because one Science grade on a report hides which one is dragging. After that, a typical hour is real exam questions worked on the shared whiteboard, with the method written out rather than described, since in Science that's where the marks are. The practicals get covered one or two a lesson, as written exam questions. At the end the tutor sets a small amount of practice on exactly what was just taught, so it sticks."
