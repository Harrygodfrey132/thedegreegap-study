  - q: "How do you work out which Maths topics my child is actually missing?"
    a: "By reading the working rather than the score. The first session is a diagnostic: recent questions get sorted into two groups on the shared whiteboard, the ones your child could not start and the ones they started and stalled on. Those two mean opposite things. A blank is almost always a topic that was never properly taught or was missed, and it rebuilds quickly once named because nothing has to be unlearned. A stall is method confidence, which needs practice rather than teaching. Most students have some of both, and the balance decides the term."
  - q: "Is Year 10 too early to start Maths tutoring?"
    a: "No, and it is usually the best time. Year 10 has room for a diagnostic to be honest and for a genuine foundation gap to be rebuilt rather than patched, which is a few weeks of work then and a scramble in the spring of Year 11. It also gets ahead of the tier decision, which is easier to have a sensible conversation about when there is evidence of what the student can do rather than what a Year 8 assessment said. Starting in Year 11 is fine too, the plan is simply tighter."
