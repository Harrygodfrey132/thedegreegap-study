  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English when they read a lot?"
    a: "Because reading well and writing to a mark scheme are different skills, and the exam only tests the second. Plenty of strong readers lose marks because they write what they noticed rather than what the question asked for, or because they quote and then move on without explaining what the writer's choice does. The examiner is waiting for the two sentences after the quotation. That is a technique gap, not a comprehension one, and it is the fastest thing in English to move because the thinking is already there."
  - q: "Does my child need a tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "In English, revision alone rarely moves a grade. Learning more quotations helps a student who does not know the text, but most students who are stuck do know it. What they cannot do is turn what they know into the shape the mark scheme rewards, and you cannot revise your way into that because there is nothing to memorise. It needs writing, marking and rewriting with someone who can point at the exact sentence that lost the mark. That is the case for a tutor in English more than in most subjects."
  - q: "Can you help a student with dyslexia or who finds writing difficult?"
    a: "Yes, and a good many families come to us for exactly this. Several tutors in the network have specific experience with dyslexia, ADHD and processing differences, and the practical work is usually about structure rather than content: planning frames, sentence starters, dictation before writing, and breaking a long answer into parts built one at a time. One-to-one helps here more than in most subjects because the pace is set by the student rather than the class. Tell us on the call what school has in place, including any exam access arrangements."
