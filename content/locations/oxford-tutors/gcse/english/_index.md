  - q: "My child reads loads. So why is English such a struggle?"
    a: "Because reading well and writing to a mark scheme are different skills, and the exam only tests the second. Lots of keen readers lose marks by writing what they noticed instead of what the question asked. Or they drop in a quote and move straight on without explaining what the writer's choice does. The examiner's waiting for the two sentences after the quote. That's technique, not understanding. And it's the quickest thing in English to fix, because the thinking's already there."
  - q: "Would more revision do the job, or does my child need a tutor?"
    a: "In English, revision on its own rarely moves a grade. Learning more quotes helps a child who doesn't know the text, but most children who are stuck do know it. What they can't do is turn it into the shape the mark scheme rewards, and there's nothing to memorise that will teach them that. It takes writing, marking and rewriting with someone who can point to the exact sentence that lost the mark. That's why a tutor helps more in English than in most subjects."

One-to-one helps here more than in most subjects, because your child sets the pace, not the class. Tell us on the call what school already has in place, including exam access arrangements such as extra time."
