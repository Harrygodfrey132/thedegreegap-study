faqs:
  - q: "What English grade does Didcot Sixth Form look for?"
    a: "It depends on what your child wants to study. Didcot Sixth Form's guide says A-Level entry generally needs at least a 6 at GCSE in the subject, or a related one, so if your child wants English at A-Level, that means a strong English grade. Plenty of other A-Levels involve long written answers too, so English skills travel. UTC Oxfordshire's sixth form and the colleges set their own entry rules. Entry rules get updated, so look at this year's guide for each place your child is considering."
  - q: "What does an hour of English with a tutor actually look like?"
    a: "Usually it starts with something your child has written recently, or an answer from a past paper. The tutor picks one skill to work on, such as choosing evidence or explaining a writer's choice, and shows how on the whiteboard. Then your child has a go with a timer running, and the tutor goes through it with them straight away. Every lesson is recorded, so your child can replay the model answer later. You'll get a short note afterwards on what was covered."
  - q: "Language and Literature are separate GCSEs. Can one tutor do both?"
    a: "Usually, yes, and it often works better that way. The two GCSEs test different things. Language is about texts your child hasn't seen before and a piece of their own writing. Literature is about the novel, play and poems they've studied, written about from memory. One tutor can split the time between them, leaning towards whichever grade needs it more. If the Literature texts are the worry, we'll look for a tutor who knows your child's ones."
  - q: "Reading and writing take my child longer because of dyslexia or ADHD. Can English tutoring help?"
    a: "Yes. English is often where dyslexia and ADHD show most, because it asks for quick reading and long written answers. On the call we'll ask what your child finds hardest, from finishing in time to getting started at all, and look for tutors with that experience. In lessons the tutor might give your child a fixed shape for every answer, or read an extract aloud with them before any writing starts. And if your child has extra time or a reader in exams, the practice can match it."
  - q: "What if my child misses a 4 in English Language?"
    a: "Then there's a resit to aim for, and a tutor can help with it. English Language and Maths are the only GCSEs that can be resat in November, and a teenager without a 4 usually keeps studying English after Year 11 anyway. A tutor looks at where the marks went on the summer papers and starts there, often the reading questions or the writing task. Whether your child is starting sixth form in Didcot or a college course somewhere else, lessons fit round the new timetable. Nobody can promise the resit grade, but there's a clear plan to work to."
