faq_1_q: "If the UTC might be on the cards for Year 10, when should GCSE tutoring start?"
faq_1_a: "There's no single right moment, but the spring of Year 9 can work well. It gives a tutor a term to firm up the Maths and Science the UTC's courses lean on, whichever school your child ends up at. Some families wait until Year 10, once their child has settled. Starting in Year 11 can still work, as long as the first few weeks go on the gaps rather than straight onto past papers."
faq_2_q: "We've changed schools and the exam board's changed with it. Is that a problem?"
faq_2_a: "It can be, at first. Most of the content is shared between AQA, Edexcel and OCR, but each board has its own way of wording questions and handing out marks. In English the set texts may change as well. A child who's moved schools can also find a topic was taught at the old school in a different term, or not yet at all. We match tutors to the board your child sits now, so lessons line up with the papers they'll actually take."
faq_3_q: "Which GCSE grades matter most for the routes around Didcot?"
faq_3_a: "It depends on the route, and each one publishes its own rules. Didcot Sixth Form's guide generally looks for at least a 6 in a subject, or a related one, if your child wants to take it at A-Level. UTC Oxfordshire's sixth form, college T Levels and apprenticeships at places like Harwell set their own entry grades. A 4 in English and Maths matters wherever your child goes, because without it they'll usually keep studying the subject after 16. So check this year's requirements for the two or three options your child likes, and work back from there."
faq_4_q: "My child is miles behind in one subject. Can a tutor still help before the GCSEs?"
faq_4_a: "Usually, yes, though the plan has to be honest about the time left. The tutor doesn't race to catch up with this week's lesson. They go back to the topic where things first stopped making sense, sometimes from Year 8 or 9, and build forward in order. Year 10 leaves space to do that properly. In Year 11 the tutor picks the topics worth the most marks first. Either way, you'll get a note after each lesson, so you can see whether it's working."
