faq_1_q: "My child's in Year 13 and has barely started revising. Is it too late?"
faq_1_a: "For the final exams, it isn't. The plan just has to be tighter. There's no point trying to reteach two years of the course. Instead, the tutor and your child agree a short list of topics from the recent mocks, then come back to them every week with past paper questions until they hold. No one can say for certain how it'll go. But there's usually more time than it feels like."
faq_2_q: "How do predicted grades work, and can a tutor change them?"
faq_2_a: "A tutor can't change the prediction itself, because teachers set it. What a tutor can change is the work those teachers see. Most of what feeds a prediction happens in Year 12, and it's sent with the UCAS application in the autumn of Year 13. So if your child is in Year 12 now, there's time to make that work stronger before anyone writes the number down. In Year 13 the target becomes the summer papers instead."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help with A-Level Physics, Chemistry or Biology?"
faq_3_a: "Yes. In all three, Year 13 builds straight on Year 12, so a shaky topic early on tends to come back later. Practical skills aren't only assessed in the lab. They're tested in the written papers too, through questions on method and data: AQA calls the experiments required practicals, OCR has its PAGs and Edexcel its core practicals. Physics and Chemistry also lean hard on Maths. A tutor who knows your child's board can work on all of that, alongside A-Level Maths if it's in the mix."
