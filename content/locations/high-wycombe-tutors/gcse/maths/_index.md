faqs:
  - q: "Which topic trips High Wycombe children up most in GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Algebra, less as one topic than as the thread through everything else. Rearranging equations, algebraic fractions and simultaneous equations turn up inside geometry, ratio questions and the wordy problems at the back of the paper. So a wobble there costs marks in places that don't look like algebra at all. After that, circle theorems and iterative methods (repeating a calculation to get closer and closer to an answer) tend to catch children out. Both come down to method. And all of them improve faster with someone explaining them one-to-one than with more practice alone."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Isn't it a bit soon to get a Maths tutor?"
    a: "No, it's a good time, and if your child's decided Maths isn't their subject, it's much better than waiting for Year 11. Everything ahead builds on what's already shaky, so closing the gap now stops it causing trouble for the next eighteen months. Year 10 lessons feel different, too. They're about rebuilding method rather than drilling past papers, which is calmer, with no exam a few months away. The same gap found in March of Year 11 has to be fixed while everything else is happening at once."
  - q: "My child keeps making 'silly mistakes' in Maths. Why?"
    a: "Usually because something from two or three years back never quite set, and in Maths nothing gets left behind. A shaky grasp of negatives or fractions doesn't stay in the year it was taught. It turns up again inside every rearranged formula and every ratio question, costing a mark or two each time, and it looks like carelessness. By Year 11 it adds up to what looks like general weakness. So in the first lesson, the tutor traces wrong answers back to the skill underneath, not the topic in the question. That's usually where the real answer is."
