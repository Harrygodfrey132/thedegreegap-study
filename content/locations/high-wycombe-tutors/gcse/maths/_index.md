faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest topic in GCSE Maths for High Wycombe students?"
    a: "Algebra, and not as a single topic but as the thread running through everything else. Rearranging equations, algebraic fractions, and simultaneous equations show up inside geometry, inside ratio questions and inside the wordy problems at the back of the paper, so a wobble there costs marks in places that do not look like algebra at all. After that, circle theorems and iterative methods come up most, both of which are method rather than understanding. All of them respond to being taught properly rather than practised more."
  - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start GCSE Maths tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the right time rather than too early, and for a student who has decided Maths is not their subject it is considerably better than Year 11. The content ahead builds directly on what is already shaky, so closing the gap now stops it doing damage for eighteen months. Year 10 sessions also look different: rebuilding method rather than drilling papers, which is calmer and does not carry the pressure of an exam a few months away. The same gap found in March of Year 11 has to be fixed while everything else is happening at once."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Usually because something from two or three years back never quite set, and Maths is the one subject where nothing gets left behind. A shaky grasp of negatives or fractions does not stay in the year it was taught. It reappears inside every rearranged formula and every ratio question, costing a mark or two each time in a way that reads as carelessness. By Year 11 the total looks like general weakness. The first hour traces wrong answers back to the skill underneath rather than the topic on the question, which is usually where the real answer is."
  - q: "Are your GCSE Maths tutors qualified teachers or university students?"
    a: "Both, and which one fits depends on the student. The network includes qualified secondary teachers, Russell Group graduates and current undergraduates who sat these exact papers recently. A student who needs a topic rebuilt from the foundations often does better with a qualified teacher who has taught that sequence many times. A student who is quietly convinced they cannot do Maths sometimes does better with someone closer to their own age who remembers feeling the same. We suggest on what the diagnostic shows rather than a house preference."
  - q: "Can my child have Maths tutoring during the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and for rebuilding an older gap holidays are often the best time of all. There is no homework competing for the evening, and a half-term or Easter block can take one topic and work through it across several consecutive days, which suits algebra well because it needs repetition close together rather than spread thin. Everything runs online, so families away from home keep their slot. Plenty of families use the summer between Year 10 and Year 11 exactly this way."
