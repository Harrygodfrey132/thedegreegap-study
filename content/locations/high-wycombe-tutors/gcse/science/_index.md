faqs:
  - q: "Is triple Science harder in High Wycombe than elsewhere?"
    a: "No. The courses are national, so the content's the same wherever your child sits it. What differs between schools, here as anywhere, is how much lesson time triple Science gets and how much of the practical work children do themselves. It's worth asking your child's school about that, because it explains more about how they're finding it than any comparison with another area. If your child moved schools partway through, that's also the most likely cause of a gap, since schools teach the topics in different orders."
  - q: "When's the best time to start in Year 11?"
    a: "Any time from September to February works, and earlier is better. Mocks in the winter feed into predicted grades, and those are what sixth forms and colleges look at when they make offers. So work that lands before the mocks counts twice. Starting after the mocks still helps, and plenty of families do. The plan just has to be narrower, picking the topics worth the most marks rather than rebuilding properly. With triple Science, the autumn term is when one weak subject can still be caught up without the other two suffering."
  - q: "Why is my child's Science predicted grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "A predicted grade is built from the mock result and the teacher's judgement. In Science, the gap is usually between understanding the ideas and writing them the way the mark scheme wants. Your child might explain a process perfectly out loud, then write three correct points with no links between them in a six-mark answer. That scores far less than it feels like it should. On triple, one of the three may also be dragging the prediction down. Looking at the actual marked paper, not just the grade, shows which it is."
  - q: "How do you choose your Science tutors?"
    a: "We interview every tutor ourselves, and only about 3% of people who apply make it onto the platform. We check subject knowledge, but that's rarely what rules people out, as most Science applicants know their stuff. What we're really looking for is whether they can explain an idea to someone who doesn't get it yet, and tell whether it's landed. Joe or Harry tests that live, in the interview. References are checked too, before any tutor starts with a family."
  - q: "My child doesn't want a tutor. What then?"
    a: "Let them meet the tutor before anything's decided. It's the worry parents raise most, and the pushback usually comes before your child has met anyone. It's about what having a tutor seems to say about them. The free 15-minute video meeting gives them a say in the match before you commit. With triple Science there's often something else going on too. A capable child can find it hard to admit one of the three has got away from them. Calling it a timetable problem, not a personal one, usually helps."
