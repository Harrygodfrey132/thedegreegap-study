faq_1_q: "My child is in Year 9. Is it worth starting GCSE tutoring now, or should we wait?"
faq_1_a: "It depends what's going on. If one subject already feels shaky, Year 9 is a good moment: the gap is still small and there's no exam pressure, so the tutor can rebuild slowly. If things are broadly fine, most families start in Year 10, when GCSE content begins in earnest and the first proper tests arrive. Starting after the Year 11 mocks still helps, but there's far less room to go back and fix the basics."
faq_2_q: "Can one tutor help with English Language and English Literature, or are they too different?"
faq_2_a: "One tutor can usually cover both, though they're very different exams. Language means reading unseen extracts and writing your own piece against the clock, so the work is technique and planning. Literature is about set texts, knowing the books and poems well enough to quote from them and argue. The tutor splits the time towards whichever paper is costing your child more, and that balance can shift as the year goes on."
faq_3_q: "My child is a long way behind in one subject. Where does a tutor even begin?"
faq_3_a: "Further back than you might expect. The tutor looks at a recent test and asks your child to talk through a few questions, which usually shows where things stopped making sense. Sometimes that's a Year 8 topic. Rather than racing to catch the class up, they rebuild from there, quickly where it's secure and slowly where it isn't, so your child has something solid underneath before the harder GCSE content lands."
