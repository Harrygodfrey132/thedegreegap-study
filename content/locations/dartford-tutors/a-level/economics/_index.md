faqs:
  - q: "Which bit of A-Level Economics trips Dartford sixth formers up most?"
    a: "Usually the macro half, and evaluation in the long essays. Micro topics come with obvious examples, the Dart Charge, Bluewater's shops, bus fares, while ideas like the multiplier or exchange rates take more work to make concrete. Then there's evaluation: plenty of students explain a point well and stop, when the top marks need a judgement about what it depends on. A tutor can usually see which it is from one marked essay."
  - q: "Between lessons, how much Economics practice is sensible?"
    a: "About an hour or an hour and a half a week, on top of school homework. That might be one essay plan, a data question and ten minutes reading the news for examples. More than that tends to turn into rewriting notes, which feels productive and doesn't move the mark much. The tutor sets the work and marks it in the next lesson, so your child can see what's changing."
  - q: "Is it a tutor my child needs for Economics, or simply more revision time?"
    a: "Look at a marked essay. If the content is right but the marks are low, it's usually technique, evaluation or timing, and that's where a tutor helps fastest. If the knowledge itself is patchy, especially on the macro topics, revision alone often goes round in circles because nobody is explaining it a different way. If your child is already scoring well, a few weeks of essay practice may be enough, and we'll tell you so."
  - q: "My child is dyslexic and the essays take ages. Can an Economics tutor help?"
    a: "Yes. Mention it on the call and we'll put forward tutors used to working with dyslexic sixth formers. A lot of the help is structure: a simple plan for every essay, diagrams doing some of the explaining, and short paragraphs built one at a time on the whiteboard. The replay means your child can go back over an explanation without asking again. And if your child has extra time in exams, the tutor practises with that timing."
  - q: "Am I allowed to watch my child's first Economics lesson?"
    a: "Of course. Plenty of parents join the first lesson to see how it works, and most step back after that, because sixth formers tend to ask more questions without an audience.
