  - q: "What happens in a GCSE English lesson with a tutor?"
    a: "A typical lesson might go like this. First, a look at whatever your child wrote since last time, marked together on the whiteboard. Then a short bit of teaching on one skill, like comparing two poems or opening a story so it grabs the reader. The rest is practice, often on a real question from your child's board, with part of it timed. Afterwards the tutor sends you a few lines about the lesson and what comes next."
  - q: "How much English work will my child have between lessons?"
    a: "A little, set by the tutor so it fits around school homework. Often that's one timed paragraph, a quick essay plan or a few quotations to learn. It's meant to take an evening, not swallow a weekend. The tutor marks it at the start of the next lesson, and that's often the most useful part, because your child sees exactly what to change. If mocks are on or there's a lot of school homework that week, tell the tutor and they'll ease off."
  - q: "Our son is autistic, and open-ended English questions make him anxious. Can a tutor help?"
    a: "Yes. Tell us on the call, and we'll suggest tutors who've worked with children who think and learn the way he does. What usually helps is taking the guesswork out, with a fixed structure for each type of question that he can see on screen while he writes. Working on one skill at a time, rather than a whole paper, tends to help too. He can rewatch any lesson as often as he likes. If the first tutor isn't right for him, we'll find another at no cost."
  - q: "What if my child doesn't get a 4 in English Language?"
    a: "It's not the end of anything. A Fleet teenager who misses a 4 usually carries on with English at college, alongside their main course. English Language is also one of only two GCSEs, with Maths, that can be resat in November as well as in the summer. A tutor can help with either. Lessons go straight to whatever cost the most marks first time, often the reading questions or the writing task. If results day doesn't go to plan, September is a perfectly good time to start."
