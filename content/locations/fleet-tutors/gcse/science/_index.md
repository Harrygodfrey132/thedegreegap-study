faqs:
  - q: "Can online lessons really help with the practicals in GCSE Science?"
    a: "Yes, because the exam doesn't ask your child to do the practicals again. It asks them to write about them: the method, the variables, the safety steps and what the results show. A tutor works through all of that on the shared whiteboard, drawing tables and graphs as they go. Each board keeps its own list. AQA calls them required practicals, Edexcel has core practicals and OCR uses PAGs, and we'll look for a tutor who knows your child's list, wherever in the UK they live. Your child just joins from home, whether that's in Elvetham Heath, Odiham or Fleet itself."
  - q: "Should my child be doing Higher or Foundation Science?"
    a: "It's the school that enters your child for a tier, but it's worth knowing what each tier allows. On Combined Science, Higher awards grades from 9-9 down to 4-4, with a 4-3 for a child who falls just short, and anything lower gets no grade at all. Foundation runs from 1-1 up to 5-5. So a Fleet teenager hoping for A-Level Biology at The Sixth Form College Farnborough, which has asked for 6-6, will need Higher. For a child working around 3-3, Foundation is often the safer choice, because on Higher they'd risk ending up with no grade. If it's borderline, the tutor can tell you how your child copes with Higher-style questions before you talk to school."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs a Science tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "It depends on what's going wrong. If your child simply hasn't learnt the content yet, a revision timetable may be enough for now. If they've revised and still lose marks, the trouble is usually in how they answer the question rather than what they know. More revision rarely fixes that on its own, and it's where a tutor helps most, because they can watch your child work. We'll give you an honest view on the call, including if we think a tutor isn't needed yet."
  - q: "What kind of person would be teaching my child Science?"

  - q: "What if my child misses the Science grade a college course asks for?"

The Sixth Form College Farnborough, for one, says it gives advice at enrolment once GCSE results are known, so a missed line isn't always the end of the conversation. Science can't be resat in November, unlike English Language and Maths, so a resit would come the following summer.
