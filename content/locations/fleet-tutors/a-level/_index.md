faq_1_q: "Our daughter's settling in at college. How will we know if she's struggling?"
faq_1_a: "Her first marked work will tell you more than she will. Most teenagers say it's fine for the first few weeks, partly because everything's new and they're still finding their way round. If essays or tests come back well below what she was getting in Year 11, that's the moment to act, rather than waiting for the first report. A tutor can look at that work with her, see whether it's the content, the method or simply time, and build the lessons from there."
faq_2_q: "How much does Year 12 count towards university?"
faq_2_a: "Quite a lot. The A-Level exams themselves don't come until the end of Year 13, but universities see predicted grades when your child applies, and teachers base those mostly on Year 12 work. Applications go in during the autumn of Year 13, so by then most of the evidence is in. Tutoring can't change a prediction directly, only the work behind it. Year 13 lessons still count, though, because the final exams decide whether an offer is met."
