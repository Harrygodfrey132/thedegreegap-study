angle_body_1: "Students learn the trigonometric identities, often thoroughly, and still cannot do the questions. The reason is that the examined skill is not recall, it is selection: looking at an expression and seeing which identity will move it somewhere useful. That is a strategic judgement and it is almost never taught directly, because lessons introduce identities one at a time with questions that obviously want the one just covered. In an exam, nothing announces which to reach for, and a student who knows all nine can still sit staring at an expression with no idea where to begin."

  - q: "What is the hardest part of A-Level Maths for Swindon students?"
    a: "The move from doing what you are told to choosing what to do. At GCSE the topic is named and the method follows; at A-Level a question announces nothing and the student has to select. Trigonometric identities are where this bites hardest, because knowing all of them is no help without a way of deciding which to use. Mechanics modelling questions are the other common sticking point, for the same underlying reason."
  - q: "How can I tell which parts of A-Level Maths my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Look at which questions were started rather than only which were wrong. Blank questions mean no route in, which is a selection problem and a specific fix. Working that begins confidently and stops partway means a method ran out. Scattered small errors mean accuracy. Those three need different terms, and a marked paper separates them in about fifteen minutes."
