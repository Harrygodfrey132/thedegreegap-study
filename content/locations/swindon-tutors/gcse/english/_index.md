  - q: "How many GCSE English tutoring sessions does it take to improve a grade?"
    a: "Eight to twelve weekly sessions for a band of movement is the usual frame, assuming writing is happening between them. Where the problem is pacing or structure it can be faster, because those are habits and habits change once a student has seen the effect on their own marks. Where a student is finding the texts themselves hard, it takes longer and an honest tutor will say so in the first few weeks. The first session is a diagnostic, and the plan from session two follows what it finds rather than a fixed programme."
  - q: "How long is each GCSE English tutoring session?"
    a: "Most are sixty minutes, which fits English well: long enough to plan an answer, write it to time and have it marked properly in the same sitting. Some families book ninety minutes closer to exams so a full paper can be attempted under conditions, though a well-used hour weekly beats a longer session done tiredly. For younger students, or where concentration is the real limit, forty-five minutes sometimes works better. It is worth raising on the consultation call rather than assuming the default."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and several tutors in the network work this way often. In English the help is structural: planning frames that hold an argument together, a fixed paragraph shape so writing does not start from nothing each time, and long answers broken into named parts. Where a student has extra time, the work is frequently about using it deliberately rather than simply having more of it, which is a skill in itself. Tell us on the consultation call what school has in place, including exam access arrangements, and we match on it."
  - q: "Can I sit in on my child's English sessions?"
    a: "You can, and some parents do for the first one. Most students work better without an audience though, and after that first session we usually suggest stepping back. What you get instead is more useful: every session is recorded so you can watch any of it afterwards, and the tutor sends a short note on what was covered and what was set. If you want a proper conversation about progress, ask at any point. Nothing about how your child is doing should come as a surprise at the end of term."
