faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Maths for Swindon students?"
    a: "The last third of the paper, more than any single topic. That is where algebra, circle theorems, iterative methods and the multi-step problem-solving questions sit, and they carry several marks each. Students who are comfortable with number and ratio can lose a grade entirely in that stretch without ever meeting a topic they have not been taught. The other common loss is arithmetic slips on the non-calculator paper, which look trivial individually and add up quickly. Both are method problems, and method responds faster than content does."
  - q: "How can I tell which GCSE Maths topics my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Get hold of a marked paper and look at question numbers rather than the total. Papers are ordered roughly by difficulty, so losses bunched at the end mean specific topics, while losses spread evenly usually mean accuracy or pace. Blank questions and crossed-out working tell you different things too: a blank means they did not know where to start, while abandoned working means the method ran out partway. Five minutes doing that tells you more than a term of reports, and it is what the first session does formally."
  - q: "What happens in a typical GCSE Maths tutoring session?"
    a: "An hour in three parts. The first fifteen minutes are spent on the work set last week, marked properly so mistakes surface rather than being glossed over. The middle half hour is the actual teaching, aimed at whichever block of the paper the diagnostic pointed at, with every step written out on the shared whiteboard rather than done mentally. The final fifteen minutes are exam questions on that topic, marked against the real scheme in front of your child. Homework is set at the end so it follows directly from what was covered."
