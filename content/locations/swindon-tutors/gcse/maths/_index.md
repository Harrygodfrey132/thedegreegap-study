faqs:
  - q: "Where in GCSE Maths do Swindon children tend to lose the most marks?"
    a: "In the last third of the paper, more than in any one topic. That's where the algebra, circle theorems, iterative methods and long problem-solving questions sit, and each carries several marks. A child who's comfortable with number and ratio can lose a whole grade in that stretch without meeting anything they haven't been taught. The other common leak is small arithmetic slips on the non-calculator paper. Each one looks trivial, but they add up fast. Both are about method, and method improves faster than content."
  - q: "Can I work out where my child's losing marks myself?"
    a: "Yes, with a marked paper. Look at the question numbers rather than the total. Questions get harder as the paper goes on, so losses bunched at the end point to specific topics, and losses spread evenly usually mean accuracy or pace. Blanks and crossings-out tell you different things too. A blank usually means your child couldn't see where to begin. Abandoned working means the method ran out partway. Five minutes of that tells you a lot, and it's what the first lesson does properly."
  - q: "What does an hour with the tutor actually involve?"

The last fifteen minutes are exam questions on that topic, marked against the real mark scheme in front of your child. Homework's set at the end, so it follows straight on from the lesson."
