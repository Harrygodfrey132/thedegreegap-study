faq_1_q: "She revises for hours and the grade doesn't move. What are we missing?"
faq_1_a: "Usually it's what the hours go on, not how many there are. Rereading a topic your child already gets feels productive, and it's where most teenagers drift because it's comfortable. But it doesn't change the mark. A tutor marks a recent paper, finds the three or four question types losing the most marks, and spends each lesson there. Quite often the revision hours go down, not up."
faq_2_q: "Year 10 or Year 11: when's the best time to start?"
faq_2_a: "Year 10, if you've got the choice. The content steps up that year, and any gaps that open are small enough to close quickly once someone spots them. Left alone, they build for a year and then all show up at once in a mock. Year 11 still works, and lots of families start then. The plan just leans more on exam technique and past papers."
faq_3_q: "Is it worth having a tutor before the mocks?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and it's one of the easiest places to see progress. In the weeks before, your child sits full papers against the clock, and the tutor marks them against the real mark scheme. Afterwards the mock itself becomes the plan, because every lost mark shows what the next few lessons need to cover. And mock grades feed into the predicted grade sixth forms see, so they're worth taking seriously."
