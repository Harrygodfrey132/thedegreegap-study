faq_1_q: "Is it worth starting A-Level tutoring in Year 12, or can it wait until Year 13?"
faq_1_a: "Year 12 is usually the better time, and the autumn of Year 12 better still. The jump from GCSE shows up early, in the first few tests, and it's much easier to fix one topic in October than five in May. Summer exams then feed the predicted grades that go off with UCAS applications in Year 13. Starting in Year 13 can still help, especially before the mocks, but there's less room to rebuild anything that went wrong the year before."
faq_2_q: "My child got good GCSEs but is really struggling with A-Level Chemistry. Is that normal?"
faq_2_a: "Very common, and it doesn't mean the subject was the wrong choice. A-Level Chemistry asks for far more explaining and far less recall than GCSE, and the maths gets heavier quickly. It also helps to know which course your child is on: the Ashcombe teaches OCR Chemistry A, while Reigate College uses OCR's Salters course, which presents the chemistry through a series of real-world contexts. A tutor who knows that course looks at the last test, finds where the method breaks, and rebuilds from there."
