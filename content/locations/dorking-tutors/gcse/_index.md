faq_1_q: "Is Year 9 too early to think about GCSE tutoring, or should we wait for Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "It depends more on what's going wrong than on the year. A Year 9 who's lost confidence in Maths gains a lot from a few months of steady lessons before the GCSE content piles up. Year 10 gives a tutor time to fix older gaps without mocks looming. And Year 11 still works, though it's busy: at the Ashcombe, sixth form applications go in by early November and every Year 11 has an interview with the Head of Sixth Form in January or February. Starting in the autumn gives your child more to show by then."
faq_2_q: "How soon would we actually notice a difference?"
faq_2_a: "Usually in how your child talks about the subject first, often within a few weeks: less dread before the lesson, a question asked in class. Marks take longer, because they come from tests, and tests arrive on the school's timetable rather than ours. With a lesson a week and a bit of practice in between, most families see a class test or mock move within a term. If nothing's shifting after half a term, we'd want to hear about it, and so would the tutor."
faq_3_q: "My child was off school for much of Year 10. Can they still catch up before the GCSEs?"
faq_3_a: "No, though it pays to be choosy. A tutor won't try to reteach two years of every subject. They look at which missed topics carry the most marks and which later topics depend on them, and start there. In Maths that's often algebra and ratio. In English it's usually how to plan an essay, which then works for every text. Your child's school can tell you what was covered while they were away, and that list makes a good starting point."
