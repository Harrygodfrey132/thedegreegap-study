faqs:
  - q: "What Maths grade will my child need for A-Level Maths or the sciences at Reigate College or the Ashcombe?"
    a: "It varies by sixth form and by course, so check each one's latest prospectus. Reigate College currently asks for a 6 in GCSE Maths for A-Level Maths and a 7 for Further Maths, and its Biology and Physics courses want a 6 on the Higher tier. The Ashcombe's guide expects A-Level Maths students to have sat the Higher paper and be on track for a 6 to 9, with a 7 to 9 for Further Maths. If your child is in Year 10, there's time to build towards it steadily."
  - q: "The Ashcombe uses AQA and the Priory uses Edexcel or Eduqas. Does the board change how GCSE Maths is taught?"
    a: "The Maths itself is mostly the same, because every board follows the same national content. The papers aren't. AQA and Edexcel both set three papers, one without a calculator and two with. Eduqas sets two papers instead. Question wording, the amount of reading in the problem-solving questions and how marks are split across the steps all differ a little. A tutor who knows your child's board can match their practice to it, and we confirm the board on the call."
  - q: "Why does my child do fine in class but lose marks in Maths tests?"
    a: "Usually because class and tests ask for different things. In class the method has just been taught, so your child knows which one to use. In a test, questions arrive mixed up, and choosing the method is half the work. Try looking at the working on a recent test paper. Neat working with marks dropped near the end often points to technique and timing. Blank or crossed-out working on longer questions suggests a method that never quite set. A tutor reads that paper first and plans from it."
  - q: "How do you choose the tutors who teach GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Carefully. Only about 3% of the tutors who apply make it onto the platform. Harry and Joe interview each one themselves, and they're listening for whether someone can explain a topic like algebraic fractions calmly and clearly to a child who's already decided they can't do it. Every tutor has at least two years of tutoring experience, and many are graduates of Russell Group universities. Each tutor's profile shows their background, so you can see it before choosing."
  - q: "Can my child have GCSE Maths lessons in the Christmas or Easter holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and lots of families find the holidays useful. With no school timetable, there's room for a longer lesson or two in a week, perhaps a full past paper one day and going through it the next. Easter is the obvious time in Year 11, with the exams a few weeks away. Because lessons are online, they carry on wherever you are, which helps if you're away for part of the break."
