faqs:
  - q: "What's the hardest part of GCSE English for Tonbridge students?"
    a: "The parts you can't simply revise. Unseen poetry and the Language reading questions use texts your child hasn't seen before, so they need a method rather than notes. Literature essays are written from memory, and many students run out of time on the second question. The Language writing task often suffers because it comes last. And at Tonbridge sixth forms like Judd and Hillview it's the Language grade that gets checked, so these are worth getting right."
  - q: "Does the exam board matter for GCSE English tutoring?"
    a: "Yes, a lot. AQA, Edexcel and OCR set different papers, and for Literature each school picks its own novel, play and poetry cluster. A tutor who has taught your child's exact texts can start on real essay questions in the first lesson, instead of spending weeks catching up. We'll confirm the board and the books on the consultation call. If your child isn't sure, it's usually on the front of a mock paper."
  - q: "Why is my child's predicted English grade lower than I expected?"
    a: "Usually because of timed conditions, not understanding. Lots of children talk well about a book in class and then write an essay that retells the story, or run out of time before their best point. Predicted grades lean heavily on mocks, so that shows up. A tutor reads a recent mock essay, finds where the marks slipped away, and practises that one habit until it holds under a timer."
  - q: "Are your English tutors teachers or students?"

Harry or Joe have interviewed every one of them."
  - q: "My child doesn't really want an English tutor. Is that normal?"
    a: "Very normal, and English is often the subject teenagers feel most touchy about. It usually changes once they've met the tutor, because it feels nothing like a lesson at school.
