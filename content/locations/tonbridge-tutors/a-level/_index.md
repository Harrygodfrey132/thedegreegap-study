faq_1_q: "He did well at GCSE and Year 12 has still gone wrong. What changed?"
faq_1_a: "The reward changed. GCSE marks a student who has learned the material. A-Level papers hand most of the marks to what gets done with it, so an answer that would have scored well at sixteen now reads as a description. Effort is rarely the problem. A tutor reads a recent script, names the move the mark scheme is waiting for, then rebuilds one answer at a time until it becomes the habit."
faq_2_q: "Can a tutor help with A-Level Chemistry and Biology, or is it mainly Maths?"
faq_2_a: "Both, and the Sciences are among the most asked for here. In Chemistry the marks tend to go on mechanisms and long calculation chains. In Biology it is the six-mark synoptic answers, where two topics have to be joined up under time pressure. Sessions run one-to-one online over Lessonspace, so the working gets written out on a shared whiteboard step by step rather than talked through and lost."
faq_3_q: "Can a tutor help with the EPQ or with coursework?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, within clear limits. A tutor helps a student work out what the question is really asking, plan the structure, find better sources and read their own draft the way a marker would. What gets handed in stays the student's own work, and any tutor worth having says so on day one. For the EPQ the most useful hours are usually early, when the title is still being settled."
faq_4_q: "My daughter is resitting an A-Level. Is that a different job?"
faq_4_a: "It is, and treating it like a fresh start wastes the time she has. The first session goes through what happened in the exam rather than through the whole course again. Ran out of time, or ran out of content? Lost marks in one paper or spread evenly? The answer decides everything after it. Resit plans are usually shorter and much narrower than a first-attempt plan, and they move faster."
