faq_1_q: "My son did well at GCSE but his Year 12 grades have dropped. Is that normal?"
faq_1_a: "Very normal, and it's usually fixable. A-Level stops telling students which method to use, and the amount to learn jumps. Add a new sixth form, new teachers and maybe a different exam board, and a first test well below GCSE level is common. A tutor reads that test, works out where the marks went, and starts there. The sooner that happens, the less the dip affects the predicted grade."
faq_2_q: "When do predicted grades get decided, and when should tutoring start?"
faq_2_a: "Teachers usually write predicted grades from the Year 12 exams and early Year 13 work, and they go off with university applications in the autumn of Year 13. So the most useful time to start is in Year 12, ideally the first term. Starting in Year 13 still helps with the final exams, and many families do. By then, though, the predicted grade is often already set."
