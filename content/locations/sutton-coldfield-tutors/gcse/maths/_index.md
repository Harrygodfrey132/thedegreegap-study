  - q: "Is GCSE Maths in Sutton Coldfield above or below the national average?"
    a: "Generally above, on the headline GCSE results, but that tells you less than you'd think. The town has schools with very different intakes, and a town-wide number smooths all of that away. It says very little about one child, in one set, sitting one tier. The comparison that helps is your child's latest paper against the paper they're entered for. That's what the first lesson looks at. It's specific enough to act on, and it also tests whether the entry itself is right."
  - q: "Does it matter whether my child's on AQA, Edexcel or OCR for Maths?"
    a: "Yes, though not because the content changes. Every board covers the same Maths, because the Department for Education sets the content. The difference is in how each board words its questions and builds its multi-step problems. Your child could know a method perfectly and still stall on an unfamiliar phrasing. So we confirm the board before we suggest anyone, and match on it, because a tutor who knows a board's habits is worth a lot in the final two terms."
  - q: "My child's only in Year 10. Isn't it early to start Maths tutoring?"
    a: "No, and if the tier's still undecided, Year 10 is the most useful year to start. Schools usually settle tiers well before the exams, so evidence gathered now still has time to change the paper your child sits. By the spring of Year 11 it's usually been settled. Year 10 lessons are calmer, too, with more time on rebuilding methods than on drilling papers. That suits a child who's decided Maths just isn't their subject."
  - q: "Will a Maths tutor know how to help my child with dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes. Several tutors on our platform do this regularly, and we'll ask about it on the call so we can match with it in mind. For Maths, the help is mostly about making each step lighter to carry. Your child might set out working the same way every time, split a long question into small, labelled chunks, and practise one method until it sticks before moving on. If timed conditions are the problem, the clock comes in slowly. Let us know what school already does, including exam access arrangements."
  - q: "Is it worth keeping Maths going over the holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and for rebuilding an older gap the holidays are often the best weeks of the year. There's no homework competing for the evening. A half-term or Easter block can take one topic and work at it over several days close together, which suits algebra, because it needs repetition. Lessons are online, so if you're away you can keep the slot. The summer between Year 10 and Year 11 is a popular time for this."
