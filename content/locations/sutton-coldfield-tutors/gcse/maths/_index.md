  - q: "How does GCSE Maths performance in Sutton Coldfield compare to the national average?"
    a: "The area sits above the national picture on headline measures, though it contains schools with quite different intakes and a county or town figure smooths all of that away. It tells you very little about one child in one set sitting one tier. The comparison that helps is your child's most recent paper against the paper they are entered for, which is what the first session looks at. That is specific enough to act on, and it also tests whether the entry itself is right."
  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE Maths, and does it matter for tutoring?"
    a: "It matters more than parents expect. Core content is close to identical, but the boards differ in how questions are worded, how multi-step problems are constructed and which formulae are printed rather than memorised. A student drilled on one board's papers can be thrown by another's phrasing while knowing exactly the same maths. We confirm the board before proposing anyone and match on it, because familiarity with a board's habits is worth a good deal in the final two terms."
  - q: "My child is in Year 10, is it too early to start GCSE Maths tutoring?"
    a: "Year 10 is the right time, and where a tier decision is still live it is the most valuable year of the three. Entries are usually confirmed during Year 10 or early Year 11, so evidence gathered now can still change the paper your child sits, whereas the same work in March of Year 11 cannot. Year 10 sessions are also calmer, weighted towards rebuilding method rather than drilling papers, which suits students who have decided Maths is not their subject."
  - q: "Can a Maths tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and a number of tutors in the network do this regularly. In Maths the help is mostly about reducing load: a consistent layout so steps do not get lost, multi-step questions broken into named stages, and one method worked until it is automatic rather than three covered in an hour. Where exam conditions are the difficulty, timed work is introduced gradually. Tell us on the consultation call what school has in place, including exam access arrangements, and we match on it."
  - q: "Can my child have Maths tutoring during the school holidays?"
    a: "Yes, and for rebuilding an older gap the holidays are often the best weeks of the year. There is no homework competing for the evening, and a half-term or Easter block can take one topic and work it across several consecutive days, which suits algebra because it needs repetition close together. Everything runs online, so families away from home keep their slot. The summer between Year 10 and Year 11 is a common time for this."
