angle_body_1: "Most children learn a paragraph shape early: a point, a quotation, an explanation of the quotation. It's a sound start, and it reliably earns middle-band marks. Higher up, it starts to hold them back. AQA's Literature mark scheme looks for a 'critical, exploratory' response at the top, and a run of tidy, separate paragraphs rarely reads that way. Each quotation gets explained word by word, but the essay never says what the writer is doing overall. Context, like Victorian ideas about poverty in A Christmas Carol, arrives in a final sentence. And in Language Paper 1, the question asking how far your child agrees with a statement gets a polite shrug instead of an answer."

  - q: "My child's essays look neat, but the English marks won't move. What's going wrong?"
    a: "Often the essay has no argument. Each paragraph makes a fair point about a quotation, but read together they don't say anything about the text as a whole, so the mark settles in the middle. The fix is usually a one-sentence answer to the question at the start, then paragraphs that each push that answer further. Context should support a point rather than sit in a paragraph of its own. A tutor works on this with your child's own essays, so it carries straight over to school."
