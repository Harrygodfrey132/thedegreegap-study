faq_1_q: "Is it harder to take an A-Level my child never studied at GCSE?"
faq_1_a: "A little, at first. Usually most of the class is new to it too, so the content isn't the main problem. What's more likely to catch your child out is the skills the subject takes for granted: weighing up a study in Psychology, a source in Politics or a diagram in Economics. The first term sets those habits, so it's a good time to get help. A tutor works on the skills alongside the content, using your child's own early essays and tests."
faq_2_q: "Could my child's A-Levels lead to a degree apprenticeship at Chelsea and Westminster Hospital?"
faq_2_a: "Possibly. The trust on the Fulham Road offers apprenticeships from Level 2 up to degree level, and they're open to anyone over 16 who isn't in full-time education, so the usual time to apply is after A-Levels. Each role sets its own entry requirements, so read the advert for the one your child likes. Science A-Levels tend to matter most for the clinical routes, and a tutor can help with whichever grades that advert asks for."
faq_3_q: "When are UCAS predicted grades decided, and can a tutor still make a difference?"
faq_3_a: "Most sixth forms base predictions on Year 12 work and the end-of-year exams, then confirm them early in Year 13, before applications go off. So the summer term of Year 12 matters more than many families realise. A tutor can still help after that, because offers depend on the real results in August, and the Year 13 mocks are a useful check on progress. But if a prediction will shape which universities your child applies to, starting in Year 12 gives far more room."
