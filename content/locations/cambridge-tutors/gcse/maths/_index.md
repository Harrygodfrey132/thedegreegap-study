faqs:
  - q: "Can a Cambridge GCSE Maths tutor help my child apply for an AstraZeneca or ARM apprenticeship?"
    a: "Yes, and Maths is usually the grade that decides eligibility. The Cambridge employers running apprenticeship schemes, AstraZeneca and ARM among them, publish their own minimum requirements, and those vary by scheme and shift from year to year, so work from the current listing rather than what a friend's older child needed. Technical and engineering routes tend to ask for a strong pass in Maths at the least. Where a student is sitting just under the line, that is a short piece of work, eight to ten weeks on what the paper actually tests."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper for GCSE Maths?"
    a: "Both, because they carry equal weight, but they fail for different reasons. The non-calculator paper punishes shaky arithmetic, index laws and surds, and there is no way to bluff past it. The calculator papers punish method, with multi-step problems where the maths is buried inside a paragraph of context and accuracy that drifts across three or four operations. Most students bleed more on one than the other. A first-session diagnostic across a full set of papers shows which within about twenty minutes."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs a GCSE Maths tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at the working on a recent past paper, not the mark. Clean, complete working that still loses marks at the back of the paper points to exam technique, and a tutor fixes that quickly. Blank space or crossed-out attempts on multi-step questions points to method confidence, and that one rarely improves alone however many hours go into it. If your child is already scoring above eighty per cent across mocks, targeted practice on two or three topics will do more than a weekly programme."
  - q: "What grade improvements have your GCSE Maths tutors actually delivered?"
    a: "The most common pattern across the families we work with is a one to two band lift over a term of weekly sessions. Some are bigger. Reviews on our Google profile describe a student moving from an E to a B, another going from Cs to As, and A-Level students turning E and U grades into passes. We do not promise a grade, because nobody honestly can. What we will do on the call is tell you what looks realistic from where your child is sitting now."
  - q: "What if my child doesn't want a tutor?"
    a: "Common, and better handled gently than pushed through. Most of the resistance is to the idea of tutoring rather than to the tutor, and it tends to soften inside two or three sessions because the format is not school-shaped: one adult, full attention, nobody in the room to be measured against.
