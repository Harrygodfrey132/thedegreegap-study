  - q: "What is the difference between AQA, Edexcel and OCR GCSE English, and does it matter for tutoring?"
    a: "It matters, and in Cambridge it matters more than usual because iGCSE sits in the mix too. The core skills overlap almost entirely. What differs is the shape of the papers: which question carries the evaluation marks, how the unseen element is examined, and how much of the grade sits in the writing sections. A tutor working to the wrong paper will teach good English and still leave marks on the table. We confirm the board and the set texts on the consultation call before matching anyone."
  - q: "How much GCSE English work should my child do between tutoring sessions?"
    a: "Sixty to ninety minutes, and in English that usually means one timed piece rather than an hour spent reading notes. Writing is the thing being assessed, so writing is the thing that has to be practised. A single essay written under exam conditions and handed in for the next session is worth more than three hours of highlighting. Past about ninety minutes the returns fall away sharply, and for a Year 11 already carrying nine subjects it stops being realistic anyway."
