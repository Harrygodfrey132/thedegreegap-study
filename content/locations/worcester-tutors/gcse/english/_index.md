  - q: "Is there one part of GCSE English that Worcester children find hardest?"
    a: "Two parts, usually: remembering quotes with the book shut, and then the comparison question. Quote recall is a memory problem dressed up as an analysis one. A child who understands a text completely can still freeze when it isn't in front of them. Comparison's hard because it needs a different shape of paragraph, one that holds two texts at once instead of doing one and then the other. Both respond quickly to focused work. That's why they're usually where a tutor starts, not where they finish."
  - q: "Why is English such a struggle for my child?"
    a: "Often it's how an exam paragraph is actually built, and reading more books won't teach that. Your child can be bright and working hard and still carry a gap in technique from earlier years that never got picked up. The other common cause is timing, where the marks go to the clock rather than to a lack of understanding. On a report the two look the same. But they need very different work, and that's what the first lesson is for."
