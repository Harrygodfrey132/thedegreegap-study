  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE English for Worcester students?"
    a: "Closed-book quote recall, followed by the comparison question. Recall is hard because it is a memory problem masquerading as an analysis problem, and students who understand a text completely can still freeze without it in front of them. Comparison is hard because it asks for a genuinely different paragraph shape, one that holds two texts at once instead of walking through them in turn. Both respond quickly to targeted work, which is why they are usually where a tutor starts rather than where they finish."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English?"
    a: "Often because nobody has taught them the mechanics of an examinable paragraph, and reading more does not supply it. This is the pattern we see most across Worcester: a bright student, a real effort, and a technique gap from earlier years that a class of thirty never had space to go back for. The other frequent cause is timing, where the marks are lost to the clock rather than to understanding. Both look identical on a report and need completely different work, which is what the first session is for."
  - q: "How do you vet your English tutors?"

References are checked before any match. For English that interview is the part that matters, because knowing why a paragraph sits in a lower band and being able to say it in a way a teenager will act on are not the same skill."
