faq_1_q: "My child revises for hours and the grade does not move. Why?"
faq_1_a: "Because hours spent on the wrong thing do not change a mark. The two causes we see most are a foundation topic that never bedded in, so every question costs extra thinking, and revision that means re-reading, which feels productive and changes very little. Both are invisible from a report and obvious from a paper. Send us a recent mock and a tutor can usually name the problem inside twenty minutes."
faq_2_q: "How far back would a tutor actually go?"
faq_2_a: "As far back as the evidence says, which in Maths is often Year 7 or 8 and in the Sciences is usually the maths inside them rather than the science itself. That sounds alarming and is generally quicker than families fear, because you are repairing a specific thing rather than reteaching three years. The alternative, working around a gap for eighteen months, costs far more time in the end."
faq_3_q: "Is Year 11 too late to start?"
faq_3_a: "No, and most of our starts are Year 11. It does change what is realistic. A gap found in Year 10 can be rebuilt before Year 11 content stacks on top of it. Found at Easter of Year 11, the same gap has to be worked around instead, which means a narrower target and more focus on exam technique. We would rather say that on the call than let you find out in June."
