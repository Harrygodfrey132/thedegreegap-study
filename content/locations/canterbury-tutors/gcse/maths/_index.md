faqs:
  - q: "How does GCSE Maths in Canterbury compare with the rest of Kent?"
    a: "Kent's selective system means the city holds a wider spread inside a small area than most places, so the average tells you very little about any individual child. What matters is not where a student sits against the city but where they sit against the requirement for the course they want next. That is a published number rather than a ranking, which makes it something you can actually work towards. The exam itself is identical wherever it is sat."
  - q: "Can the same tutor cover Maths and Physics?"
    a: "Often yes, and there is a genuine advantage. The equations, rearranging and graph work in GCSE Physics test the same skills as the Maths paper, so a tutor holding both can fix a Maths weakness using Physics questions and the other way round. It also keeps it to a single hour a week instead of two. Where a student needs Maths and an essay subject you want separate specialists, and it is better to accept that than stretch one person across both."
  - q: "How can I tell which topics are losing the marks?"
    a: "By where on the paper they go rather than by the total. Marks going missing at the front of the paper usually mean the content itself was never secure. Losses concentrated at the back, in the multi-step and reasoning questions, mean the content is there but the method is not, which is far quicker to fix. Send a recent mock and a tutor can usually read this within twenty minutes and tell you which of the two you have."
