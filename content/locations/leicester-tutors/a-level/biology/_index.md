faqs:
  - q: "How much A-Level Biology work should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Sixty to ninety minutes across the week, in short sittings rather than one block, and it should be exam questions rather than reading. Answering cold and then marking against the scheme builds the thing the paper tests; rereading builds the feeling of knowing, which does not transfer. Where the content is genuinely missing, short recall work on pathways two or three times a week does more than one long session."
  - q: "How do I know if my child needs a Biology tutor or just more revision?"
    a: "Look at what the revision is before deciding. If it is notes, flashcards and videos and the grade has not moved in two terms, more of it will not help, and a tutor changes the method rather than the volume. If your child is already answering past questions honestly and stalling on the long answers, a tutor is the fastest fix because that is a teachable habit. If neither is happening yet, start with structured past-paper practice and we will say so."
  - q: "Is the A-Level Biology standard in Leicester similar to neighbouring cities?"
    a: "Comparing cities tells you very little about one student. Leicester contains schools and colleges with quite different intakes, and your child sits one specification at one institution. The comparisons that help are their most recent marked paper against the paper they will sit, and their predicted grade against the requirement published this year by the course they want. Both of those are specific enough to act on."
  - q: "Can I sit in on my child's Biology sessions?"
    a: "You can, and some parents do for the first one. At A-Level most students work better without an audience, because the useful part of the hour is the bit where they admit they do not follow something. Every session is recorded, so you can watch any of it afterwards, and the tutor sends a short note on what was covered and what was set. Ask for a progress conversation whenever you want one."
  - q: "What grade improvements have your A-Level Biology tutors delivered?"
    a: "We do not promise grades, because nobody honestly can. The pattern is that the move tracks the cause. A student who knows the biology and is losing marks to how answers are written can shift a band in a term, since the fix is narrow.
