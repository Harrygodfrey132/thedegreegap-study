faqs:
  - q: "What should my child actually be doing between lessons?"
    a: "An hour or so, maybe ninety minutes, spread through the week in short goes. And it should mostly be exam questions, not reading. Answering cold and then marking against the scheme builds what the paper tests. Rereading builds the feeling of knowing, which doesn't carry over into the exam. If content really is missing, short recall work on the big processes, like respiration, two or three times a week beats one long session."
  - q: "We've tried more revision. Why hasn't it worked?"
    a: "Usually because it's more of the same kind. Look at what the revision actually is before deciding anything. If it's notes, flashcards and videos and the grade hasn't moved in two terms, more of it won't help, and a tutor changes the method, not the volume. If your child is already doing past questions properly and stalling on the long answers, a tutor is the quickest fix, since that habit can be taught. If neither is happening yet, start with structured past-paper practice, and we'll say so."
  - q: "Is A-Level Biology harder in Leicester than in other cities?"
    a: "Comparing cities tells you very little about your own child. Leicester has schools and colleges with very different intakes, and your child sits one exam board at one of them. What helps is holding their latest marked paper up against the real exam, and checking their predicted grade against the entry grades for the course they want. You can act on both of those."
  - q: "Can I sit in, or see what's been covered?"

  - q: "Can you tell us what grade our child will get?"
