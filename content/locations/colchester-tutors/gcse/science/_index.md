angle_body_1: "Most Science revision is about facts, because that's what the guides and flashcards are made of. The papers want more than that. A practical question might ask why a step was there, or what had to be kept the same. A six-mark answer is marked on how the points are ordered and linked, not just what's in it. A concentration question can go wrong on a unit before the Chemistry even starts. And an 'evaluate' question wants your child to weigh up both sides and come down on one. That's a writing skill, dropped into a Science exam. More flashcards won't touch any of it."

If A-Level Sciences might be on the cards, have a look at the local sixth forms' entry grades while your child is in Year 10. What they ask of Combined isn't always what they ask of Triple."

faqs:
  - q: "What trips Colchester children up most in GCSE Science?"
    a: "Two things, again and again: the required practicals and the calculations. Your child meets each practical once in a school lab and answers questions on it months later. The content sticks. The method often doesn't. Then there's the number work, mostly moles and concentration in Chemistry and multi-step Physics questions with a unit change. Both feel as if they should be fine, so they get skipped in revision. And they lose marks slowly rather than dramatically, which is why nobody notices until a mock."
  - q: "Do we need a separate tutor for each of the three sciences?"
    a: "Usually not. For Combined Science, one tutor for all three is normally the sensible set-up. Lots of tutors cover Biology and Chemistry together, or Physics and Maths. That second mix works well if your child does Triple alongside higher tier Maths, the harder of the two levels, because the calculations overlap. The exception is a child who's secure in two sciences and really struggling with the third. Then a tutor for just that subject tends to get further, faster."
  - q: "How much Science homework should my child do between lessons?"
    a: "About an hour to an hour and a half across the week. It should be past-paper questions on that week's topic, not re-reading notes, and two or three short sets is plenty. Past 90 minutes it stops helping much. In Science, spending hours making revision cards is one of the most common ways to feel busy without the grade moving. The tutor sets the work and marks it at the start of the next lesson."
  - q: "How much could my child's Science grade realistically go up?"
    a: "Nobody can promise a grade, but we can tell you what's typical. If the problem is technique (practical questions, six-mark answers, accurate calculations), one grade over a term of weekly lessons is a fair aim. It's the one we most often work towards. If whole topics from Year 9 or 10 never went in, it's more like two terms. The first lesson is there to tell you honestly which of those your child is facing."
  - q: "What if my child's Science result comes in lower than they needed?"
    a: "It happens, and there's still a plan. Unlike Maths and English, Science can't be resat in November, so a resit means waiting for the next summer. Often the better first step is a conversation with the sixth form or college. If your child landed just under the grade they needed, ask what's still open to them with the grade they've got. We're happy to talk it through with you. And if a summer resit is the right call, a tutor can plan the year around the paper that let them down."
