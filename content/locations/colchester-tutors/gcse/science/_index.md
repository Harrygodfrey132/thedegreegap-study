angle_body_1: "Science revision is nearly always content revision, because that is what the guides and the flashcards are made of. The papers ask for more than that. Required practicals want the method, the control variable and the reason a step exists, not the result. The long six-mark answers are marked on how the response is organised. The calculations running through Chemistry and Physics lose marks on units and rearranging far more often than on the underlying science. And evaluate questions want a judgement with reasons on both sides, which is a writing skill dropped into a science exam."

faqs:
  - q: "What is the hardest part of GCSE Science for Colchester students?"
    a: "Two things come up repeatedly. The first is the required practicals, because students meet them once in a lab and then answer questions about them months later, so the method has faded while the content has not. The second is the quantitative side, mostly moles and concentration in Chemistry and multi-step calculations with unit changes in Physics. Both feel like they should be secure and both bleed marks steadily rather than dramatically, which is why they often go unnoticed until a mock."
  - q: "Do we need a separate tutor for each of the three sciences?"
    a: "Usually not. For combined science, one tutor across all three is normally the sensible arrangement. Many tutors are comfortable across Biology and Chemistry, or across Physics and Maths, which suits students taking triple science alongside higher-tier Maths because the calculation skills overlap directly. Where a student is secure in two sciences and struggling badly in the third, a specialist in that one subject tends to produce faster movement than a generalist covering everything at once."
  - q: "How much Science homework should my child do between sessions?"
    a: "Around 60 to 90 minutes across the week, and it should be past-paper questions on the topic covered in the lesson rather than re-reading notes. Two or three question sets is usually the right volume. Beyond about 90 minutes the returns drop off sharply, and for Science in particular, more time spent making revision materials is one of the most common ways students feel busy without moving the grade. The tutor sets the work and marks it at the start of the next session."
  - q: "What sort of Science grade movement is realistic to expect?"
    a: "We describe what is typical rather than promise a number, which matters in a subject where families are often quoted outcomes elsewhere. Where the gap is technique, meaning practical method, six-mark structure and calculation accuracy, one grade band across a term of weekly sessions is a realistic target and the one we most often work towards. Where content from Year 9 or 10 is genuinely missing, it takes closer to two terms. The first session exists to tell you honestly which situation you are in."
  - q: "What happens if my child does not get the Science grade they needed?"
    a: "We work with families through results day and the November resit window, and it is worth saying that this is a normal part of the year rather than a failure. An eight-week block starting in September typically targets the move from a 3 to a 4, or a 4 to a 5, which are the most common resit goals. Where a student needed a particular grade for a sixth-form place and landed just under, the more useful conversation is often with the sixth form about a remediation plan, and we help with that too."
