The city grew quickly and its schools grew with it, so a family can move a few miles and find a noticeably different intake. The paper is identical wherever it is sat."
  - q: "Should my child focus on the calculator or non-calculator paper?"
    a: "Both, because they carry equal weight, but they fail differently. The non-calculator paper punishes shaky arithmetic, index laws and surds, and there is nowhere to hide on it. The calculator papers punish method: multi-step problems where the maths is buried in context, and accuracy that drifts across several operations. Most students bleed noticeably more on one than the other, and a diagnostic across a full set of papers shows which within about twenty minutes."
  - q: "Can the same tutor cover Maths and Physics?"
    a: "Often yes, and there is a real advantage to it. The equations, rearranging and graph work in GCSE Physics are the same skills the Maths paper tests, so a tutor who holds both can fix a Maths weakness using Physics questions and vice versa. It also means one hour a week rather than two. Where a student needs Maths and an essay subject, you want separate specialists and it is better to accept that than stretch one person across both."
