  - q: "Which Maths paper should my child put more work into, calculator or non-calculator?"
    a: "Both matter. The three papers count the same, one without a calculator and two with, but they trip children up in different ways. The non-calculator paper shows up shaky arithmetic, index laws (the rules for powers) and surds (square roots left as roots), and there's nowhere to hide. The calculator papers test method: problems with several steps, where the Maths is wrapped up in a real-life setting and small slips add up. Most children lose noticeably more on one than the other. Going through a full set of papers with a tutor shows which in about twenty minutes."
  - q: "Could one tutor help with both Maths and Physics?"

If your child needs Maths and an essay subject like English or History, though, it's better to have two separate tutors than to stretch one person across both."
