faqs:
  - q: "Which part of GCSE English costs Birmingham children the most marks?"
    a: "Often it's the last question on the paper, because time ran out before your child got there. After that come the Literature comparison, the unseen poem and the creative writing on Language Paper 1. All three get easier with a routine: a comparison built the same way every time, a set of questions to ask of any poem, and a quick plan for the story or description before the writing starts. Once those are in place, the last question stops being a rush."
  - q: "My child's school does Edexcel, not AQA. Does that matter?"
    a: "Yes, mostly at the top end of the grades. A lot of the content overlaps, but the papers are built differently. The two boards set different tasks on the unseen texts and weight the writing questions differently, and the set texts vary from school to school. A tutor used to the other board can spend lessons on the wrong kind of question. So we check your child's board and texts on the free call, and the practice papers match what the school is teaching."
  - q: "How do I work out where my child's English marks are going?"
    a: "Look at a recent marked paper, question by question. Low marks on the comparison usually mean the essay structure needs work. Lost marks on the poetry question often mean your child is describing the poem rather than saying what it's doing. Low marks on the creative writing usually come down to writing well against the clock. The tutor reads that pattern in the first lesson and starts with whichever is weakest."
