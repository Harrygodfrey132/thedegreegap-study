faq_1_q: "Should we start in Year 10, or is that too soon?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10 works well for a lot of children. There's less riding on each test, so if a topic from Year 8, say ratio or fractions, never really went in, a tutor can spend a few weeks on it without a mock date hanging over either of them. For a child who gets home late, one lesson a week in Year 10 is often easier to fit in than a scramble later on. And if your child's already in Year 11, it's still worth starting. The tutor just goes straight to the topics costing the most marks."
faq_2_q: "My child's shattered after the bus home. Won't a lesson just be one more thing?"
faq_2_a: "It's a fair worry, and it's better to plan around it than hope it goes away. You pick the slot with the tutor, so if Mondays leave your child flat, book a Thursday, or start a bit later once they've eaten. An hour with one person, going at your child's pace, often feels lighter than another hour alone with a revision guide."
faq_3_q: "How do you know which exam board my child is on?"
faq_3_a: "It's often listed on the school's website, and if not, your child's subject teacher will know. One timetable can mix boards, so it's worth checking each subject rather than assuming. It matters more than people expect. In GCSE Maths, for example, AQA, Edexcel and OCR set three papers while Eduqas sets two, and each board words its questions in its own way. Once we know, the profiles we send are tutors familiar with those papers."

faq_4_a: "That's not unusual, and nobody's at fault. Some children want a tutor who's brisk and gets on with it, others need someone who'll slow right down, and you can't always tell which from a profile. Let us know, even after a single lesson, and we'll send new profiles, with nothing extra to pay.
