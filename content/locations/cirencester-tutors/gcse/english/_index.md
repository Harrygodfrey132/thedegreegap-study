faqs:
  - q: "Which bit of GCSE English loses the most marks?"
    a: "Usually it's the timed analysis on the Language papers, where your child knows what a writer is doing but can't explain it fast enough, and the essay on an unseen poem in Literature. Both get easier with a clear plan and plenty of practice against the clock. The details differ from board to board, and a pupil at Cirencester Deer Park may sit different papers from one at Westonbirt, so the tutor practises your child's own board rather than a general version."
  - q: "What actually happens in an online English lesson?"
    a: "Most start with something your child has written, a homework answer or a timed paragraph, up on the shared whiteboard. Your child and the tutor go through it line by line, then your child has another go at one section with the tutor's comments beside it. Some weeks it's quotations and context for a set text, others it's a Language question from a past paper. You'll get a short note afterwards on what was covered and what's next."
  - q: "Between lessons, how much English practice is enough?"
    a: "About an hour across the week is plenty for most children, split into short pieces. That might be one timed question from a past paper and a handful of quotations to learn. Much more than that and it starts eating into other subjects and sleep, which matters when the school day's already long. The tutor sets something realistic each week and checks it at the start of the next lesson."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia?"
    a: "Yes. Mention it on the free call and we'll look for tutors who've worked with dyslexia before. In English, that might mean a colour-coded plan your child fills in before writing, or a short bank of quotations learnt a few at a time rather than all at once. And because every lesson's recorded, your child can go back to the part on structure the night before an essay."
  - q: "What if my child misses the English grade Cirencester College asks for?"
    a: "It happens, and there's a way back. English Language is one of the two GCSEs that can be resat in November, alongside Maths, so your child can try again within a few months. A tutor can use the autumn to work on the exact questions where marks were lost. It's worth talking to the college about how a resit fits with the course your child wants, since every route has its own requirements."
