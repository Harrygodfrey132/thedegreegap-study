faq_1_q: "Year 10 or Year 11, when does GCSE tutoring make sense here?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10 if the choice is yours. Year 11 content stacks straight onto Year 10, so a gap closed now stops doing damage for eighteen months, and Year 10 sessions are calmer because they rebuild method instead of drilling papers. Year 11 still works and plenty of families start there. The plan just narrows towards technique and past papers, and the useful window closes around the winter mocks that the sixth forms end up reading."
faq_2_q: "The school is strong and the grade still has not moved. What does a tutor add?"
faq_2_a: "Time on one student. A teacher with thirty in the room cannot stop the lesson to rebuild a Year 8 method for one of them, however good the school is, and that is the whole gap. A tutor sits with a marked script, finds the four or five places marks are leaking, and works only on those. Most of what changes in the first month is not new content. It is the answer-writing habits nobody has had an hour to correct."
faq_3_q: "Do your tutors work on the board my child's school teaches?"
faq_3_a: "Yes, and we confirm it before proposing anyone. Boards differ in how questions are worded, how required practicals are examined and which formulae are printed rather than memorised, and those differences decide marks at the top of a paper. Barnet schools do not all sit the same one, so the board goes in the notes on the first call alongside the tier and the set texts."
faq_4_q: "What if my child has fallen a long way behind in one subject?"
faq_4_a: "Then we go back rather than forward, which sounds slower and usually is not. The tutor works backwards through your child's own wrong answers until the understanding runs out, and that point is normally earlier than anybody guessed. From there the rebuild runs alongside school instead of repeating it, and the two meet somewhere around the middle of the term. It needs a term rather than a fortnight, and we will say so honestly on the call."
