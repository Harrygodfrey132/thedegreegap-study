faq_1_q: "Is Year 10 the time to start, or can it wait until Year 11?"
faq_1_a: "Year 10, if you've got the choice. Year 11 content stacks straight onto Year 10, so a gap closed now stops doing damage for the next eighteen months, and Year 10 lessons are calmer because they rebuild method instead of drilling papers. Year 11 still works, and plenty of families start then. The plan just narrows to technique and past papers, and the most useful window closes around the winter mocks the sixth forms end up reading."
faq_2_q: "My child's school is good, and the grade still hasn't moved. What would a tutor add?"
faq_2_a: "Time on your child alone. However good the school, a teacher with thirty in the room can't stop the lesson to rebuild a Year 8 method for one pupil, and that's the whole gap. A tutor sits with a marked paper, finds the four or five places marks are leaking, and works only on those. Most of what changes in the first month isn't new content. It's answer-writing habits nobody's had a spare hour to correct."
faq_3_q: "Barnet schools use different boards. Does that change which tutor we get?"
faq_3_a: "Yes. We confirm your child's board before suggesting anyone. Each board has its own style of question and its own mark schemes, and knowing them well is worth marks, especially near the top of a paper. So on the first call we'll note the board alongside the tier (higher or foundation) and any set texts."
faq_4_q: "What if my child has fallen a long way behind in one subject?"
faq_4_a: "Then we go back rather than forward, which sounds slower and usually isn't. The tutor works back through your child's own wrong answers until the understanding runs out, and that point's normally earlier than anyone guessed. From there the rebuild runs alongside school instead of repeating it, and the two meet somewhere around the middle of the term. It takes a term rather than a fortnight, and we'll tell you that plainly on the call."
