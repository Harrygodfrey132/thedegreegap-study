faqs:
  - q: "How does GCSE English performance in Barnet compare with the national picture?"
    a: "Barnet sits clearly above it, and has done for several years across the borough as a whole. That is useful context and almost useless for planning, because a borough figure says nothing about one student on one paper. What it does change is the comparison your child is making in their own head, since the person sitting next to them is often doing well too. The number worth looking at is the gap between their last marked essay and the band above it."
  - q: "How can I tell which parts of GCSE English my child is losing marks on?"
    a: "Sort a marked paper into three piles rather than reading the total. Pile one is reading questions on the extract, pile two is the longer essay on a set text or the poetry comparison, pile three is the writing task. Nearly every student is clearly weaker in one of the three, and each needs different work: evidence selection, essay structure, or accuracy and control under a clock. Ten minutes with a script tells you more than a term of general revision."
  - q: "Why is my child struggling with GCSE English in Barnet?"
    a: "Most often because nobody taught them what a paragraph has to contain. Reading was never the problem; the writing is. A student can understand a text well, talk about it fluently, and still hand in three sentences that restate the question, because in conversation a listener fills the gaps and a mark scheme does not. The other common root is an earlier one, a text introduced when the class had already moved past it, which shows up two years later as avoidance."
  - q: "Can an English tutor help a child with dyslexia, ADHD or processing differences?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about it during matching rather than waiting for it to come up. Several tutors work with neurodivergent students regularly. The practical help is structural: a consistent essay frame so planning does not eat the time, long questions broken into named stages, and timed work introduced gradually where the clock is the real difficulty. Tell us what school already has in place, including any exam access arrangements, and we match accordingly."
  - q: "Can we do GCSE English tutoring on a budget?"
    a: "Yes, mostly by being deliberate about when rather than buying more hours. One weekly session from Year 10 does more than two a week started in April, because writing habits need months of repetition to hold under exam conditions. The other route is a short block, six to eight sessions aimed squarely at whichever paper the mock says is doing the damage, then stopping. Sessions are paid one at a time, with no contract and no joining fee."
