faqs:
  - q: "Barnet's GCSE results are above average. What does that mean for my child's English?"
    a: "Barnet does sit clearly above the national average across the borough as a whole, and has for several years. That's useful background, but it won't help you plan, because a borough figure says nothing about one child on one paper. What it can change is how your child sees themselves, since the person sitting next to them is often doing well too. The number worth looking at is the gap between their last marked essay and the band above it. That's where a tutor starts."
  - q: "How can I tell which bits of GCSE English my child's losing marks on?"
    a: "It's easier than you'd think. Sort a marked paper into three piles instead of reading the total. Pile one is the reading questions on the extract. Pile two is the longer essay on a set text or the poetry comparison. Pile three is the writing task. Nearly every child is clearly weaker in one of the three, and each needs different work: choosing evidence, essay structure, or accuracy and control against the clock. Ten minutes with a marked script tells you more than a term of general revision."
  - q: "Why does my child understand the texts but still struggle with GCSE English?"
    a: "Usually it's the writing, not the reading. A paragraph has to contain certain things to score, and a teacher with a whole class can't go through every child's paragraphs with them one by one. Your child can understand a text well and talk about it fluently, then hand in three sentences that restate the question. In conversation a listener fills the gaps. A mark scheme doesn't. Sometimes the cause goes further back, to something the class moved on from before it had sunk in, and it shows up two years later as avoidance."
  - q: "Can you find an English tutor who understands dyslexia or ADHD?"
    a: "Yes, and we ask about it on the call rather than waiting for it to come up. Several tutors on our platform do this kind of work week in, week out. The help is mostly practical. A consistent essay frame, so planning doesn't eat the time. Long questions broken into named stages. Timed work brought in gradually if the clock's the real difficulty. Let us know what support school's already given, such as extra time in exams, and we'll bear it in mind when we match."
  - q: "We're watching the budget. Can GCSE English tutoring in Barnet still work for us?"
    a: "Yes, and it's mostly about being smart with timing rather than buying more hours.
